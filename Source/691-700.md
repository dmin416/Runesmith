Chapter 689 – Testing The Arcane.

“Now then, try it again. I adjusted the pen to better handle arcane energies.”

“Master really can do anything…”

“Ah… I’m not so sure about that…”

Roland shook his head when he saw Millie’s eyes sparkling again. The two were testing her new class inside the workshop. They were currently in the room his apprentice used for studying, which would likely become the main place where she practiced this craft. Sheets of reinforced parchment had already been stacked neatly beside Millie, along with several small bottles of shimmering dark blue ink.

The metallic pen Roland had created rested in his hands. The runes engraved across its surface still shimmered faintly as he performed some final runesmithing adjustments to attune it to the arcane energies she was using. It looked rather simple at first glance, but beneath the polished silver casing were dozens of miniature runes designed to stabilize the arcane flow. The nib itself had been forged from dwarven steel mixed with etherium to improve mana conduction.

“Still, be careful. Arcane energy is far more unstable than regular mana.”

“Of course, Master. Don’t worry!”

Millie carefully accepted the pen from his hands as though she had just been entrusted with a priceless artifact. The silver surface reflected the workshop lights faintly while the tiny runes hidden beneath the casing pulsed with a soft glow.

“It feels heavier compared to the old quill. I’ve never seen one like this before…”

“You’ll get used to it in no time. For now, try to infuse the magical ink with arcane energy, then we can move on to the next step.”

“I’ll give it my all, Master!”

Millie nodded seriously. Unlike yesterday’s excitement, she now looked completely focused. Roland stepped back from the desk and crossed his arms while observing her carefully.

The workshop had been prepared in advance for this experiment. Several layered barriers shimmered faintly along the walls beneath engraved metal plates. If the arcane energies spiraled out of control, the resulting explosion would at least remain contained within the room. Roland was also there to protect Millie if anything went haywire.

“Let’s start with the ink bottle first. Try using your ‘Arcane Ink Infusion’ skill on the vial. There isn’t much ink inside, but still be careful. Your bracelet will help compensate for any mana loss.”

“Here I go, Master…”

She picked up the small container of liquid. Though it resembled glass, it was made from a far more durable material. Roland had no intention of risking it shattering and injuring his apprentice. The moment Millie activated her skill, the ink inside the vial reacted.

A faint blue glow spread through the liquid. The ink itself was pitch black, making it appear as though tiny blue veins of mana were coursing through it. However, something soon happened that caused Roland’s brow to rise. The color of the energy shifted from blue to purple, and the vial began glowing red.

‘There is a clear increase in heat and…’

He glanced at Millie’s status, focusing mainly on her MP. It was dropping rapidly, to the point that her bracelet had to compensate for the loss. Under normal circumstances, she would have already run out of mana, which made him suspect this was not the intended use of her new skill. Then there was the heat produced by the concentrated mana, which would have caused a normal vial to burst long ago.

“That’s enough, Millie. Stop for now.”

“Uh…”

Millie was still holding the vial in one hand, and the moment he told her to stop, she obeyed. It was clear that the girl was exhausted. Even though the bracelet compensated for the lost mana, the skill was far more taxing than regular mana calligraphy.

“It seems that infusing the ink inside the bottle might not be possible, at least not for now. Instead, we’ll try using the pen directly.”

He picked the pen up from where Millie had set it down and showed her how to insert the vial into the side of the fountain pen. The cylindrical chamber clicked softly into place beneath the casing once secured.

“This compartment acts as both a reservoir and a regulator. The runes inside distribute the mana more evenly before it reaches the nib and, once it does, infuse it with arcane energy. After that, the ink flows through the smaller channels before reaching the tip through the final slit. At that point, you’ll probably need to activate your other skill, ‘Arcane Penmanship’ to guide the arcane-infused ink through the paper.”

Millie continued nodding as he explained the process, and soon they attempted it a second time. Now her main task was to infuse the ink once it reached the nib at the end of the pen.

The moment Millie activated the skill again, the runes hidden within the pen responded immediately. A faint silver light spread across the metallic casing while the dark ink inside the reservoir began to swirl and react. Just as Roland had instructed, the process continued, and this time it worked far better than before.

Instead of violently heating the entire vial at once, the arcane energy was infused while the ink traveled through the pen’s channels. With a much smaller amount of liquid to handle, the process became easier for Millie, and after a few tries, the pen finally responded.

“M-master, something is happening!”

Millie shouted as she saw a faint purplish aura forming around the pen tip. The silver metal trembled softly in her grasp while the ink inside glowed with streaks of deep violet light.

“The ink has absorbed the arcane energy. You should be able to put it on paper now. Why don’t you try one of the easier runes first?”

Roland instructed her to attempt one of the old enchantments she already knew. Curious about what would happen if she tried creating a runic structure with the altered ink, he watched closely as she obeyed.

“Right… here I go…”

Just as advised, she began slowly. Arcane Penmanship guided her hand across the paper. The signs of arcane mana were unmistakable as the ink glowed purple and pulsed like living veins. Yet Roland immediately noticed that something was wrong. The moment she completed the structure, the glow faded almost instantly, and all the energy he had sensed moments before vanished.

“A… failure?”

“I’m sorry, Master… let me try again!”

“You did well, Millie. It’s not really your fault. The rune structure itself needs adjustments to function properly.”

Roland’s multiple minds quickly worked through what had just happened. The spell he had created was quite simple, only a magical light meant to shine above the scroll once activated. However, the moment arcane energy was introduced, the runes stopped functioning correctly. The mana density was far higher than usual, and it seemed to clog both the traces and the runic structures themselves. For this to work, he would need to adapt the design to the new magical wavelength and make multiple adjustments.

“How about you try recreating the spell you learned during the ascension trial?”

“That one? Sure, I have it right here, Master!”

Millie pointed to the notebook resting on the table. Inside was a sketch of the arcane enchantment she had used during the trial. It looked like a fusion of runic language and magical circles. The usual runic traces had been replaced with circular formations along with several unfamiliar components.

Roland watched as his apprentice carefully recreated the drawing. Earlier, he had intended her to fail the first rune so he could observe how the arcane energy reacted to it. Now that he could study the correct structure properly, he finally understood why the previous rune had collapsed. As Millie continued, he also began to grasp the underlying logic and how the runes could be adapted accordingly.

‘Interesting…’

The construct looked unstable, yet he could feel the magic within it surpassing what a tier one spell scroll should normally contain. It was far more potent than the runic scrolls he had learned to scribe at the beginning.

‘The destructive potential of arcane spells is well known, and this is why…’

The moment the scroll was completed, it activated on its own. Just as Millie had described earlier, a brilliant flash erupted from the center of the parchment. During her trial, she had only seen the blinding light at the very end, but now Roland witnessed it himself.

The spell was some form of light magic, intense enough to flood the room with brilliant white radiance for several seconds. Roland quickly covered both his own eyes and Millie’s before the glare could blind them.

“Ack…”

“Quite the light show…”

“W-wait, was that it?”

Millie peeked out from beneath Roland’s hand, staring at the burnt page she had just finished. The ink had burst apart the moment the spell activated, revealing yet another flaw in this style of scribing.

“That’s not a bad start, but you’ll need to work on your finishing technique. The scroll shouldn’t activate so easily on its own.”

Millie lowered the smoking parchment and stared at the charred center in disbelief.

“But it worked, didn’t it?”

“It did.”

Roland stepped closer and picked up the ruined sheet before the remaining ash could crumble across the desk. Despite the spreading burn marks, parts of the circular arcane structure were still visible. Thin traces of violet energy lingered for a moment before fading completely.

“The spell formation itself succeeded. The problem lies in its stability and spell grade.”

“Grade?”

She looked up at him, and he nodded.

“Yes. It simply means you need more practice. Start by creating a stable version of this spell first.”

“I can do that!”

Millie was already brimming with energy and eager to continue. Roland did not stop her, though he did hand her a pair of protective glasses to shield her eyes from the blinding flashes. The spell was stable enough that she could work alone, but he still instructed Sebastian to keep watch while he turned his attention to another matter.

‘I need to convert some of the runic spells and figure out the logic behind their components…’

It was all too similar to when he had become a runic mana scribe. Back then, he had used his debugging skill to steal various runic enchantments and catalogue their components into groups. He could do the same here by reverse engineering the initial spell Millie had created. His True Runic Sight skill had already helped him transform the arcane enchantment into a runic one, and with both versions in mind, he could begin deducing how everything worked.

Thankfully, this was only a tier one enchantment, something he could analyze rather quickly. When he had been a tier one class holder himself, it had taken him half a year to figure out which component did what, but after that, his runic creation skills had soared.

‘What she needs are a few simple spells to help her get used to arcane energies.’

While his student worked, he started devising a way to repurpose simple runic enchantments into arcane ones. She had no way of creating new spells without blindly experimenting on her own. Neither did he know of any place that stored such magical enchantments, but there were ordinary arcane magicians out there, along with books detailing their spells and techniques.

From what he knew, there had once been people who crafted arcane scrolls. However, they were quickly overshadowed by regular enchanted scrolls and runic ones. Arcane scrolls were simply too unstable to be practical for monster hunting, so the craft gradually faded away as people turned to more reliable forms of magic. In the end, raw power meant little if it could not be used effectively.

For the rest of the day, he worked on a system for his apprentice. It consumed much of his time, but after several hours, he finally managed to crack the code. Once he understood the underlying structure, it became much easier to transcribe old runic spell schematics and create a simple study guide for her.

By the time evening arrived, Millie had already burned through an entire stack of reinforced parchment. The room smelled faintly of scorched paper and magical smoke. Thin curls of smoke still drifted upward from her latest failed attempt when Roland entered carrying a notebook.

“That should be enough for now. Go through these notes, and you can try again tomorrow.”

“Mm… I think I’m starting to get the hang of this, master…”

Millie sounded somewhat disappointed, as most of her attempts had ended in failure. Even so, while she only gained experience from successfully completing a scroll, her skill levels still improved through repeated arcane calligraphy practice.

“You’re improving, but don’t get ahead of yourself. Study the material in the notebook first, then try again.”

“Yes, master!”

With that, Millie headed home. Two guards waited for her outside to escort her back, and Roland finally had time to focus on other matters.

‘When I’m at the institute, I should go through the library and gather everything I can on arcane magic.’

He had been postponing the visit for quite some time, wanting to make the most of his days in Albrook, especially since the Headmistress no longer asked him to lecture there regularly. Still, the time had finally come for him to return and deal with the teenage students again.

After eating dinner and spending some more time working on the mobile armor, he went to bed beside his wife. Early the next morning, he handed Millie another notebook to study.

“I might not be able to come here as often anymore, so if you don’t see me in the workshop, ask Sebastian for advice.”

“Oh? Where are you going, Master?”

Millie stretched and rubbed her eyes as she spoke. Her gaze had already been fixed on the notebook, but the moment he mentioned where he was headed, her attention snapped to him almost instantly.

“For now, I’m heading to Xandar’s Institute of Wizardry through the gate. After that, I have a few other matters to attend to…”

There were two main things he wanted to take care of. One was finishing the large golem for the competition, and the other was helping both himself and Agni grow stronger. However, he still needed to create several magical items for other students, which would likely take a few more days.

“Xandar’s Institute of Wizardry… that sounds like a magic school!”

“Ah, yes, it is.”

His assistant, who had been focused on her work moments ago, now seemed completely captivated by the idea. The fascination was written plainly across her face. He had no intention of bringing her along since she would probably slow him down, but it was also a place where she could learn a great deal through the library alone.

While he could always create guides and notebooks that got straight to the point, there were benefits to broadening one’s knowledge through reading. Titles could be gained that way, and the mind was much like a muscle. The more information it absorbed, the stronger it became.

“Hm… perhaps if you work hard enough, I’ll show you the institute library.”

“Really!?”

She nearly jumped from her seat in excitement, and he immediately regretted saying it. The promise had already been made, though in truth, it would probably be good for her to experience the world of magic and meet mentors like Arion.

“Yes, of course. I’ll need to ask if your parents are comfortable with it. We would be traveling through the gate into the kingdom proper.”

“Oh, I’m sure Mama and Papa will agree. I’ll make certain of it!”

Roland could only sigh as he watched her enthusiasm rise once again. She already looked ready to drag her parents to the gate herself if necessary.

“Do not pressure them too much. Allowing their daughter to travel alone into the kingdom is no small matter. Anything could happen.”

“I know, I know… but I also know I can count on you, Master!”

“Is that so…”

Roland was not entirely sure where her confidence came from, but if he ever decided to take her along, he would need to live up to it. Traveling alone was one thing. Bringing his disciple with him was another entirely. Constructing a few tier three golemic defenders for her protection might be a good idea.

‘With those schematics I received from Master Hasim, it should not be too difficult… but first.’

With that, the two parted ways, and he headed toward the teleportation gate in his workshop. The trip had become routine after so many journeys, and the discomfort he once felt around the students there had almost disappeared.

Even so, he still paused for a moment to gather himself before stepping through. His introverted tendencies had not vanished completely, but he understood that this was necessary.

On the other side, he was greeted by the familiar magician and one of his closest allies, Arion the Rune Mage.

“My friend, you are here! It feels as though we have not spoken in ages!”

“In person, perhaps… so, how are things? Have the students gathered?”

“They are gathering as we speak. Why don’t we head over and see what the institute’s brightest young minds are like?”

Roland needed to determine which students were best suited for which runic aid devices. There would be many candidates, and some would benefit more than others. Those decisions would ultimately fall to him and Arion.

Soon, the two moved through the halls together: one armored man and a floating black cat at his side.

Chapter 690 – Ability Scanner.

“The place seems busier than usual…”

Roland commented as he noticed far more activity than normal throughout the Institute. Students filled nearly every corridor. Some hurried between classrooms carrying stacks of books, while others gathered in small groups, speaking excitedly among themselves.

Even several instructors seemed unusually occupied, moving from one wing of the building to another with documents tucked beneath their arms. Others, however, appeared different. They were not busy at all, just unusually cheerful as they wandered about carrying spatial bags and backpacks.

“Of course it is. The exams have ended, and you know what that means.”

“Ah... It's like that...”

Roland had not been at the school for very long and had been too busy to pay much attention to the students and their lives. Most of the exams were over, which meant the students finally had some free time for themselves. Those who had not passed still needed to take supplementary examinations, but judging by the atmosphere, most had already escaped that fate.

“They seem awfully excited.”

“Wouldn't you be? They get to see their families and relax. Others will be allowed to venture outside and take on jobs with adventurers. It's a big step toward becoming adults.”

Arion floated beside Roland in his black cat form. His tail swayed lazily as the two observed the students. Their faces were filled with anticipation and excitement. Some spoke about returning home for the holidays, while others compared plans for dungeon expeditions and temporary work contracts. Roland even overheard a group discussing which adventuring parties were recruiting apprentice mages this season.

The sight reminded him of when his brother Robert had arrived in Albrook with the woman who later became his wife. They had participated in a joint expedition between a magic academy and a knight academy. Some of the students here were now at that same age and would soon be leaving for several months on similar missions. They would gain valuable experience and earn merit points in the process.

It was not just about gaining combat experience in the field. These expeditions also gave them the opportunity to forge connections with promising young knights. Some mages hoped to become tutors for noble families or enter their service directly. Others dreamed of joining the mage towers of powerful tier three mages, while some wished to remain at the Institute and teach future generations of students.

“A lot of new students are running around as well. You should perhaps change the scheduling.”

Arion laughed as Roland pointed out the chaos in the halls.

“Ah, perhaps we should.”

His voice remained light as his floating black cat form tilted in midair, tail curling lazily behind him.

“Though if we changed the schedule now, I suspect half the faculty would start complaining. Everyone is already used to it.”

“Hm...”

Roland grumbled quietly. The Institute's schedule had always struck him as inefficient. Mages were an odd bunch. Instead of spreading their workload more evenly, they tended to cram everything into a handful of particularly busy days. They seemed to prefer working intensely for short periods rather than maintaining a steady pace.

For Roland, who was constantly working anyway, this was not much of a problem. However, when ordinary people who were not accustomed to such a demanding rhythm tried to keep up, mistakes were almost inevitable.

He could already see confused new students getting lost in the halls and teachers rushing around with various lists, trying to find them and guide them to the correct dorms. To make matters worse, the corridors had not been designed to handle this much traffic at once.

Groups of students struggled to squeeze past one another in the narrower passages, while upperclassmen carried oversized luggage enchanted to hover just above the ground. One such piece of luggage was suddenly launched into the air when a rushing student accidentally kicked it, forcing Roland to catch it with his mage hand spell.

“Don’t run in the halls.”

His voice echoed through the corridor, amplified by magic. The moment he spoke, everyone stopped and turned to look at him.

“It’s the Full Mithril Enforcer…”

“You idiot, he’s the Full Mithril Deputy now.”

“Oh, right…”

The students whispered among themselves while staring at his armored figure. He was wearing the same suit of armor he usually used here, though he could switch to one of his other sets whenever he wished. Since arriving at the academy, he had become somewhat infamous, earning scorn from some and respect from others. As far as he knew, many students feared him. Rumors had spread about him fighting knights and even killing people.

“Ha ha, you’re as famous as ever, Deputy Professor Wayland!”

Arion laughed, and the moment he spoke, the corridor grew even quieter. The students began moving more cautiously, and many stepped to the sides of the hallway. It was clear they were waiting for him to pass before continuing on their way.

“Sure…”

‘Well, this isn’t so bad. At least they won’t bother me if they’re afraid of me.’

His reputation did have its advantages. The peace and quiet it brought was one of them. With a single sentence, he had silenced the crowd and cleared a path through the corridor. As he walked away, he could already hear the noise returning behind him. Some students even cast silencing spells to avoid being overheard, but he could still catch snippets of their conversations. Some were relieved he was gone, while others wondered where he was headed.

“So, Professor Arion, the examinations are over, and since you called for me, I assume the initial selection process has been completed?”

“That’s right.”

Arion nodded, and Roland quickly moved on to another matter.

“Have you prepared ‘that’ as I asked?”

“By that, you mean…”

Arion’s tail wiggled slightly before he pointed a paw at Roland and traced a strange shape in the air that resembled a rune.

“Yes, that. We’ll need it for the secondary selection process.”

“Indeed. Don’t worry, everything has been prepared, and all the students should have gathered by now.”

“Good.”

Roland nodded. He had arranged something that would help him determine the most suitable equipment for the students. The competition between the schools did not involve only the younger participants. Some events, such as the golem competition, were contested by entire departments. Low level mages could not truly create golems. At best, they could summon magical constructs that resembled them.

The competition was a mix of youths competing against other youths with the guidance of their teachers. However, the teachers also had several ways to compete against one another, and Roland would need to find ways to help his side secure victory and achieve a strong ranking.

He was the one who had designed the graphics displayed on the current monitor. A recreation of the student's body appeared on one side of the screen, while all of their statistics were listed on the other. Their level, attributes, learned skills, and even titles could be recorded.

Whether they were best suited for a treasure hunt or a flag battle, the system would perform its calculations and provide the three most suitable options.

After the scan concluded, it began moving across a sheet of paper, writing down the student's name along with the three competitions available to them. While the academy could have assigned events directly, they preferred to give the young mages a choice that they could mark down themselves.

Nevertheless, not every competition had room for everyone. Most of the youths would naturally choose the events that offered the greatest number of merits rather than those best suited to their abilities. Because of this, the system maintained priority rankings and compatibility percentages for every role while also taking academic performance into account. Everything was calculated through the system Arion and Roland had devised.

Every so often, she glanced in his direction as if she were afraid to face him. He was not entirely sure why, but perhaps she worried that he would disapprove of her decision to leave the institute.

Although the interschool competition was a prestigious event where young mages could prove themselves, injuries were not uncommon. Some participants took it very seriously and had no qualms about cheating or harming their rivals. Yet Roland could not help wondering whether she would resent him for it. The thought made the decision far more difficult than it should have been but a choice needed to be made.

Chapter 691 – Treasure Tracker.

“Now then, Lucienne Arden, step forward.”

Arion had finally called out Roland’s sister’s name. The moment he did, Roland looked in her direction. The other students were not paying much attention, and none of her friends were present either, as she was the only one who had made it this far.

Her friends, Atasuna and Marlein, were not bad students, but they were more ordinary in terms of talent. Only the top two or three students from each class could ever hope to reach this competition. With multiple classes and hundreds of students in each year, earning a place here was no easy feat.

‘That girl Margaret could have probably gotten in, but she has not returned since Robert’s trial... neither is that prince here.’

The girl he suspected was a member of royalty or high nobility was absent. So was the prince who had caused the entire incident with Viola Castellane. High nobles seemed to have a tendency to create chaos and then disappear before facing the consequences. With enough wealth and influence to hire the finest private tutors, attending a magic academy like this was often little more than a formality.

Lucienne had grown considerably, and it was becoming clear that she was maturing into a proper noble lady. Roland could easily imagine a line of suitors eager to seek her hand in marriage. The only thing delaying such advances was her place at the institute, where she could continue living and studying.

He was not entirely sure what plans his sister had for the future. She never seemed particularly eager to marry, but with Robert’s disappearance, she had become the only unmarried member of the family. His older brothers and sisters had already wed. Some had children, while others were too occupied with their duties to start families of their own. That left Lucienne as the family's final bargaining piece if they ever wished to strengthen political ties through marriage.

‘I hope she can choose her own path... and if she ever wants my help…’

Roland had already done it once. If Lucienne ever decided she wanted to leave the family behind, helping her disappear was not out of the question. Still, he could not imagine her making such a choice because of her mother. Robert’s disappearance had already taken a heavy toll on her, and for the foreseeable future, they needed to keep his continued presence within the kingdom a secret.

Pushing those intrusive thoughts aside, Roland watched quietly as Lucienne made her way through the rows of students. Unlike many of the others who had stepped forward with confidence, she appeared noticeably nervous. Her shoulders were straight and her uniform immaculate, but there was a subtle hesitation in her movements that only someone familiar with her would notice.

The blonde girl reached the platform and stopped before the scanner. For a brief moment, their eyes met, and Lucienne offered an awkward smile, as if she was unsure how to react. Roland resisted the urge to sigh and instead turned his attention toward Arion, who activated the scanner.

Just as it had many times before, the device began to move. Bright lights swept across her body as it scanned her status while concealing details such as her class from curious onlookers. Normally, such secrecy would not be permitted, but the trust placed in the academy was immense. Besides, a student's class could often be identified even without the machine's assistance.

Roland already knew what the results would be before they appeared on paper. Lucienne's class was that of a Clairvoyant, an uncommon class that was not considered particularly powerful. At first glance, some people might wonder what use such a class could have in a competition, but in reality, there were many.

Tracking spells existed and could be used by almost everyone. The same could be said of classes with tracking abilities, but all of them paled in comparison to the clairvoyant line of classes that could eventually become oracles. These mages could pierce even the strongest barriers and locate almost anything within their range. In competitions where finding hidden objects often determined the outcome, Lucienne's class was almost unfair.

A class like hers could uncover not only hidden traps inside a dungeon but also paths leading to treasure that most people would overlook. However, due to its lack of offensive and defensive capabilities, it lagged behind other mage classes.

She could not cast devastating elemental spells, disable opponents with debilitating effects, or summon powerful creatures to her aid. It was primarily a support class and, when it came to spelunking, often had more drawbacks than advantages. An experienced tracker could usually perform well enough while still maintaining far greater combat effectiveness.

Yet with enough preparation and a capable defensive force, an oracle could turn the tide of battle. They could predict ambushes or uncover hidden routes long before the enemy realized they had been exposed. Entire expeditions had survived solely because someone with such abilities had been present.

‘A valuable military asset... I wonder what Wentworth intends to do with Lucienne…’

As he reviewed his sister's results, his thoughts turned to his father, a military man who held the rank of Marshal. Lucienne could potentially be used to predict attacks from rival empires, something that became increasingly possible as an oracle's powers grew. An oracle could even listen in on private conversations and relay that information to their allies. It was much like a camera, except one that could not be detected until it was too late.

"Please, take this student, and be sure to return this paper after marking down your event. Now then, let us continue with the next!"

Arion set aside the paper listing Lucienne's events. At the top was the treasure hunt competition, one of the academy's most sought-after events. The choice was obvious, as her ability to detect hidden objects made her exceptionally suited for it.

Groups of mages would be sent into a vast area filled with concealed treasures. Some would be hidden in chests, others tucked away in hollow tree trunks or buried beneath the ground. The team that recovered the most valuables would be declared the winner. A clairvoyant was perfectly suited for such a competition. Their ability to search was extraordinary, making them akin to a living radar capable of detecting almost anything within range.

The other two events assigned to her were different but not surprising. One was the reconnaissance exercise, a competition focused on gathering information while avoiding detection. Teams would be placed within a controlled environment and tasked with locating designated targets, observing enemy movements, and reporting their findings before time ran out.

For most students, it was considered one of the less glamorous events. Its purpose was to test stealth magic and intelligence-gathering capabilities. With Lucienne on the team, they would have someone capable of detecting enemy movement with ease. She would likely be able to spot ambushes and identify dummy targets before anyone else.

A clairvoyant's ability to gather information was invaluable. There were limits to what she could perceive, but even at Tier One, she could listen in on conversations as long as someone concealed her with stealth magic and brought her close enough to the target. As she advanced through her class progression, those abilities would only become more powerful.

The last one was somewhat similar to the first, as it involved a maze and item retrieval. A group would need to overcome various obstacles, solve magical puzzles, and recover an artifact hidden somewhere within the labyrinth. Their time would be measured, and the team with the fastest record would emerge victorious.

The treasure hunt was an academy favorite because it combined exploration, strategy, magical combat, and more than a little drama.

With Lucienne serving as the team's primary tracker, they would need a capable vanguard. Someone had to hold back opponents while she focused on locating targets. Although he had not initially supported the idea, he was still here to secure a strong placement for their magic school. The competition would be fierce, but if he managed to accomplish what he was planning, he could not see himself losing.

For most people, creating custom runic accessories to enhance magical abilities would be a difficult task. With the help of this machine, however, crafting something perfectly suited to each student would be child's play. It would simply require a considerable amount of time.

People stepped aside when they saw him, though the halls were still crowded. By the time he arrived at the large park area, he noticed far fewer students relaxing, as most were probably busy packing. Instead of heading to the Headmistress's tower, he made his way to his own office beneath it. From there, he could contact Yavenna without any trouble.

Once seated at his desk, he activated the spell connecting him to her. The wood began to shift, and a small flower emerged from the surface. A voice soon drifted from within it.

That was the main reason he had contacted her. After reviewing the Convocation's rules, he had found them surprisingly lax. Nearly all forms of enchanted equipment were allowed, provided they did not exceed a student's tier. A Tier One student, for instance, was restricted to Tier One runic equipment.

Because of the extensive research he had conducted on his disciple Millie and her bracelet, he was confident he could reproduce similar equipment for others. Mages relied heavily on mana, and their options for sustaining it were generally limited to mana potions or items that increased their mana reserves or regeneration.

Roland, however, could provide something far superior. Bracelets powered by runic batteries. Such devices could absorb a significant portion of a spell's mana cost, granting students a tremendous advantage in competitions that relied primarily on magic. It was an advantage that other schools either had not pursued or, more likely, could not produce on a large scale. Perhaps they could secure similar items for a handful of elite students, but the average young mage would never receive such treatment.

Roland, on the other hand, had already begun scanning every student, cataloging their strengths, weaknesses, and mana patterns. With that information, he could craft versions of Millie's bracelet tailored to each of them.

“That’s an intriguing idea. Could you tell me more?”

He could tell that the Arch Magus was interested in what he was planning, so he quickly explained everything to her. While the rules did not forbid it, he wanted to make sure he was not dragging the institute into some kind of scandal. He knew the other schools might protest if they saw this type of technology.

“What do you think, Headmistress? Would the other mages be offended?”

“Offended? Hah. Let them be. I give you my full permission, Deputy Professor.”

Roland was not sure what Yavenna's expression looked like on the other side of the call, but her tone sounded rather cheerful.

“Then I shall do it.”

After confirming that he had permission to proceed and that there were very few restrictions, he felt relieved. The golem was one thing, but winning only that single event would not give him what he wanted.

“You’ve given me something to look forward to, Deputy Professor.”

“I’ll try not to disappoint you, Headmistress.”

“I know you won’t.”

He was not sure where her confidence came from, but he chose not to respond. Soon, the flower faded back into the wooden desk. He was preparing to leave when he noticed someone entering the park. It was his sister.

“Does she want to talk to me?”

Lucienne looked both nervous and excited as she approached the tree that concealed the path to his office. Few people visited this place, and even fewer knew it existed. Roland remained where he stood for a moment, watching his sister move through the monitoring system outside.

“Come in.”

His voice echoed beyond the office, and the path opened before her. His sister nearly stumbled and fell to the ground, but she managed to recover and soon disappeared inside.

“D-Deputy Professor... I...”

“You can just call me by my name here.”

“Brother Roland, I wasn’t trying to hide anything. It’s just...”

It seemed she was worried about how he would react to her gaining a position in the Convocation.

“You don’t need to worry. I’m not angry, and it’s not as if we can speak freely under these circumstances.”

He stopped her before she could work herself into a nervous mess. Instead, he asked a simple question.

“So, which competition interests you the most?”

“Which one?”

Both Robert and their mother were quite protective of Lucienne, which had left her with little freedom and made her hesitant to share anything that might worry others. It was one of the main reasons the Viola incident had occurred, but he would not let something like that hold his sister back. He was prepared to help her achieve victory and help her gain more trust in her own skills.

Eventually, tier four monsters would no longer provide enough experience to level up. Even a super dungeon like the one on Dragni's island contained only a single creature of that power, and it had never been defeated.

Those who reached the final tier of power rarely continued seeking battle. Most assumed positions of authority, ruling kingdoms, dedicating themselves to research, or simply enjoying the rewards of reaching the summit. Risking one's life became far less appealing when an entire nation depended on one's continued existence. Yet wars still erupted between nations from time to time, and when tier five leaders clashed, entire cities and battlefields could be devastated. These people were the atomic bombs of this world, and perhaps one day he would become one of them as well.

Lucienne clutched the paper a little tighter.

"It is. Though the protective array is only one reason the Convocation has endured for so long. The participating academies invest far too many resources in their students to allow needless deaths."

Roland leaned back in his chair as he continued.

"The students represent the future of their nations. Losing talented young mages would be a waste."

Chapter 695 – Stuck Teaching.

“That about covers the fundamentals of our strategy.”

Roland finished his presentation and turned to face the five people before him. Their reactions varied. The muscular youth at the back had a glazed look in his eyes, as if he had understood almost nothing that had been said.

In short, Roland had laid out their objectives and explained which branches of magic each of them needed to focus on. Most children their age wanted nothing more than to jump into the fray and unleash the most eye-catching spells they could muster. He, however, had asked for restraint. The tank needed to be an immovable wall focused on defense rather than offense, which seemed to run contrary to Oxian's instincts.

‘The tactic should be sound, but getting these kids to follow orders might be difficult...’

Oxian was one concern, but Elian was another, and much of their strategy depended on him. He possessed a special class known as the Spark Mage. Normally, elemental mage classes only became available at tier two, but there were exceptions. This class was similar to Roland's own Runic Blacksmith class, which allowed him to enchant equipment with runes, something that should not have been possible at tier one.

The same principle applied here. Elian could cast tier one fire spells with far greater potency than any ordinary tier one mage, making him the ideal choice for the group's primary damage dealer. However, despite his talent, he still needed time to cast those spells and required protection from the others while doing so.

Leron had not voiced any complaints, and Roland could not glean much from his expression, but he was fairly certain the youth understood his role: to distract enemies and eliminate anyone who managed to break through their defensive line.

“You will all begin practical training after familiarizing yourselves with the strategy guides.”

He snapped his fingers, activating one of his spatial runes. Five booklets emerged from within, accompanied by several rectangular metal plates engraved with runes.

“Your strategy guide is marked with your name and assigned position. These runic memory cards can be used at one of the newest facilities within our Runic Department. Go there and ask one of the assistant professors to help you access the training modules stored inside.”

Roland flicked his wrist, and the five metallic cards, along with the notebooks, floated down onto a table to be collected. For this first gathering, this was all he had to offer. He knew the students had many questions, but his time was extremely limited. As a result, he drew on lessons from his own college days and designed the program so the students would study most of the material on their own.

The strategy guide included everything they needed to know. It outlined the various spells and combinations they should focus on, detailed which spells should be practiced together, specified the timing of their casting windows, and described the formations they were expected to maintain during engagements. Roland had essentially condensed complex squad tactics into a compact manual.

The runic memory sticks he had brought could be connected to runic monitors to display battle formations for practice. They also contained visual representations of possible combat scenarios, making the material easier to understand.

The assistant professors and other instructors had been given similar guides, which they would later use to evaluate the students. Roland would not be their primary instructor, as this group of five was only one among many that required guidance. His role was to formulate strategies and contingency plans for each group while addressing the most common challenges they were likely to encounter.

The overall plan was ambitious in scope but simple in execution. For the first few months, every student would train exclusively within their assigned group. Once their teamwork had improved, they would be given access to support spellcasting devices. It was important that they first learned to operate without them so they would not become overly dependent on the increased mana reserves those devices provided.

“Now then, pick up your guide. For any further guidance, please consult Professor Arion or any other assistant professor from the Runic Department.”

Roland still had many more groups like this to meet. Thanks to the scanner and the sorting system, they could identify the most effective student combinations for every event. This allowed him to map out optimal team compositions in a fraction of the time traditional observation would require. Leron suddenly appeared from behind the large youth's shadow as though he had been waiting for the perfect moment to pounce. Roland could tell he was using a combination of illusion and concealment spells to make himself difficult to notice. Like the previous student, he took the booklet and quickly disappeared through the exit.

Standing before him was Rathos, the vice headmaster of the institute. His appearance had not changed, still resembling the iconic wizard from the old tales of Roland's home world.

The old man stood tall, his long silver beard catching the faint glow of the luminescent crystals lining the hallway. Rathos carried himself with an air of not only authority but also as the prime example of what a mage should be. Roland remained still, wondering if there was some hidden meaning behind the words. Normally, the other departments would have been involved and offered assistance, but the Headmistress had closed off that possibility. It would not be surprising if the vice headmaster felt his position was being challenged, considering he had long been the de facto authority around the institute.

“I see. Then I won’t keep you any longer from your well-deserved rest.”

‘The old man is probably just worried about whether I’m doing a good job, or perhaps unhappy that the Headmistress made this decision without consulting him...’

Roland did not think much of it and continued toward the teleportation gate. While his work at the institute was finished for the time being, other responsibilities awaited him back home.

‘Maybe if this were some kind of team deathmatch, this would work... but if they lose their tracker, they won't be able to find any treasure...’

He shook his head and initiated the shutdown procedure for the training chamber occupied by the five youths. The puppets froze instantly, and their illusory forms faded away to reveal their true bodies beneath. The environment around the youths shifted as well, the chamber's magic restoring everything to its original state.

‘It's still quite a sight, even after seeing it so many times. It may not be on the level of Tier Four magic, but it can reshape the environment to simulate a wide variety of scenarios.’

These training facilities had existed long before Roland arrived. Normally, they were reserved for mages deemed worthy enough to use them in a controlled environment. However, they were far from perfect. It was only after the addition of the golemic puppets that the training chambers truly became exceptional. With the Runic Department programming their behavior, they could simulate combat scenarios with an unprecedented degree of realism.

“Ah, he did it again...”

Arion chuckled as he watched Oxian punch one of the stationary puppets while it was still in the process of shutting down.

“He certainly has a fiery temper.”

Roland wondered whether he had made a mistake when selecting some of these recruits. Their abilities suited the task, but their personalities often worked against the team.

“I'm sure you can rein them in, my friend.”

“I'll try...”

Roland sighed and headed toward the chamber. The only one who truly required little guidance was Selphie. His sister was struggling as well. More often than not, she became a burden to the team because she could neither defend herself properly nor keep her composure during live combat. Still, he intended to remedy their shortcomings through harsher and more focused training.

# 699: Chapter 697 – Secluded Training.

Chapter 697 – Secluded Training.

The heavy reinforced doors of the training chamber slid open with a hiss. Inside, the air was cool thanks to the filtration system that had been installed recently. A lone figure stood in the doorway, one that had previously been almost invisible to the naked eye.

‘It's always fascinating to see these spaces once you step inside.’

Roland entered the training chamber beneath the institute. There had been little room to build below the structure, but thanks to spatial magic, the area was many times larger than it appeared from the outside. What should have been a chamber of roughly a hundred square meters had been expanded into a space several football fields across, providing more than enough room for students to practice their magic.

As he stepped forward, the space around him unfolded into a vast expanse. In the distance, he spotted Oxian standing over a deactivated puppet. Its head rested in his large hand after being punched clean off. The young man was trying to reattach it, but each attempt ended the same way, with the head slipping free and falling back to the ground.

"Professor..."

Elian was the first to notice Roland's arrival. He immediately straightened his posture and tried to look presentable despite being out of breath. The other teenagers quickly followed suit, abandoning their post-battle squabbles. Oxian discreetly kicked the detached puppet head beneath a nearby bench that had appeared once the illusory scenery vanished.

Without the illusion magic, the surrounding space was almost entirely empty. Enchanted symbols covered every surface. The room itself was one enormous runic construct, designed to generate solid illusions that recreated terrain, rocks, and scenery. The assembled puppets then simulated monsters and other hostile opponents.

With everything deactivated, the chamber resembled a massive hangar. The spatial enchantment remained active at all times and formed the foundation of the entire structure. The illusion magic had been layered on top of it after the space was expanded. If the spatial enchantment were ever removed, the chamber would collapse back into its original dimensions, and the illusion arrays would have to be forged again from scratch. Maintaining the facility required a constant supply of magical energy.

Roland walked forward, his footsteps echoing through the vast chamber. He stopped a few paces from the group. His expression remained hidden behind his helmet, but his tone made his mood clear. He was not pleased.

"Oxian Goodwill. The purpose of a tank in a five-man squad is to control the battlefield and maintain proper spacing while preventing enemies from reaching the backline. It is not to engage in a glorious duel with the first target that catches your eye."

"But Professor... I, uh..."

Oxian grumbled and crossed his massive arms. Whatever eloquence he possessed seemed to vanish the moment the battle ended. Either he could not properly express his reasoning, or he simply did not know how to defend himself before a professor of Roland's caliber.

"You what? You left Elian exposed and unable to finish his chant."

"..."

Oxian glared at Elian when Roland mentioned him. He said nothing, but his irritation was obvious. Roland suspected he blamed the other student for failing to complete the spell. There had still been enough time to cast it, but Elian had panicked. His chanting had also been painfully slow, riddled with hesitations and repeated stumbles over the words.

“And Elian Plomyk.”

Roland turned his attention to the other young man, who visibly flinched when Roland looked at him.

“Your role as the primary DPS is useless if you cannot maintain your composure. A battle will never provide a sterile, quiet environment for you to chant your spells. If a single stray mana bolt causes you to fumble your words and stutter, then you are a liability to this entire team.”

“I apologize, Deputy Professor…”

Elian muttered, looking down at his boots.

“The pressure... When the illusions intensify, it feels like everything is happening so fast. I cannot seem to keep the incantations straight in my head.”

Roland did not offer any immediate comfort. He was aware that these youths needed personalized training. Instead of forcing them to work as a group from the beginning, they first needed to understand their individual roles within the team. Oxian needed to feel as though he was losing ground whenever he allowed someone to slip past him, while Elian needed proper concentration and chanting exercises in mentally taxing situations.

“Yes, we will have to give all of you special training... but first, Leron Vaugrenard, come out and stop hiding.”

Leron finally revealed himself. For some reason, he had been lying flat on the ground and had concealed himself perfectly within the scenery using illusion magic. The young man clearly knew he had made a mistake, and instead of apologizing, he had hidden away as though Roland would somehow forget about him.

“Just get over here... The only one among you who did an adequate job is Selphie Astarel. Lucienne Arden, you also need to learn how to protect yourself. You are the main tracker, but knowing how to respond to attacks is something every mage should be capable of doing.”

“Y-yes, Deputy Professor.”

Even though she was his sister, he could not treat her differently. In fact, he had to be stricter to ensure she would not become a target during the actual competition.

“Lucienne was just surprised. I am sure she will do better next time, Deputy Professor.”

Selphie defended her new friend, even though she was the only one who had avoided a scolding. Her large hat continued to slip lower as she stared at him with pleading eyes, making Roland wonder if he was the villain in this situation.

“Just gather together and stay here for now. We will start your special training right here and now.”

Roland pointed to the spot where the five students were to assemble. Fortunately, thanks to his reputation, they followed his orders without question. At least they did when he was present.

“Arion, remove the safety guards on this training chamber and give me full control.”

Through his helmet, he communicated with Arion and the Runic Department staff in the control room. The feline professor nodded, and after making a few adjustments to the runic operating system, the runes throughout the chamber began to shimmer.

“I have transferred all control to you, Deputy Professor. What are you planning to do?”

Arion was clearly curious.

“Just watch and see. This chamber has many features that people do not know about. You can even use them like this.”

Roland had spent a great deal of time researching within this institute and had personally helped build its monitoring system. As a result, he probably understood how everything worked in greater detail than many of the mages who had spent half their lives teaching here.

“What is the Professor doing?”

Elian asked in a whisper to both Lucienne and Selphie.

“I don’t know... but this amount of mana is tremendous!”

Selphie’s hat almost slipped off her head again as she sensed a massive concentration of mana surrounding their teacher’s armored figure. Even Oxian swallowed hard as he stood there gawking. Before them, the entire room began reacting without anything being visibly activated. It was as if the mana Roland was releasing was affecting every rune in the chamber. Some of them even seemed to shift and rearrange themselves.

‘Illusion magic is quite handy in situations like this.’

The chamber began to transform, dividing itself into four separate sections. No machinery moved. The change was accomplished entirely through illusion magic. Although most people believed illusions only affected the mind, skilled practitioners could solidify them into tangible constructs capable of creating real environments.

There were even legends of fourth and fifth-tier mages who could create entire pocket realms through illusion magic, worlds that were indistinguishable from reality. What Roland was doing here was far less complex. He was simply creating smaller training areas within the chamber to give the troublemakers a proper environment to practice in.

Shimmering walls of hardened illusion magic rose from the floor, dividing the massive hangar into four distinct, isolated zones. At first, light fractured through the semi-transparent barriers, but they soon solidified into rocky partitions.

The illusions were not particularly durable. They resembled ordinary stone and could be broken by basic first-tier spells. However, when made thick enough, they served as effective barriers for young mages like these.

“Oh, the puppets. They’re fixing themselves... is that also the power of a rune mage?”

While the others focused on the newly formed training rooms, Selphie noticed something else. The white puppet that had lost its head was repairing itself, and it was clearly being restored through their professor’s mana. Before long, the puppets gathered together and began moving into the smaller chambers that had appeared.

“That should be enough.”

It took around five minutes for all the chambers to fully form. Each entrance was sealed off and fitted with what appeared to be an iron door. The students were clearly captivated by the massive spell that had transformed the entire area. For Roland, however, the feat was relatively simple. He was drawing not only on his own mana but also on the power of the institute itself.

The institute was practically a magical power plant, sustained by numerous methods of generating mana. If Roland ever wanted to rival its output through technology alone, he would probably need to build a nuclear power plant.

“Elian Plomyk.”

“Ah, yes, Professor!”

“Come with me.”

Elian was first. Selphie gave him a pat on the back while Lucienne simply smiled.

“Yes, sir!”

The two entered the first chamber. Once inside, Elian looked around in confusion, unsure of what exactly he was supposed to learn there. The room contained a circle in the center, walls lined with openings of various shapes and sizes, and several wooden practice dummies wearing robes at the far end.

“This is your training room. Stand in the middle and do not leave that circle. Once you get used to that space, it will begin to shrink. Why don’t you give it a try? Cast one of your spells and hit one of those practice dummies.”

“Of course, Professor.”

Elian did not ask for an explanation and simply carried out the orders. Not only did he struggle to focus, but he was also surprisingly timid when it came to listening to his professors and rarely asked too many questions.

The young mage stepped into the center of the marked circle, his hands trembling slightly. Roland waited for him to begin chanting, and as soon as the boy's mana started taking shape into a spell, he activated the room's primary function.

One of the holes in the wall began to glow. Without warning, just as Elian was about to complete the spell, a small ball of blue energy shot toward him. It moved quickly and, with no chance to react, struck him in the shoulder. A brief jolt of energy ran through his body. The pain was mild, but the numbing effect instantly disrupted the spellcasting process.

“Your primary flaw is your lack of spatial awareness and your inability to concentrate while moving or under pressure. This chamber will fix that.”

Roland gestured toward the holes lining the walls and ceiling.

“Each of these openings contains a spell construct capable of targeting you with harmless but painful numbing jolts. Their frequency and speed will increase as you improve, until you can successfully cast without interruption. Your task is to destroy the target dummies as quickly as possible. If you step outside this circle, you will immediately fail the exercise, and it will reset.”

Elian looked at the dozens of openings surrounding him, his face growing slightly pale.

“B-but how do I know where the spell will come from, Deputy Professor? W-what should I do?”

“Feel the mana as the spell forms. Once you can sense it, you will know where the attack is coming from. After that, avoiding it should be easy.”

“Easy…”

Elian looked deeply uncomfortable as he stared at the countless holes in the walls. An attack could come from any direction and at any point during his casting. It was a difficult training method, but Roland did not have time for the boy to progress through the institute's standard curriculum. There was little time left, and this was one of the fastest ways to improve his skills short of fighting real opponents.

“Now then, begin your training. I'll go tend to your teammates.”

“Professor? You're leaving?”

“Of course. Stay in here for the next hour and focus on hitting the dummies. You are not allowed to leave before then.”

“But…”

The boy looked frightened, but Roland paid it little mind as he closed the door behind him. The chamber would not harm the youth. He simply needed to frighten him enough to ensure he took the training seriously. If Elian was struck too many times, the program would automatically shut down. It had also been designed to prevent actual injury, producing only a brief sensation of paralysis rather than any real damage.

'I suppose this would also be a good way to build resistance to paralysis...'

Roland considered this as he approached the main troublemaker, Oxian. The youth was taller than Roland himself, but his face still revealed his lack of experience.

“Oxian Goodwill, come with me. Your room is the next one.”

Oxian followed Roland into the second chamber without resisting or saying anything. The young man was quite heavy-footed. With each step he took, his boots clanked against the floor. Even now, he was using some sort of body-enhancing spell that surrounded him with a layer of rock.

The chamber they arrived in was much longer than the one used by the previous youth. At the far end stood three wooden puppets that looked quite fragile. Along the sides were several other puppets, some designed for close combat and others capable of launching ranged attacks.

“Your training is simple.”

Roland gestured toward the wooden golemic puppets at the end of the chamber.

“You must escort those wooden puppets from the end of the room back to the entrance. However, the moment you approach them, the combat puppets will begin attacking. Some will focus on you, while others will target the people behind you. If any of those three wooden puppets are injured, you will fail.”

Oxian nodded, seemingly confident in his abilities. The scenario appeared simple, but it would likely prove challenging. He would need to match the pace of those he was protecting, something he had always struggled with.

“Begin.”

Roland watched as Oxian strode toward the puppets with a confident glint in his eye. It seemed he wanted to clear this test on his first attempt to prove himself, but just as Roland expected, he rushed in.

Oxian charged ahead, the rocky armor around his limbs grinding together with a heavy scraping sound. He reached the three wooden puppets at the far end of the chamber and turned to face the attackers. The constructs began shuffling forward with slow, awkward steps that mimicked human movement.

The moment they crossed a certain threshold into the main corridor, the opposing combat puppets sprang to life. One charged directly at Oxian and, as he had done many times before, he immediately focused on driving it back with all his strength. The golemic puppet was sent flying, but in the process, one of the wooden puppets behind him stumbled and was struck by a simple mana bolt cast by one of the mage puppets.

“You fail. Start from the beginning.”

Roland spoke as everything in the chamber came to an immediate halt. The puppets returned to their starting positions.

“You could have intercepted that spell with your body, Oxian, but once again, you focused too much on your direct opponent. Stay here as long as necessary, but you must understand that a tank's priority is protection, not destruction. You have two hours to get those constructs safely across.”

Oxian grunted and cracked his knuckles as the reality of the task began to sink in. Leaving the stubborn youth to his repetitive trials, Roland exited the second chamber and headed toward the third door, where the remaining students were waiting.

“Leron Vaugrenard. This one is for you. Come with me.”

The young man nodded as the third door swung open. Leron's main problem was target priority and a lack of awareness when his teammates were struggling. His role was to support the primary damage dealer while introducing an element of unpredictability to the battlefield.

It was a highly flexible position that allowed him to defend, attack, or operate independently when necessary. The problem was that he only wanted to do one thing: conceal himself and wait for the perfect opportunity to strike. In a team-based scenario, however, that opportunity was not always available.

# 700: Chapter 698 – More Special Chambers.

Chapter 698 – More Special Chambers.

The heavy iron door creaked open, revealing an environment that looked radically different from the previous two rooms. Instead of the rather sterile spaces filled with little more than empty ground, this one was dense with trees and scattered boulders. Some of the rocks even contained hollow openings large enough to hide inside.

This had been the hardest room to create, and the illusion magic had continued shaping it while Roland presented the previous two rooms to the other youths. Leron's weaknesses could not be addressed with a generic training environment. His greatest strength lay in using terrain to conceal himself and attack from hidden vantage points. However, he was equally poor at recognizing the right moment to strike.

One of his biggest flaws was spending too much time searching for the perfect hiding place instead of anticipating where his teammates would move. He performed best when acting independently, shadowing the group from a distance before slipping into position and launching a surprise attack when the enemy's attention was elsewhere.

To address this, Roland had prepared a scenario filled with countless hiding places and two teams of puppets. One team represented Leron's allies, whom he needed to support and protect, while the other consisted of aggressive melee and caster constructs that represented the opposing force.

"Your objective here is to make every battle easier for your team."

Roland began explaining as he pointed toward the four golem puppets representing Leron's teammates. To make them easier to distinguish, each had a different shape and size that loosely matched the members of his actual team. Creating training scenarios of this complexity while controlling so many golems was quite an achievement, but they were still only tier one and tier two creations, making them relatively inexpensive.

"First and foremost, you must learn to choose your targets wisely. You might think focusing on the most powerful mage or the greatest threat is always the right decision, but that will not always be the case."

"I understand."

Leron nodded confidently, as though he already knew the lesson. His previous performance, however, had shown that he did not.

"You do? We shall see."

Roland had intended to explain the exercise in greater detail, but the young man seemed far too certain of himself. Target priority was important, but it was not everything. The opposing side could disguise its true intentions or even bait attackers into carefully prepared traps. Leron would have to face several different scenarios Roland had designed, and hopefully each one would chip away at that overconfidence.

"If you wish to begin, press the button on that tree. If even one of your teammates becomes incapable of fighting, you lose. Now begin."

He pointed toward a bright red button embedded in the trunk of a tree, conspicuously out of place within the otherwise natural surroundings. It served as the control to begin the trial and restart it after each failure. Judging by what Roland had seen so far, there would likely be quite a few of those.

Once Leron pressed the button, Roland stepped outside and continued observing the exercise through the monitoring system. Just as expected, Leron immediately encountered his first obstacle. He spent too long searching for the ideal hiding spot. There were trees, bushes, trenches, and rocky hollows scattered throughout the field, each offering its own advantages. Yet while he hesitated, trying to find the perfect vantage point, the simulation had already begun.

The allied team puppets began advancing down the central path, and almost immediately, the enemy forces sprang an ambush. A massive, heavily armored vanguard puppet charged straight down the middle, drawing everyone's attention, while two smaller caster puppets began chanting from the flanks.

Leron was slow to find the best angle, as he had been many times before, and when he finally reacted, he chose the wrong target. Just as he was sneaking around to attack from behind, an enemy puppet with a role similar to his was doing the same. While Leron targeted one of the caster puppets, his counterpart struck at their greatest weakness, the Lucienne puppet. Once it was taken down, the enemy did not press the attack. They simply retreated because with their main tracker eliminated, there was no longer any treasure hunt to win.

*Bzzz*

A strange sound filled the training chamber after the puppet collapsed. Just as Roland had explained, the simulation came to an immediate halt as soon as a single allied puppet was destroyed. Leron stared at the frozen constructs, seemingly confused as to why everything had ended when he still had plenty of time left.

Sometimes it was better to let these headstrong mages figure things out on their own instead of handing them the answer from the beginning. Still, if they failed to improve quickly enough, he might have to intervene.

"Now then, Lucienne Arden, you are next. Follow me."

Out of the entire group, his sister was still the weakest link. The others did not require much guidance, nor did they need to learn any new skills or get better at them. Lucienne, however, was different. She had obtained the Clairvoyant class, one that was entirely new. She needed time to grow accustomed to its unique skills and spells.

"Of course, Deputy Professor."

In settings like this, Lucienne had to behave as though she did not know him personally. The monitoring system could be accessed by other people, and if they ever revealed their true relationship, the secret would be out before long.

"Your problem lies in using your skills effectively alongside your team. You are not just the one who finds the treasure. You can do far more to support your allies. Tell me, what do you think is the most valuable resource on the battlefield? Magical power? Numbers?"

"Um..."

He led her into a smaller chamber, one similar to the area Leron had used, though much more compact. Trees, boulders, and shallow pits filled the space, but there were also far more distractions. Birds sang overhead, water flowed somewhere in the distance, and random gusts of wind rustled the leaves.

"It's information. If you always know where your enemies are, they can never ambush you, and you can easily avoid any traps they try to set."

Lucienne listened intently, her eyes darting across the hyper-realistic forest. The rustling leaves and distant rushing water felt remarkably real, but she knew the entire environment was an illusion.

"Information..."

She repeated the word softly, as though searching for its deeper meaning.

"I think I understand, Professor."

"Good. Your task is simple. Find the hidden enemies within the forest while ignoring the unnecessary sounds and visual distractions. Several specialized camouflage puppets are concealed throughout this area. They will not attack you, but they will constantly shift positions to avoid detection. Your objective is to locate every hidden one before any of them reveal themselves."

Lucienne nodded, her expression turning serious. She closed her eyes and focused her mana. The Clairvoyant class was tailor-made for finding the positions of others. It was far more precise than any other tracking class, and if she truly mastered it and raised her skill level, there would be no one at tier one capable of hiding from her.

‘Her range is limited for now, but once she gains more levels and starts using the mana-boosting equipment, she should be comparable to a tier two tracking mage.’

Roland thought to himself as he left the training chamber where he had been alone with his sister.

"You can do it, Lucienne!"

The fifth member of the group was also waiting, though her training would be quite different from everyone else's.

"I am ready, Professor! What do you want me to do? Where is my private training chamber? I didn't see another door... Oh, could that be part of the test? Do I have to find the door first?"

The girl was as chatty as ever, but she would probably be disappointed once she learned what he actually had planned for her.

"No, you won't need a personal training room. Here, you'll be studying this instead."

Selphie looked up at Roland as he activated one of his spatial runes and pulled out a thick book before placing it into her hands. The moment he let go, she nearly toppled over under its weight.

"A... a magic tome?"

Selphie blinked, her large hat tilting precariously to one side as she struggled to keep her balance beneath the heavy book.

"Something like that. It contains various spells that might prove useful and will probably surprise your opponents."

"Spells?"

The girl was clearly surprised by this turn of events. She already knew more spells than anyone else in the group. When it came to magical knowledge and applying spells effectively, she was by far the most accomplished caster among them. However, these spells were different.

"Professor... these spell formulae... You want me to cast these? But..."

She managed to open the first page while trying not to lose her balance. After pushing her hat back into place, she noticed something unusual. The spells were unlike anything she had seen before.

"Indeed. I want you to learn these. There isn't much else you need to improve when it comes to teamwork or your role within the group."

"But Professor... I don't think I'll be able to use these..."

"Is that so? Are you worried about the amount of mana they require?"

She nodded as she closed the tome. It was a reasonable concern. The spells inside were modified tier one spells that incorporated tier two principles. They were significantly stronger than ordinary tier one spells, but as a tier one class holder, her mana pool was quite limited. What she did not know was how much freedom she would gain once she began using the support equipment.

"Yes, Professor."

"Don't worry about your mana. That problem will be easy to solve. For now, just learn the theory behind these spells. Once you're familiar with them, report to the Runic Department."

"Um... sure, Professor."

Selphie still looked unconvinced, but she was also fascinated by the intricate magical theory spread across the pages. Her eyes widened as she grasped the complexity of the modifications. They were completely unlike the standard academic curriculum taught at the institute.

“This... this is quite complex.”

“It is, but someone like you should be able to do it.”

Roland replied, knowing that this girl was a maniac when it came to acquiring new knowledge. She was already ahead of the others, so this was the only thing she needed to focus on to make her utility spells more powerful.

“You may leave now. I will give you a week's time to memorize as much as you can.”

“Only a week?”

She flinched at the timeframe, but they were running dangerously short on time, and their group was performing poorly during the live training exercises.

“Not enough?”

He asked, but she shook her head while looking toward the door Lucienne had gone through earlier.

“No... I'll try... leave it to me, Professor!”

He nodded at her response and allowed her to leave the training chamber.

‘The others are doing poorly now, but they should improve.’

Now that he had assigned everyone their tasks, he glanced toward the training chambers where the students were hard at work. Oxian was already on his fourth attempt with little progress to show for it. Elian lay on the ground, twitching after being paralyzed, while Leron was already pressing the red button again to restart after another failure. Roland sighed quietly inside his helmet, realizing that breaking their bad habits would probably take time and consume a great deal of materials in the process.

‘Well, Rome wasn't built in a day…’

He pushed the thought aside and removed himself from the situation. The other members of the Runic Department had already been briefed on the purpose of the trials, but that did not mean he was free to leave just yet.

“Professor Wayland, how did you come up with such innovative ways of using those puppets and the illusory chamber? This has to be added to the Institute curriculum!”

“I just did...”

Once he returned to the control room where Arion and the others were waiting, he was greeted with applause from every assistant teacher and professor present. Stephen was there as well, and for some reason, his glasses were completely fogged up, as though he had been panting for quite some time.

“You hear that, everyone? He just did! Oh, Professor Wayland!”

Arion chuckled, clearly unconvinced by his explanation. Roland was hardly about to admit that he had drawn upon knowledge from his old world along with everything he had learned in this one. However, the five students he had helped were not the only ones who needed guidance. Now that he had demonstrated what could be accomplished with the illusory chambers, even more work was piled onto his shoulders.

Over the next few days, he spent his time traveling back and forth between the institute and the teleportation chamber. He was tasked with developing helpful training programs for other students, including those who had already reached tier two, which only made the work more challenging.

The week passed in a blur as he juggled his responsibilities at the institute with his work back home. Every other day, he had to venture into the dungeon with Agni to level up. During the days in between, he either worked on the mobile runic armor or returned to the institute, where everyone seemed to be asking for his opinion on nearly every aspect of the upcoming competition.

Roland stood before the main observation console. He had been keeping up with their progress and was pleasantly surprised when he returned two days later to check on them. Things looked very different now.

Oxian finally seemed to understand the core principle of his role. On the screen, the large youth was no longer charging ahead blindly. Instead, he positioned himself like a solid wall in front of the fragile puppet constructs.

When a combat puppet lunged forward, Oxian simply deflected the blow with a controlled bash of his rocky fist. He had even begun incorporating other defensive spells, raising shields of earth around himself and creating earthen barriers that protected the group from attacks coming from the front.

He let his rocky armor absorb the impacts without abandoning the group. From time to time, however, he still made a few blunders, allowing enemies to slip through the defensive perimeter he was trying to maintain.

His gaze then shifted to the other young man, Leron, who was currently crouching motionless inside a hollow tree trunk.

Previously, Leron had only used these hiding spots to launch quick attacks or avoid combat, but this time he was using them defensively. This particular hiding place was close to his team, and instead of targeting the puppets attacking them directly, he waited for one that tried to sneak around their position. The moment it revealed itself, he leaped from the tree, cast a binding spell to halt its advance, and then worked together with the puppet constructs to destroy it.

'These two are getting there but...'

While Oxian and Leron had made big strides, the same could not be said for Elian. His form had improved slightly, but he still struggled to predict where attacks were coming from and to concentrate on casting his spells under pressure. His progress remained slow, and perhaps Roland would need to find a better way to help him overcome his fear.

Then there was Lucienne, who had neither surged ahead nor fallen behind. Her progress was steady and roughly where Roland had expected it to be. Her skills still needed considerable improvement, but with the help of the mana recovery devices and continued practice, he knew she could make even greater progress.

'I'll need to introduce this sooner rather than later.'

While he had originally planned to wait another month before handing out the new devices, the problems these students were facing called for an extra push. He wanted them to develop at their own pace to some extent, but time was beginning to run short.

Selphie had also grasped the concepts behind the spells he had rewritten. However, he had underestimated her eagerness to cast them. She had pushed herself so hard that she nearly suffered a mana backlash and upset her stomach by drinking too many mana potions in an attempt to keep casting.

'I suppose this is also an opportunity for me to learn...'

Roland almost chuckled to himself as he walked away from the control room. He was not a teacher and knew little about explaining concepts or guiding young minds. Although he had recently taken on his own disciple, he was discovering just how difficult it was to teach others when every mind worked differently.

He made his way through the corridors toward the training area. The time had come to give the youths the customized items he had been preparing and teach them how to handle their newfound power responsibly.
