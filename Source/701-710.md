Chapter 699 – Magic Equipment.

“Fire bolt!”

A bolt of fire shot forward and collided with a set of wooden targets at the end of the chamber. The moment the blazing projectile struck, the wood splintered in every direction. The explosion was far larger than a standard tier one spell should have produced, and the flames even reached the two targets on either side.

“Yes, I did it... Ack!”

Elian smiled as he watched the targets shatter, but his moment of triumph was short-lived. He had finally managed to cast the spell successfully and dodge the first attack coming his way, only to be struck by another that followed immediately after.

“Even if you manage to hit your target, Elian, you still have to watch out for any secondary threats that might follow immediately after.”

Roland entered the room to find Elian sprawled across the ground. Although his progress had been slow, he was getting better at keeping his composure. Every now and then, he managed to complete his spell and hit his target, but more often than not, something still went wrong.

“There will be students from other magical academies waiting for you to let your guard down. Never relax unless you are absolutely certain there is no one around you.”

“Y-yes, Deputy Professor.”

Elian replied as he rubbed the back of his head. Even though a paralyzing bolt had struck him, he seemed to have grown accustomed to the sensation, making Roland wonder if he should increase its strength.

“But what brings you here... could it be? Is this training over now?”

His eyes widened as though salvation itself had appeared. He had spent half of every day for the past week enduring this exercise, and it was obvious he wanted nothing more than for it to end. However, Roland had not come to dismiss them. Instead, he intended to add another layer to their training.

“Not quite. Come with me for now.”

“Ah...”

The young man's shoulders slumped in disappointment. Roland turned and walked into the main chamber, with Elian following close behind. The other students had already gathered there, with Selphie hurrying in from outside and nearly tripping over her own feet.

“My apologies, Deputy Professor!”

She stumbled to a stop just before Lucienne. It was obvious she was not herself today. She looked exhausted, with dark bags beneath her eyes, the result of spending the past week cramming magical formulae into her head.

“You've all worked hard. Some of you have begun to grasp the essence of your new roles, while others still have a long way to go.”

Elian's expression fell, as though he believed those words had been directed at him. Lucienne lowered her head as well, but Roland knew they had all been putting in genuine effort, and that counted for more than talent alone.

“It's fine. Don't get discouraged if your progress has been slow. It's only been a week since you began learning these skills properly.”

He did not want to sound too harsh. Some of these young mages had probably never struggled this much before, especially after spending their lives being praised for their talent. Being confronted with their own shortcomings day after day could easily shatter their confidence if he pushed them too far.

“What matters is that every one of you has improved. However, the competition will not wait for you. To give you the edge you need, I have prepared something for each of you.”

Roland gestured toward a large table that looked completely out of place in the illusory chamber. Resting on top of it was a large rectangular crate covered with multiple locks and engraved with runes. As he approached, the runes flashed briefly, and the locks slowly disengaged. A mage hand spell lifted the lid, and the students leaned forward as curiosity got the better of them.

“What are these, Deputy Professor?”

Selphie's eyes sparkled as she looked inside the crate. Her hat, as always, slipped over her eyes, but she quickly pushed it back into place.

“As you already know, the Runic Department will be providing magical devices for your use during the Convocation. These are prototypes that will help you manage your spells and mana far more efficiently than any enchanted equipment currently available.”

Roland continued as he removed the most important part of the devices first, the runic batteries. There were several ways to attach them to the body, and they were only slightly larger than a modern day 9 volt battery. They shared the same rectangular shape but were about twice as long. Their metallic surface was covered in miniature runes.

There were many shapes the batteries could have taken, but in the end he settled on a rectangular design instead of a cylindrical one. He had relied on other smiths to manufacture them, and producing a rectangular shape was much easier than machining a perfectly cylindrical one to fit a specific holster. Flat surfaces also made it easier to engrave runes without imperfections that would reduce the overall grade.

“Each of you will receive one battery along with a customized receptor device. The battery stores mana in advance, so your own reserves will no longer be the only source you can rely upon.”

Roland placed one of the rectangular batteries on the table before lifting what looked like a leather harness. There were several ways to carry the batteries, and he had brought a variety of harnesses, belts, and straps for the students to choose from.

It was possible to place a battery inside a spatial rune that would power the enchantment from within. However, the amount of mana required meant the battery would have to be both quite large and made from tier three materials to function properly. Such equipment was forbidden during the competition.

“Stores mana, this block does?”

Roland stepped aside as Selphie spoke.

“Yes, it does. Please choose whichever harness you prefer for carrying it. Pick one that best suits your fighting style and will not interfere with your casting or movement.”

These were still prototypes, and this group would be the first to test them. If it had been his choice alone, Roland would have recommended the belts. They were the least intrusive, could be easily concealed beneath robes or armor, and were comfortable to wear. However, everyone had their own preferences. Even the chest harness he had designed for Armand was among the options, and Oxian already seemed interested in it.

After examining the contents of the crate, each student selected the option that suited them best. Oxian chose the chest harness, which could be easily hidden beneath his clothes while remaining protected by the rocky armor he formed around his body.

Leron chose a slender thigh strap that could be securely fastened over his light trousers. As a scout who relied heavily on agile movement and silent maneuvering, he could not afford any extra bulk around his chest or waist.

Elian chose a wide leather belt that rested comfortably around his waist, and Lucienne made the same choice. Selphie, on the other hand, opted for a combination of a belt and a shoulder strap with multiple battery slots positioned across the front, allowing her to replace them quickly. It was as if she expected to use a great deal of mana and knew that being able to swap batteries without delay would be essential.

"Once you have secured the harness, place the battery into the slot."

Roland instructed, and the students followed his directions.

"You will not feel anything just yet. First, take these armbands. Each one has been fashioned to match your specific mana pattern and will only work for you. For the time being, you will be limited to the armbands, but they can later be crafted into other forms, including armor, if you wish."

The students slipped the armbands over their wrists, watching curiously as the metal bands glowed with a faint, pulsing light. Roland observed them closely. Matching the devices to their individual mana signatures had been a straightforward process thanks to the all-purposeASA scanners and his previous experience with his disciple. There was virtually no chance that any of them would reject their user, but another issue remained.

The armbands, batteries, and even the runes had all been made from tier one materials. Because of that, their lifespan was limited. They might need to be replaced after several hours of continuous use or sooner if they were subjected to an exceptionally prolonged exchange of spells. This was one of the things Roland intended to test today. Once he had gathered enough data, he would begin upgrading every component.

"Now then, channel a small amount of your mana into the armband to activate it."

Some of the students hesitated at first. Testing an unfamiliar magical device always carried a degree of risk, but once Lucienne activated hers, the others quickly followed.

Roland knew the sensation would feel strange at first. The armband forcefully redirected the user's mana, and every mage instinctively tried to resist and regain control. Once the flow stabilized, however, the equipment began functioning exactly as intended.

"Oh... this feels strange."

Selphie said as she quickly chanted a simple light spell, producing a glowing orb above her fingertip. She stared at it for a moment and immediately realized it was much brighter than she had intended.

"Oh, I barely used any mana. The power output is stronger, and the casting speed has increased too!"

Lucienne cast a clairvoyance spell to detect the people around her, and her eyes widened in surprise. The mental feedback was far clearer than usual. It felt as though a veil had been lifted from her mind, allowing her to perceive everything with remarkable clarity.

"The range has increased, and casting feels so effortless..."

Oxian clenched his fists and activated his signature earthen skin spell. A rocky layer formed almost instantly, coating his arms with far less delay than usual. Even after his entire body became encased in sand and stone, the armband and batteries continued to function flawlessly.

Leron simply used his stealth magic to blur his body before them. He activated and deactivated the spell several times, each attempt improving the speed of the casting while consuming very little mana, just as Roland had said.

Even Elian was shocked when the fire bolt spell he cast was produced much faster and at half the mana cost of the original version. Each of the students looked at the armbands as though they were legendary treasures, but they soon turned to Roland with concern on their faces.

"Deputy Professor... Does this magical equipment have any drawbacks?"

Selphie asked directly while creating a small light show above her head as she tested the limitations of the armband.

"There are no drawbacks to using these runic devices. The only real downside is the batteries. They will need to be replaced after a certain amount of your mana has been used."

The question gave him the perfect segue into the next device he had prepared. Roland knew the treasure hunt could last for quite some time, and that the students had limited space in the spatial bags they could carry, but there was a solution.

"Take a look at this. It is called a runic battery charger. If your batteries run out of mana, you can recharge them manually by transferring your own mana into them. Use it whenever you have the time and your mana reserves are full."

"Oh!"

Selphie's eyes widened again as Roland presented the charger. It was not much to look at, just another rectangular box crafted from polished dark steel. It featured several distinct slots along its upper surface where the batteries could be inserted.

Roland demonstrated its use by placing an uncharged battery into one of the slots and channeling some of his own mana into the device. As soon as the process began, a red light appeared on the side to indicate that the battery was charging.

"This light will gradually change color. It will go from red to orange, then yellow, and finally green to indicate the battery's charge level. You can also use it to check how much charge your current batteries have left, although that will not be necessary forever. A feature like that will eventually be integrated into your armbands."

With that, the presentation was over and the students were fully equipped. All that remained was for them to return to their personalized training chambers and practice with their new equipment.

"Return to your training chambers and try out your new equipment."

As soon as he gave the order, the five students hurried back to their respective simulation chambers. Only Selphie remained by the table, eager to experiment with a few more spells. Roland returned to the central observation room to watch their progress, and the improvement in their proficiency became obvious almost immediately.

Elian, who had been falling behind the most, displayed a drastic change in demeanor. His spellcasting was sharper, his chants were faster, and there was a reason for it. The enchantment on his armband maintained a constant clarity spell that eased the stress that had been weighing on him and making every task more difficult. Although it consumed some of the battery's charge, it was the boost he needed to finally keep his composure under pressure.

This time around, he was able to dodge more of the random bolts fired at him, and casting fire bolt spells came much more naturally. He reached the point where his targets had to be replaced several times. Eventually, the difficulty could be increased, and the circle he stood in was made smaller, something he clearly did not appreciate.

The others performed well too. Oxian moved much faster than before and could conjure larger stone walls to protect his puppets. Leron's concealment magic had grown stronger, and his offensive spells had improved as well. He sliced through the hidden puppets to support his team. Lucienne could now repeatedly cast her new spells and make better use of her skills to continue progressing.

"Everything is working within the expected parameters."

"It certainly seems that way, Professor Wayland."

Arion commented as another round of applause filled the control room. The other teachers from the Runic Department had gathered to watch Roland explain the prototypes and present them alongside the students.

"There is no need for that. We still need to gather more data on the prototypes before we can produce more for the other students."

"Leave it to us, Deputy Professor Wayland!"

Stephen saluted him for some reason, as though Roland were some kind of military commander, and the others in the room followed suit. Roland was not quite sure how to react, so he simply nodded. At least now he could devote more of his attention to other matters. Time continued to pass, but it felt well spent.

‘At least for the next week, I should be able to leave them on their own and focus on other issues.’

Once everything was finished, he returned home, where more work awaited him. The upper section of the mobile runic armor had been assembled and was almost ready for its first live walking test. Then there was Agni, whose levels continued to rise steadily.

The next few weeks passed in a blur. Work on the golem consumed nearly half of his time, while Agni's training took up another third. With help from the other members of the Runic Department, Roland was able to reduce his own involvement at the Institute.

His training methods were adopted by many of the other competition teams. Personalized training rooms were created for the remaining students, and improved versions of the runic battery equipment were introduced to give them time to adapt. Finally, with less than three months remaining before the competition began, Agni achieved the breakthrough.

"Awooo!"

"Oh? You finally leveled up. Good job."

Roland stood in front of a runic monitor as Agni came padding back. By this stage of their training, he could even leave him alone to fight the creatures. Agni no longer needed Roland's help to earn the final level.

Under normal circumstances, it would have taken many more years, but thanks to Roland's knack for finding the best grinding spots, he had achieved one of the fastest progressions possible.

"Well then, Agni, are you ready to change your form?"

Chapter 700 – Agni Evolves Further.

“Woof!”

“Awooo!”

“Woof!”

“Okay, okay, can you just stop jumping around? You’re going to crack the floor.”

Roland said to Agni, who was bounding through one of the corridors leading to the reinforced testing chamber. They had just returned from the dungeon through the teleportation gate and were only moments away from reaching a much safer environment.

The last time Agni had undergone an evolution had been quite some time ago, but the transformation had been extreme. The entire compound had been illuminated by the flames Agni produced, and his body had grown considerably larger. It had been a major structural and skeletal transformation that was quite volatile.

Compared to humans gaining new classes, monsters experienced far more drastic changes. If Roland received another class, his body would remain the same, and he certainly would not unleash powerful surges of mana like his companion. Agni's last evolution had been quite the spectacle, so this time Roland had made sure it would take place inside a heavily reinforced, bunker-like chamber.

‘I’ve heard that some evolutions can even cause powerful explosions and affect the surrounding environment.’

The massive door before them slowly slid open, its enormous weight making the movement heavy and sluggish. It was a reinforced vault plated with layers of specialized, high grade alloy that Roland had personally treated to withstand intense heat and devastating kinetic impacts. Large runic inscriptions lined the walls, glowing with a faint blue light that showed the defensive barriers were fully charged and ready.

He was not taking any chances and had already evacuated everyone from the compound. His wife was in the city running the school as its headmistress, the children staying in the dormitories were there as well, and everyone else, including Rastix, had been instructed to leave for the time being. There was no telling what a second legendary monster evolution might bring, and he had to make sure that any magical radiation remained contained within the facility.

Under normal circumstances, and just like previously, it would have been wiser to perform the evolution outside, where the heat could dissipate instead of building up enough pressure to cause an explosion. However, with so many eyes on him and the compound, he needed to ensure that no energy escaped. The chamber had been equipped with safeguards designed to contain the magical discharge while safely venting the superheated air.

‘How will the Solarian Church react to this evolution...’

Agni leaped into the center of the chamber, his paws clicking loudly against the dense metal floor. Despite the confined space, the massive wolf could barely contain his excitement. The air around him was already beginning to shimmer from the latent heat radiating from his fur as his anticipation continued to grow.

“Calm down, boy, or you’re going to set off the defensive sensors before we even start.”

Roland ran a hand through the flaming mane in an attempt to calm the wolf. Once Agni settled down, he finally turned his attention to the choices he had been given.

A legendary creature that represents the cold, silent majesty of the moon and the cosmic void. It possesses silver and midnight blue fur along with deep blue eyes. Its body is surrounded by crystalline armor that resembles frost, and a soft chilling aura surrounds it at all times. Mystical Ruby Fenrir

[ Fire/Earth/Beast ]

A rare ruby variant of the legendary Fenrir wolf. It combines the massive frame of a Fenrir with the magical aptitude of a Blazing Alpha Mystical Dire Ruby Wolf. Larger than its lesser counterpart, its every movement is powerful enough to cause small tremors.

“The choice is kind of obvious... unless you wish to become an Ice Wolf, Agni.”

“Worf!?”

Agni snorted in response, making it clear that he had no desire to take that path. To Roland, after reading through the descriptions, it was quite a strange option to come across. It was a monster variant that seemed to be the complete opposite of the Solar Wolf Agni was meant to become. Instead of possessing an affinity for fire and the divine power of Solaria, it was aligned with ice and the divinity of Lunaria and Lunaris.

‘I wonder how those nutcases from the church would react…’

The image of Aurelia Valerian entered his mind. She was quite the zealot, and it would not be surprising if someone like her tried to put him on the chopping block for forcing a divine creature to transform into a beast associated with a rival deity.

The Sun and Moon elves had been competing for millennia. They were practically two sides of the same coin, yet they had been locked in an ideological struggle for as long as history could remember. Choosing the Wolf of Eclipse would practically be inviting a confrontation. Roland could already imagine the Moon elves showing up to inspect a companion beast connected to their god.

‘The last thing I need is a holy crusade in my backyard…’

The next two options were somewhat underwhelming. They were certainly powerful, but they lacked the unique divine aspect that Agni had already unlocked. Choosing either of them would mean abandoning all the rare traits he had worked so hard to cultivate throughout their previous adventures.

There was also the Fenrir variant, which appeared to be even larger than the previous one. To Roland's knowledge, its next evolution would be a Greater Fenrir, a tier four creature capable of contending with dragons and growing just as large as some of them. That was not something he wanted Agni to become. A beast of that size would make dungeon diving with him practically impossible.

That left the final choice, the Solar Wolf with the Ruby Form included. It was the natural progression of Agni's current path, allowing him to retain his dual form while elevating his divine status even further. It was exactly what they needed, although Roland still was not entirely sure what the outcome would be.

“Alright, boy. Looks like we're sticking with the sun motif until we find something better.”

Roland reached out and patted Agni's snout.

“Are you ready?”

Agni answered with a low, resonant growl, his body tensing as he prepared himself for what would likely be a painful transformation.

“Don't worry, Agni. The array you're standing on has a pain-relieving function. Drink this as well. It'll numb your body, and it should make the process much easier.”

He handed him a bowl filled with a potion Rastix had prepared specifically for this occasion. Once the wolf lapped up every last drop, it was finally time to begin the evolution. The last time, Agni had refused the pain reliever, but this time he seemed to know better.

Roland watched the creature move around the chamber. It was not stumbling over its own feet and effortlessly avoided the few obstacles that had been placed in its path. It was clear to him that it could see everything in front of it.

“It's a success…”

“With this, the Lord's mother will finally be able to…”

Several other people stood in the observation room with him. Some were maids from the estate, while the rest were members of the research team working under him. This was the final test before Roland felt confident enough to begin trials on humans and the other races.

The device attached to the monster's head could replace its eyes, a breakthrough far more significant than anyone in the room realised. The immediate goal was to restore Aeloria's sight, but the implications of this research reached much further. It proved that long lost senses could now be restored. Sight was only the beginning. Even sensation in paralysed or damaged limbs might one day be recovered without relying on tier four healing spells.

‘How far can this technology go?’

While the others celebrated and congratulated one another, Roland's thoughts drifted to the future. Tier four healing spells were incredibly powerful, but they still had their limits.

He had stepped into the field of prosthetic replacement. He could now restore sight without reconnecting damaged optic nerves. Although the hobgoblin appeared to be wearing an artificial eye that fit into its sockets, that was not how the device actually worked. Everything functioned through the mana phantom and its connection to the soul, allowing a lost sense to be restored without repairing the original organ.

For now, the single golemic eye embedded in the centre of the device served as the subject's new source of vision, though it could eventually be replaced with a far less conspicuous design. The only thing left was to obtain permission from Arthur's father to examine his mother. Without studying Aeloria's mana pattern and tailoring the device specifically to her, restoring her sight would be impossible.

# 705: Chapter 703 – Quick Improvements.
Chapter 703 – Quick Improvements.

“I see, so it’s finished…”

“In theory, yes, but without being able to make the proper adjustments, it will be impossible to finish.”

Roland stood before Arthur, the Lord of Albrook and son of the Duke. In his hands was a sketch of the accessory he planned to place on Arthur's mother, one that would allow her to see again without the help of tier four or five magic.

“My father is not going to like this.”

Arthur muttered, leaning back in his leather chair.

“He is already wary of anyone coming into the castle, and even I can’t visit Mother more than twice a year now…”

This was the main problem. Arthur’s mother was kept like a bird in a cage. To some, it seemed the Duke was being unreasonable, but Roland could see the logic behind it.

Protecting Aeloria was far easier within the safety of that stronghold. Someone wanted her dead, so the cage she found herself in was, to some, a necessary precaution. Until the person behind the assassination attempt was identified, letting her roam freely would be unwise, and Arthur understood that as well.

“If only we knew who was behind this…”

“There are many possibilities. Your father’s wives are all potential suspects, but without solid evidence, pointing fingers is a death sentence.”

Arthur sighed deeply, the weight of his noble lineage visibly pressing on his shoulders. He stared at the sketch on his desk, his eyes tracing the delicate runic patterns Roland had drawn.

“I know.”

Arthur’s voice was barely above a whisper.

“My half-brothers are possible suspects as well. There is no one in that house I can truly trust…”

Roland watched Arthur’s expression tighten. His noble friend was usually good at hiding his emotions and playing the role of a composed lord, but whenever the subject of his mother came up, his anger always found a way to show itself.

“Has Mary been able to gather any more information?”

Roland asked, glancing down at the drawing he had presented. It depicted an elegant accessory that could be worn over the face while still looking stylish enough for a noble lady.

“Nothing much. This assassination group seems to be very well connected, and none of the information brokers are even willing to investigate them…”

He nodded. It made sense.

“An organization willing to carry out murder contracts in a duke’s backyard must have powerful backing.”

“Regretfully, yes. We still managed to gather some information thanks to your efforts, but Mary is still looking into it. You have my gratitude, as always. For now, however, I need some time to think.”

The assassin he had captured had been through a great deal, and while they had managed to extract some useful information from him, much of it led to dead ends. The organization they were up against was exceptionally skilled at covering its tracks and avoiding exposure. Still, from what he had heard, there was finally a light at the end of the tunnel.

“I’m just doing my job.”

“You should stop being so modest.”

Arthur laughed, and for a brief moment, some warmth returned to his face.

“Modest? Wait until you see next month’s bill.”

Roland, in turn, took a jab at the finances, referring to how most of the city's revenue was funneled back into runic research and the continued expansion of its runic infrastructure.

“You might be the most expensive runic craftsman in the entire kingdom, but you always deliver results, so it’s hard to complain.”

Arthur shrugged with a chuckle, and soon the two parted ways. There was not much new within their territory. Theodore was scheming as always, but with the underground train becoming fully operational, the situation with the merchants had been resolved. Even after the fake bandit groups stopped appearing, the train remained the best method of transportation.

They just need to clear out those tunnels, and the subway system under this island can finally begin.

Roland thought to himself as he left Arthur's office. The project he had started with Halbrecht was still ongoing. He was not thrilled about meeting the man again, but they had been exchanging correspondence through the runic mailing system he had established. It was much faster than the spell birds used to carry letters, and at this stage of development, it was far safer since the technology was still unknown to the public.

"Now then... what's next?"

He mumbled to himself as he descended into the underground tunnels. The golem was almost finished and nearly ready for proper field testing. Its size was somewhat of a problem, as it stood close to ten meters tall. Luckily, if laid flat on the ground, it could still fit through the city's main portal gate.

The dimensions had been chosen specifically so it could fit through the standard gates, based on what he knew. In the past, when the Convocation was still in its early years, each academy transported its golems separately, which caused all kinds of problems.

Some smiths transported the individual parts by other means and assembled the construct at the event. Others simply had their golems walk the entire distance, and many never arrived. There were countless reasons for the failures. Monster attacks, bandits, and even rival academies sabotaging the route by destroying the only bridge leading to the venue. Eventually, when portal technology became more widely available, the organizers decided to use it to transport the constructs instead, making the competition fair for everyone.

‘I haven't checked up on those guys for a while. I wonder how they're doing…’

Roland had devoted most of his time to leveling and golem production. Now that the project was almost complete, he finally had the chance to visit the students who had been using the support devices he had created. By this point, every student taking part in the competition had received the base model, but he had not really had the time to see how the other teachers and professors had implemented his teaching methods.

"Welcome, Deputy Professor."

He nodded to the mage responsible for the teleportation gate and made his way forward. Around two months remained until the competition, and the academy was once again filled with students. Everyone who had left for vacation had returned, and the new academic year had begun.

‘Each time I return here, their reactions to me become stranger…’

At first, all he saw was fear in their eyes. Whenever he walked by, they parted before him, pressing themselves against the walls or turning their heads away. Now, however, they could not stop staring or pointing at him.

"It's the Deputy Professor!"

"Hey, Deputy Professor! Can I be your disciple!?"

"No, I want to be his disciple!"

If you discover this tale on Amazon, be aware that it has been stolen. Please report the violation.

His infamy had transformed into outright fame. He had a suspicion of why. The students under his direct supervision had undergone a drastic change.

"He even managed to change that Elian..."

He almost flinched when he heard Elian's name. Before assembling the team, he had no idea the young man had become so infamous. His magical aptitude had always been exceptional, but his nerves often got the better of him, causing him to fumble at the worst possible moments. That habit had apparently led to more than a few self-inflicted injuries while learning new spells and, on occasion, had nearly dragged other people into the accidents as well.

‘Had he really improved that much?’

Roland could not judge the extent of Elian's progress. He only monitored the training chamber the boy occasionally used and watched recordings of the team's practice sessions while working his way through the underground dungeon. Perhaps his standards for teenagers were simply too high, because rumors of their rapid improvement seemed to be everywhere.

‘Am I being too harsh on them?’

Once again, he questioned whether his training methods were too draconian. At their age, he had already been running his own shop in Albrook, though he had the advantage of possessing the mind of an adult from another world. He was not entirely sure what should be expected of ordinary teenagers, but judging by the results, the training he had designed was paying off.

"Ah, Professor Wayland, just look!"

The moment he arrived, Arion greeted him enthusiastically and pointed a paw toward one of the monitors. Roland looked over and saw Elian practicing his spellcasting. The boy was performing far better than before. He was not the only one, either. The number of monitors had doubled since Roland's last visit, and each displayed young mages hard at work.

It was not only the group Roland and his sister personally trained. There were younger students and older ones as well. All of them were dodging magical paralysis bolts while maintaining their concentration and casting spells.

"You've made more?"

"Indeed! The Vice Headmaster approved the construction of additional chambers, and we even incorporated them into this year's curriculum. The results speak for themselves."

Arion beamed with pride as he pointed to another monitor displaying a spreadsheet. It compared the casting times of students who trained in the new chambers with those who followed the traditional methods. Those using the new devices had not only improved their spellcasting speed but had also significantly reduced the time needed to prepare their spells.

Even the tier one students were achieving results that were normally not seen until after obtaining a tier two class. They had not reached that level yet, but they were steadily closing the gap, much like Roland had once done by creating tier two runes despite only possessing a tier one class.

"Though there is a small problem now..."

"Oh? There is?"

"Indeed. The energy required to maintain the illusion chambers is staggering. There is not enough capacity for every student, so a waiting line has formed."

"That is a problem... You could restrict access through a merit system, but that would hurt the students at the bottom the most."

Roland muttered the thought to himself, though everyone nearby heard him and gave him puzzled looks. He knew exactly why. He came from a more modern world, one where people were not simply left behind if they fell behind the others. At the very least, everyone was usually given an equal opportunity to prove themselves.

That way of thinking was still uncommon in this world. It was considered perfectly normal for wealthy or exceptionally talented students to monopolize the training chambers. To Roland, that approach made little sense, especially for an academy that competed with others. The more the school invested in its students as a whole, the greater its prestige would become, making it far more attractive to high-profile nobles choosing between rival academies.

“We could, but as you say, Professor, that would make it less fair for everyone. But what should we do then? There is no way to increase the academy's mana output. We would need to find another relic or build more towers…”

Arion did not know what to do, but to Roland, the answer was quite simple.

“Why does it need to be in an illusory chamber in the first place?”

“Oh... you're right!”

Sometimes it felt like some people were too focused on what was directly in front of them to notice the obvious solutions. In the first place, he had used the illusory chamber because it was the fastest way to achieve the results he wanted. Nothing was stopping them from recreating those training chambers through conventional means and runic magic.

“Here.”

“This is...”

Arion's catlike eyes went wide with shock as he looked over the detailed schematics of various training rooms. Roland had recreated every variation he had used to help the students train with runic devices, designing each one to be as inexpensive as possible. While hunting in the dungeon, he had plenty of time between monster fights, so coming up with this solution had been fairly trivial.

“By the old magic gods, this is wonderful. If we build these, the cost of maintaining them would be much lower, and we are already using the golemic puppets, so that is not even an added expense! I need to go to the Vice Headmaster!”

Arion floated out of the room while the other teachers continued applauding just as they had before. Roland had already grown accustomed to this level of flattery, though that did not mean he appreciated it. At least it made it easier to hand off much of his work to the eager teachers, with the most enthusiastic of them all being the stuttering Stephen.

“D-Deputy Professor.”

“Stephen... these are...”

As he had many times before, the man brought over a massive stack of papers, though this time they were organized into several thick binders.

“I have c-calculated all the i-improvements in the s students' performance, their casting speed, mana efficiency, and their response t-times to u-unexpected stimuli!”

Stephen stammered, pushing his thick glasses up his nose as he struggled to balance the heavy stack.

“Didn't I tell you to just input all of this into the runic operating system instead?”

Roland asked, as he much preferred reviewing the data through the runic system instead of sorting through stacks of paper. To his surprise, the assistant teacher already had an answer prepared.

“D-don't worry, sir. I already input e-everything just the way you like it!”

“I see... so you did both...”

“Stephen, I really appreciate your hard work, but you should take better care of yourself and stop drinking those stamina potions.”

Roland could see the dark circles beneath the young man's eyes. While they could be treated with healing elixirs, there was a limit to how much strain the body could endure. Even though the young man was doing excellent work, Roland did not want him to shorten his lifespan by overworking himself.

‘I wonder... is this how people see me whenever I work too much?’

The image of his worried wife came to mind, along with his assistant. Whenever he worked through the night or for days on end, they would give him disapproving looks, but they knew they could not really change his mind, so eventually they stopped trying. The only exception was his wife Elodia, who would sometimes drag him out of the workshop by force whenever he stayed there for too long.

“I-I'm fine, really. T-this is all to gain more knowledge!”

“Sure, you can gain more knowledge tomorrow. Go eat something and get some sleep. Until then, your research privileges are revoked.”

It seemed that he needed to put his foot down on this issue. This man was not a tier three class holder, and his body was nowhere near as resilient as Roland's. Roland himself could more or less go without sleep for close to a week by now.

“N-no. A-anything but that!”

He had expected some resistance, but before he could shake his head and refuse again, the man dropped to all fours and started begging as though Roland were some villain trying to steal his child.

“Please, Deputy Professor, do not take away my rune terminal access! Anything but that!”

Surprisingly, Stephen was no longer stuttering while pleading to keep his research privileges, but Roland could not relent now. The other teachers were watching him, and if he showed that he could be swayed by emotion, they would surely exploit it in the future. Before long, he would have crowds of teachers clinging to his legs in the hallways, begging for more research funding.

“My decision is final. You can either take a break now and return tomorrow morning, or have your access revoked for another three days. The choice is yours.”

“B-but... just some more research... a few more hours.”

“No. And if you keep arguing, I'll revoke your access for a whole week.”

Stephen let out a quiet, defeated whine, his shoulders slumping as he slowly pushed himself up from the floor. He looked like a man who had just been sentenced to exile, but beneath Roland's unyielding, glowing visor, he finally nodded, grabbed his coat, and shuffled out of the research room.

The remaining teachers watched the exchange with a mixture of awe and terror. Roland ignored their stares and turned his attention to the monitors near the main desk. There he found the five students he cared about most, the party that included his sister.

‘They really have improved. If this keeps up, victory shouldn't be difficult.’

He smiled beneath his helmet as he watched Lucienne move through the illusory landscape. She darted around with far more confidence than before, using spells to enhance her physical abilities. With the support device, the process was both simple and effective.

She could now keep track of the enemies thanks to her improved Clairvoyant abilities, while the other students protected her well. With each passing day, they looked more and more like a proper adventuring party, though there was still room for improvement.

‘Good. Everything is progressing well. Now I just need to help them level up before the event starts, and we should be ready to go.’

Roland stepped away from the screens. He had already planned a short training expedition for all the participants. They would spend a few days fighting real monsters, and once that was over, they would be more or less ready for the convocation.

The rumble of heavy metal wheels and the steady footsteps of mechanical golems echoed between the nearby buildings, drawing curious glances from passersby. Students from other institutions had already spread throughout the plaza, taking in the sights of the ancient floating city.

Beyond the teleportation gate lay a city unto itself. The buildings were far from empty. Instead, they housed all manner of magical creatures peddling enchanted wares. Merchants filled the streets as well, eager to profit from the gathering of mages, who were known to have deep pockets.

Roland continued monitoring the magical readings while keeping an eye out for anything suspicious. He could not deploy his floating golems to scout the area as he normally would. The city was protected by ancient wards that would immediately alert the resident mages to the presence of his devices. Doing so would only attract unwanted attention and could even get him expelled through the gate before the golem match had even begun.

"This place... are all places with magic... this strange? It's very different from Albrook."

Robert looked around as they continued walking. Having never visited a place with such a high concentration of magical beings, everything felt foreign to him. He was not used to summoned creatures wandering the streets, floating objects drifting overhead, or enchanted brooms sweeping the roads on their own. Albrook was far more modern thanks to runic technology, and it felt much less chaotic than what he was seeing here.

"It depends on the location. Academies that focus on exotic summoning rituals are known for being quite unique. A lot depends on the headmaster and how much freedom they give their mages."

Mages were an odd bunch. Some were highly intelligent and wise, while others relied almost entirely on the game-like world system to cast their spells. That often led to strange incidents and accidents, especially in places with little oversight. Some academies even permitted inhumane experiments involving not only monsters but people as well. Such institutions were commonly known as Dark Academies. They were barred from events like this because of their ties to black magic, curses, and even necromancy.

"I see... I think Lucille would enjoy this place. I think we passed a shop selling runic scrolls."

"She probably would. I've got the gate coordinates, so maybe once we're done here, I could arrange something."

"You could? But won't we be found?"

"Here? I don't think so. The people living here don't share the nobility's sensibilities. Many of them openly despise nobles. If you told them you were being hunted by one, they might even hand you a free magical item."

Roland and Robert chatted as they made their way down the wide street. If his brother was being pursued, he could come here. There were masks that concealed faces and magical spells that obscured identities. In a place like this, no one would even bother verifying who anyone really was as long as someone vouched for their identity, which he could. It was relatively safe, but at the same time, many things could still go wrong if magic spun out of control.

The logistics team turned onto a quieter avenue away from the main commercial district. The crowd thinned out, replaced by rows of identical white stone barracks reserved for the visiting delegations. Xandar's Institute had been assigned a midsized compound near the eastern edge, offering a clear view of the surrounding barrier and the flowing waters beyond it.

Two assistant teachers from the Runic Department were already waiting outside the heavy iron gates of the compound. Upon seeing Roland and the transport container, they motioned for the golems to proceed into the inner courtyard.

"D-Deputy Professor, over here!"

"I can see you, Stephen. You can relax and go see the sights."

"T-thank you, sir!"

Stephen had become something of a linchpin during the event. He had received the layout of the area beforehand and made sure everyone knew where to unload the container carrying the golem. His work ethic was similar to Roland's, and with each passing day he seemed to be building up his Sleep Resistance skill.

"Is he okay? He looks like he hasn't slept for a week..."

Robert commented as he watched the disheveled Stephen shuffle past him.

"I'm not sure, but he's earned the chance to explore the area."

Roland replied as he watched the golems place the container in the middle of the large storage building. It was quite heavy, but with the help of his magic and a dozen or so golem assistants, they lowered it from the transport platform.

"Good, set it down. Lock all the doors and don't let anyone who isn't affiliated with the institute inside. Now let's go through the students' support equipment. We need to inspect every piece. Report to me if you find any damage or inconsistencies."

“Aye!”

Some of the dwarven craftsmen from the institute were here as well. They helped set up the midsized compound by bringing out workbenches and placing various support items on them that looked like ordinary accessories. There were bracelets, pendants, rings, and even pieces that resembled belts or shoes. They came in all kinds of designs, each tailored to perfectly suit the needs of every student.

'They should be fine here without me...'

Roland mumbled to himself as he stepped off to the side. Leaning against the wall, he made it look as though he was keeping an eye on the runic department staff. In reality, he was focused on the display inside his helmet.

"Oh, Roland, calling back already?"

"Yeah, I just arrived. Things are peaceful here. I think I should be able to get back home within a week."

"That's great!"

Elodia's face appeared on the small screen, which did not rely on his runic network but instead used crystal ball communication magic. For now, they were too far apart for his own technology to work, but fortunately the older method was still reliable and could be used by altering runes.

"So, what have you been up to? Grading the kids' papers? How are they doing?"

"Kids? Oh, yeah. They are improving every day, though some still need help with writing and reading."

For a brief moment, he noticed her flinch when he mentioned her students. She seemed nervous for some reason, probably because of her growing workload.

"Well, if you ever need anything, just call. It might take me a while to respond, but is there anything important I should know?"

"Important... um, not really. If anything comes up that we can't handle here, I'll be sure to send you some R-mail."

"Great."

Roland chatted with his wife for a little while longer, but soon he had to return to work. The golem needed one final inspection before the competition began, and he wanted to make sure everything was in perfect working order.
