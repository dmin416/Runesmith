# Ned (Needle Worm companion)

Roland’s first tamed companion. Named **Ned**. Core-bearing Floor-2 **Needle Worm**; worn as a green scarf after Ch 12 **Basic Taming** L1. Species entry: `Creatures.md`. Mounts index: `Mounts.md`.

## Diet (Ch 13 half-year)

Eats various **leaves** and discarded **monster parts**. Hides a preferred leaf stash: those leaves are a **mild poison source** (weaker than his spikes) with a vibrant refreshing aroma like **parsley** (**parsleaves**). Roland samples one afternoon and locks that link. Besides occasionally harvesting Ned’s ever-growing **needles** and **blood**, Roland also **dries and stockpiles** parsleaves. After Roland harvested Ned’s blood for BBQ seasoning, Ned pressed to know why; Roland started feeding him small drips of his own blood via Mana Hands (Ch 13). Ned does not taste like a humanoid: he mainly senses how much power the flesh holds and the experience it grants. Monsters can level by eating (unlike humanoids who need actions); that feeds their presence and faster climb. Roland only feeds his blood in-room (**150 mL**/day, about **5 oz**); he uses Ned’s blood and needles as seasoning and keeps the skill-leveling cheat quiet. Worm blood is armpit-smelling but incredibly nutrient-rich even when Ned only ate magic stones; Roland extracts as much as he wants comfortably. Sleeps with Ned as a cool ectothermic pillow. He talks to Ned as an outlet for self-talk: future plans, skill thought process, training ideas (sneaking, throwing, adapting, healing and more). Dislikes bark-colored **Needle Moths**; shows Ned how weak that evolution line is while feeding him poisonous wings and occasionally stones. Drills the vision: speed, penetration, toughness, recovery, poison, stealth, ranged attack. After taming, Ned can pass **stair throats** (wild monsters cannot); strangers assume a Floor-2 trophy scarf and Roland never corrects them. Once strong enough, Ned solo-hunts dungeon rats and other Needle Worms; monsters cooperate in packs but also fight each other for strength.

Design below is the charging-caterpillar write for Ned (Earth biomechanics anchors for skin, spring and toxins). Map to Caldris; do not paste Earth taxonomies into prose as if they are local.

**Ramp design:** In-form caps **3 / 7 / 13 / 21 / 31**. Each evolution jump equals the tier just finished (**+1 / +2 / +3 / +4**), landing on square floors **4 / 9 / 16 / 25**. A later T6 would jump **+5: 31x → 36x**. Evolving always spikes; in-form growth stays smooth under the next floor.

**Why multipliers, not +stats per level:** Capability Stats are a **1x dog-scale body** (1 m / 10 kg). If each level added a flat share of the L1 package, hundreds of levels would mean hundreds of raw points and break the creature. Scaling the whole sheet by a ratio keeps the body honest: **10–20×** an adult man’s Strength packed into a dog-sized charger is already enormous (impact speed, tip pressure, spring energy). Late **Ormr** near **31x** is extreme on purpose, not “+1 Str × 500 levels.”

### Combat balance (locked)

Life is balanced. Ned dumps **Dexterity** and **Intelligence** (no hands, no plans, aim is body pointing). In return he is built for:

- **Single explosive attacks** – spring charge / spike hit; load slow, release once, hit hard
- **Durability** – skin, Vitality, clotting seal, hard to finish

Do not write him as a clever duelist or a multitasking fighter. Do not punish a sealed wound as if he were still open; after a fast seal he is closed and tanky. Soften output only when the **spring / spike root / nerve** is actually compromised, not because a scar exists.

## Development notes (training / growth)

How Roland steers Ned’s loadout as he levels. Not automatic freebies; practice and exposure unlock or deepen them.

- **Throwing:** Practice hurling spikes and rocks → ranged needle launch / improvised rock throw accuracy and force.
- **Stealth:** Focus on staying hidden → camouflage (leaf-green blend, stillness, ambush timing).
- **Poison:** Eat more poisons and foreign spikes / venoms → broader toxin repertoire and **Poison Resistance** (he is already immune to his own).
- **Attack build:** Push harder spikes, faster regeneration, and higher durability so charges and needle strikes hit harder and recover between fights.

### Fastest recovery (seal first)

Combat priority is **close the bag fast**: living epidermis + hemolymph / glue seal, cast off dead scrap, neighboring cells stick the edges. That makes him **hard to kill** on the Dex/Int dump trade. Deeper remodel of spring and spike bed can lag; a sealed Ned is still a durable explosive animal, not an open corpse.

**Tun / cryptobiosis** is survival stasis (dry shell, revive with water). It is **not** the fast recovery mode.

### Circulatory fluid restore (Ned hemolymph)

M = ((Vitality + Endurance) / 2) / 15

**1 g** caterpillar: **~0.15–0.25 mL** hemolymph. Fluid restores in **6–12 h** via feeding and gut uptake. Hemocytes form in tissue all over the body, so cellular refill keeps pace with fluid (no marrow bottleneck).

- Hemolymph volume **~1.5–2.5 L** (body size stays **10 kg**; multipliers do not grow the tank)

# Ned skill leveling model (revised)

Skills cap at **L9**. Filling the L9 bar triggers a **skill evolution** instead of L10. Progress counts **actions**, not hours.

## Core formula

**Effective actions = action count × action weight × Aptitude**

**Aptitude** = √(governing stat now ÷ 15), so 1.0 equals adult-human learning speed.

**Action weight:**

- Coached rep with Roland demonstrating: **1.0**
- Solo rep: **0.5**
- Real hunt or injury stakes: **2.0**

**Level cost:** the bar for level n costs **100 × n²** effective actions at Aptitude 1.0.

| Level reached | L2 | L3 | L4 | L5 | L6 | L7 | L8 | L9 | Evolve |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative actions | 100 | 500 | 1,400 | 3,000 | 5,500 | 9,100 | 14,000 | 20,400 | 28,500 |

## What counts as one action

| Skill | Action | Governing stat | Aptitude at 1x | Aptitude at 13x |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spike Throwing / Needle Launch | One spike or rock thrown at a target | Dexterity | 0.58 | 2.08 |
| Silk Production | One strand spun for binding or line | Dex + Int | 0.58 | 2.08 |
| Stealth / Camouflage | One held hide or ambush attempt | Agility + Willpower | 0.89 | 2.90 |
| Spring Charge | One full coil and release | Strength | 1.15 | 4.10 |
| Seal / Regeneration | One wound sealed | Vitality | 1.29 | 4.65 |
| Poison Resistance | One dose ingested or exposure survived | Vitality | 1.29 | 4.65 |

- **Spring Charge** is throttled by the ~12 s reload and by muscle cost, so practical volume is a handful per day.
- **Seal / Regeneration** is bottlenecked by injury supply. Roland's needle and blood harvesting gives small controlled wounds that feed it safely.
- **Poison Resistance** is exposure-gated. Repeat doses of a toxin already mastered count at **0.25**, and each new toxin eaten counts at **5x** for its first dose.

## Body evolution interaction

Body evolutions no longer raise a skill cap. Each jump (+1 / +2 / +3 / +4) lifts every stat at once and therefore every aptitude, which gives a **leveling surge**. Greater Needle Worm takes Throwing aptitude from ~1.0 to ~1.15 in one step and raises every level bar's effective speed by the same ratio.

## Pacing outcomes

- **Body-native skills** (Charge, Seal, Poison Resistance) lead early through combat and injury volume.
- **Precision skills** (Throwing, Silk) lag until Dex aptitude passes 1.0 near overall level 25.
- **Stealth** evolves first because drill volume is high and Agility + Willpower aptitude is the strongest of the precision group.
- **Silk** stays the slowest skill to evolve because of the Int dump and low action volume.

# Charging Caterpillar: Strengths

## Skin
- Soft to the touch with a rubbery resilin-rich outer layer
- Crisscrossed chitin-protein fibers at 35 to 70 MPa spread stabs and bites sideways
- Fluid-filled inner cushion absorbs blunt force
- Resilin returns about 95% of impact energy so dents rebound within seconds
- Living epidermis repairs cuts and punctures in place with no molt needed
- Clotting hemolymph seals wounds fast

## Spikes
- Grow continuously from a living base like rodent incisors or a narwhal tusk
- Core of hardened cuticle at 200 to 300 MPa
- Zinc or manganese reinforced tips at 1 to 3 GPa hardness
- Chipped or worn tips regrow and stay sharp through use
- Tapered cone pierces hide and light armor at charge speed
- Spare spike buds behind the main ones replace any that break off entirely
- Spike length increases with age so older individuals hit harder

## Body Spring Charge
- Dorsal band of resilin-chitin fibers runs head to tail and stores energy
- Muscles curl the body into a tight C and a latch locks it
- Release straightens the body in milliseconds and throws the spiked head forward
- Pressurized body fluid carries the force into the fused head plates
- Spring recoil at the end of the charge adds force to the spike impact
- Peak power exceeds direct muscle output because energy is stored slowly and released fast
- Long segmented body spreads the recoil along its length
- Compact 1 m body packs the spring energy into one eighth of the mass of a 2 m body

## Stubby Legs
- Four short thick pairs in tardigrade style moved by fluid pressure and muscle
- Claw tips dig in at launch so the release pushes the body forward
- Low ground clearance keeps the coil stable
- Legs steer and brace while the body supplies all the speed
- Claws double as grip and light slashing weapons

## Tardigrade Traits
- Dsup-style protein shields DNA so tissue survives repeated strain and repairs quickly
- CAHS-style glass-forming proteins stabilize cells against shock and dehydration
- Cells tolerate pressures in the hundreds of MPa so impact does not rupture them
- Emergency tun state: legs and head pull in and the body dries into a dense shell that revives with water
- Cryptobiosis survives long droughts and starvation between hunts

## Body
- No molt means no soft vulnerable period
- Segmented nervous system keeps undamaged segments working after a wound
- **Hemolymph** (insect “blood”) is an open, non-centralized bath: no heart-dependent choke point; clotting seals local leaks without a single vessel map to cut
- **Oxygenation** is non-centralized: tracheal air tubes feed segments directly; a row of **spiracles** along the flanks keeps breathing local so one crush or puncture does not stop the whole body
- Air tubes spread through the body so a single injury cannot cut off breathing
- Huge midgut converts plant matter into mass and repair material quickly
- Fat body stores energy for fast recovery after injury
- **Immune to his own toxins** (venom, sequestered tissue poisons, sprays). Self-puncture or sprayed mist does not poison him; enemy exposure still applies

## Insect Toxins

### Injected venoms (spike candidates)
- **Lonomia venom** (giant silkworm caterpillar): anticoagulant that causes internal bleeding and kidney failure
- **Poneratoxin** (bullet ant): neurotoxic peptide that locks sodium channels open and causes hours of extreme pain
- **Solenopsin** (fire ant): piperidine alkaloid that kills tissue and causes pustules
- **Melittin** (honeybee): peptide that tears open cell membranes and drives pain and swelling
- **Maricopa harvester ant venom**: among the most toxic insect venoms measured by lethal dose
- **Diamphotoxin** (Diamphidia beetle larvae): protein that destroys red blood cells and is used by San hunters on arrow tips
- **Parasitoid wasp venom** (tarantula hawk and others): paralyzes prey without killing it

### Contact and blistering toxins
- **Cantharidin** (blister beetles): blocks protein phosphatase and raises blisters on contact
- **Pederin** (Paederus rove beetles): made with bacterial partners and causes severe skin lesions from tiny amounts
- **Urticating setae** (tussock and browntail moth larvae): barbed hairs that lodge in skin and trigger histamine release

### Sequestered from food (stored in tissue)
- **Cardenolides** (monarch caterpillars from milkweed): cardiac glycosides that disrupt heart muscle in predators
- **Pyrrolizidine alkaloids** (tiger moths): plant-derived liver toxins that also protect eggs
- **Batrachotoxin** (Choresine beetles): the same nerve toxin found in poison dart frogs that keeps sodium channels open
- **Coccinelline** (ladybugs): bitter alkaloid released in bleeding hemolymph

### Sprays and gases
- **Benzoquinones** (bombardier beetle): hydroquinones and hydrogen peroxide react explosively and spray at about 100 C
- **Formic acid** (ants): sprayed acid that burns skin and eyes
- **Hydrogen cyanide** (Zygaena burnet moth larvae): released from stored cyanogenic glycosides when tissue is damaged
- **Osmeterium acids** (swallowtail caterpillars): foul-smelling isobutyric and 2-methylbutyric acid from a forked gland behind the head

### Best fits for the charging caterpillar
- Lonomia-style anticoagulant in the spike base glands so every puncture keeps bleeding
- Poneratoxin-style pain toxin for instant deterrence against anything that survives the charge
- Cardenolide sequestration so the tissue itself is poisonous to predators
- Bombardier-style spray as a last-resort defense while the body recoils for the next charge
- Ned is **immune to all of the above on himself**; they only threaten other creatures

## Melee Use

### Swung by a superhuman (3x strength)
- Swing alone: about 8 m/s and 0.3 kJ with roughly 0.3 kN of pull on the arm
- A curl release at the last second adds the 8 kJ spring and sends a 35 to 70 kN spike back through the grip
- Bone in a 3x arm fails around 5 to 12 kN so a fractured forearm or upper arm and a dislocated shoulder are likely if the grip holds
- Otherwise the grip on the soft skin tears loose first and the caterpillar launches out of the hands
- The target takes about 8.5 kJ: roughly nine times a hard sledgehammer blow and about 40 times what breaks a forearm
- A blocking arm shatters and the spikes pierce through

### Thrust as a launcher
- Hold the rear segments with both hands in a braced stance so the recoil goes through the torso and legs
- Thrust like a spear since a swing loads the arm sideways at release
- Lunge as the body uncoils so the wielder's speed adds to the spring
- Throw it low so the claws dig in at launch and the reaction goes into the ground

### Bouncing
- Each landing recaptures some elastic energy in the resilin so hops keep roughly 30 to 50% of the spring loaded (estimate)
- Good for closing distance fast and staying unpredictable
- Poor for finishing blows since a half-loaded spring delivers about 2.5 to 4 kJ
- Best pattern: hop in to keep partial charge then pause for a full coil and thrust

### Best method: C to C flail
- The wielder swings the caterpillar on a rope like a flail while it holds a loaded C shape
- Just before contact the body snaps into the reverse C so head and tail whip around and hit the target together as a pincer
- The flip is a bistable snap like a slap bracelet or a flytrap so stored energy passes from one curl to the opposite curl with little loss
- The release fires about 15 cm before contact at an 8 m/s swing since each end needs 15 to 20 ms to close
- Energy splits at about 4.1 kJ per end at roughly 67 m/s plus 0.3 kJ from the swing for about 8.5 kJ total
- A target sandwiched between both ends absorbs nearly all of the energy instead of being knocked away
- The reverse curl is already the loaded state for the next strike so reload needs only a re-latch and a muscle top up
- Every swing can end in a snap but a full-power hit needs about 12 s of reloading after the energy is dumped into a target
- The release is internal with both ends cancelling so the arm carries only the swing pull (about 0.3 kN) instead of the 35 to 70 kN thrust recoil
- The recoil snap back to the original C can pull the ends off the target and catch it a second time on the return
- Only the head has spikes so the tail lands as a blunt anvil and continuous spike growth could add a spiked tail

## Energy Formula

### 1 m body (10 kg)
```
E (J)   = 409 x Strength
m (kg)  = 10
v (m/s) = sqrt(2E / m) = about 9 x sqrt(Strength)
F (N)   = E / 1 m = about 409 x Strength
```
- Spring energy is muscle force times stroke so with a fixed stroke it is linear in Strength
- Speed comes from energy at fixed mass so it grows with the square root of Strength
- Total blow = E plus the wielder's swing energy
- At the current Strength 20: E = 8.2 kJ and v = about 40 m/s
- Full reload time = E / (33 W x Strength) = about 12 s at Strength 20
- The stroke is half that of a 2 m body so the dorsal band must deliver twice the force with denser resilin-chitin fibers and a thicker band
- Momentum is roughly a third of the 2 m body so there is less knockback
- The energy lands in a smaller area at higher speed so penetration improves
- The pincer closes in about half the time so the release fires later in the swing
- The 10 kg body is far easier to swing and nearly all of the blow comes from the spring

### 2 m body reference (80 kg)
```
E (J)   = 409 x Strength
v (m/s) = about 3.2 x sqrt(Strength)
```
- Same energy in eight times the mass makes the ends about 2.8 times slower
- A larger body raises mass and stroke so it needs its own scaling on top of this
