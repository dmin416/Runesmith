## How to Read This

- **Total neurons:** neurons in the whole brain or nervous system.
- **Forebrain neurons:** neurons in the cerebral cortex (mammals), pallium (birds) or mushroom bodies (insects). This is the best single predictor of intelligence currently known.
- **Brain mass:** approximate adult brain weight. "n/a" where no reliable figure is available.
- **Intelligence tier (1 to 10):** a rough composite of problem solving, learning, memory, tool use and social reasoning.

### Intelligence Tier Scale

| Tier | Level | Typical abilities |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reflex | Fixed responses to touch, light and chemicals |
| 2 | Basic learning | Habituation and simple conditioning |
| 3 | Insect level | Associative learning, navigation, memory of locations |
| 4 | Advanced small brain | Concept learning, counting to small numbers, communication |
| 5 | Basic vertebrate | Spatial learning, individual recognition, simple problem solving |
| 6 | Small mammal / bird | Mazes, flexible learning, some social learning |
| 7 | Clever mammal | Multi-step problem solving, reading human cues, play |
| 8 | Highly intelligent | Tool use, planning, mirror self-recognition in some species, rich social reasoning |
| 9 | Great ape level | Complex tool use, culture, symbol learning, deception, long-term planning |
| 10 | Human | Language, abstract reasoning, cumulative technology |

Total neuron count alone does not rank intelligence. Elephants have three times as many neurons as humans but most sit in the cerebellum, which handles movement. Forebrain neuron count tracks intelligence far better: humans lead all measured land animals at about 16 billion.

## AI Models

AI models have no neurons. The closest equivalent is **parameters**: numerical weights loosely comparable to synapse strengths.

| System | Size |
|---|---|
| Mouse brain | ~1 trillion synapses |
| Cat brain | ~10 trillion synapses |
| Human brain | ~150 trillion synapses |
| Small AI models (phones, laptops) | 1 to 10 billion parameters |
| Mid-size AI models | 30 to 100 billion parameters |
| Large open-weight AI models | Several hundred billion to about 1 trillion parameters |
| Frontier closed AI models | Not published; outside estimates run into the trillions |

**Size comparison:** the largest published models have roughly as many parameters as a mouse brain has synapses. A single parameter is far simpler than a biological synapse, which changes strength over time and uses many chemical signals.

**Rough intelligence:** AI does not fit on the animal tier scale because its abilities are uneven.

- **Strong:** language, writing, coding, math, recall of knowledge across almost every field. Top models score at or above skilled human level on many written and reasoning tests.
- **Weak:** physical interaction with the world, learning continuously from new experience after training, memory across sessions without added tools, reliable judgment in unfamiliar real-world situations.

A crow or a rat beats any current AI model at navigating and surviving in a physical environment. Current AI beats every animal including most humans at broad written knowledge and speed of text-based reasoning.

## Crow Brain

Crows sit at tier 8, the same level as monkeys and parrots.

| Measure | Crow |
|---|---|
| Total neurons | ~1.5 billion (rook, a close relative); ravens reach 2.2 billion |
| Forebrain neurons | ~820 million (rook); ravens reach 1.2 billion |
| Brain mass | ~10 g |
| Caterpillar brain equivalent | 15,000 to 150,000 |
| Child equivalent | Matches 5 to 7 year olds on some cause and effect puzzles |

### What Crows Can Do

- **Recognize individual human faces** and remember them for years. Crows that were trapped by researchers in masks kept scolding that mask long after. They also teach other crows which faces are dangerous, including young crows that never saw the original event.
- **Appreciation and reciprocity.** There are documented cases of crows leaving small objects for people who feed them regularly. They track who treats them well and who treats them badly.
- **Make tools.** New Caledonian crows bend wire into hooks and use one tool to get another tool to reach food.
- **Plan ahead.** Ravens save a tool for a task they expect to face later.
- **Understand displacement.** Crows drop stones into water to raise the level and reach floating food.
- **Understand zero** as a quantity.
- **Gather around dead crows** to learn what killed them and avoid that danger afterward.

## Copying a Brain Into Crystal: Best Choices

Assumes the brain can be copied or programmed into crystal where it functions and grows.

| Goal | Best brain | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Best efficiency overall | Raven or crow | Recognition, loyalty, grudges, tool making and planning in a 10 to 15 g brain |
| Most forebrain neurons per gram | Macaw or parrot | 1.9 billion forebrain neurons in 20 g, more than a rhesus macaque cortex in a 90 g brain |
| Maximum capability | Human | 16 billion forebrain neurons, unmatched on land |
| Controlling many limbs or constructs at once | Octopus | Two thirds of neurons sit in the arms, so each limb runs semi-independently |
| Cheap mass-produced workers | Honeybee | Under 1 million neurons yet capable of navigation, counting, communication and face recognition |
| Best for growth | Any brain copied young | Young brains have far more connections and plasticity than adult ones |

### Crow Compared to Other Options

- **Crow vs parrot:** a macaw has more forebrain neurons (1.9 billion vs 820 million) but crows have the stronger record for tool making, planning and holding grudges or loyalty.
- **Crow vs raven:** a raven is the upgraded version, about 50% more forebrain neurons with similar abilities.
- **Crow vs dog:** similar forebrain neuron counts but a crow brain is 7 to 10 times smaller.

For a compact crystal brain that recognizes individuals, remembers how they were treated and responds with loyalty or hostility, a raven or crow is the most efficient option available.

## Baby and Child Mental Capacity

A newborn already has roughly the full adult count of about 86 billion neurons in a brain of about 350 to 400 g. What it lacks is wiring. Synapses per cortical neuron jump from about 2,500 at birth to about 15,000 by age 2 to 3, then get pruned back as the brain specializes.

| Age | Brain mass (% adult) | Rough tier | Abilities |
|---|---|---|---|
| Newborn | ~25% | 5 | Recognizes mother's voice and smell, prefers faces |
| 6 months | ~50% | 6 | Recognizes familiar people, early object permanence |
| 1 year | ~70% | 7 | First words, pointing, understands simple commands |
| 18 months | ~75% | 7 to 8 | Mirror self-recognition |
| 2.5 years | ~80% | 8 to 9 | Matches chimpanzees on physical puzzles and beats them on social tasks |
| 4 to 5 years | ~90% | 9 | Understands that others have different beliefs |
| Adult | 100% | 10 | |

A copied baby brain would have the highest potential of anything listed but would need years of experience to reach it.

## Could a Human-Minded Caterpillar Befriend Bees

Yes, within limits. The caterpillar could not hold a conversation because a bee brain of about 1 million neurons cannot process language. It could communicate in the bees' own channels: smell, vibration, touch and food.

### Real-World Precedent

Some butterfly caterpillars already do this with ants, which are close relatives of bees.

- Many blue butterfly caterpillars secrete sugary droplets that ants drink. In return ants guard them from predators.
- The large blue caterpillar mimics ant colony scent and imitates the sounds of a queen ant. Ants carry it into the nest and feed it.
- The death's-head hawkmoth coats itself in chemicals matching bee scent to walk into hives unharmed.

### How It Would Work With Bees

- **Acceptance:** guard bees attack anything that smells foreign. The caterpillar would need to match hive odor by producing the right body chemicals or coating itself in comb wax.
- **Friendship through food:** bees learn reward sources in one or two visits. Offering sugar water or nectar would make foragers seek it out. This is the fastest route to being treated as valuable.
- **Directing bees:** bees have been guided to specific locations by a robotic bee performing the waggle dance. A caterpillar could wiggle its body and drum on comb to imitate it. It lacks wings to produce the wing buzz, so accuracy would be weaker.
- **Warnings:** bees use a head-butt with a vibration pulse called the stop signal to warn about danger at a food site. A caterpillar could copy that.

### Limits

- Bees recognize colony membership by smell more than individual identity. The friendship would be with the hive, not with specific bees.
- Messages are limited to what bees already understand: food location, food quality, danger, colony identity.
- After metamorphosis the adult butterfly or moth would gain wings. It could dance far more accurately and fly with foragers.
