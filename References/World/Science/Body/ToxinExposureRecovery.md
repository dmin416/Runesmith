# Toxin Exposure: Complete Recovery Cycle (Average Adult Man)

> Earth reference / invent loot. Not locked law. Poison Resistance / Recovery: `../../../Progression/SkillsDesign.md`, `../../../Progression/Attributes.md`. Diet→toxin sequestration: `../../Fauna/ToxinSequestration.md`. Antivenom manufacture: `../Medicine/Antivenom.md`. Acid burns (corrosive, not toxin status): `BurnsHealing.md`. Terra **Poison Resistance** cuts poison / toxin effect (−10%/level). **Recovery** speeds organ and soft-tissue knit after the dose. Clearance of the toxin and healing of the damage are separate clocks; resistance shrinks the hit, Recovery does not clear bone-stored metals or replace dead neurons.

---

## 1. How the Body Handles Any Toxin

Every poison passes through four stages, known as ADME.

| Stage | Process | Speed |
|---|---|---|
| **Absorption** | Enters blood through lungs, gut, skin, injection or bite | Inhaled and injected: seconds to minutes. Swallowed: 15 min to hours. Skin: minutes to hours |
| **Distribution** | Spreads to organs; some toxins concentrate in fat, bone or specific tissues | Minutes to hours |
| **Metabolism** | Mainly liver. Phase I (oxidation by cytochrome P450 enzymes) then Phase II (attaching molecules to make it water-soluble). Some toxins become more dangerous after Phase I | Hours to days |
| **Elimination** | Kidneys (urine), liver (bile and stool), lungs (breath), sweat | Defined by half-life |

### Half-life rule
The half-life is the time for blood levels to drop by half.

| Half-lives passed | Amount remaining |
|---|---|
| 1 | 50% |
| 2 | 25% |
| 3 | 12.5% |
| 4 | 6.25% |
| 5 | About 3% (functionally cleared) |

**Clearance of the toxin and healing of the damage are separate clocks.** A toxin can leave the body in hours while the damage it caused takes weeks, months or forever to heal.

## 2. Organ Recovery Rates (Applies Across Toxins)

| Organ | Regeneration capacity | Recovery time after moderate injury |
|---|---|---|
| **Liver** | Very high; can regrow from about 30% of its mass | Enzymes normalize in 1-4 weeks; mass largely restored in 4-8 weeks |
| **Kidney** | Moderate; tubule cells regenerate; lost filtering units (nephrons) do not | Acute kidney injury often recovers in 1-3 weeks; some cases leave permanent reduced function |
| **Lung lining** | Moderate | Airway and alveolar lining regrows in 2-6 weeks; fibrosis (scarring) is permanent |
| **Bone marrow** | High | White cells recover in 1-3 weeks; red cells in 1-2 months |
| **Gut lining** | Very high; replaced every 3-5 days | Days to 2 weeks |
| **Skin** | High | 1-4 weeks for superficial damage |
| **Peripheral nerves** | Moderate; regrow about 1 mm/day | Months to years |
| **Brain and spinal cord neurons** | Very low | Dead neurons not replaced; partial function recovered by rewiring over months to 2 years |
| **Heart muscle** | Very low | Dead heart muscle replaced by scar |

## 3. Alcohol (Ethanol)

| Aspect | Value |
|---|---|
| Absorption | Peak blood level 30-90 minutes after last drink |
| Metabolism | Liver; alcohol → acetaldehyde (toxic) → acetate |
| Elimination rate | About 0.015% BAC per hour (roughly one standard drink per hour); fixed rate regardless of amount |
| Hangover | Begins as BAC approaches zero; lasts up to 24 hours. Caused by acetaldehyde, dehydration, inflammation, poor sleep and low blood sugar |
| Sleep | Suppresses REM in first half of night; rebound awakenings second half |

**Chronic heavy use recovery after stopping:**
| Damage | Recovery |
|---|---|
| Fatty liver | Reverses in 4-6 weeks |
| Alcoholic hepatitis | Months; may not fully reverse |
| Cirrhosis | Does not reverse; progression slows |
| Brain volume | Partial recovery over months to a year |
| Peripheral nerve damage | Partial over months to years |
| Withdrawal | Symptoms begin 6-24 h; seizure risk 12-48 h; delirium tremens 48-96 h; acute withdrawal resolves in 5-7 days |

## 4. Carbon Monoxide

| Aspect | Value |
|---|---|
| Mechanism | Binds hemoglobin about 200-250 times more strongly than oxygen; blocks oxygen delivery; also poisons cell energy production |
| Half-life in room air | 4-6 hours |
| Half-life on 100% oxygen | 60-90 minutes |
| Half-life in hyperbaric oxygen | 20-30 minutes |

| Blood CO level | Effects |
|---|---|
| Under 10% | Smokers' baseline range; minimal effect |
| 10-20% | Headache, nausea |
| 20-30% | Throbbing headache, dizziness, weakness, poor judgment |
| 30-40% | Confusion, vomiting, collapse |
| 40-60% | Seizures, coma |
| Over 60% | Usually fatal |

**Delayed neurological sequelae:** after apparent recovery, memory loss, personality changes, movement disorders or difficulty concentrating appear 2-40 days later in roughly 10-30% of significant poisonings. Many cases improve over a year; some are permanent.

## 5. Acetaminophen (Paracetamol) Overdose

Common example of a delayed-onset organ toxin.

| Stage | Time | Signs |
|---|---|---|
| I | 0-24 h | Mild nausea or no symptoms; appears fine |
| II | 24-72 h | Liver damage begins; right upper abdominal pain; liver enzymes rise |
| III | 72-96 h | Peak liver injury; jaundice, bleeding, confusion; possible liver failure and death |
| IV | 4 days to 2 weeks | Survivors recover; liver regenerates with no lasting damage in most cases |

**Antidote:** N-acetylcysteine is nearly fully protective when started within 8 hours of ingestion.

## 6. Snake Venom (Pit Vipers: Rattlesnakes)

| Time | Events |
|---|---|
| 0-30 min | Burning pain, swelling at bite |
| 1-6 h | Swelling spreads; bruising; blood clotting abnormalities develop |
| 6-36 h | Peak swelling; blisters (often blood-filled); possible low platelets and bleeding |
| Days 2-7 | Swelling slowly resolves; tissue death at bite may become apparent |
| Weeks 1-2 | Recurrent clotting problems can appear after antivenom wears off |
| Weeks 2-6 | Local tissue heals |
| Months | Stiffness, reduced range of motion or tissue loss in severe cases |

**Neurotoxic venoms** (coral snakes, many elapids): cause paralysis over hours; recovery depends on venom type and can take days on a ventilator.

## 7. Nerve Agents and Organophosphate Pesticides

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Mechanism | Block acetylcholinesterase, the enzyme that switches off nerve signals; nerves and muscles stay overstimulated |
| Acute signs | Pinpoint pupils, drooling, sweating, tearing, vomiting, diarrhea, muscle twitching, then weakness and breathing failure |
| Enzyme recovery | Once "aged" (permanently bound), recovery requires the body to make new enzyme. Red blood cell enzyme recovers at about 1% per day (roughly 3 months for full return). Plasma enzyme recovers faster (weeks) |
| Intermediate syndrome | 24-96 h after exposure; weakness of neck, limb and breathing muscles; can last 1-3 weeks |
| Delayed neuropathy | Some organophosphates cause nerve damage appearing 1-3 weeks later; weakness and numbness in legs; recovery over months, often incomplete |
| Long-term | Memory, concentration, mood and sleep problems reported in survivors for months to years |

## 8. World War I Era Chemical Agents

### Chlorine
- Reacts with moisture in airways to form acids.
- Immediate eye, nose and throat burning; coughing; chest tightness.
- Severe exposure: fluid in lungs (pulmonary edema) within hours.
- Survivors: airway healing over 2-4 weeks; some develop chronic bronchitis or asthma-like symptoms.

### Phosgene
- Less irritating at first; may produce only mild cough and a smell of cut hay.
- **Delayed pulmonary edema:** 2-24 hours later (up to 48 h), lungs fill with fluid. Exertion during this window worsens outcome.
- Survivors of the critical first 48 hours usually recover over 1-3 weeks; some have lasting reduced lung capacity and chronic bronchitis.

### Sulfur mustard (mustard gas)
| Time | Events |
|---|---|
| Exposure | Little or no immediate pain; garlic or mustard odor |
| 2-24 h | Eye irritation, redness of skin, sore throat, hoarseness |
| 12-48 h | Large yellow blisters on skin, especially warm moist areas (armpits, groin); eye swelling and temporary blindness |
| Days 2-7 | Airway lining sloughs; high risk of pneumonia; bone marrow suppression after heavy exposure |
| Weeks 2-8 | Skin lesions heal; mustard blisters heal slower than equivalent thermal burns; temporary blindness usually resolves in 1-2 weeks |
| Months | Skin color changes; healing of deep lesions |
| Years | Chronic bronchitis, airway narrowing, recurrent eye inflammation, delayed corneal damage (sometimes 10-40 years later), increased cancer risk |

Most WWI mustard casualties survived. Many were disabled for weeks and some for life.

## 9. Botulinum Toxin (Botulism)

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Mechanism | Blocks release of acetylcholine at nerve endings; muscles cannot be activated |
| Onset | 12-36 hours after eating contaminated food (range 6 h to 10 days) |
| Pattern | Descending paralysis: eyes, face, throat, then arms, breathing muscles, legs |
| Recovery mechanism | Blocked nerve endings do not recover. Nerves must grow new terminals (sprouting) |
| Breathing support | Weeks to months on a ventilator in severe cases |
| Full recovery | Months; fatigue and shortness of breath can persist a year or more |

## 10. Heavy Metals

| Metal | Where it goes | Half-life | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lead** | Blood, soft tissue, bone | Blood about 30 days; bone 10-30 years | Bone acts as reservoir and leaks lead back for years; nerve and brain effects partly permanent |
| **Mercury (methylmercury)** | Brain, kidneys | About 50-70 days | Brain damage often permanent |
| **Arsenic** | Skin, hair, nails | Blood: hours to days | Nerve damage recovers over months to years; skin changes persist |
| **Cadmium** | Kidney, liver | 10-30 years | Kidney damage largely permanent |

## 11. Heavy Plant and Mushroom Toxins

### Amanita mushrooms (death cap)
| Time | Events |
|---|---|
| 0-6 h | No symptoms |
| 6-24 h | Severe vomiting, watery diarrhea, cramps |
| 24-72 h | False recovery; feels better while liver damage progresses |
| 3-7 days | Liver and kidney failure; death in severe cases |
| Weeks | Survivors' livers regenerate; some require transplant |

## 12. General Recovery Pattern After Significant Poisoning

| Time | Body state |
|---|---|
| Hours | Toxin peak; acute symptoms |
| 1-3 days | Organ damage reaches peak (liver, kidney, lung) |
| 3-14 days | Organ repair begins; fast-renewing tissues (gut, blood, skin) recover |
| 2-8 weeks | Liver and kidney function largely restored if damage was not severe; fatigue common |
| Months | Peripheral nerve regrowth; muscle strength rebuilding |
| 1-2 years | Maximum brain and nerve recovery reached |
| Permanent | Lung scarring, lost kidney filtering units, dead neurons, cirrhosis, heart scar, bone-stored metals |

## 13. What Recovers and What Does Not

| Fully recovers | Partially recovers | Does not recover |
|---|---|---|
| Gut lining | Kidney function after severe injury | Dead brain and spinal neurons |
| Bone marrow (after moderate damage) | Peripheral nerve function | Lung fibrosis |
| Liver (short of cirrhosis) | Lung capacity after chemical injury | Cirrhosis |
| Skin lesions (most) | Memory and cognition after CO or nerve agents | Lost nephrons |
| Muscle after paralysis (via nerve sprouting) | Vision after corneal injury | Heart muscle death |
| Blood oxygen capacity after CO | | Cumulative bone lead and cadmium |

## 14. Factors That Speed or Slow Recovery

**Faster:** early decontamination (removing clothing removes most skin-surface chemicals), rapid antidote, oxygen, intact liver and kidney function, hydration, nutrition, sleep, younger age.

**Slower:** delayed treatment, preexisting liver or kidney disease, alcohol use, multiple toxins combined, older age, repeated exposure, malnutrition.
