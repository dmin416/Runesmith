Terra **Recovery** multiplies **speed** of existing repair / regen programs (−10% time per level → ~10× at L9). It does **not** unlock blastema, scarless capacity or neuron replacement. Missing limbs, spinal cord, heart muscle death and critical-size defects still need magic, surgery or capacity-tier skills when those exist.

## 1. Two Kinds of Healing

The body has two different ways of fixing damage. The difference explains every limit below.

| | Repair | Regeneration |
|---|---|---|
| **Result** | Scar tissue fills the gap | Original tissue rebuilt with original structure |
| **Speed** | Fast | Slow |
| **Function** | Patch; weaker; no specialized parts | Full function |
| **Default in adult humans** | Most tissues | Liver, bone, blood, gut lining, skin surface, cornea surface |

Evolution favored fast scarring in mammals because a fast patch stops bleeding and infection. Slow regeneration risks death before completion.

## 2. Recovery Limits by Tissue

| Tissue | Loss that fully regenerates | Point where it fails | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blood** | Up to ~40% of volume with care; ~30% without | Over 40% lost quickly without transfusion | Shock kills faster than replacement (red cells take 4-8 weeks) |
| **Liver** | Up to ~70-75% removed; regrows to full mass in about 4-8 weeks | Under ~20-25% remaining | Remnant cannot support the body while regrowing |
| **Bone** | Fractures and small gaps; heals with no scar | Gaps over ~2-2.5 cm in long bones or loss of half the circumference (critical-size defect) | Healing cells cannot bridge the distance; no scaffold |
| **Skin surface** | Any depth that leaves hair follicles and sweat glands alive | Full-thickness loss: only creeps in from edges (~1 mm/day); wounds over a few centimeters close mainly by contraction and scar | Regrowth cells live in deep follicles and glands |
| **Skin (whole body)** | | Full-thickness burns over roughly 70-80% of body surface often fatal even with modern care | Infection and metabolic collapse before coverage |
| **Skeletal muscle** | Small injuries regenerate from satellite cells | Losing more than about 20% of a muscle's volume (volumetric muscle loss) | Stem cells and scaffold lost; scar fills the gap; permanent weakness |
| **Peripheral nerve** | Crush injuries regrow at ~1 mm/day | Gaps over a few millimeters need grafts; gaps over ~3 cm recover poorly; muscles disconnected over 12-18 months cannot reconnect | Regrowing fibers need a guide tube and a waiting target |
| **Spinal cord** | Essentially none | Any full cut is permanent | Scar and growth-blocking signals; neurons do not regrow long fibers |
| **Brain** | Neurons not replaced; function shifts to surviving tissue | Large losses survivable (hemispherectomy in children) but lost memories and skills are gone | Neurons do not divide; information stored in them is destroyed |
| **Heart muscle** | About 1% of heart cells renewed per year at 25; under 0.5% per year by 75 | Losing ~40% of the left ventricle causes cardiogenic shock | Heart cells almost never divide; scar replaces them |
| **Kidney** | Tubule lining regenerates after acute injury | Lost filtering units (nephrons) never return; one kidney is enough; under ~15% total function means dialysis | Nephrons are not made after birth; survivors enlarge to compensate |
| **Lung** | Lining regrows in weeks | Fibrosis permanent; can live with one lung | Alveolar architecture lost to scar |
| **Intestine** | Lining replaced every 3-5 days; remaining gut adapts and lengthens absorption | Under ~100-200 cm of small intestine remaining (normal ~600 cm) | Not enough surface to absorb food |
| **Cartilage** | Almost none | Any significant damage | No blood supply; cells barely divide |
| **Teeth** | Enamel never | Any enamel loss | Enamel-forming cells die when the tooth erupts |
| **Inner ear hair cells** | None | Any loss is permanent | Mammals lost the ability; birds still have it |
| **Eye** | Cornea surface regrows in days | Cornea inner layer, retina and lens do not regenerate | Non-dividing cells |
| **Fingertip** | Can regrow if cut beyond the nail bed (well documented in children; occurs in some adults) | Cut behind the nail bed: no regrowth | Nail bed stem cells drive the only true limb-tip regeneration in humans |
| **Limbs** | None | Any amputation | Adult humans cannot form a blastema |

## 3. Maximum Survivable Loss of the Whole Body

The body survives massive structural loss as long as the core systems stay running.

- **Removable entirely:** limbs (all four), stomach, spleen, gallbladder, colon, one lung, one kidney, most of the liver, most of the small intestine, eyes, reproductive organs.
- **Hemicorporectomy:** surgical removal of everything below the lower spine (legs, pelvis, lower bowel and bladder). Patients have survived for decades. This is roughly 40-50% of body mass.
- **Cannot lose:** brainstem, heart function, enough lung, enough liver and either kidney function or dialysis.

**Time limits that end survival regardless of healing:**
| Event | Time to irreversible damage |
|---|---|
| Brain without oxygen | About 4-6 minutes |
| Severed major artery | Minutes |
| Heart stopped | About 4-6 minutes without CPR |
| Limb without blood flow | About 4-6 hours |

## 4. Why Healing Fails: The Five Barriers

1. **Cells that do not divide:** neurons, heart cells, inner ear hair cells, nephrons, enamel cells.
2. **Lost scaffold:** cells need a framework (the extracellular matrix) to rebuild on. Large defects remove it.
3. **Scar wins the race:** scar forms in days and blocks slower regeneration permanently.
4. **Lost positional information:** limb and organ shape require developmental signals that adults switch off.
5. **Oxygen diffusion limit:** cells farther than about 0.1-0.2 mm from a capillary die. New tissue can grow only as fast as blood vessels grow into it.

## 5. Multiplying the Body's Healing

Two different things can be multiplied. The results differ sharply.

### A. Multiplying speed only (same programs, faster)

Speed alone does not unlock anything new. Scar still forms where scar formed before. A missing limb still stays missing.

| Multiplier | Effect |
|---|---|
| **×2** | Fractures in 3-10 weeks; cuts heal in half the time; nothing new becomes possible |
| **×5** | Simple fractures in 1-3 weeks; burns close before infection sets in, so survival of large burns rises sharply; concussion recovery in days |
| **×10** | Deep cuts seal in hours; femur heals in about 2 weeks; blood volume replaced in days; liver regrows in under a week; nerves regrow ~1 cm per day so a full-arm nerve injury recovers in about 2 months before muscle receptors are lost |
| **×100** | Cuts close in minutes; fractures knit in a day; still scars; still no limb, heart cell or neuron regrowth |

**What speed changes most:** survival. Many deaths come from infection, blood loss and organ failure racing against repair. Faster repair wins those races. Nerve regeneration also benefits because it is a race against muscle receptor decay.

**What speed cannot fix:** critical-size defects, spinal cord, brain, heart muscle, cartilage, teeth, inner ear, limbs. These fail because the program does not exist, not because it runs slowly.

### B. Multiplying capacity (unlocking regeneration programs)

Each tier exists somewhere in nature, so each is biologically plausible.

| Tier | What becomes healable | Real-world precedent |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Scarless healing** | Skin regrows with hair, sweat glands and full strength; large muscle loss regrows; tendons and ligaments reach 100% strength; burns leave no scars | Human fetuses before about 24 weeks of gestation heal skin without scars |
| **2. Restarting dividing in "permanent" cells** | Heart muscle after heart attack; cartilage; kidney nephrons; inner ear (deafness reversed); retina; teeth replaced | Zebrafish regrow ~20% of the heart ventricle in about 60 days; birds regrow ear hair cells; sharks and alligators replace teeth continuously |
| **3. Blastema formation** | Fingers, hands, full limbs, jaw, spinal cord, eye lens | Axolotls regrow full limbs, spinal cord and parts of heart and brain; newts regrow lenses; zebrafish regrow spinal cord in about 8 weeks; deer regrow antlers yearly (up to ~2 cm of bone per day) |
| **4. Whole-body regeneration** | Regrowing large body sections; organs from small fragments | Planarian flatworms regrow a full body from a fragment 1/279th of the original |

### Combined: speed ×10 plus Tier 3 capacity
- Severed finger regrows in about 1-2 weeks.
- Severed hand regrows in roughly 3-6 weeks.
- Severed leg regrows in roughly 2-3 months.
- Spinal cord cuts reconnect; paralysis reverses.
- Heart attack damage fully replaced in about a week.

## 6. Hard Limits Even With Unlimited Healing

### Information loss
Regrown brain tissue is new, blank tissue. Memories, skills and personality stored in destroyed neurons do not come back. Regeneration can rebuild a brain region without restoring the person who used it.

### Time to death
Healing cannot outrun events that kill in minutes: brain oxygen loss (4-6 minutes), decapitation, destruction of the brainstem, heart destruction, massive arterial bleeding faster than clotting.

### Matter and energy
New tissue has to be built from food.
- A leg is about 16% of body mass: roughly 13 kg for an 80 kg man.
- Building lean tissue costs roughly 2,000-3,000 kcal per kg.
- Regrowing a leg costs roughly 30,000-40,000 kcal plus large amounts of protein, calcium and phosphorus.
- At normal eating that is weeks of surplus food. Without it the body cannibalizes its own muscle and fat.

### Heat
Metabolism produces heat. The body at rest produces about 100 watts; elite athletes sustain about 1,000-1,500 watts briefly before overheating.
- Regrowing a leg in 3 hours would require burning about 10,000 kcal per hour, about **11,600 watts**. The body would cook itself.
- At a sustainable ceiling of roughly 600-1,000 watts, the fastest physically possible leg regrowth is about **2 days**, assuming unlimited food and perfect efficiency.
- Practical biological limit: rapid regeneration is capped by heat dissipation, not by cell speed.

### Cancer
Regeneration and cancer use the same growth pathways. More cell division means more chances for mutation. Animals that regenerate well (axolotls, naked mole rats for longevity) rely on strong tumor-suppression systems. Multiplied healing without multiplied tumor suppression produces tumors.

### Aging
Every cell division shortens telomeres and accumulates mutations. Constant high-speed healing would accelerate cellular aging unless telomere maintenance and DNA repair scale with it.

## 7. Summary

| Ability | Survives/heals | Fails at |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal human** | Liver loss to 70%, bone gaps under ~2 cm, blood loss to ~30-40%, skin with surviving follicles | Neurons, heart cells, cartilage, nephrons, limbs, spinal cord, critical-size defects |
| **Speed multiplied** | Same list, faster; much higher survival from infection, bleeding and burns | Same failures as normal |
| **Capacity multiplied** | Scarless skin and muscle, heart, ear, teeth, cartilage, limbs, spinal cord | Brain information, instant-death injuries, energy and heat budget, cancer |

## Terra readout (soft)

| Skill | Effect on this baseline |
|---|---|
| **Recovery** | Speed only (−10% time/level; ~10× at L9). Same programs; scar still scar; no limbs |
| **Rapid Recovery** | Continues the speed ladder toward floor 1% close time; still not capacity Tier 1–4 |
| **Vitality / Endurance M** | Base blood / soft-tissue rates; Recovery multiplies them |
| **Holy / potions / surgery** | Can stand in for capacity where the body has no program |

Ch 19 keeps damage shallow so Recovery cycles living tissue. Amputations, critical-size bone gaps and spinal cuts stay outside Recovery until a capacity skill or magic is on the sheet. Heat and food budgets still cap how fast large mass can be rebuilt even then.
