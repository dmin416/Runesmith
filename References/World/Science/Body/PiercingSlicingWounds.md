# Piercing and Slicing Wounds: Complete Healing Cycle (Average Adult Man)

> Earth reference / invent loot. Not locked law. Sharp Resistance / Recovery: `../../../Progression/SkillsDesign.md`, `../../../Progression/Attributes.md`. Blood restore **M**: `Attributes.md`. Blunt counterpart: `BluntTraumaHealing.md`. Ned poke / bleed grind: `../../../../Story/Notes/Capabilities/EstateWoundGrind.md`. Fatigue during repair: `FatigueRestSleep.md`.

Terra **Sharp Resistance** cuts harm taken (−10%/level). **Recovery** cuts soft-tissue and blood close time (−10%/level, stacks with Vit/End **M**). This file is the Earth baseline those skills modify. Does not regrow limbs or restore nerves past the reinnervation window.

---

## 1. Wound Types

| Type | Cause | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Incision (slice)** | Sharp edge drawn across skin | Clean straight edges; long relative to depth; bleeds freely |
| **Laceration** | Tearing (blunt or irregular edge) | Ragged edges; tissue bridges; more contamination |
| **Puncture** | Narrow point (nail, needle, tooth) | Small entry; deep; hard to clean; high infection risk |
| **Stab** | Blade driven in | Deeper than wide; organ and vessel risk far exceeds visible damage |
| **Gunshot** | Projectile | Permanent cavity plus temporary cavity stretch damage; often contaminated with clothing |
| **Avulsion** | Tissue torn away | Missing skin or flesh; heals by granulation or graft |

## 2. Blood Loss

Average adult man: about 70 mL of blood per kg. An 80 kg man holds about 5.5 L.

| Class | Volume lost | Approximate amount | Signs |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Under 15% | Under 750 mL | Minimal; slight increase in heart rate |
| II | 15-30% | 750-1,500 mL | Heart rate over 100; anxiety; narrowed pulse pressure |
| III | 30-40% | 1,500-2,000 mL | Heart rate over 120; blood pressure drops; confusion |
| IV | Over 40% | Over 2,000 mL | Heart rate over 140; profound shock; death without rapid intervention |

**Bleeding speed by vessel:**
- Capillary: oozes; stops on its own in minutes.
- Vein: steady dark flow; usually controlled with pressure.
- Artery: bright pulsing spurts. A severed femoral artery can cause death in about 3-5 minutes. A severed carotid or major thoracic vessel can cause unconsciousness in under a minute.

### Blood replacement after loss
| Component | Recovery time |
|---|---|
| Plasma volume | 24-48 hours |
| Platelets | About 1 week |
| White cells | Days |
| Red cells (after ~500 mL) | 4-8 weeks |
| Red cells (after large losses) | 2-3 months |
| Iron stores | 8 weeks to several months |

Red cell production can increase several-fold under erythropoietin drive but needs iron, B12 and folate to do so. Terra stack: base × Vit/End **M** × Recovery_rate (`Attributes.md`).

## 3. The Healing Sequence for a Skin Wound

| Phase | Timing | Process |
|---|---|---|
| **Hemostasis** | Seconds to ~10 min | Vessels constrict; platelets plug; fibrin clot. Minor cuts usually stop bleeding in 3-10 minutes |
| **Inflammation** | 0-5 days | Neutrophils within hours; macrophages from day 2-3; wound edges red, warm, swollen, tender |
| **Epithelial seal** | 24-48 hours (closed wound) | Skin cells migrate across a sutured or tightly closed cut and seal the surface |
| **Proliferation** | Day 3 to week 3 | Granulation tissue (red, bumpy, bleeds easily) fills gaps; collagen laid down; new capillaries |
| **Contraction** | Week 1-3+ (open wounds) | Myofibroblasts pull edges inward at roughly 0.6-0.75 mm per day |
| **Remodeling** | Week 3 to 12-18+ months | Scar shifts from red and raised to pale and flat; collagen reorganizes |

### Scar strength compared to unwounded skin

| Time | Tensile strength |
|---|---|
| 1 week | 3-5% |
| 3 weeks | About 20% |
| 3 months | About 50-60% |
| 1 year and beyond | Maximum 70-80% |

**Scar tissue never reaches 100% of original strength.** It also lacks hair follicles, sweat glands and oil glands.

## 4. Primary vs Secondary Healing

| | Primary intention | Secondary intention |
|---|---|---|
| **Method** | Edges brought together (stitches, staples, glue, tape) | Left open; fills from the bottom up |
| **Speed** | Surface sealed in 1-2 days; functional closure 1-3 weeks | Weeks to months depending on size |
| **Scar** | Thin line | Wide, contracted scar |
| **Used for** | Clean cuts closed within about 6-12 hours (up to 24 h on the face) | Contaminated wounds, bites, punctures, late wounds, avulsions |

**Delayed primary closure:** a contaminated wound is left open 3-5 days to clear infection risk, then closed.

## 6. Healing Time by Depth

| Depth | Example | Time to close | Scar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Superficial (epidermis) | Paper cut, scrape | 3-7 days | None |
| Partial dermis | Shallow knife slip | 1-2 weeks | Faint |
| Full dermis | Gaping cut | 2-3 weeks with closure | Visible line |
| Into fat | Deep slice | 3-4 weeks with closure | Visible; may depress |
| Into muscle | Stab, deep laceration | 4-8 weeks; strength months | Internal scar; some weakness |

## 7. Damage to Deeper Structures

### Muscle
- Cut muscle heals mainly by scar tissue bridging the gap rather than full regeneration.
- Repaired muscle regains roughly 50-80% of original strength depending on size of the cut.
- Function returns over 2-6 months with rehabilitation.

### Tendon
| Time | State |
|---|---|
| Weeks 0-3 | Weakest point; repair held mainly by stitches; rupture risk high |
| Weeks 3-6 | Collagen bridges form; protected movement |
| Weeks 6-12 | Light loading |
| 12 weeks | Return to most activity |
| 6-12 months | Mature; still weaker than original |

Adhesions (tendon sticking to surrounding tissue) are a common cause of permanent stiffness, especially in hand flexor tendons.

### Nerve
- Cut nerve fibers below the injury die back (Wallerian degeneration) within days.
- Regrowth rate: about **1 mm per day** (roughly 1 inch per month).
- A cut at the wrist needs 4-6 months to reach the fingertips. A cut high in the arm can need 1-2 years.
- **Hard limit:** muscles disconnected for longer than about 12-18 months lose their nerve receptor sites. Nerves arriving after that point cannot restore movement.
- Sensation often returns incompletely. Mis-wiring causes odd sensations (tingling, sensitivity, wrong location).

### Blood vessels
- Small vessels regrow freely.
- Major arteries must be surgically repaired. Limb tissue tolerates loss of blood flow for about 4-6 hours before muscle death becomes irreversible.

### Bone
- A blade or projectile that chips or breaks bone follows the fracture healing schedule (6-20 weeks depending on bone). See `BluntTraumaHealing.md` §6.

## 8. Penetrating Injury to Body Cavities

| Region | Main threats | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| **Chest** | Collapsed lung (pneumothorax), blood in chest (hemothorax), heart puncture (tamponade) | Chest tube 2-5 days; lung reexpands; return to normal activity 4-6 weeks |
| **Abdomen** | Bowel perforation, liver/spleen bleeding | Perforation leaks bacteria; peritonitis develops within hours. Post-surgery hospital stay 5-10 days; recovery 6-8 weeks |
| **Neck** | Airway, carotid, jugular, spinal cord | Highly variable |
| **Head** | Brain tissue destruction, bleeding, infection | Dead neurons not replaced; rehabilitation months to years |

**Hidden injury:** a stab wound's skin opening says little about internal depth. A 2 cm opening can hide a 15 cm tract.

### Gunshot specifics
- **Permanent cavity:** tissue directly destroyed by the projectile.
- **Temporary cavity:** tissue stretched outward for milliseconds. Elastic tissues (muscle, lung, bowel) tolerate this well. Inelastic organs (liver, spleen, kidney, brain) can split.
- **Fragments and yaw** multiply the damage path.
- Retained bullets are often left in place if not near critical structures.
- Recovery is driven by which structures were hit, not by the wound size.

## 9. Infection Timeline

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| 0-6 hours | "Golden period": bacteria present but not yet established |
| 24-72 hours | Signs of infection usually appear: spreading redness, increasing pain, heat, pus, red streaks (lymphangitis), fever |
| 3-21 days (avg ~7-10) | Tetanus incubation period. Highest risk with deep punctures, dirty wounds and dead tissue |
| Days | Abscess formation if pus collects |

**Highest infection risk:** punctures, bites (especially human and cat), wounds with soil or clothing fibers, wounds over 12 hours old, hands and feet.

**Infection effect on healing:** stalls the wound in the inflammatory phase. Can double or triple healing time and produce wider scars.

## 10. Scar Maturation

| Time | Appearance |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1-4 | Pink to red; flat or slightly raised |
| Months 1-3 | Reddest, thickest, most itchy |
| Months 3-6 | Begins to soften and pale |
| Months 6-18 | Matures to pale, flat, soft |

**Hypertrophic scar:** raised but stays within wound borders; often improves over 1-2 years.
**Keloid:** grows beyond wound borders; does not regress; tends to recur after removal.

## 11. What Recovers Fully and What Does Not

| Fully recovers | Partially recovers | Does not recover |
|---|---|---|
| Superficial skin (no scar) | Deep skin (70-80% strength scar) | Hair follicles and sweat glands in scar |
| Blood volume and red cells | Muscle strength | Nerves not reconnected within ~12-18 months |
| Small vessels | Tendon strength and glide | Brain tissue destroyed |
| Bone | Nerve sensation and precision | Muscle dead from prolonged loss of blood flow |
| Liver tissue | Grip and fine motor after hand injury | Original skin texture and color in scar |

## 12. Factors That Speed or Slow Healing

**Faster:** early cleaning and closure, good blood flow, adequate protein, vitamin C, zinc, sleep, nonsmoker, moist wound environment, keeping tension off the wound.

**Slower:** infection, contamination, smoking (constricts vessels), diabetes, poor nutrition, steroids, movement across the wound, dead tissue left in place, older age, location on lower legs or feet.
