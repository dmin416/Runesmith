## 1. Burn Types

| Type | Source | Special features |
|---|---|---|
| **Thermal (flame)** | Fire, explosion | Often deep; clothing ignition increases depth |
| **Scald** | Hot liquid or steam | Often partial thickness; steam can burn the airway |
| **Contact** | Hot metal, surface | Deep but small; sharply bordered |
| **Flash** | Brief intense heat (explosion, arc) | Usually superficial over large exposed areas |
| **Chemical** | Acid or alkali | Continues burning until removed; alkalis penetrate deeper than acids |
| **Electrical** | Current through body | Small skin wounds hide massive internal muscle damage; heart rhythm risk |
| **Radiation** | Sun, radioactive sources | Delayed onset; affects dividing cells |

**Tissue damage threshold:** skin is damaged at 44°C (111°F) over about 6 hours. At 60°C (140°F), full-thickness damage can occur in about 3-5 seconds. At 70°C (158°F), under 1 second.

## 2. Burn Depth

| Depth | Old term | Layers | Appearance | Pain | Heals in | Scar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Superficial** | 1st degree | Epidermis | Red, dry, no blisters, blanches | Painful | 3-7 days | None |
| **Superficial partial thickness** | 2nd degree | Epidermis + upper dermis | Blisters, moist, pink, blanches | Very painful | 1-3 weeks | Minimal; color changes |
| **Deep partial thickness** | 2nd degree (deep) | Into deep dermis | Blisters, white or mottled, does not blanch well | Pressure only | 3-8 weeks; often grafted | Significant |
| **Full thickness** | 3rd degree | Entire dermis | White, leathery, brown or black, dry | Painless (nerves destroyed) | Cannot heal on its own except edges; graft needed | Severe |
| **4th degree** | | Muscle, tendon, bone | Charred | None | Graft, flap or amputation | Severe; loss of function |

**Why depth matters:** skin regenerates from cells lining hair follicles and sweat glands deep in the dermis. Burns that leave these structures alive regrow skin. Burns that destroy them cannot regrow skin except slowly from the edges (about 1 mm per day).

**Rule of thumb:** a burn not healed within 3 weeks is likely deep enough to scar badly and is usually grafted.

## 3. Zones of Injury (Jackson Model)

| Zone | Location | Fate |
|---|---|---|
| **Coagulation** | Center | Dead; irreversible |
| **Stasis** | Middle ring | Reduced blood flow; salvageable for 48-72 hours with good care; dies with dehydration, infection or swelling |
| **Hyperemia** | Outer ring | Increased blood flow; recovers fully |

**Burn conversion:** a burn often deepens over the first 48-72 hours as the zone of stasis dies. Initial depth estimates are unreliable for 2-3 days.

## 4. Size Estimation (Rule of Nines, Adult)

| Region | % of body surface |
|---|---|
| Head and neck | 9% |
| Each arm | 9% |
| Front of torso | 18% |
| Back of torso | 18% |
| Each leg | 18% |
| Groin | 1% |

The victim's palm (including fingers) equals about 1% of body surface.

**Burns over about 20% of body surface** (partial or full thickness) cause whole-body effects: burn shock, hypermetabolism and immune suppression.

## 5. Systemic Timeline

### Phase 1: Burn shock (0-48 hours)
- Capillaries throughout the body leak fluid into tissues, not just at the burn.
- Massive swelling; blood volume drops; blood pressure falls.
- Fluid resuscitation estimate (Parkland formula): 4 mL × body weight in kg × % body surface burned over 24 hours. Half in the first 8 hours. An 80 kg man with a 40% burn needs about 12,800 mL in 24 hours.
- Swelling peaks at 24-48 hours.
- Circumferential deep burns on limbs or chest form a rigid shell (eschar) that can cut off circulation or breathing. Escharotomy (cutting the eschar) relieves this.

### Phase 2: Hypermetabolic and flow phase (day 3 to 1-2+ years)
- Metabolic rate rises up to 150-200% of normal in burns over about 40% body surface.
- Body breaks down muscle for fuel even with aggressive feeding.
- Heart rate, body temperature, stress hormones (cortisol, catecholamines) stay elevated.
- Can persist up to 1-2 years after major burns.
- Lean body mass loss of 10-20% or more is common in large burns.

### Phase 3: Infection risk (day 3 onward)
- Burned skin no longer blocks bacteria; immune function is suppressed.
- Infection and sepsis are the leading cause of death after the first few days.
- Common organisms: Staphylococcus, Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, fungi.

## 6. Local Wound Healing Timeline

| Time | Superficial partial | Deep partial / full thickness |
|---|---|---|
| 0-24 h | Blisters form; pain; redness | Pale, leathery or charred; little pain in full thickness |
| 24-72 h | Blisters enlarge; wound weeps | Conversion; depth becomes clearer |
| Days 3-7 | New skin starts from follicles | Dead tissue begins separating; surgery to remove eschar (excision) typically day 3-7 |
| Days 7-14 | New pink skin covers much of the wound | Grafting after excision |
| Days 14-21 | Healed | Graft taking; donor sites healing |
| Weeks 3-8 | Pink/red, fragile, itchy | Deep partial burns left ungrafted slowly close with heavy scarring |
| Months 1-6 | Color normalizes | Scar thickening (hypertrophic scar) begins and peaks |
| Months 6-24 | Mature | Scar slowly matures; contractures may form |

## 7. Skin Grafts

| Stage | Timing |
|---|---|
| Graft sticks via fibrin | Day 0-1 |
| Graft absorbs nutrients from wound bed (plasmatic imbibition) | Days 1-2 |
| New capillaries connect (inosculation) | Days 2-3 |
| Full blood supply ("graft take") | Days 4-5 |
| Graft stable for gentle movement | Days 5-7 |
| Donor site heals (split thickness) | 10-14 days |
| Graft matures | 6-12 months |

**Split-thickness graft:** thin layer; donor site regrows; can be meshed to cover larger areas but leaves a mesh-patterned scar.
**Full-thickness graft:** better appearance and less contraction; donor site must be closed.

Grafted skin generally lacks sweat glands and hair, has altered sensation and contracts more than normal skin.

## 8. Scarring and Contracture

| Time | Scar state |
|---|---|
| 1-3 months | Scar becomes raised, red, firm, itchy |
| 3-6 months | Peak thickness and redness |
| 6-12 months | Begins softening |
| 12-24 months | Matures: paler, flatter, softer |

**Hypertrophic scarring** occurs in a large share of deep burns, especially those taking more than 3 weeks to heal.

**Contractures:** scars shrink and pull across joints, limiting movement (neck, armpit, elbow, hand, knee). Prevented with splinting, positioning and stretching. Often require surgical release.

**Pressure garments:** worn about 23 hours per day for 12-18 months to flatten scars.

**Itch:** severe itch affects most burn survivors and can persist for years.

## 9. Inhalation Injury

- Caused by hot gas (upper airway), smoke chemicals (lower airway) and carbon monoxide or cyanide (whole body).
- Upper airway swelling can close the airway within hours.
- Lower airway injury develops over 24-72 hours: inflammation, sloughing of airway lining, pneumonia.
- Inhalation injury significantly raises death risk independent of burn size.
- Airway lining regenerates over 2-3 weeks. Some survivors have lasting reduced lung function.

## 10. Electrical Burns

- Current follows nerves, blood vessels and muscle (low resistance).
- Small entry and exit wounds can hide extensive muscle death.
- Muscle breakdown releases myoglobin; kidney failure risk.
- Heart rhythm disturbances: highest risk at the time of injury.
- Cataracts can develop months to years later.
- Nerve damage can appear or progress over months.

## 11. Survival

**Baux score:** age + % body surface burned. Adding 17 points for inhalation injury (revised Baux). A score around 130-140 historically approached 100% mortality. Modern burn centers have pushed survival limits higher.

**Major survival factors:** age, burn size, inhalation injury, speed of fluid resuscitation, speed of excision and grafting, infection control.

## 12. What Recovers and What Does Not

| Fully recovers | Partially recovers | Does not recover |
|---|---|---|
| Superficial burns | Deep partial burns (scarred) | Hair follicles in full-thickness or grafted areas |
| Superficial partial burns (normal skin) | Sensation in grafted skin | Sweat glands in full-thickness or grafted areas |
| Blood volume after shock | Skin pigment (often lighter or darker) | Normal skin elasticity in scar |
| Airway lining | Muscle mass after hypermetabolism (months to years) | Fingers, toes, limbs lost to 4th degree burns |
| | Joint range after contracture release | Normal temperature regulation over large grafted areas |
| | Lung function after inhalation injury | |

**Temperature regulation:** survivors of large burns sweat only from unburned skin. Heat tolerance in exercise and hot climates can be permanently reduced.

## 13. Factors That Speed or Slow Healing

**Faster:** cooling with cool running water for 20 minutes within 3 hours of injury (reduces depth), early excision and grafting, high protein intake (1.5-2.0 g/kg/day; more in major burns), high calorie feeding, moist wound care, infection control, early mobility.

**Slower:** infection, smoking, diabetes, older age, malnutrition, delayed surgery, dehydration, ice application (causes further tissue damage), burns over joints, hands, feet and groin.
