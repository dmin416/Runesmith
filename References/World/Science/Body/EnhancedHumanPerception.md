## Part 1: Sensing Light-Speed Forces

Premise: a human with 15 times normal intelligence, 15 times normal sensitivity and 15 times normal mental perception of time.

Detecting light-speed signals is not the hard part. Humans already do it: eyes detect electromagnetic radiation and skin feels infrared as heat. Anything moving at light speed reaches the body almost instantly. The real limits are transducers and the speed of nerves.

### Magnetic Fields

Humans have no confirmed magnetoreceptor organ. A 2019 study by Caltech and the University of Tokyo found that rotating an Earth-strength magnetic field around subjects caused alpha brain waves to drop by as much as 60 percent in some participants, though the subjects reported feeling nothing. Fifteen times sensitivity amplifies existing senses but does not create new ones. The likely gains are indirect:

- **Magnetophosphenes:** Faint flashes of light in vision occur when strong changing magnetic fields stimulate the retina. Measured thresholds sit around 10 to 20 millitesla at 20 to 100 Hz, levels found near MRI scanners and heavy industrial equipment. A 15x more sensitive retina would lower that threshold to roughly 0.7 to 1.3 millitesla. That is still more than ten times Earth's field (about 50 microtesla) and far above the tiny shifts during geomagnetic storms.
- **Electric fields:** Skin and hair react to static charge, so this person could notice the buildup before lightning, near power lines or around charged objects well before anyone else.
- **Inference:** High intelligence would let them connect these faint cues into reliable conclusions, much like an expert reading subtle signs.

### Time Perception

Human senses separate events about 3 to 5 milliseconds apart for hearing and roughly 20 to 50 milliseconds for vision. At 15x, hearing resolution drops to a fraction of a millisecond. Light crosses a room in about 10 nanoseconds, tens of thousands of times shorter than even that. The change would register as instantaneous with no sense of it traveling. Nerve conduction (up to about 120 m/s) and neuron firing rates would cap perception anyway.

### Summary

This person plausibly senses incoming storms, live wires, strong magnets and electronics humming at the edge of perception. Feeling a light-speed wave sweep past would require an entirely new organ.

## Part 2: What Dowsing, Applied Kinesiology and Muscle Testing Can Detect

Premise: dowsing, applied kinesiology and muscle testing are real. A human can detect things in their surroundings up through energetic and spiritual levels, cellularly or autonomically.

Within that framework, the body acts as a yes/no instrument. The subconscious or autonomic system registers something and signals it through a pendulum swing, a rod crossing or a muscle that holds strong or goes weak.

Locked skill framing and what Terra Gut Check already owns: `ART.md`.

### Core Rule: Material Readout Only

There is no fortune telling. Every detection comes from something physically or energetically present: a substance, a body, an emotion carried by a living being or an imprint left on a place or object. Information that exists only as meaning has no readout.

- **Card example:** The face of a card cannot be read because every card is made of the same paper and ink. A card painted in poison reads differently from a card painted in water because the materials themselves differ.
- **No future:** Events that have not happened leave no material trace, so outcomes, timing and guidance cannot be sensed.
- **No symbols or abstractions:** Words on a page, images on a map, numbers and names carry no signal beyond the ink and paper they are made of.
- **No reading through proxies:** A map, photo or written name is just material. Locating something requires the sensor's range to reach the thing itself.

Within those limits, the following can be detected.

### Physical Environment

- Underground water, its depth and flow direction
- Mineral veins, ore, oil and buried metal
- Lost objects, hidden cavities, tunnels and graves
- Geopathic stress zones such as Hartmann grid lines, Curry lines and ley lines

### The Body at a Cellular Level

- Food sensitivities and substances the body rejects, tested by holding the item
- Nutrient deficiencies and which supplement or remedy fits best
- Toxins, pathogens and stressed organs
- Meridian blockages and imbalanced organ systems
- How the body reacts to a given amount of a substance

### Mind and Emotion

- Truth versus falsehood, since a statement believed false weakens the muscle
- Subconscious beliefs that contradict conscious ones
- Emotions stored in the body from past events, often tied to a specific age
- Suppressed memories and root causes of fears or habits

### Energetic Level

- Chakra states, open, blocked or overactive
- Aura size and integrity
- Imprints left on objects and places by people or events
- Whether food, water or a space carries harmful or beneficial energy

### Spiritual Level

- Presence of spirits or entities in a location
- Attachments, curses and negative intentions directed at a person
- Intentions and honesty of other people
- Compatibility between people or places that are physically present

### Limits Within the Framework

The readout is binary, so results depend entirely on how questions are framed. Strong desire for a particular answer distorts the signal. The tester's own state, such as fatigue, dehydration or emotional stress, also affects accuracy.

### Applied to the 15x Sensitive Person

The signal would arrive as a conscious felt sense rather than through a tool. Water underground might register as a pull, a toxin as a tightening in the gut, a lie as a physical wrongness and an entity as pressure or cold.

## Part 3: Pinpointing People Nearby

Premise: the person can sense everything listed above.

People would be among the easiest things to detect. A living human is a loud source on nearly every channel at once, so presence is simple. Exact position, count and identity get harder with distance and crowding.

### Why People Stand Out

- **Physical:** Body heat felt as infrared on the skin, breathing, heartbeat, scent, air displacement and the body's own electric field. The heart produces the body's strongest electrical and magnetic signals, detectable with sensitive instruments near the chest, so a 15x sensitive person might feel it directly at close range.
- **Energetic:** Each person carries an aura, chakra activity and an emotional state that radiates outward.
- **Mental:** Intentions and focus, especially attention aimed at the sensor, would register strongly. Hostile intent would likely be the sharpest signal of all.

### Difficulty by Situation

- **One person within a few feet:** Trivial. Position, emotional state and likely intent would be clear even with eyes closed.
- **Several people in a room:** Moderate. Presence and rough direction come easily. Exact placement takes a moment of attention, similar to locating sounds in a dark room.
- **Through walls or at a distance:** Physical cues drop out quickly. Energetic and mental signals carry further but turn vague, giving "someone is there, roughly that way, feeling anxious" rather than a precise spot.
- **Crowds:** Hardest. Dozens of overlapping fields blur into noise. Picking one person out works best when that person carries a distinct signal such as fear, aggression or attention focused on the sensor.

### Pinpointing Techniques Within the Framework

- Turning slowly until the pull peaks, like a dowsing rod locking onto water
- Triangulating by shifting position and noting where the signal strengthens
- Asking narrow yes/no questions internally, such as "is someone behind the door," which works only when the person is within sensing range

### Complicating Factors

- People who are calm, emotionally flat or skilled at shielding would read faintly
- Strong emotions in the sensor drown out external signals
- Places with heavy imprints could create false readings of people who are no longer there
- Animals would register as living presences and might be confused with people at range

### Summary

This person would be extremely hard to sneak up on and nearly impossible to ambush with hostile intent. Precise counts in a crowd or exact positions through several walls would remain the weak points.

## Part 4: Detection Speed

Speed depends on the channel. Physical senses are fastest, autonomic body responses are slower and tool-based readouts like a pendulum are slowest.

### Normal Human Baseline

- **Touch and sound:** About 150 milliseconds to react
- **Sight:** About 200 to 250 milliseconds
- **Heat on the skin:** About 0.5 to 1 second, since temperature nerves conduct slowly
- **Smell:** About 0.4 seconds or longer, often only noticed once it builds
- **Conscious awareness of a stimulus:** Research suggests up to 0.5 seconds before the brain fully registers it as experienced
- **Muscle tone shifts:** A few hundred milliseconds, which is the signal muscle testing relies on
- **Autonomic responses:** Skin conductance shifts take 1 to 3 seconds and heart rate changes take a few seconds
- **Dowsing tools:** Several seconds, since the rod or pendulum has to physically move

Studies on gut feelings show the body sometimes reacts before the mind notices. In the Iowa Gambling Task, a card-choice experiment, sweat responses to risky decks appeared well before subjects could say why they avoided them.

### The 15x Person

Reaction time has three parts: sensory transduction, brain processing and nerve plus muscle travel. Processing takes roughly half or more of a normal 200 millisecond visual reaction. Cutting it by 15x leaves the fixed sensory and nerve-muscle delays, giving a reaction around 80 to 120 milliseconds. The bigger change is subjective. A normal 200 millisecond reaction would feel like 3 seconds, so there is room to notice, evaluate and choose a response within what feels like a calm moment.

The felt sense of the framework skips the tool entirely. A toxin, a lie or a hidden presence would register as fast as the body reacts, likely a few hundred milliseconds in real time and stretched over several subjective seconds.

### Factors That Slow Detection

- **Weak signals:** Faint sources take time to cross the threshold of notice
- **Distance:** Signals fade with range and take longer to resolve
- **Attention:** Unfocused awareness misses or delays signals
- **Habituation:** Constant signals, like a steady field in a room, fade from notice within seconds to minutes
- **Gradual change:** Slow shifts are harder to catch than sudden ones

### Factors That Speed Detection

- **Sudden change:** A spike or drop stands out instantly
- **Threat:** Hostile intent and danger trigger the fastest response channels
- **Familiarity:** Practiced sensing of a specific signal, like a known person's presence, registers almost immediately


## Part 5: Fascia and How Fast Information Moves Through It

Fascia carries information through six channels at very different speeds. A full body-length trip ranges from near instant to more than a day.

### 1. Nerves Embedded in Fascia (Main Information Channel)

- **Density:** Estimates put about 100 million nerve endings in dense fascia, rising to about 250 million under a broader definition. Skin has about 200 million. These are calculated estimates, not direct counts.
- **Receptor types:** Most are free nerve endings on slow fibers. Fascial free endings activate at about half the mechanical threshold of skin and muscle endings. Some fascia, such as thoracolumbar and superficial hip fascia, contains only free nerve endings with no Pacini or Ruffini corpuscles.
- **Sympathetic share:** About a third to 40 percent of fascial nerves are sympathetic fibers controlling blood vessels and tissue tone.
- **Speeds:**
  - Proprioceptive fibers (Golgi, Pacini, Ruffini, muscle spindles): 30 to 120 m/s
  - A-delta fibers (sharp pain, pressure): 10 to 30 m/s
  - C fibers (dull pain, temperature, interoception): 0.5 to 2.5 m/s
- **Foot to brain (about 1.5 m):** Roughly 15 to 50 ms for fast fibers, 50 to 150 ms for A-delta and 0.6 to 3 seconds for C fibers.

### 2. Mechanical Tension and Waves (Fastest Physical Channel)

- **Compression waves:** Sound travels through soft tissue at about 1450 m/s in fat and 1580 m/s in muscle.
- **Shear waves:** These travel much slower, around 1 to 10 m/s in soft tissue.
- **Tension:** A pull on a taut sheet registers along its length almost at once, like tugging a tight rope.
- **Body-wide links:** A 2016 cadaver review found strong evidence of continuous connections along three myofascial chains (the superficial back line and the front and back functional lines). It found mixed evidence for the spiral and lateral lines and none for the superficial front line.
- **In living people:** Passive ankle flexion moved hamstring tissue in the thigh by about 5.8 mm on average. In rats, stretching one muscle lowered a neighboring muscle's force by about 7 percent through shared connective tissue.

### 3. Mechanotransduction Inside Cells

- Force applied to a cell surface activated signaling enzymes more than 20 micrometers away in under 0.3 seconds. A chemical growth factor took about 12 seconds, at least 40 times slower.
- Stress reached sites 30 micrometers away in under 5 ms, with an estimated wave speed of about 30 m/s along the cell's internal cables.
- This speed only applies within single cells. It is not a body-wide signal.

### 4. Cell-to-Cell Chemical Signals

- **Calcium waves:** These spread between connected cells at about 1 to 50 micrometers per second. Fibroblasts run about 12 micrometers per second.
- **Range:** Waves usually die out within about 100 to a few hundred micrometers.
- **Distance math:** At 12 micrometers per second, crossing one meter would take over 23 hours, so this channel works locally.
- **Network:** Fibroblasts connect through gap junctions into a cellular network spread through connective tissue.

### 5. Contraction and Remodeling (Slowest Channel)

- Fibroblasts change shape within minutes after tissue is stretched.
- Fascia contains contractile myofibroblasts. Contraction begins within about 30 minutes and peaks around 20 to 40 minutes.
- TGF-beta1 triggers slower stiffening over hours, continuing up to 20 hours in cell cultures.
- Adrenaline raises TGF-beta1 in fibroblasts after about a 24-hour delay. This is one proposed link between stress and stiff fascia.

### 6. Electrical and Energetic Claims

- **Piezoelectricity:** This is established. Collagen produces tiny charges under strain, on the order of picocoulombs. Streaming potentials from fluid movement are likely larger. No study shows these charges carry signals across the body.
- **Electromagnetic fields:** Fields from those charges move at light speed but are too weak to carry measurable information.
- **Proton conduction:** This has been shown in hydrated collagen, but only over nanometers to micrometers.
- **Structured water:** Water layers next to surfaces exist in the lab. Whether they persist in living fascia is not established.
- **The "living matrix" (Oschman):** This describes fascia as a fast semiconductive network that outpaces chemical signals. No propagation speed has been measured.
- **Quantum coherence:** At body temperature it lasts about 10^-13 seconds, far too short for tissue-level signaling.

### Speed Summary

- Mechanical compression: about 1500 m/s, essentially instant at body scale
- Tension wave along dense fascia: about 600 to 800 m/s (calculated)
- Fast proprioceptive nerves: 30 to 120 m/s
- Signals inside cells: under 5 ms across 30 micrometers
- Pain nerves: 0.5 to 30 m/s
- Shear waves: about 1 to 10 m/s
- Calcium waves: micrometers per second, local only
- Contraction and remodeling: minutes to days

### For the Sensing Framework

Fascia makes the whole body one connected tension network. A 15x sensitive person feels a change anywhere in the web almost as soon as it happens. Mechanical shifts arrive essentially instantly, and the dense low-threshold endings report them at nerve speed. That gives a full-body read in tens of milliseconds for pressure and position and up to a few seconds for the slow interoceptive layer. Within the framework's material readout rule, fascia is the most plausible physical substrate for a body-wide felt sense.

### Additional Science on Fascia

#### Tension Wave Speed Along Fascia (Calculated)

Measured stiffness values allow a direct estimate of how fast a pull travels along a taut fascial sheet. Wave speed equals the square root of stiffness divided by density.

- **Stiffness:** Plantar fascia about 404 MPa and fascia lata about 477 MPa in lab samples. Fascia lata measured in living people reaches about 657 MPa. Superficial nasal fascia is far softer at about 1.1 MPa.
- **Density:** Soft tissue sits near 1100 kg/m³.
- **Result:** A tension wave along dense fascia travels roughly 600 to 800 m/s. A pull at the foot reaches the hip in about 1 to 2 milliseconds.
- **Holding force:** Under a sustained load, fascia relaxes its internal stress over roughly 60 seconds.

#### Receptor Response Speeds

- **Pacinian corpuscles:** Rapidly adapting. Tuned to vibration around 200 to 300 Hz. Fire at the start and end of a change, then go quiet.
- **Ruffini endings:** Slowly adapting. Report sustained stretch, deep tension and shear for as long as it lasts.
- **Adaptation timing:** Mechanically activated currents in sensory neurons fade with time constants of about 18 ms for rapidly adapting types, about 56 ms for intermediate types and over 100 ms for slowly adapting types.
- **PIEZO2:** This ion channel converts mechanical force into nerve signals. It is required for light touch and proprioception in mice and humans.
- **PIEZO2 loss in humans:** A 2016 NIH study of two patients missing working PIEZO2 found they could not tell which way a joint was moved without looking and stumbled when blindfolded. They could not separate two nearby points pressed on the palm. Pain, itch and temperature sensing were normal. Both relied heavily on vision to move and balance.

#### Fascial Pain Sensitivity

- **Chemical irritation:** Hypertonic saline injected into the thoracolumbar fascia of 12 volunteers produced more pain than the same injection into the back muscle beneath it. The painful area was far larger and matched typical "lumbago" locations. Only fascia injections drew descriptions such as "agonizing" and "torturing."
- **After hard exercise:** Two days after eccentric elbow exercise, electrical pain thresholds dropped about 91 percent in biceps fascia and about 84 percent in brachialis fascia. Muscle thresholds dropped about 56 percent. This suggests fascia drives much of delayed onset muscle soreness (10 participants).
- **Inflammation:** Inflamed thoracolumbar fascia grows more nerve fibers carrying the pain signaling molecules CGRP and substance P.

#### Gliding Layers and Hyaluronan

- **Fasciacytes:** A cell type described in 2011 and characterized in 2018. They sit in the loose layer between deep fascia and muscle and secrete hyaluronan, a slippery molecule that lets layers glide.
- **Densification:** Hyaluronan can thicken and turn sludgy. Proposed causes include elevated pH, overuse, injury and surgery. Thickened layers may trap free nerve endings and generate pain.
- **Evidence status:** The cell type is documented. Most claims about densification and its reversal come from practitioner reviews and small case series.

#### The Interstitium

- **Finding:** A 2018 study led by NYU researchers described fluid-filled spaces held open by a lattice of collagen and elastin. They sit beneath the skin, along the gut, lungs and urinary tract, around blood vessels and in the fascia between muscles.
- **Why it was missed:** Standard slide preparation drains the fluid and collapses the spaces, making them look like solid tissue.
- **Function:** The spaces drain into the lymphatic system. The researchers proposed calling the network an organ, a label still debated.
- **Flow speed:** Fluid in lymphatic capillaries moves only a few micrometers per second.

#### Cells and Chemical Receivers in Fascia

- **Fibroblasts:** The most numerous cells. They build and remodel collagen.
- **Myofibroblasts:** Contractile cells that stiffen fascia over minutes to hours.
- **Telocytes:** Identified in human fascia lata in 2015 from seven donors. They are sparse cells with long thin extensions, the longest measured about 22 micrometers in fascia. Their role in fascia is unknown.
- **Hormone receptors:** Fascial fibroblasts carry estrogen receptor alpha and relaxin receptor 1. Levels appear lower after menopause (8 patients). This may help explain sex differences in myofascial pain.
- **Cannabinoid receptors:** Fascial fibroblasts carry both CB1 and CB2 receptors, first shown in 2016.
- **Speed implication:** These receptors let fascia respond to hormones and chemicals carried in blood. That channel runs at endocrine speed, from seconds to hours.

#### Connective Tissue Grip

- Rotating an acupuncture needle in one direction raised the force needed to pull it out by 167 percent. Rotating back and forth raised it 53 percent.
- The proposed cause is connective tissue winding around the needle, showing how strongly fascia couples to mechanical input at a single point.
