# Mind-Body Disconnect in Skilled Action

Earth cognitive-science note. **Memory is storage and recall. Drawing (and other craft) is motor execution.** They are separate skills.

## Core idea

Remembering an image and producing an image are separate skills run by different systems.

## Why perfect recall does not produce drawing ability

An eidetic or photographic memory holds a detailed sensory record. Drawing requires converting that record into a sequence of precise hand movements. That conversion is a trained skill. It depends on:

- **Perceptual decomposition:** breaking a scene into edges, angles, proportions, values and negative space. Someone can see everything and never have practiced isolating those components.
- **Visuomotor mapping:** translating "this line curves this way at this angle" into pressure, speed and direction of the hand. This is procedural memory, built through repetition, the same way a pianist trains finger control.
- **Error correction loops:** skilled artists constantly compare the mark against the reference and adjust. This feedback loop is learned.

## Why an artist without perfect recall still draws well

Artists rarely rely on a mental photograph. They rely on learned schemas (how a hand is built, how light wraps a form), stored knowledge about structure and a trained eye-hand loop working from reference or observation. Their skill lives in procedural and conceptual memory rather than in raw image fidelity.

## The underlying science

- **Declarative vs procedural memory:** knowing what something looks like is declarative. Knowing how to make a hand produce it is procedural. They rely on different neural circuits (hippocampus and temporal cortex versus basal ganglia, cerebellum and motor cortex).
- **Dual coding of perception and action:** the brain represents seeing and doing in partially separate pathways, often described as the ventral ("what") stream and dorsal ("how") stream. Recognition and recall can be excellent while the action pathway is untrained.
- **Skill transfer is narrow:** motor skills improve mostly with specific deliberate practice. Having the information does not automatically produce the ability to execute.

## A clean way to say it

Remembering a detail perfectly and reproducing it by hand are different abilities. The first is about storing information. The second is a trained motor skill that translates information into precise movement. Having one does not give you the other, just as memorizing sheet music does not make someone a pianist.

## Research: how long does improvement take?

**Bottom line:** Measurable change appears within weeks. Competent drawing takes months to a couple of years. Realism at a high level takes years.

### Motor learning research

- Motor learning runs in phases: a fast early phase within a session, an offline consolidation phase between sessions and a slow phase of smaller steady gains over days, weeks and months.
- In a 10-day hand-skill study, rapid learning covered days 1 and 2, consolidation days 3 to 7 and stable performance days 8 to 10.
- Sleep supports long-lasting storage of newly acquired motor traces after training.

### Drawing-specific research

- A 16-week observational drawing course produced improved drawing skills in a study of 45 participants and was linked to brain function changes. Earlier work found functional brain reorganization after about three months of training.

### Practice-based estimates (non-peer-reviewed guides, treat as rough)

- One to three months of daily practice covers basics like line control and simple shapes.
- Six to twelve months at 30 to 60 minutes daily produces competent drawings.
- Proportions are the slowest component: roughly 6 to 12 months to reach proficiency and 2 to 3 years for mastery. This is the exact skill where a perfect visual memory does not transfer automatically.
- Frequency beats session length. Ten minutes daily is often claimed to outperform one long weekly session, with a noticeable shift in seeing within two to four weeks.

### The 10,000-hour rule

- The number was the average deliberate practice of top violinists by age 20 in one study. Ericsson said the popular version was a misreading.
- Quality matters more than raw hours. Deliberate practice demands intense focus that can usually be sustained for only 60 to 90 minutes at a time.

### Application to the eidetic case (inference, not a sourced finding)

The perceptual input is already strong so the bottleneck is the hand-side mapping and decomposition skills. Those follow the same weeks-to-months motor timeline as anyone else.

**Caldris / system note:** skill levels and class bonuses can accelerate the grind on the sheet. They do not erase the mind-body split. A clear Diagnosis overlay helps decomposition; the hand still has to learn the marks.
