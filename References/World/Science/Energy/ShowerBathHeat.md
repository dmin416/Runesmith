# Energy to Heat Shower and Bath Water

> Earth heat-load sheet. Incoming 50°F, a 105°F shower, gpm ratings, tank and tankless heater sizes, and the 40°F winter tap are Earth anchors. They are not Caldris well temperatures.

`Pumps.md` owns head, pipe strength, and the mixing valve that holds a stable shower temperature. This file is only the heat in the water. Hair-dryer heat stays in `QuietBlowDryer.md`. The joule peg stays in `../../../Runes/Energy.md` (1 mana = 10 J). Do not price a shower from the watt figures here without that path's efficiency.

Assumes 50°F incoming water heated to 105°F (a 55°F rise).

- Per gallon: about 459 BTU (0.134 kWh)

## Continuous Power to Heat a Running Shower

| Flow rate | BTU/hr | kW |
|---|---|---|
| 1.8 gpm | 49,500 | 14.5 |
| 2.0 gpm | 55,000 | 16.1 |
| 2.5 gpm | 68,800 | 20.2 |
| 5.0 gpm (old showerhead) | 137,600 | 40.3 |

Whole-house tankless electric heaters run 18 to 36 kW and gas tankless units run 150,000 to 199,000 BTU/hr to meet these loads. A standard 4.5 kW electric tank heater cannot keep up in real time and relies on its stored hot water.

## Total Energy per Event

| Water volume | Example | BTU | kWh |
|---|---|---|---|
| 37 to 50 gal | Long shower or standard bath | 17,000 to 22,900 | 5.0 to 6.7 |
| 60 to 80 gal | Large soaking tub | 27,500 to 36,700 | 8.1 to 10.8 |
| 110 gal | Oversized tub | 50,500 | 14.8 |

## Winter Adjustment

Incoming water at 40°F (a 65°F rise) raises every figure by about 18%.
