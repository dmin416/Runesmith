# Engines

Hub: `../Science.md`. Shaft power into electricity: `Generators.md`. Line shafts and lathes: `../Invent/MechanicalPrecision.md`. Shop build order: `../../../Food/KitchenCraft.md`. Era lock: `../../Tech/Technology.md`.

Heat engines turn a temperature difference into shaft power. Electric motors turn current into shaft power. A generator is a motor run backward.

## Where each one wins

| Need | Machine |
|---|---|
| Strong slow shaft from fire, for pumps, looms, line shafts, traction | Steam piston |
| High shaft speed straight into a dynamo or a screw | Steam turbine |
| Any heat source, including a rune or waste heat, with no water drum | Stirling |
| A few watts from a small temperature gap (hand, sun, warm stone) | Low-difference Stirling |
| Many small shafts, or a shaft far from the boiler | Electric motor |
| Electricity at all | Generator on a steam, Stirling, water, or wind shaft |

## Invent order

Water wheels and windmills already turn shafts. Steam is the next prime mover, and Caldris already has boilers, condensers, and mana-stone rail. A Stirling wants the same bored cylinder plus a regenerator and a hot-end seal, so it follows a working steam piston rather than replacing the first one. Electric motors want copper wire, iron, and either a battery or a dynamo. The usual chain is heat engine, then generator, then motor.

Mana-stone heat can fire a boiler or a Stirling heater without coal. A heating rune is a poorer fit inside a timed explosion piston than it is under a kettle or a heater head.
