# Soft Close Toilets and Toilet Design: Mechanisms, Materials, Design Notes

Earth toilet and soft-close hinge reference for magitech plumbing and civilian invent beats. Pumps / faucet logic: `../Energy/Pumps.md`. Pipe: `../Metallurgy/CopperPipe.md`, `../Metallurgy/Brass.md`. Mobile bath fit: `../Vehicle/MobileHome.md`.

## 1. Toilet Fundamentals

### 1.1 Core Components

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| Bowl | Receives waste; shaped to direct water and maintain a water seal |
| Rim and rim holes / rim channel | Distributes rinse water around the bowl (absent on rimless designs) |
| Siphon jet | Port at the bottom of the bowl that fires water straight into the trapway to start the siphon |
| Trapway | S-shaped internal passage that holds the water seal and carries waste to the drain |
| Water seal (trap seal) | Standing water that blocks sewer gas |
| Tank | Holds flush water (gravity and pressure-assist types) |
| Flush valve | Opening between tank and bowl (2 in, 3 in or 4 in diameter) |
| Flapper or canister seal | Seals the flush valve until the flush is triggered |
| Fill valve (ballcock) | Refills the tank and sends a refill stream down the overflow tube to restore the bowl seal |
| Overflow tube | Prevents tank overflow; also carries bowl refill water |
| Trip lever / push button | Lifts the flapper or canister |
| Flange (closet flange) | Fitting on the floor drain that anchors the toilet |
| Wax ring or waxless gasket | Seals toilet outlet horn to flange |
| Closet bolts | Bolts from flange through toilet base |
| Seat, lid, hinges | User interface; where soft close mechanisms live |

### 1.2 How a Gravity Siphonic Flush Works

1. Lever lifts the flapper. Tank water drops into the bowl through the rim and the siphon jet.
2. Water rises in the bowl until it overtops the trapway weir.
3. Trapway fills completely with water. A full column of water falling down the far leg creates a siphon.
4. Siphon pulls bowl contents through the trapway into the drain.
5. Bowl level drops until air enters the trapway. Siphon breaks (the gurgle at the end of a flush).
6. Fill valve refills the tank. Refill tube restores the trap seal.

Key performance factors: flush valve diameter (faster discharge), trapway diameter (2 in standard; 2-1/8 in and larger reduce clogs), fully glazed trapway (less friction), jet design and timing of rim water versus jet water.

### 1.3 Flush System Types

| Type | Principle | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Gravity siphonic | Tank water starts a siphon in the trapway | Most common North American residential type |
| Washdown | Water pushes waste over the weir with momentum; no siphon | Common in Europe, Australia and Asia; wider trapway, fewer clogs, more visible bowl soiling, smaller water spot |
| Pressure-assisted | Sealed vessel inside the tank compresses air using supply line pressure; releases a forceful flush | Loud; very strong; needs minimum supply pressure (commonly around 20 to 25 psi) |
| Dual flush | Two volumes: partial for liquids, full for solids | Often uses a canister flush valve instead of a flapper |
| Flushometer | No tank; valve releases a measured volume straight from a high-flow supply line | Commercial and institutional; needs a 1 in supply line and high flow |
| Tornado / cyclonic | Nozzles create a swirling rinse instead of rim holes | Example: TOTO Tornado Flush |
| Power-assist pump | Electric pump boosts flush flow | Used where gravity alone underperforms at low volumes |
| Macerating / upflush | Grinder pump liquefies waste and pumps it up or horizontally | Basements and remote bathrooms without gravity drain access |
| Vacuum | Pressure differential pulls waste through small pipes | Aircraft, ships, trains, some buildings; extremely low water use |
| Composting | Aerobic decomposition, no water | Off-grid cabins, parks |
| Incinerating | Burns waste to ash electrically or with gas | Remote sites |

### 1.4 Water Use

- US federal maximum since 1994: 1.6 gallons per flush (gpf), from the Energy Policy Act of 1992.
- EPA WaterSense label: 1.28 gpf maximum with a minimum performance requirement.
- California, Washington and several other states: 1.28 gpf maximum for new residential toilets.
- High efficiency: 1.28 gpf. Ultra high efficiency: 1.1 gpf down to 0.8 gpf.
- Older pre-1994 toilets: 3.5 to 7 gpf.
- UK: 6 litre maximum for new installs. Australia: WELS rated, commonly 4.5 / 3 litre dual flush or lower.
- Concern at very low volumes: drainline carry. Less water means waste travels a shorter distance in long horizontal drain runs, which can cause blockages downstream in older plumbing.

### 1.5 Performance Testing

- **MaP (Maximum Performance) testing**: independent test using soybean paste test media wrapped in latex. Scores in grams removed in one flush, capped at 1000 g. WaterSense minimum is 350 g. MaP Premium requires 1.06 gpf or less with at least 600 g.
- **ASME A112.19.2 / CSA B45.1**: North American standard for vitreous china plumbing fixtures (flush tests, water absorption, trap seal depth, ball pass tests).
- **ASME A112.19.14**: dual flush devices.
- **EN 997**: European standard for WC pans with integral traps.

### 1.6 Form Factors and Dimensions

- **Rough-in** (wall to center of drain): 12 in standard; 10 in and 14 in exist.
- **Bowl shape**: round front (about 16.5 in from bolt holes to front) is compact; elongated (about 18.5 in) is more comfortable and hygienic for most adults.
- **Height**: standard about 14 to 15 in to the rim; comfort / chair height about 16 to 17 in to the rim. ADA requires 17 to 19 in to the top of the seat.
- **One-piece**: tank and bowl fused into one casting. No tank-to-bowl gasket to leak; easier to clean; heavier; more expensive.
- **Two-piece**: separate tank bolted to bowl. Cheaper; easier to carry; tank-to-bowl gasket is a leak point.
- **Wall-hung**: bowl mounted to a steel in-wall carrier with a concealed tank (e.g. Geberit, Grohe). Floor is fully cleanable; height adjustable at install; repairs to the tank happen through the actuator plate.
- **Back-to-wall**: floor-standing bowl pushed flush to the wall with a concealed cistern.
- **Skirted / concealed trapway**: smooth sides covering the trapway bumps. Easier to wipe; harder to access bolts.
- **Rimless**: no overhanging rim channel. Water is directed by a shaped spreader or nozzles. Removes the most hidden bacteria site in the bowl.
- **Integrated smart toilets / bidet seats**: heated seats, warm water wash, dryers, deodorizers, auto-flush, automatic lid opening and closing (e.g. TOTO Washlet and Neorest, Kohler Numi, Bio Bidet).

## 2. Toilet Materials

### 2.1 Vitreous China (Standard Bowl and Tank Material)

**Raw materials**
- Ball clay (plasticity and green strength)
- Kaolin / china clay (whiteness, refractoriness)
- Feldspar (flux; melts into glass during firing to bind particles and close porosity)
- Silica / quartz (structure; controls shrinkage and thermal expansion)

**Manufacturing**
1. Raw materials blended with water into slip (liquid clay suspension).
2. Slip cast into molds. Traditional plaster molds draw water from the slip by capillary action. Modern plants use high-pressure casting in porous resin molds for faster cycle times and longer mold life.
3. Green ware demolded, trimmed, holes cut, pieces joined (one-piece models are assembled from several castings while still leather hard).
4. Dried slowly to avoid cracking.
5. Glaze sprayed on. Glaze is a glass frit with zirconium silicate as the white opacifier plus pigments for color.
6. Fired once in a tunnel kiln at roughly 1200 to 1250 °C (2200 to 2300 °F). Clay body vitrifies; glaze fuses to the body.
7. Total shrinkage from cast to fired: roughly 10 to 13 percent. Molds are built oversized to compensate. Shrinkage variation is why toilet dimensions are listed with tolerances.
8. Inspection: visual, flush test, leak test. Rejects are crushed.

**Properties**
- Water absorption 0.5 percent or less (vitrified, non-porous body)
- Hard glaze surface resists staining, scratching and chemicals
- Brittle; cracks from impact, overtightened bolts or thermal shock
- Very heavy; high embodied energy from kiln firing

**Advanced glazes**
- TOTO CeFiONtect: ion-barrier glaze smoothed at the microscopic level to resist adhesion
- American Standard EverClean: antimicrobial surface treatment
- Duravit HygieneGlaze: glaze with antibacterial additives
- General principle: smoother glaze surface means fewer anchor points for biofilm, mineral scale and stains

### 2.2 Other Bowl Materials

| Material | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fireclay | Some large or specialty fixtures | Thicker, stronger body; more dimensionally stable for big pieces |
| Stainless steel (typically type 304, often 14 gauge) | Prisons, institutions, high-abuse public sites, marine | Vandal resistant; no cracking; can be seamless welded; louder |
| Polyethylene / ABS (rotomolded or injection molded) | RVs, portable toilets, boats | Light; cheap; scratches and stains more easily |
| Solid surface / resin composites | Designer and specialty fixtures | Moldable; repairable; less heat and scratch resistant than glaze |

### 2.3 Tank Internals

| Part | Common Materials | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fill valve | ABS, acetal (POM), polypropylene; rubber or silicone seals | Modern float-cup valves (e.g. Fluidmaster 400 series) replaced brass ballcocks |
| Flapper | EPDM, natural rubber, silicone | Chlorine and chloramine in municipal water degrade rubber; chlorine-resistant compounds (often red or blue) last longer |
| Canister flush valve seal | Silicone | Longer life than flappers; common on dual flush |
| Flush valve body | ABS, PVC | Molded and bonded to the tank with a spud gasket |
| Trip lever | Zinc die-cast (Zamak) with chrome plating, brass, plastic | |
| Tank bolts | Brass, stainless steel | Steel bolts rust inside tanks |
| Tank-to-bowl gasket | Sponge rubber, EPDM | Main leak point on two-piece toilets |

### 2.4 Floor Seal and Flange

- **Wax ring**: petroleum wax, sometimes with a plastic horn sleeve. Conforms to irregular surfaces; one use only; fails if the toilet rocks.
- **Waxless seals**: foam or rubber gaskets (e.g. Fluidmaster Better Than Wax, Danco Perfect Seal). Repositionable; not affected by heat.
- **Flange materials**: PVC, ABS, cast iron, brass, stainless steel ring with plastic body. A rusted or broken flange ring is a common cause of toilet rocking and seal failure.

## 3. Toilet Seats

### 3.1 Seat Materials

| Material | Description | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermoset plastic (urea-formaldehyde, melamine; sold in Europe as "Duroplast") | Compression molded resin that cures irreversibly | Hard, glossy, scratch resistant, heavy and solid feel, does not warp | Brittle on hard impact; more expensive |
| Thermoplastic (polypropylene) | Injection molded | Cheap, light, flexible, chemical resistant | Scratches; can feel flimsy; may yellow or flex |
| Molded wood | Wood flour or fiber mixed with resin, molded, coated with enamel | Heavy, warm feel, inexpensive | Enamel chips; exposed core absorbs water and swells |
| Solid wood | Oak, bamboo, teak, etc. | Aesthetic | Needs sealing; cracks and finish wear over time |
| Polyresin | Clear or colored resin cast with embedded objects | Decorative | Heavy; can crack |
| Cushioned / soft | Vinyl skin over foam | Comfort | Splits, absorbs moisture, hygiene issues |
| Stainless steel / antimicrobial plastics | Institutional | Durable | Cold; utilitarian |

### 3.2 Seat Standards and Fit

- Mounting hole spacing: 5.5 in (140 mm) center to center, nearly universal in North America.
- Seat must match bowl shape: round or elongated.
- Open-front seats are required by US model plumbing codes in public restrooms.
- ANSI Z124.5 covers plastic toilet seats.

### 3.3 Hinge Hardware

- **Bolt materials**: nylon or polypropylene (no corrosion, can strip), stainless steel (strongest; 304 grade common), chrome-plated zinc alloy (looks good; plating pits and corrodes in humid rooms).
- **Bottom-fix**: bolt drops through hinge and bowl; nut tightened from underneath.
- **Top-fix**: expanding rubber anchors or toggle anchors tightened from above. Needed for wall-hung and skirted bowls where the underside is closed.
- **Quick-release**: hinge posts stay bolted to the bowl; seat lifts off with a button press or a firm pull. Allows full cleaning of the hinge area. Examples: Bemis Easy-Clean and Change, Kohler Quick-Release, many European designs. Frequently combined with soft close dampers in the same hinge block.
- **Anti-loosening systems**: Bemis STA-TITE and similar use locking nuts or cam fasteners to keep seats from shifting.

## 4. Soft Close Mechanisms

### 4.1 The Problem Being Solved

A falling lid or seat converts gravitational potential energy into rotational kinetic energy and dumps it into the bowl in one impact. Results: noise, chipped glaze, cracked seats, pinched fingers and children startled or hurt.

**Gravity torque on a hinged seat or lid**

τ = m · g · r · sin(θ)

- m = mass of seat or lid
- g = 9.81 m/s²
- r = distance from hinge axis to center of mass
- θ = angle measured from vertical (0° when upright, 90° when horizontal / closed)

Torque is near zero when the seat is upright. It peaks as the seat approaches horizontal. A free-falling lid therefore accelerates hardest right before it hits.

**Example energy**
- Lid mass 1.5 kg, center of mass 0.2 m from hinge
- Potential energy released from vertical to closed: m·g·r = 1.5 × 9.81 × 0.2 ≈ 2.9 J
- Peak gravity torque at closed position: ≈ 2.9 N·m
- The damper must dissipate that energy over the full arc instead of in one impact. Typical soft close dampers are rated somewhere around 1 to 3 N·m, matched to the mass of the part they control.

**Behavior target**
- Seat or lid released near vertical needs a light nudge to start (gravity torque near zero).
- Descent is slow and steady across the arc.
- Total closing time commonly about 3 to 8 seconds.
- Opening is free and light (one-way damping).
- The part stays upright when raised (rests past top dead center against the tank or a stop).

### 4.2 Rotary Vane Damper (Dominant Design)

This is the barrel-shaped cartridge found inside most soft close hinges.

**Construction**
- Cylindrical housing filled with silicone fluid (polydimethylsiloxane, PDMS).
- Rotor shaft with one or two vanes running inside a chamber.
- Fixed partitions inside the housing divide the chamber into sections.
- Rotation pushes the vane through the fluid, forcing fluid from the shrinking side of the chamber to the growing side through a very small clearance gap or orifice.
- Shaft end is D-cut, double-flat, hex or splined; it keys into the hinge post fixed to the bowl. The housing rotates with the seat (or the reverse arrangement, depending on the brand).
- O-ring or lip seals at the shaft exit keep fluid in.
- End cap is ultrasonic welded, crimped or snap fit.

**One-way action (check valve)**
- A flexible flap, movable vane lip or ball check is built into the vane or partition.
- Opening direction: check valve opens; fluid bypasses freely; seat lifts with almost no resistance.
- Closing direction: check valve shuts; fluid can only escape through the tight gap; strong resistance.

**Angle-dependent damping**
- Better dampers vary chamber geometry or gap width along the arc so resistance is low near vertical (so the seat starts falling without a push) and highest near horizontal (where gravity torque peaks).
- Cheaper dampers with uniform resistance often require a push to start or slow down unevenly.

**Typical rotation range**: about 100° to 120°, limited by internal stops.

**Materials**
| Part | Common Materials |
|---|---|
| Housing | POM (acetal), PBT, PA66 (nylon, often glass filled), zinc die-cast on premium hinges |
| Rotor / vane | POM, PA66 glass filled |
| Seals | NBR or EPDM O-rings (silicone fluid can swell silicone rubber seals, so silicone is avoided at the dynamic seal) |
| Fluid | Silicone oil (PDMS), high viscosity grades from roughly 10,000 cSt up to several hundred thousand cSt |

**Why silicone oil**
- Viscosity changes less with temperature than mineral or hydrocarbon oils
- Chemically inert; does not attack plastics
- Very low volatility; does not dry out
- Nontoxic; stable for years
- Wide viscosity range available, letting manufacturers tune torque by changing fluid grade instead of retooling parts

### 4.3 Continuous Rotary Shear Damper

- Rotor disc spins in a thin film of viscous fluid. Resistance comes from fluid shear, proportional to speed.
- Allows unlimited rotation and smooth damping.
- Torque per volume is low, so toilet seat versions are rarer; this type is more common in small appliance lids, CD trays and glove boxes.
- Often paired with a gear train to multiply damping torque.

### 4.4 Linear Piston Damper with Cam

- Hinge rotation drives a cam or crank that pushes a piston through a cylinder.
- **Hydraulic version**: oil forced through an orifice; behaves like a tiny door closer or cabinet damper.
- **Pneumatic version**: air forced through a small vent. Cheaper; less consistent; sensitive to vent clogging; air compresses so motion can feel springy.
- Cam profile can shape the damping curve along the arc.
- Used in some older soft close designs and some lid-only systems.

### 4.5 Spring and Counterbalance Systems

- Torsion springs or compression springs partially counter the weight of the seat or lid.
- **Lift-assist / soft open**: spring makes lifting the lid easier; aid for elderly or limited-strength users.
- **Hybrid spring plus damper**: spring offsets most of the weight while a small damper controls the remainder. Gives consistent speed with less dependence on fluid viscosity.
- Spring fatigue is a long-term wear factor.

### 4.6 Friction Hinges (Constant Torque)

- Stacked spring steel friction clips or a split bushing clamp the shaft.
- Holds the seat at any angle; resists motion at all speeds equally.
- Not true soft close because friction torque does not rise with speed. Used on some lids and institutional products as "stay where placed" hinges.

### 4.7 Motorized / Electronic Systems

- Found in smart toilets and high-end bidet seats (TOTO Neorest and Washlet, Kohler Numi, etc.).
- Gear motor drives the lid and sometimes the seat.
- Proximity or infrared sensors open the lid when a person approaches and close it after they leave.
- Motor control profiles provide soft opening and soft closing. Many still include a mechanical damper as backup.
- Manual override clutches prevent gear damage when someone pushes the lid by hand.

### 4.8 Supporting Features

- **Bumpers**: rubber, TPE or silicone pads on the seat underside and lid. Absorb small final contact shocks; space the seat above the rim for airflow and cleaning.
- **Hinge covers / caps**: hide bolts and keep grime out of damper housings.
- **Integrated quick-release**: damper sits in the removable seat portion; posts stay on the bowl.

### 4.9 Separate Control of Seat and Lid

- Lid is usually heavier than the ring seat and often has a farther center of mass.
- Many designs use one damper per side (one controls the seat, the other the lid) or two dampers per side.
- The lid damper is rated for higher torque.
- When the lid closes it may push down on the seat. Damper ratings must account for combined loads.

### 4.10 Temperature Effects

- Cold bathroom: fluid viscosity rises; seat closes slower.
- Warm bathroom or seat near a heater: fluid thins; seat closes faster.
- Silicone fluid reduces this effect but does not eliminate it.

### 4.11 Failure Modes

| Symptom | Likely Cause |
|---|---|
| Seat slams or drops fast for the last part of the arc | Fluid leaked past worn seals; damper running dry |
| Seat falls freely the entire way | Check valve stuck open, shaft stripped, damper disengaged from hinge post |
| Seat will not close without pushing | Damper too strong for seat mass; fluid too viscous; cold room; misaligned hinge binding |
| Seat closes slowly then stops midway | Binding hinge, bent post, overtightened bolts pinching the hinge |
| Oily residue near hinge | Seal failure; fluid leaking |
| Clicking or grinding | Cracked housing, broken internal stop, debris |
| Seat shifts sideways | Loose bolts; worn rubber anchors |

**Most common cause of damage**: users pushing the seat or lid down by hand. This creates far higher pressure inside the damper than gravity does. Seals blow out or the D-shaft rounds off. Overload clutches (see design notes) address this.

**Repair**: many brands sell replacement hinge damper cartridges or full hinge sets. Matching shaft shape, rotation direction and torque rating matters. Universal replacement soft close hinges exist but must match seat mass. Otherwise the full seat is replaced, which is often cheaper for low-end seats.

## 5. Design Thoughts

### 5.1 Soft Close Damper Improvements

- **Torque curve matching**: shape internal chamber or bypass grooves so damping torque tracks the sin(θ) gravity curve. Produces constant angular velocity from release to closed.
- **Overload clutch**: slip clutch or breakaway spline between shaft and damper so a hand-forced closing does not pressurize the fluid past the seal limit.
- **Temperature compensation**: bimetallic or wax-actuated orifice that narrows as temperature rises, offsetting fluid thinning.
- **Adjustable damping**: small screw-adjusted bypass orifice so the user can tune close time to seat mass and room temperature.
- **Standardized cartridges**: a common cartridge format across brands (like faucet cartridges) would make repair cheaper than seat replacement.
- **Eddy current damping**: copper or aluminum disc spinning between permanent magnets produces speed-proportional braking with no fluid, no seals and nothing to leak. Torque is low, so a step-up gear train is needed. Lifetime would be limited by gear wear instead of seal wear.
- **Magnetorheological fluid**: viscosity changes under a magnetic field; enables electronically tuned damping for smart toilets.
- **Sealed hinge geometry**: fully enclosed damper with no gaps where urine and cleaning chemicals collect.

### 5.2 Bowl and Flush Design

- **Rimless bowls** remove the hardest cleaning area. Trade-off: splash control relies on precise water distribution.
- **Larger trapways** reduce clogs at low flush volumes.
- **Larger flush valves** (3 in and 4 in) give faster discharge with less water.
- **Glazed trapways** reduce friction and residue.
- **Water spot size**: larger surface area keeps the bowl cleaner and reduces odor; smaller water spots save water but soil more easily.
- **Splash reduction**: deep water seal versus low splash height is a constant trade-off.
- **Drainline carry**: ultra-low-flush designs must still move waste down long horizontal runs in older houses.

### 5.3 Ergonomics and Accessibility

- **Height**: comfort height helps tall, elderly and mobility-limited users; standard height suits children and shorter adults.
- **Posture**: squatting or knees-above-hips posture straightens the anorectal angle. Footstools replicate this on Western toilets.
- **Lift-assist seats and lids** for weak grip or arthritis.
- **Grab bars**, wall-hung adjustable height, bidet functions reduce need for twisting and wiping.

### 5.4 Hygiene

- **Lid-down flushing** reduces aerosol plume spread from flushing.
- **Touchless flushing** via sensors.
- **Seat material**: hard nonporous surfaces (thermoset, glazed) clean better than porous wood or split cushion vinyl.
- **Quick-release hinges** allow cleaning of the hinge area, the dirtiest spot on most toilets.
- **Antimicrobial additives** in glazes and plastics slow bacterial growth on surfaces; they do not replace cleaning.

### 5.5 Sustainability

- Vitreous china firing consumes significant energy. Reject rates and transport weight add to footprint.
- Old porcelain toilets can be crushed into aggregate for concrete, road base or pathway material ("porcelain recycling" programs).
- Low-flow fixtures cut household indoor water use substantially. Toilets historically account for the single largest share of indoor residential water use.
- Greywater toilets refill tanks from sink or shower water.
- Composting and vacuum systems remove most or all flush water.

### 5.6 Noise

- Flush noise: pressure-assist is loudest; gravity siphonic is moderate; wall-hung concealed systems are quietest.
- Fill valve noise: modern pilot-operated valves are quieter than old ballcocks.
- Soft close dampers eliminate the single loudest bathroom noise (seat slam).

## 6. Troubleshooting Reference (Whole Toilet)

| Problem | Common Causes | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Running toilet | Worn flapper; chain too short; fill valve set too high; cracked overflow tube | Replace flapper; adjust chain slack; lower float; replace flush valve |
| Phantom flush (tank refills by itself) | Slow leak through flapper | Replace flapper; clean flush valve seat |
| Weak flush | Low tank water level; clogged rim holes or siphon jet (mineral scale); partial trapway clog | Raise water level to fill line; clean jets with vinegar or descaler; auger the trapway |
| Frequent clogs | Small trapway; low flush volume; drain venting issues; drainline partial blockage | Upgrade toilet; check vent stack; snake drain |
| Toilet rocks | Uneven floor; broken flange; loose closet bolts | Shim; repair flange ring; replace seal after fixing rock |
| Water at base | Failed wax ring; cracked bowl; condensation; tank bolt leak | Reset with new seal; inspect for cracks; check tank-to-bowl gasket |
| Sewer smell | Dry or failed seal; broken wax ring; trap seal siphoned by vent problem | Replace seal; check venting |
| Gurgling | Partial drain clog; poor venting | Clear drain; check vent |
| Sweating tank | Cold supply water in humid room | Insulated tank liner; anti-sweat mixing valve |
| Soft close seat slams | Damper fluid lost | Replace damper cartridges or seat |

## 8. Quick Glossary

- **Ballcock**: older style fill valve with a float arm.
- **Closet**: plumbing trade term for a toilet (water closet).
- **Damper torque**: resistance torque a soft close cartridge produces, usually specified in N·m or kgf·cm.
- **Duroplast**: European trade name for thermoset urea-formaldehyde seats.
- **gpf / lpf**: gallons per flush / litres per flush.
- **PDMS**: polydimethylsiloxane, the silicone fluid inside soft close dampers.
- **Rough-in**: distance from finished wall to drain center.
- **Siphon jet**: water port aimed into the trapway to start the siphon.
- **Trap seal**: water standing in the trap that blocks sewer gas.
- **Trapway**: internal passage of the toilet that leads to the drain.
- **Vitreous china**: glass-bonded ceramic with very low water absorption.
- **Washdown**: non-siphonic flush using water momentum only.
- **Weir**: highest point in the trapway that sets the bowl water level.
