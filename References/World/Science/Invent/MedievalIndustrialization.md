# Medieval Industrialization (Roland craft path)

Earth research note for a master with **modern knowledge**, a working forge, hammer and anvil. Timeline from bare forge to screw-cutting lathes and the products that unlock along the way.

**Caldris overlay:** the kingdom already has water mills, steam / mana-stone trains and magitech (`../../Tech/Technology.md`). Roland does **not** start from a stone-age blank. Use **Part 1** when he lacks local files, steel and mill access. Use **Part 2** when infrastructure, files and water power (or mana motors) are already in reach. Civilian product shortlist and shop build order: `../../../Ideas.md`. Century product catalog (500s–1700s): `CraftsmanByCentury.md`. Metallurgy shortcuts: `../Metallurgy/CraftMetal.md`. ## Part 1: Timeline from bare forge to water-powered screw-cutting lathe

### Baseline estimate

- Solo master smith: **3 to 6 years**
- Small crew of **3 to 5** helpers: **1 to 2 years**

The lathe sits at the end of a long dependency chain. The chain sets the timeline.

### Phase sequence (solo master smith)

| Phase | Timeframe | Work |
|---|---|---|
| Infrastructure | Year 0 to 0.5 | Charcoal production, bloomery or purchased iron, clay for crucibles and molds, bellows, stone or brick forge |
| Steel and tooling | Year 0.5 to 1.5 | Carburizing (cementation) for tool steel, heat treatment of files, chisels and punches |
| Hand-driven lathe and wooden machinery | Year 1 to 2 | Treadle or bow-driven wood lathe, then metal-turning lathe with hardened centers and tool rest |
| Water power | Year 1.5 to 3 | Mill site, dam or leat, wooden wheel, wooden cog and lantern gears, line shaft, leather belting |
| Screw-cutting | Year 2 to 4 | True lead screw by the Maudslay method, then taps and dies in graduated sizes |
| Reliable tapping | Year 3 to 6 | Repeatable thread form, hardened tool steel, clean flute cutting |

### Key bottlenecks early on

- Iron quality: bloomery iron is inconsistent and full of slag.
- Files: making them by hand takes weeks each and early machine building is mostly filing.
- Timber seasoning: wooden frames need time to cure.
- Millwright labor: heavy and timber-intensive, where extra hands save the most time.

### What speeds it up

- Knowing the target design in advance
- Reliable iron and steel supply
- Helpers for wheel building, charcoal and bellows work

### Historical comparison

Maudslay's screw-cutting lathe (1800) came after centuries of accumulated toolmaking. Full knowledge compresses that history dramatically but does not remove the material and tooling steps.

## Part 2: Compressed timeline (infrastructure, files and power already available)

If infrastructure, files and water power or motors are already in place, the timeline drops to roughly **6 to 18 months**. This is the closer match for Roland once he has shop money, guild access or a mill partnership in Caldris.

### Main time sinks

1. **First accurate lead screw (2 to 6 months).** Everything downstream inherits its errors. The first screw is filed by hand against a template, then improved by lapping against a nut and cutting a second, better screw from the first. Two or three generations are usually needed.

2. **Rigid, flat lathe bed and ways (1 to 3 months).** Beds need hand scraping to flatness. The three-plate method produces true flats without a master. A sloppy bed ruins an accurate screw.

3. **Heat treatment iteration (1 to 3 months of lost time).** Taps and dies crack, warp or come out too soft. Without a pyrometer, color judgment and quench timing must be learned per steel batch. Expect a high early scrap rate.

4. **Tap and die geometry (1 to 2 months).** Flute shape, relief and thread form must be right so the tap cuts instead of tearing. Sets need taper, plug and bottoming versions for each size.

5. **Standardizing thread form and pitch (2 to 4 weeks).** Easy to skip and costly to regret. Fix a thread profile and pitch series early and cut master gauges.

6. **Gear train and change gears (1 to 2 months).** Requires a dividing engine or dividing plate, itself a precision tool to build first.

7. **Measurement (parallel, constant drag).** Calipers, micrometers, straightedges and surface plates must exist to verify accuracy.

### Critical path

Lead screw, then bed, then dividing plate. Everything else depends on these. Tap geometry and heat treatment work can overlap with them, which is where a small crew gains the most.

## Part 3: Most useful things to make in a medieval-leaning world

Ranked by leverage. Caldris already has some of these (mills, steam / stone trains). Prefer gaps and local monopolies over reinventing crown rail.

### 1. Tool-making multipliers

| Product | Application |
|---|---|
| Water-driven trip hammer | Multiplies forging output several times over |
| Water-powered blast furnace bellows | More iron, plus cast iron for gears, pots and machine frames |
| Crucible steel and good files | Base of every precision tool afterward |

### 2. Food production

| Product | Application |
|---|---|
| Iron moldboard plow and iron-shod spades | Deeper turning of heavy soil raises yields |
| Scythe | Cuts hay and grain several times faster than a sickle |

## Part 4: Recommended production order

1. Tools and steel (files, chisels, punches, trip hammer)
2. Agricultural iron (plows, scythes, horse collars, horseshoes)
3. Water-powered milling and blast furnace bellows
4. Screw-based equipment (presses, pumps, vises, jacks)
5. Information and sight (paper, eyeglasses; printing press invent-only, not baseline)
6. Health and sanitation (soap, quicklime, distilled alcohol, glass)
7. Coordination tools (clocks, compasses)
8. Precision machinery (screw-cutting lathe, dividing engine, measurement tools)

Early steps generate surplus food and income. That surplus pays for each later stage.

## Kitchen shop tool chain

> Shop tool tiers for kitchen work. Kitchen gear lists: `../../../Food/KitchenKit.md`. Cookware production methods: `../../../Food/KitchenCraft.md` (Food domain; not metallurgy law).

### Build order (tiers)

Everything below depends on the tier before it. A smith cannot skip tiers without magic or purchase.

### Tier 0: Bare hands and found materials
- Stone anvil or stump anvil
- Charcoal fire pit
- Hand hammer (stone or bought iron)
- Water bucket for quenching

### Tier 1: Basic forge shop
- Forge hearth with clay or brick lining
- Bellows (leather and wood) or a bellows rune
- Anvil (forged wrought iron body with steel face, or bought)
- Hammers (cross peen, straight peen, rounding)
- Tongs (forge-welded or riveted)
- Hot chisels and punches
- Swage blocks and fullers
- Vise (leg vise, forged)
- Files (forged then cut by hand with a chisel and hardened)
- Charcoal supply (a charcoal burner is its own project)

### Tier 2: Precision hand tooling
- Drawplate (hardened steel plate with tapered holes) for making wire
- Grindstone (sandstone wheel on a hand crank or treadle)
- Pole lathe or treadle lathe for wood
- Spoon knife, drawknife, plane, saw, auger
- Sand molds and flasks for casting
- Crucible (clay and graphite or clay and sand)
- Raising hammers and stakes for hollow forms
- Scales (balance beam with stone or brass weights) for calibration

### Tier 3: Water or wind power
- Water wheel (undershot, overshot, or breast wheel) or windmill
- Line shaft with belts (leather) or gears (wood or iron)
- Cam shaft driving a tilt hammer
- Powered grindstone
- Powered bellows
- Powered drawbench for wire

### Tier 4: Machine tools
- Metal lathe (cast iron bed, hand-filed ways, lead screw cut by a hand-made tap and die set)
- Boring bar for cylinders
- Rolling mill (two iron rollers, geared, water driven) for sheet and bar
- Screw press (forged and cut screw, wooden or iron frame)
- Hydraulic press (see Part 4)
- Shaper or planer for flat surfaces

### Magic shortcuts at any tier
- Rotation rune replaces a water wheel or crank (limited by mana cost and runtime)
- Heat rune replaces bellows and fuel for a forge
- Hardening or tempering rune holds an exact temperature
- Shaping spell moves metal without hammering (high mana cost and skill)
- Precision spell for measurement or alignment (replaces a lathe for small jobs)

### Machines to build first

### Lathe
- **Wood (pole lathe):** A frame with two centers, a springy pole overhead, a cord wrapped around the work, and a foot treadle. The cord spins the work back and forth. Hand-held tools cut on the forward stroke.
- **Wood (treadle with flywheel):** Add a crank and a heavy flywheel for continuous rotation.
- **Metal:**
  1. Cast or forge a rigid bed. Cast iron is best.
  2. Make a headstock with a hardened spindle and bearings (bronze bushings).
  3. Make a tailstock with a sliding center.
  4. Make a tool rest or a sliding carriage (slide rest).
  5. Cut a lead screw by hand with a file and a guide, then use that screw to cut better screws.
  6. Power from a water wheel through belts or an enchanted rotation rune.
- **Bootstrapping note:** The first lead screw is cut by hand and is inaccurate. Each lathe generation makes a better screw for the next. This is a good story beat (precision compounds).

### Motor (non-electric)
- **Water wheel:** Wooden frame and buckets or paddles. Needs a stream or a millrace. Drives a shaft with gears or belts. Output is steady but site-bound.
- **Windmill:** Sails on a rotating cap. Needs wind. Less steady.
- **Treadle or hand crank:** Portable, low power. Use a flywheel to smooth output.
- **Steam engine:** Boiler, piston, cylinder, valves. Needs bored cylinders (lathe and boring bar), pressure-safe boiler plate (rolling mill and riveting), and good seals. A major leap and a good late-story milestone.
- **Magic motor:** Rotation rune or an elemental (wind or water) bound to a shaft. Mana source decides runtime. A mana crystal battery can store energy.

### Press
- **Screw press:** Cut a large screw and a nut (wood or iron). A lever turns the screw to drive a platen down. Used for stamping, pressing sheet, and gluing.
- **Hydraulic press:**
  1. Bore a thick-walled cylinder (cast iron or forged bronze) on a lathe.
  2. Make a piston with leather cup seals (a soaked and shaped leather ring that seals better as pressure rises).
  3. Make a small pump cylinder and piston with a lever handle.
  4. Connect the two with a pressure pipe. Add check valves (ball and seat).
  5. Fill with water or oil.
  6. Build a heavy frame to contain the force.
  - A small pump piston area against a large ram area multiplies force. Output force equals pressure times ram area.
  - **Hard parts:** Sealing, strong castings, and a frame that does not fail.
- **Magic version:** A force enchantment on a platen or a bound earth elemental pressing down.

### Rolling mill
- Two hardened iron rolls, geared together, with adjustable spacing by screws. Power from a water wheel. Pass heated bar through repeatedly, tightening the gap each time.

### Tilt hammer
- A heavy hammer head on a pivoting arm. A cam on a water wheel shaft lifts the tail and lets it fall. Replaces a striker.

### Suggested story pacing

1. Basic forge and hand tools (Tier 1)
2. Knife and spoon-class goods (cutting and hand tools)
3. Drawplate and wire (whisk, mesh, rivets)
4. Sand-cast skillet and pot
5. Grindstone and pole lathe
6. Balance scale and measures
7. Water wheel and line shaft
8. Tilt hammer and rolling mill (sheet pan, saucepan, colander)
9. Metal lathe (precision parts, thermometer, blender)
10. Screw press and then hydraulic press
11. Optional: steam engine

Each step unlocks better output on the previous steps. Magic can compress or skip steps at the cost of mana and skill.
