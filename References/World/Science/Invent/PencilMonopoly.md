# Pencil Monopoly

> Earth invent loot, scored **up** for Caldris-style merchant guilds (active protection, 50/50, magical patent registry, allied-market blacklisting). Not locked Caldris law. ## Short Answer

Smaller than the match at first, but steadier, more prestigious and, with strong merchant guilds behind it, able to climb into the same dynasty tier. The pencil's market is limited to people who write, draw or measure. In the medieval period that is a thin slice of society. In the Renaissance, with cheap paper, printing, double-entry bookkeeping and an explosion of art and engineering, the market grows every decade. Guild distribution, stationer networks and crown letters patent turn that growth into locked recurring sales across allied cities. Under Earth-alone patent terms it is a solid merchant fortune. Under Caldris guild terms it becomes a major one in one kingdom and can rival banking-house wealth across several realms as literacy rises.

## What It Competes Against

| Existing tool | Weakness the pencil exploits |
|---|---|
| Quill and ink | Needs ink, a pot, a knife for trimming and drying time. Useless while traveling, on horseback or in the rain. |
| Wax tablet and stylus | Bulky, erases with heat, cannot be stored as a record |
| Leadpoint / silverpoint | Faint marks, needs a prepared surface |
| Chalk and charcoal | Smudges badly, crude lines |

The pencil wins on portability, clean dry marks and erasability. Bread crumbs erased graphite long before rubber erasers (1770).

## Who Buys

- **Merchants and clerks:** Field notes, tallies, inventories at docks and markets where ink is impractical.
- **Clergy and scholars:** Annotation, drafts, marginalia. Universities alone supply steady demand.
- **Artists:** Renaissance workshops sketched constantly. Pencil drawing becomes a premium market.
- **Builders and craftsmen:** Masons, carpenters and shipwrights marking measurements on materials.
- **Military and state:** Surveyors, engineers, cartographers, navigators keeping logs at sea.

## Rough Numbers (England, c.1500)

| Factor | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Regular writers and drafters | ~100,000 (clergy, clerks, merchants, students, craftsmen) |
| Use | ~6 pencils per year each |
| Price | ~2d each (early pencils were not cheap) |
| Revenue | ~1.2 million pence, about £5,000/yr |

That is roughly a quarter of the match estimate for the same kingdom on Earth alone. The advantage is growth. The user base expands steadily from 1450 onward as printing spreads literacy, and premium artist and drafting lines carry high margins. Across Western Europe the figure multiplies 15 to 20 times, and doubles again within a century or two as literacy rises.

**Guild uplift (Caldris):** Partner stationers already sell paper, ink and ledgers. Bundling pencils into every clerk kit, school pack and merchant satchel raises effective buyers and annual use well above the lone-inventor table. Allied-market blacklisting keeps prices from collapsing when copycats appear. A realistic guild-backed band for one kingdom sits closer to **half to two-thirds of the match** once paper and schooling boom, not a permanent quarter.

## The Real Moat: Graphite Plus Guild

A bare patent is weak on Earth. Venice's 1474 statute granted only 10 years. England's later standard was 14. Copying a wooden case around a writing core is trivial once seen.

The true control point is the core, and Caldris guilds amplify both:

- **Raw graphite (Earth):** The Borrowdale deposit in Cumbria, found in the 1560s, was the only known source of pure solid graphite in Europe. It was so valuable that the mine was opened only periodically to restrict supply, guarded by armed men, and theft of graphite became a felony by Act of Parliament in 1752. Whoever owns that mine owns the pencil market.
- **Process secret:** Impure powdered graphite existed elsewhere (Bavaria, Bohemia). Nicolas-Jacques Conté's 1795 method of mixing graphite powder with clay and firing it broke England's monopoly overnight. An inventor who discovers this process early holds a second, independent monopoly that does not depend on any single mine. Holding both is the strongest position possible.
- **Caldris path:** No large native deposit to claim. Guild control of **wood-to-graphite synthesis** (and later a toxin-free Ned-spike alternate core) replaces the mine. Process secrecy plus magical patent registry plus compartmentalized workshops replaces Earth's weak paper patent. Detail: `GuildPatents.md`.

## Strategic Complication

Graphite also lined crucibles and molds for casting cannonballs. That makes the raw material a military asset. The crown will want control of supply regardless of pencils. As with the match, the profitable path is a partnership: the crown and partner smiths secure military graphite while the inventor and stationers hold civilian manufacturing and distribution. Strong merchant guilds make that bargain easier. Kings need the tax stream and the loans; seizure costs them both.

## Risks

- **Short patent terms (Earth):** Protection lapses fast without secrecy or resource control. **Caldris offset:** magical registry, staged follow-on patents and guild blacklisting stretch the effective moat for decades.
- **Copycats:** Nuremberg had a pencil-making industry by the 1660s once the idea spread. Expect imitators within years. **Caldris offset:** confiscation and market bans from every allied guild city hurt harder than Earth lawsuits.
- **Guild opposition:** Scriveners, stationers and quill sellers have political weight in towns. **Caldris offset:** those guilds are the partners. Quill sellers outside the deal are the ones who lose.
- **Timing:** Before cheap paper (14th to 15th century in most of Europe), parchment users are few and wealthy. Inventing it in 1200 yields far less than inventing it in 1450. Guild paper improvements accelerate the good timing.

## Bottom Line

| Scope | Outcome (Earth alone) | Outcome (strong merchant guilds) |
|---|---|---|
| Patent only, no material control | Brief windfall, then commodity | Longer premium window; still weak without process lock |
| Patent plus graphite mine / synthesis | Wealthy family with crown favor | Partner guilds richer than most noble houses within a generation |
| Mine / synthesis plus clay-firing process, multiple kingdoms | Dynastic fortune that grows with literacy | Banking-house tier; can approach match ceiling as literacy and schooling boom |

The match sells a faster version of something everyone has. The pencil sells a better tool to a smaller group that keeps growing. The match makes more money sooner. With guild engines behind distribution and enforcement, the pencil compounds longer and can catch the match's wealth tier over decades.
