# Perfect Glider: Design B "Falcon-scale"

Hub: [PerfectGlider.md](PerfectGlider.md).

One person, 100 kg total, wings about the size of a superhero backpack rig.

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Wingspan | 3.5 m |
| Root chord / tip chord | 0.67 m / 0.53 m |
| Wing area | 2.1 m² |
| Aspect ratio | 6 |
| Total mass | 100 kg (pilot and gear) |

## Two pilot configurations

| Config | Drag area (with tail) | Glide ratio, Ideal | Glide ratio, Real | Best-glide speed | Min sink (Real) | Stall clean | Stall with flaps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exposed pilot, streamlined suit | 0.123 m² | 8.4 | 8.0 | 95 km/h | 3.0 m/s | 81 km/h | 70 km/h |
| **Pilot inside thin rigid shell** | **0.033 m²** | **15.0** | **14.2** | **127 km/h** | **2.2 m/s** | **81 km/h** | **70 km/h** |

For scale, a wingsuit glides at about 2.5 to 3. The exposed version is roughly three times better than a wingsuit. The shelled version matches a hang glider. Joints matter least here because the wing has only a few sleeves.

## Hard limits of small wings

- **No foot landing.** Running to a stop works below about 25 to 30 km/h. That takes ~16 m² of wing at 100 kg. This wing stalls at 70 to 81 km/h.
- **Landing options:**
  - **Water landing** is the most survivable.
  - **Ballistic parachute.**
  - **Belly skid in the rigid shell** on smooth grass, sand or snow. Stopping from 70 km/h over 20 m averages about 1 g. Peak loads from bumps, a dug-in wingtip or a tumble run several times higher and are hard to predict. Rough ground makes it dangerous. This is a rough estimate, not a settled figure.
- **Launch needs 80+ km/h airspeed:** a cliff or tower dive of ~25 to 35 m, a horse or winch tow, or strong ridge wind.
- **Thermals:** Circling sink is 2.7 to 3.7 m/s shelled and 3.7 to 5.0 m/s exposed. Only strong desert thermal cores give any climb.
- **Ridge lift:** Strong trade winds on steep cliffs give 1 to 3 m/s. Enough to hold height in the shelled version only.
- **Human power is impossible.** Level flight needs 2.9 kW shelled or 3.8 kW exposed. A trained cyclist sustains ~250 W.
