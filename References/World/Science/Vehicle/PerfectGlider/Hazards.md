# Perfect Glider: Practical Limits and Hazards

Hub: [PerfectGlider.md](PerfectGlider.md).

- **Joints:** Grit jams sleeves. Unseated tapers leak air and add drag. Every flight starts with checking each joint is seated.
- **Deployment in wind:** A partly extended 25 to 31 m wing twists and lifts unevenly. Deploy in light wind, into the wind.
- **Clear space:** Fully deployed, Design A needs ~16 m of clear space on each side and Design C ~13 m.
- **Gusts and roll:** Design C cannot hold its wings level at best-glide speed against moderate thermal edges. It must fly faster in rough air. Design A needs wing ballast for comfortable rough-air handling.
- **Contamination:** Rain and insects on the leading edge cut glide ratio by 20 to 40%. Laminar flow is fragile.
- **Landing speeds:** Design A at full ballast lands at 93 km/h even with flaps. Design B always lands at 70+ km/h. Design C lands at a run.
- **Dynamic soaring:** Harvesting wind shear has pushed a 3.3 m radio-controlled glider to 882 km/h (548 mph, 2021). Design B is close to that size. The technique requires flying within meters of a ridge or wave tops. It is the one way Design B could cover long distances unpowered. It is extremely dangerous for a person aboard.
- **Navigation:** Downwind island-to-island, steering by Mauna Kea and Haleakalā. Both summits usually rise above the morning clouds.
