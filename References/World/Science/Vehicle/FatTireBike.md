**Best fit: a 29 to 36 inch wheel fat-tire bike with the frame sized to the rider's body.** Raw power stops being the limit almost immediately. The road surface, traction and parts strength set the ceiling.

## Why that size

- **Big wheels (29 to 36 inch)** roll over ruts, roots, cobbles and potholes far better than small ones. Rolling resistance on rough ground drops as wheel diameter grows.
- **Wide tires (3 to 4.8 inch)** float over mud, sand and soft dirt, which describes most medieval roads outside a few paved Roman-style routes.
- **Going past about 36 inches backfires.** The center of gravity rises, the rider can't step over or dismount fast, turning in narrow streets and gateways gets clumsy, and a wheel caught in a rut throws the rider forward (the penny-farthing problem).
- **Frame size follows the body, not the power.** A bigger frame adds nothing except leverage against the rider.

## Where superhuman output actually caps out

- Aero drag needs about 1,100 W at 60 km/h and about 2,600 W at 80 km/h. A superhuman covers that easily.
- Dirt traction (friction around 0.5 to 0.6) limits acceleration, braking and cornering no matter how strong the legs are.
- A realistic safe pace is 25 to 35 km/h on rutted tracks and 50 to 70 km/h on good paved roads. Reflexes and sightlines decide the upper end of that range.

## Build changes the power requires

- Motorcycle-grade chain and hubs, since a standard bike chain snaps under superhuman torque
- Very tall gearing so the rider isn't spinning out at speed
- Heavy-gauge steel frame that a village smith can repair
- Strong brakes, since stopping on loose ground takes much longer than speeding up
- Full suspension, or at least a suspension fork, to keep the tires on the ground over broken surfaces
- Solid or heavily puncture-proofed tires where no rubber supply exists
