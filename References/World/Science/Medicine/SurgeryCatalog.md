# Surgery Catalog

Design loot. Setting assumptions: `MedievalSurgery.md`. Kit: `MedievalSurgeonKit.md`. Liver detail: `LiverSurgery.md`. Hub: `Index.md`.

Rating key:

- **A**: Routine and reliable with this kit.
- **B**: Feasible; significant risk; benefits clearly outweigh risk.
- **C**: Heroic; only for otherwise fatal conditions.

## Wounds, Skin and Soft Tissue

### Wound debridement and closure (A)
- **For**: lacerations, battle wounds, bites.
- **How**: irrigate with large volumes of boiled saline; cut away dead and contaminated tissue until it bleeds; remove foreign bodies; close clean wounds in layers; leave dirty wounds open with packing and close in 3 to 5 days.
- **Magic**: suction irrigation; barrier holding edges.
- **Watch**: tetanus (no vaccine; thorough debridement is the defense), gas gangrene in deep crushed muscle.

### Abscess incision and drainage (A)
- **For**: boils, carbuncles, breast abscess, perianal abscess, deep muscle abscess.
- **How**: incise over the fluctuant point, break internal walls (loculations) with a finger, suction pus, irrigate, pack loosely with linen wick.
- **Watch**: never close an abscess cavity.

### Excision of cysts and lipomas (A)
- **How**: elliptical skin incision; blunt dissection around the capsule (cysts and lipomas shell out along an areolar plane); remove whole to prevent recurrence.

### Skin grafting (A to B)
- **For**: burns, large wounds, ulcers.
- **How**: split-thickness graft shaved from the thigh with a very sharp blade at uniform thickness (magic knife ideal); meshed by small slits to spread and drain; laid on clean granulating bed; pressure dressing; donor site heals in about 2 weeks.
- **Full-thickness grafts** from behind the ear or groin for face and hands.
- **Magic**: barrier pressure dressing ensures contact (grafts die if fluid lifts them).

### Escharotomy (A)
- **For**: circumferential full-thickness burns of a limb or chest choking circulation or breathing.
- **How**: longitudinal cuts through the burned dead skin down to fat (painless through full-thickness burn).

### Fasciotomy for compartment syndrome (A)
- **For**: crush injury, fracture, reperfusion causing tense swollen compartment, pain on passive stretch.
- **How**: long incisions opening all compartments (lower leg has four: two incisions); leave open.

### Flap reconstruction (B)
- **Z-plasty** for scar contracture: lengthens a scar band by transposing two triangular flaps.
- **Rotation and advancement flaps** for defects after tumor removal.
- **Forehead flap rhinoplasty** (Sushruta, ancient India) for nose loss: forehead skin rotated down on its blood supply.
- **Italian arm flap** (Tagliacozzi, 16th century) as alternative.

### Excision of skin cancers (A)
- **How**: margin of normal skin (about 4 mm for basal cell; 1 to 2 cm for melanoma depending on depth); close or graft.

### Pilonidal sinus excision (A)
- Excise sinus tracts to fascia; leave open or close with an off-midline flap.

### Ingrown toenail (A)
- Remove the lateral nail edge and destroy the matrix under it (phenol not available; excise or cauterize the matrix).

## Head and Neurosurgery

### Burr hole for epidural hematoma (B)
- **For**: head blow, lucid interval, then decline; dilating pupil on side of the bleed; weakness on opposite side.
- **How**: incision over the temple on the side of the dilated pupil; trephine through the skull; clot sucked out; bleeding middle meningeal artery tied or cauterized; enlarge with rongeurs if needed.
- **Magic**: suction is essential; magic knife or trephine through bone.
- **Watch**: one of the most rewarding operations: a dying patient can recover fully.

### Acute subdural hematoma evacuation (C)
- Larger skull flap (craniotomy); open dura; suction clot; bleeding bridging veins controlled. High mortality from underlying brain injury.

### Chronic subdural hematoma (B)
- Elderly patient, weeks after minor fall, confusion. Two burr holes, irrigate the liquid "motor oil" collection out.

### Depressed skull fracture elevation (B)
- Burr hole beside the fracture; lever fragments up; remove contaminated pieces; repair dura.

### Brain abscess drainage (B)
- Burr hole and aspiration.

### Hydrocephalus (C)
- Ventricular drainage possible; long-term shunt needs tubing and a valve. Barrier magic one-way valve is a fictional solution.

### Myelomeningocele closure (C)
- Closing the defect in newborns; long-term hydrocephalus follows.

## Eye

### Cataract extraction (B)
- **Medieval baseline**: couching (pushing the lens back into the eye with a needle); poor results, frequent blindness from inflammation.
- **Better**: extracapsular extraction (Jacques Daviel 1747). Incision at the edge of the cornea; open the lens capsule; express the lens nucleus; irrigate the remaining cortex; close the corneal wound with very fine silk or let it seal.
- **Postoperative**: aphakic vision requires strong convex spectacles (about +10 diopters); glass lenses are within medieval reach.
- **Magic**: magic knife for a clean self-sealing corneal incision; barrier keeps the eye still.

### Trichiasis / entropion surgery (A)
- **For**: trachoma (endemic in many premodern societies) scars the eyelid, turning lashes inward to scratch the cornea, causing blindness.
- **How**: bilamellar tarsal rotation: incise across the eyelid, rotate the lash margin outward, suture.

### Pterygium excision (A)
- Remove the fleshy growth from the cornea; cover with a conjunctival graft to prevent recurrence.

### Enucleation (A)
- Remove a ruined, painful or infected eye.

### Chalazion incision and curettage (A)

### Removal of corneal foreign bodies (A)

### Glaucoma iridectomy (B)
- For acute angle-closure: small opening in the iris to relieve pressure.

## Ear, Nose and Throat

### Tracheotomy (A)
- **For**: airway obstruction (diphtheria, swelling, tumors, facial trauma).
- **How**: neck extended; midline vertical incision below the cricoid; separate the strap muscles bluntly; divide the thyroid isthmus if needed; open the trachea between rings 2 and 3; insert a silver tube; secure with tape.

### Cricothyrotomy (A)
- Emergency: incision through the membrane between thyroid and cricoid cartilages; insert a tube. Fastest surgical airway.

### Tonsillectomy (A)
- Recurrent tonsillitis, peritonsillar abscess, airway obstruction in children. Grasp the tonsil; blunt dissection in the plane between tonsil capsule and the throat muscle; snare the base; pressure and ties for hemostasis.
- **Watch**: postoperative bleeding at days 5 to 10.

### Peritonsillar abscess drainage (A)

### Mastoidectomy (B)
- **For**: mastoiditis (ear infection spread into the bone behind the ear), common and lethal before antibiotics.
- **How**: incision behind the ear; open the mastoid cortex with gouge or drill; remove infected air cells; drain.
- **Watch**: facial nerve, sigmoid sinus, brain covering.

### Myringotomy (A)
- Small cut in the eardrum to drain middle ear pus.

### Nasal polypectomy (A)

### Foreign body removal from ear, nose, airway (A to B)
- Airway: rigid bronchoscope (a straight metal tube with a reflected light) plus suction.

### Septoplasty and nasal fracture reduction (A)

### Cleft lip repair (A to B)
- Infant around 3 months. Rotation-advancement flap (Millard): rotate the inner lip segment down; advance the outer segment into the gap; close muscle, skin and mucosa in layers.

### Cleft palate repair (B)
- Around 9 to 18 months. Mucoperiosteal flaps from both sides of the hard palate moved to the midline; soft palate muscles reconstructed for speech.
- **Watch**: bleeding into a baby's airway; suction is vital.

## Dental and Oral

### Tooth extraction (A)
- Elevators and forceps; surgical extraction by removing bone around impacted teeth.

### Dental abscess drainage (A)
- Incise over the swelling inside the mouth or under the jaw; extract the tooth or open the pulp chamber.

### Ludwig's angina drainage (B)
- Swelling under the jaw lifting the tongue; airway threat. Incisions under the chin and jaw; blunt dissection to open the spaces; tracheotomy if needed.

### Root canal treatment (B)
- Open the pulp chamber; remove pulp with fine files; irrigate; fill with a sealing material (gutta-percha is from Southeast Asian trees; beeswax, resin or metal as alternatives); seal the opening.

### Fillings (A)
- Remove decay with hand instruments or a bow drill; fill with gold foil (classic), silver-tin amalgam (silver, tin and mercury), or resin.

### Jaw fracture fixation (B)
- Wire the teeth of upper and lower jaws together with silver or steel wire (intermaxillary fixation) for about 6 weeks; liquid diet.

### Jaw dislocation reduction (A)
- Thumbs on the back lower molars, push down then back.

## Neck

### Thyroidectomy (B)
- **For**: large goiters compressing the airway (common in iodine-deficient inland regions); thyroid overactivity.
- **How**: collar incision; separate strap muscles bluntly along the midline; tie the superior thyroid vessels close to the gland (protect the external laryngeal nerve); tie the middle thyroid vein; find the recurrent laryngeal nerve in the groove beside the trachea (blunt dissection, never clamp blindly); preserve the parathyroid glands (small tan bodies behind the gland) with their blood supply; tie the inferior thyroid artery branches close to the gland; remove the lobe.
- **Watch**: bleeding into the neck compressing the airway (open the wound immediately); voice loss from nerve injury; low calcium (tingling, spasm) from parathyroid damage.
- **Prevention is better**: iodized salt or seaweed in the diet prevents most goiters.
- **Magic**: barrier around the recurrent laryngeal nerve once found; magic knife sealing vessels.

### Neck abscess drainage (A)

### Excision of thyroglossal duct cyst (B)
- Remove the cyst with the central part of the hyoid bone (Sistrunk) to prevent recurrence.

### Lymph node excision (A)
- Diagnosis of tuberculosis (scrofula) or tumors; drainage of tuberculous abscesses.

## Chest

### Chest tube (tube thoracostomy) (A)
- **For**: hemothorax, pneumothorax, empyema.
- **How**: incision in the 5th intercostal space just in front of the mid-axillary line; blunt dissection over the top of the rib (vessels and nerves run under each rib); puncture pleura with a clamp; finger sweep; insert tube toward apex for air, toward base for fluid.
- **Underwater seal**: tube end under water in a bottle so air escapes but cannot return. Suction attached gently helps re-expand the lung.
- **Magic**: barrier one-way valve.

### Needle decompression of tension pneumothorax (A)
- Large needle in the 2nd intercostal space mid-clavicular line or 5th space at the side; hiss of air; follow with chest tube.

### Thoracentesis (A)
- Needle drainage of pleural fluid.

### Empyema drainage and rib resection (A to B)
- Thick infected pleural fluid: resect a short segment of rib to place a large drain.

### Decortication (B to C)
- Peel the thick inflammatory rind off a trapped lung (blunt dissection along the plane between rind and lung) so it can expand. Requires intubation and ventilation or barrier lung support.

### Thoracotomy for penetrating chest trauma (C)
- **How**: incision through the 4th or 5th intercostal space; rib spreader; control lung bleeding by clamping the lung root or wedge resection; repair holes in lung with sutures.
- Requires positive pressure ventilation through an endotracheal tube with bellows, or barrier support of the lung.

### Cardiac stab wound repair (C)
- First successful suture of the heart: Ludwig Rehn, 1896.
- **How**: left anterior thoracotomy; open the pericardium in front of the phrenic nerve; release clotted blood (tamponade); finger on the hole; horizontal mattress sutures with pledgets of fabric to avoid tearing heart muscle; avoid coronary arteries.
- **Magic**: barrier patch on the hole buys time; suction clears the field.

### Pericardiocentesis (A to B)
- Needle from below the xiphoid aimed toward the left shoulder; aspirate fluid around the heart.

### Pericardial window (B)
- Small opening in the pericardium below the xiphoid draining recurring effusions (tuberculosis was a major cause).

### Lung abscess drainage (B)

### Lobectomy (C)
- For bronchiectasis, tuberculosis or tumors. Needs isolated lung ventilation; the hilar vessels are tied individually; bronchus stapled or oversewn. Bronchial stump leak is the main killer.

### Thoracoplasty (B to C)
- Historical tuberculosis treatment: remove several ribs to collapse the diseased lung. Deforming; now obsolete but within medieval capability.

### Mastectomy (B)
- Simple mastectomy: elliptical incision including the nipple; elevate skin flaps in the plane between breast fat and skin fat; remove the breast off the chest muscle fascia.

## Abdomen: General Principles

- Incision: midline for flexibility; right lower quadrant (McBurney) for appendix; right subcostal (Kocher) for gallbladder and liver.
- Bowel handled gently; wrapped in moist warm linen.
- Peritoneal contamination: wash out with many liters of warm boiled saline.
- Closure: fascia with strong continuous silk or linen with small bites close together (stitch length about four times wound length); skin closed or left open if contaminated.
- **Bowel anastomosis**: two layers. Inner layer: full-thickness running absorbable stitch (catgut). Outer layer: interrupted seromuscular silk stitches (Lembert stitch) folding the edges inward so smooth outer surfaces touch. Ends must be well perfused, tension free and clean.

## Abdomen: Procedures

### Exploratory laparotomy (B)
- For abdominal trauma or acute abdomen without a clear diagnosis.

### Appendectomy (A to B)
- **For**: appendicitis: pain moving from navel to right lower abdomen, fever, vomiting, tenderness.
- **How**: McBurney incision (oblique, one third of the way from the hip bone point to the navel); split muscles bluntly along their fibers (external oblique, internal oblique, transversus); open peritoneum; deliver the appendix; tie and divide the mesoappendix vessels; tie the base of the appendix; divide; invert the stump with a purse-string suture.
- **Perforated**: washout; drain any abscess; leave skin open.
- **Watch**: without surgery, appendicitis killed large numbers; this operation alone saves many lives.

### Perforated ulcer repair (Graham patch) (B)
- Sudden severe upper abdominal pain, rigid board-like abdomen. Close the hole in the stomach or duodenum with sutures and tie a tongue of omentum over it; wash out the abdomen.

### Gastrojejunostomy (B)
- Bypass for blocked stomach outlet (scarred ulcer, cancer): join a loop of jejunum to the stomach.

### Partial gastrectomy (C)
- Billroth I (1881): remove the lower stomach; join stomach to duodenum.
- Billroth II: close the duodenal stump; join stomach to jejunum.
- Stomach cancer or bleeding ulcer.

### Gastrostomy (A to B)
- Feeding tube placed into the stomach through the abdominal wall for patients who cannot swallow.

### Small bowel resection and anastomosis (B)
- Gangrenous bowel from strangulated hernia, volvulus, trauma or tumor. Clamp, resect to healthy bleeding bowel, tie mesenteric vessels, anastomose in two layers, close the mesenteric gap.

### Adhesiolysis for bowel obstruction (B)
- Divide scar bands that kink bowel; blunt and sharp dissection.

### Volvulus detorsion (B)
- Sigmoid volvulus (common in older and constipated patients): untwist; resect if gangrenous; colostomy if contaminated.

### Intussusception reduction (B)
- In infants: squeeze the telescoped bowel back out from the far end (never pull the near end); resect if dead.
- Non-surgical first attempt: air or saline enema under pressure.

### Colostomy and ileostomy (B)
- Bring a bowel end out through the abdominal wall to divert stool. Life-saving after colon perforation, obstruction or trauma. Collection: waxed linen or leather pouch with a barrier seal.

### Colon resection (C)
- Tumors, diverticular disease. Anastomosis of colon leaks more than small bowel (lower blood supply, more bacteria). Diverting stoma for safety.

### Cholecystectomy (B)
- First performed by Carl Langenbuch, 1882.
- **For**: gallstones with repeated pain, inflammation, jaundice.
- **How**: right subcostal incision; retract liver up; grasp gallbladder fundus; blunt dissection in Calot's triangle (cystic duct, common hepatic duct, liver edge) until only two structures enter the gallbladder: cystic duct and cystic artery (critical view of safety); tie and divide each; separate the gallbladder from the liver bed by blunt and sharp dissection; hemostasis of the bed.
- **Watch**: common bile duct injury (a disaster); bleeding from the right hepatic artery; bile leak.
- **Magic**: suction and barrier retraction of the liver make visualization far better.

### Cholecystostomy (A to B)
- Severely ill patient with infected gallbladder: open the gallbladder, remove stones, sew in a drain tube. Safer than full removal when inflamed tissue makes anatomy unclear.

### Common bile duct exploration (C)
- Stones blocking the duct with jaundice: open the duct; remove stones with scoops and irrigation; T-shaped tube drain.

### Splenectomy (B)
- **For**: ruptured spleen after trauma; massive spleen from malaria or blood diseases.
- **How**: upper midline or left subcostal; mobilize the spleen by dividing its ligaments (splenorenal, splenocolic, splenophrenic); bring it forward; tie short gastric vessels (careful of stomach wall); tie the splenic artery and vein at the hilum separately; avoid the tail of the pancreas.
- **Splenorrhaphy** (repair) for small tears preserves immune function.
- **After splenectomy**: lifelong risk of overwhelming infection by encapsulated bacteria. No vaccines available; prompt treatment of any fever.

### Liver procedures (B to C)
- Hepatorrhaphy, resectional debridement, perihepatic packing, wedge resection, left lateral sectionectomy, left and right hepatectomy, hydatid cyst surgery, abscess drainage. See `LiverSurgery.md`.

### Pancreatic pseudocyst drainage (B)
- A fluid collection after pancreatitis: sew the cyst wall to the back wall of the stomach (cystgastrostomy) so it drains internally.

### Pancreatic necrosis debridement (C)
- Remove dead infected pancreas by blunt finger dissection; extremely high mortality.

### Paracentesis (A)
- Needle drainage of abdominal fluid (ascites) through the lower abdomen.

## Hernia

### Inguinal hernia repair (A to B)
- Common; strangulation kills.
- **Bassini repair** (1887): open the inguinal canal; separate the hernia sac from the cord structures by blunt dissection; tie the sac at its neck; stitch the conjoint tendon and transversalis fascia to the inguinal ligament behind the cord to rebuild the back wall.
- **Shouldice repair**: multilayer overlapping repair of the transversalis fascia with continuous suture; very low recurrence.
- **Mesh** (Lichtenstein): not possible without sterile inert mesh. Woven silver wire mesh is a historical option (used early 20th century) but causes problems.
- **Watch**: strangulated hernia needs urgent surgery and bowel resection if dead.

### Femoral hernia repair (B)
- Below the inguinal ligament; high strangulation rate. Close the femoral canal with sutures between inguinal and pectineal ligaments.

### Umbilical hernia repair (A)
- Close the fascial defect with overlapping sutures (Mayo vest-over-pants).

### Incisional hernia repair (B)

## Anorectal

### Hemorrhoid banding or ligation (A)
- Internal hemorrhoids tied at their base with a tight silk ligature or a small ring (above the dentate line, so painless).

### Hemorrhoidectomy (A)
- Excision of external and internal components, leaving skin bridges between excisions to prevent narrowing.

### Anal fistula: seton or fistulotomy (A)
- Ancient Indian and Greek technique. Probe the tract; lay it open if it involves little sphincter; otherwise thread a silk seton through it and tighten over weeks to cut slowly while scarring behind.

### Anal fissure: lateral internal sphincterotomy (A)
- Small cut in the internal sphincter to relieve spasm.

### Rectal prolapse repair (B)
- Perineal approach: remove the prolapsed segment and join the ends; or abdominal fixation of the rectum to the sacrum.

### Imperforate anus (newborn) (B to C)
- Simple low forms: open the membrane; complex forms: colostomy then later reconstruction.

## Urology

### Urethral catheterization (A)
- Silver catheter (historical) or a waxed linen or gut tube.

### Suprapubic cystostomy (A)
- Bladder drainage through the lower abdomen when the urethra is blocked: needle confirms urine; small incision; tube into bladder.

### Bladder stone removal (A to B)
- Bladder stones were extremely common in medieval times (diet, dehydration), especially in children.
- **Historical perineal lithotomy**: high complication rate (incontinence, fistula, impotence).
- **Better**: suprapubic cystolithotomy: open the bladder from above; remove stones; close in two layers with catgut; catheter drainage.

### Kidney stone removal (pyelolithotomy) (C)
- Flank incision; open the renal pelvis; remove stone; close.

### Nephrectomy (C)
- Destroyed infected kidney (tuberculosis, stones with pus) or tumor. Flank incision; tie the renal artery then vein then ureter.

### Prostatectomy (C)
- Urinary retention from enlarged prostate. Suprapubic approach (Freyer): open the bladder; finger enucleation of the prostate adenoma (classic blunt dissection: the finger finds the plane between the enlarged gland and its compressed capsule and shells it out); pack and drain; heavy bleeding is the main danger.

### Urethral stricture: dilation and urethrotomy (A to B)

### Circumcision (A)
- Phimosis, recurrent infection.

### Hydrocele repair (A)
- Open the tunica vaginalis; evert it behind the testis (Jaboulay) or plicate it (Lord).

### Testicular torsion detorsion and orchidopexy (A)
- Sudden severe testicular pain in a young male: untwist within hours; fix both testes to the scrotum with sutures.

### Undescended testis (orchidopexy) (B)
- Infant or young child: free the testis and cord from the inguinal canal; fix it in the scrotum.

### Orchiectomy (A)
- Testicular tumor, ruined testis.

### Vasectomy (A)
- Small scrotal incision; divide and tie the vas deferens.

## Obstetrics and Gynecology

### Cesarean section (B)
- The operation with the largest potential impact on maternal survival in a premodern world.
- **How (lower segment)**: lower midline or transverse incision; open peritoneum; incise the loose peritoneum over the lower uterus and push the bladder down bluntly; transverse incision in the lower uterine segment, opened bluntly with the fingers (less bleeding than a knife cut); deliver the baby and placenta; clear the cavity; close the uterus in two layers with catgut (Max Sänger 1882 showed closing the uterus was essential; before that most women bled or became infected and died).
- **Watch**: hemorrhage, infection, uterine rupture in later pregnancies.
- **Magic**: barrier protecting bladder; suction; autotransfusion.

### Postpartum hemorrhage control (B)
- Uterine massage; bimanual compression; manual removal of retained placenta; uterine packing; B-Lynch compression suture (sutures wrapped around the uterus squeezing it); tie the uterine arteries (O'Leary stitch); hysterectomy as last resort.
- **Magic**: barrier compression of the uterus or aorta (aortic compression against the spine stops pelvic bleeding).

### Repair of perineal tears (A)
- Layer-by-layer closure including the anal sphincter when torn.

### Vesicovaginal fistula repair (B)
- From prolonged obstructed labor. J. Marion Sims (1849, with silver wire). Separate bladder from vagina; close each in layers without tension; catheter drainage for 2 weeks.

### Dilation and evacuation / curettage (A)
- Retained products after miscarriage or birth (a major cause of maternal sepsis and bleeding).

### Ectopic pregnancy surgery (B)
- Ruptured ectopic pregnancy (sudden abdominal pain, collapse, missed period): open; clamp the bleeding tube; remove the tube (salpingectomy). Autotransfusion of the abdominal blood with suction is very effective here.

### Ovarian cyst removal / oophorectomy (B)
- Ephraim McDowell (1809) removed a 10 kg ovarian tumor without anesthesia or antisepsis; the patient lived 30 more years.

### Hysterectomy (B to C)
- Fibroids with severe bleeding, uterine cancer, prolapse, uncontrolled postpartum bleeding. Ureters run close to the uterine arteries: identify them first.

### Vaginal hysterectomy for prolapse (B)

### Symphysiotomy (B)
- Obstructed labor where cesarean is impossible: divide the pubic symphysis cartilage to widen the pelvis. Historical alternative.

### Cervical cerclage (A)
- A purse-string suture around the cervix in women who lose pregnancies from cervical weakness.

## Orthopedics and Trauma

### Closed fracture reduction and casting (A)
- Traction to restore length and alignment, then plaster of Paris cast (gypsum heated to drive off water; mixed with water it sets hard; Mathijsen 1852).

### Traction (A)
- Weights and pulleys for femur fractures (Thomas splint, 1870s: drastically reduced death from femur fractures in WWI).

### Dislocation reductions (A)
- Shoulder: external rotation technique, scapular manipulation, Kocher maneuver.
- Elbow: traction and flexion.
- Hip: Allis maneuver (traction upward with knee bent).
- Fingers, kneecap, ankle.

### Amputation (A to B)
- **How**: plan flaps (long posterior flap below knee); cut skin and muscle with the knife; separate the periosteum; saw bone; smooth edges; tie each major artery and vein individually (Ambroise Paré revived ligatures in the 16th century instead of cautery); cut nerves short under gentle traction so they retract; close muscle over bone, then skin over muscle.
- Guillotine amputation (straight cut, left open) for heavily infected limbs, revised later.
- **Magic**: barrier tourniquet; magic knife through bone.

### Open fracture debridement and stabilization (B)
- Wash, debride, remove dead bone fragments, stabilize with external fixation, leave the wound open.

### External fixation (B)
- Steel pins drilled into bone above and below the fracture, connected by an external bar with clamps. A blacksmith can build this. Avoids leaving metal in the wound.

### Open reduction and internal fixation (C)
- Plates and screws of sterile metal. Medieval metals: iron corrodes; bronze and copper are toxic; silver and gold are soft but inert; carefully made steel may work for a few months. Infection risk is the barrier. Barrier-magic sterility and pressure sterilization make it more feasible.
- **Cerclage wiring** with silver wire is simpler: wire loops around spiral fractures.

### Intramedullary nailing (C)
- A rod down the shaft of femur or tibia (Küntscher, 1939). Needs precision reaming.

### Tendon repair (A to B)
- Core suture technique (Kessler) through both tendon ends; fine running suture around the edge; early protected movement.

### Nerve repair (B)
- Align the bundles; fine sutures through the outer sheath (epineurium) without tension. Recovery slow (about 1 mm per day).

### Osteomyelitis sequestrectomy (B)
- Chronic bone infection: remove dead bone fragments (sequestra), curette, pack open, allow healing from the base. Maggot therapy supports this.

### Septic arthritis drainage (A)
- Hot swollen joint with fever: open, wash out, drain. Untreated it destroys the joint in days.

### Clubfoot correction (A)
- Ponseti method: weekly serial casts gradually correcting the foot; small percutaneous Achilles tendon cut at the end; a brace with boots on a bar until age 4. Mostly non-surgical; transforms lives.

### Osteotomy for malunited fractures or deformity (B)
- Cut and realign bone; cast or fix.

### Excision arthroplasty (Girdlestone) (B)
- Removal of a destroyed infected hip head; leaves a mobile but short leg.

### Carpal tunnel release (A)
- Divide the transverse carpal ligament in the palm under direct vision.

### Trigger finger release (A)
- Divide the A1 pulley at the base of the finger.

### Dupuytren's fasciectomy (B)
- Remove the diseased cords in the palm with careful dissection around the digital nerves.

### Ganglion excision (A)

### Bunion correction (B)
- Osteotomy of the first metatarsal.

### Spine: drainage of tuberculous spinal abscess (Pott's disease) (B to C)
- Costotransversectomy: remove the rib head and transverse process to drain the abscess in front of the spine.

## Vascular

### Ligation of bleeding arteries (A)
- Proximal control above the bleeding point before attacking the wound.

### Arterial repair (B)
- Alexis Carrel (1902) triangulation technique: three stay sutures at equal intervals pull the vessel end into a triangle; each straight side is sewn with very fine silk on fine needles, all layers, smooth inner surface. Allows end-to-end repair or vein grafts.
- Vein graft from the saphenous vein (reversed so its valves do not block flow) to replace a damaged artery segment.
- **Magic**: barrier clamps above and below; suction.

### Aneurysm ligation (B)
- John Hunter (1785) tied the femoral artery above a popliteal aneurysm; collateral vessels kept the leg alive.

### Varicose vein ligation and stripping (A to B)
- Tie the great saphenous vein at the groin junction; strip with a wire passed through it; small incisions to remove side branches.

### Arteriovenous fistula repair (B)

### Embolectomy (B)
- Sudden cold white painful leg from a clot (common with irregular heartbeat): open the artery at the groin; remove the clot with forceps and suction (modern Fogarty balloon catheter unavailable; a balloon of animal bladder is a creative substitute).

## Miscellaneous Life-Saving Procedures

### Venous cutdown (A)
- Expose the saphenous vein in front of the ankle; tie in a cannula for fluids.

### Intraosseous access (A)
- Needle into the bone marrow of the shin (children) for fluids when no vein can be found.

### Bladder irrigation, rectal tubes, nasogastric tube (A)
- Stomach tube through the nose to decompress blocked bowel or feed.

### Lumbar puncture (A)
- Diagnosis of meningitis (cloudy fluid); relief of pressure in some cases.

### Snakebite and dog bite wound care (A)
- Debridement and irrigation; no incision-and-suction for snakebite (harmful). Rabies has no cure after symptoms; Pasteur's vaccine (1885) from dried infected rabbit spinal cord is theoretically within reach. Antivenom: `Antivenom.md`.

## Beyond Reach or Nearly So

| Procedure | Missing element |
|---|---|
| Organ transplantation | Immunosuppression; tissue typing; preservation solutions |
| Open-heart surgery (valve replacement, bypass) | Heart-lung machine; anticoagulation (heparin); artificial valves. Barrier magic pumping blood is a fictional solution |
| Joint replacement | Inert implant materials, bone cement, reliable sterility |
| Most cancer surgery with cure intent | Staging imaging; pathology with stains; chemotherapy and radiation backup |
| Major pancreatic resection (Whipple) | Possible technically; mortality without modern support likely over half |
| Laparoscopic surgery | Insufflation, cameras, fiberoptics |
| Microsurgery (finger reattachment, free flaps) | Operating microscope and very fine sutures (glass lenses can partially substitute) |
| Neurosurgical tumor removal | Imaging to localize; brain swelling control |

## What Kills the Patient and How This Kit Answers

| Killer | Medieval reality | Answer with this kit |
|---|---|---|
| Pain and shock during surgery | Speed was the only defense (amputation under a minute) | Ether anesthesia allows slow careful surgery |
| Hemorrhage | Cautery, crude ties | Ligatures, hemostats, Pringle-type control, barrier clamps, suction visibility, autotransfusion, crossmatched transfusion, saline |
| Infection | Pus considered normal ("laudable pus") | Handwashing, boiling, pressure sterilization, alcohol, iodine, barrier sterile field and gloves, debridement, drains, delayed closure |
| Aspiration during anesthesia | Common | Fasting, suction, positioning, intubation |
| Dehydration | Unrecognized | Saline IV, oral rehydration, rectal fluids |
| Poor visibility | Daylight only, blood-filled field | Suction, barrier retraction, mirrors, shielded lamps |
| Tissue damage from crude tools | Dull blades, crushing | Magic knives, forged fine instruments, blunt dissection along planes |
| Postoperative clots and pneumonia | Prolonged bed rest | Early walking, breathing exercises |
| Wound breakdown | Weak closure, malnutrition | Layered closure with proper suture spacing, nutrition |

The decisive advances are not the dramatic operations. They are handwashing, sterilization, ether, ligatures, suction, saline, crossmatched blood and debridement. With these in place, appendectomy, cesarean section, hernia repair, abscess drainage, bladder stone removal, burr holes, amputation and trauma laparotomy move from near-certain death to likely survival. Liver resection, gastrectomy and thoracic surgery become possible but remain dangerous. Barrier magic is most valuable as a perfect atraumatic clamp, a sterile field and a containment seal.
