# Biological Silks

Earth research anchors for Ned's silk (binding, lines, Bindweave). Map to Caldris; do not paste Earth species names into prose as if they are local. Companion: `../../../People/Ned.md`. Process pipeline: `Biomaterials.md`. Insect / silk craft notes: `RubberAndInsect.md`.

## Spider Silks

**Dragline (major ampullate)**
- Tensile strength: ~1.1–2.7 GPa
- Extensibility: ~30%
- Toughness: high, exceeds steel and Kevlar by weight
- Function: web frame, lifeline drop

**Darwin's bark spider silk**
- Toughest biological material measured
- Roughly double the toughness of most other spider silks

**Capture silk (flagelliform)**
- Very high extensibility, low strength
- Function: absorbs prey impact without snapping

**Egg case silk (tubuliform)**
- Stiff and strong
- Low extensibility
- Function: protection

## Moth and Caterpillar Silks

**Bombyx mori (domesticated silkworm)**
- Tensile strength: ~0.5–0.6 GPa
- Extensibility: ~20%
- Bred for yield and spinnability, not mechanical performance

**Tussah (Antheraea pernyi / A. mylitta)**
- Slightly lower tensile strength than Bombyx mori
- More extensible, often tougher overall

**Muga (Antheraea assamensis)**
- Golden color
- Strength and luster increase with washing
- Comparable to tussah

**Eri (Samia ricini)**
- Lower tensile strength
- Valued for durability and cottony texture over strength

**Caterpillar dragline (tent caterpillar, gypsy moth larvae)**
- Toughness approaching spider dragline silk
- Function: fall arrest when dropping from foliage

**Communal nest silk (fall webworm, tent caterpillar)**
- Low individual fiber strength
- Optimized for bulk and weatherproofing over tensile performance

## Other Biological Silks

**Bee and wasp larval silk**
- Reinforces and seals brood cells
- Not built for tensile load

**Caddisfly silk**
- Underwater adhesive, cures while submerged
- Strength not the priority

**Sea silk / byssus (Pinna nobilis)**
- Fine and lustrous
- Mechanically weak
- Now extremely rare

## Comparison to Synthetic Fibers

| Material | Tensile Strength | Extensibility | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kevlar | ~3.6 GPa | ~3–4% | 1.44 g/cm³ |
| Spider dragline silk | ~1.1–2.7 GPa | ~30% | 1.3 g/cm³ |
| Nylon 6,6 | ~0.5–0.9 GPa | ~20–40% | 1.14 g/cm³ |

Kevlar leads on raw strength. Spider silk leads on toughness due to high extensibility combined with strength. Nylon trails both but is cheap and easy to mass-produce.
