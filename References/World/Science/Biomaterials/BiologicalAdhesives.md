Natural dry, wet and capture adhesives for craft and Ned sticky targets. Related insect/latex stocks: `RubberAndInsect.md`. Silks and byssus fibers: `BiologicalSilks.md`. Harvest pipeline: `Biomaterials.md`. Nav: `Index.md`.

## Dry adhesion (no glue)

- **Gecko setae:** Millions of hairs split into nanoscale spatulae that bond by van der Waals forces. One seta holds about 200 µN. All setae engaged at once could theoretically hold about 130 kg. Fully reversible and self-cleaning.
- **Spider and anole lizard foot hairs:** Same van der Waals principle at smaller scale.

## Underwater permanent cements

- **Mussel byssus:** Foot proteins rich in DOPA (catechol) bond to almost any wet surface using metal coordination with iron and hydrogen bonding. A single DOPA bond to titanium holds about 0.8 nN. Plaques reach roughly 1 MPa.
- **Barnacle cement:** Multi-protein amyloid-like network that cures into a near permanent bond on rock, ships and whales.
- **Oyster cement:** Mostly calcium carbonate mineral bound with cross-linked proteins. Hard and rigid unlike the soft protein glues.
- **Sandcastle worm:** Oppositely charged proteins form a coacervate that sets in under 30 seconds when it hits seawater's higher pH.
- **Caddisfly larva silk:** Phosphoserine bridged with calcium glues stones and sticks into a case underwater.
- **Kelp holdfast:** Polyphenols (phlorotannins) cross-linked with alginate.

## Temporary or reversible wet adhesives

- **Sea star tube feet:** Two-gland system secretes glue then a separate de-adhesive to release.
- **Limpets:** Mucus combined with suction.
- **Tree frog toe pads:** Hexagonal cells and mucus channels give wet adhesion and friction.
- **Insect pads:** Beetles and flies use hairy pads with oily fluid. Ants and stick insects use smooth pads (arolia).

## Suction

- **Clingfish:** Microvilli-lined disc grips rough wet rock with forces well over 100 times its body weight.
- **Remora:** Lamellae with spinules lock onto sharks at speed.
- **Octopus suckers:** Muscular cup creates strong negative pressure.

## Defensive and capture glues

- **Spider aggregate glue:** Viscoelastic glycoprotein droplets on stretchy thread.
- **Velvet worm slime:** Protein fluid that turns into stiff fibers when stretched. Can redissolve in water and be re-spun.
- **Dusky slug mucus:** Tough double-network gel that inspired a surgical "tough adhesive."
- **Australian crucifix frog skin glue:** Protein glue that sticks in wet conditions. Tested for cartilage repair.
- **Salamander tail secretions:** Sticky defense against predators.
- **Sundew mucilage:** Highly stretchy polysaccharide trap.

## Microbial

- **Caulobacter crescentus holdfast:** Polysaccharide anchor measured around 68 MPa (68 N/mm²). Strongest natural adhesive per unit area ever recorded.
- **Bacterial biofilms:** Extracellular polysaccharides, proteins and DNA glue colonies to teeth, pipes and implants.

## Plants

- **English ivy:** Rootlets secrete nanoparticles of arabinogalactan protein.
- **Boston ivy:** Adhesive discs rich in pectin that survive decades on walls.
- **Mistletoe viscin:** Cellulose fiber glue that sticks to bark and skin.
- **Burdock burrs:** Mechanical hooks that inspired Velcro.
- **Tree resins and latex:** Sticky defensive exudates.

## Parasites

- **Tick cement:** Glycine-rich protein cone anchors mouthparts in skin for days.

## Body and human-harvested

- **Fibrin:** Blood clotting protein used as surgical sealant.
- **Collagen (hide and fish glue):** Classic woodworking glue.
- **Casein:** Milk protein glue.
- **Shellac:** Resin from lac insects.
- **Birch bark tar:** Used by Neanderthals to haft tools.

## Strongest by category

- **Raw strength:** Caulobacter holdfast
- **Underwater permanent:** Mussel and barnacle
- **Fastest underwater set:** Sandcastle worm
- **Reversible dry:** Gecko
- **Reversible wet:** Clingfish and sea star
