# Magic-Assisted Powder Metallurgy

> Shop gist: `CraftMetal.md`. Metals / conversion: `../../Materials/Metals.md`. Vacuum: `Vacuum.md`, `VacuumForcefieldHeat.md`. Invent ceiling: `../../Tech/Technology.md`.

With magic handling dies, compression, heat and vacuum, most Earth PM drawbacks vanish. Limits move upstream to **powder production** and **mage labor**.

## What magic removes

| Real-world PM con | Magic fix |
|---|---|
| Expensive hardened dies | Conjured dies cost mana. Mana-made mass disperses, so a die only needs to hold for one press |
| Press tonnage limits part size | Mana → kinetic force with little loss; size limited by pool, not machine frame |
| Residual porosity | Magic compression + heat ≈ HIP. Full density → wrought-level strength and fatigue |
| Oxide films on powder | Vacuum stops oxidation; clean surfaces bond directly |
| Uneven density in tall parts | Pressure from all sides at once, not only top/bottom punches |

**Vacuum welding is real physics.** Clean metal surfaces in vacuum fuse on contact (no oxide between atoms). Satellites seize this way. Au / Ag / Cu cold-weld readily. Fe / steel need more pressure and heat.

## What magic does not remove

**Powder still has to be made.** Usual route: melt → atomize (spray droplets). Melting moves upstream. Alternatives that skip melt:

- Mechanical grinding or kinetic shatter by magic (brittle OK; ductile metals smear)
- Electrolytic deposition (good for Cu, Ag)
- Solid-state reduction (bloomery sponge iron → crush)

**Purity.** Melting slag-cleans. PM keeps whatever was in the powder. Impure ore → impure parts unless purification is also magical.

**Labor.** Every part costs mage time and mana. T1 mage is the entry bar; runesmiths rarer. A coal furnace and steam hammer need no mage.

**Realistic split:** melt stays bulk (rails, boilers, wheels, plows, nails). Magic PM is for high-value parts where uniformity, complexity or impossible alloys pay for mage hours.

## Per metal

### Steel

- Best gain of the four. Fine even carbides at full density → blades/tools that out-hold and out-tough cast steel.
- Graded one-press: high-C powder at edge, low-C at spine → hard edge / tough body without forge weld.
- Small complex parts (locks, gears, clockwork) pressed near-final, little machining.
- **Canon burn:** casting *through* steel can burn from local spikes. Compaction/heat *acting on* the steel (not pathing through it) may dodge that. Aurium / insulator lining on a conjured die covers the case where it does not.

### Silver / mythril

- Cold-welds readily → least pressure/heat.
- High powder SA: mana hits every grain at once. For **mythril** conversion (`../../Materials/Metals.md`), powder saturates more evenly and faster than a solid bar (no surface-inward cook).
- Composites that will not mix as liquids: Ag + powdered arcanium/aetherium for mana channels (Earth analog: Ag–W contacts).
- Mana flows well in Ag → magical heating spreads evenly through a compact.

### Copper / aurium

- Full density removes Earth PM conductivity penalty → magic-PM Cu ≈ wrought Cu.
- Cu–W style composites for heat + extreme T (engines, airships, arc/fire focus).
- Porous bronze on purpose: stop short of full density, fill pores with oil / wax / Needle Worm lube → self-lubricating train/mill bearings.
- Bronze/brass by mixing Cu + Sn or Zn powders and sintering → skips Zn fume/loss of melt brass.
- Converted **aurium** powder would cook faster and more evenly than bar stock (same SA logic as silver).

### Gold / orihalcum

- Smallest gain. Gold melts clean, never oxidizes, alloys well → melt stays easy.
- Cold-welds so readily that magic compaction alone nearly finishes.
- Uses: binding other powders, gold-faced composites, fine detail; minimal loss matters on the dearest metal.
- **Orihalcum** powder still fights further conversion (MR = %); fine SA helps soak start, then stalls the same way as bar.

## Coinage

Coin dies are the big Earth mint cost and wear under striking. Conjured dies erase that; powder press in one step skips cast blanks + strike. Any skilled mage could mint → quality tracks the minter, not kingdom engravers. Counterfeiting gets easy unless official coin carries something a die cannot copy (enchantment, mana signature, precise alloy ratio checked by skill).
