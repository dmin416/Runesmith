**Laterite formation:** ultramafic parent (~0.2 to 0.3% Ni in olivine/serpentine) → tropical leach Mg/Si → Fe stays as goethite (limonite) → Ni reprecipitates downward in saprolite garnierite → 5 to 15× enrichment. Hawaiian basalt ~100 to 200 ppm Ni is too lean for laterite Ni ore.

## Industrial: Sulfide Ore

1. Mine (0.5 to 3% Ni) → crush/grind ~75 to 150 µm.
2. Flotation (xanthate + frother): pentlandite/chalcopyrite float → 6 to 20% Ni concentrate. Depress/reject pyrrhotite.
3. Smelt flash/EF ~1200 to 1300°C + silica → furnace matte 15 to 45% Ni+Cu. SO₂ → acid plant.
4. Convert (Peirce-Smith): air + silica → Bessemer matte ~70 to 75% Ni+Cu, <1 to 4% Fe, ~20% S.
5. Slow cool matte → crush → magnetic alloy (PGMs) → float Cu₂S from Ni₃S₂.
6. Refine (below).

**Orford (historical):** Cu-Ni matte + Na₂S → CuS in light "tops", NiS "bottoms".

## Industrial: Laterite

### RKEF (ferronickel / NPI)

Dry saprolite → rotary kiln calcine/reduce ~800 to 1000°C with coal → EF ~1500 to 1600°C → FeNi 15 to 40% Ni or NPI 2 to 15% Ni → ladle refine. Mainly stainless feed.

### HPAL

Limonite slurry + H₂SO₄ in Ti-lined autoclaves ~245 to 270°C, 40 to 55 bar, 60 to 90 min. >90% Ni/Co dissolve; Fe reprecipitates as hematite. Neutralize, CCD wash, Fe/Al at pH ~3 to 4. Precipitate MHP (MgO, pH ~7 to 8) or MSP (H₂S, pH ~2.5 to 3.5) → refine to battery chemicals or metal.

### Caron

Reduction roast ~700 to 750°C → ammonia + ammonium carbonate leach with air → Ni/Co ammines; Fe stays. Co sulfide out → steam strip → basic Ni carbonate → calcine to NiO. Recovery ~75 to 80%, high energy, simpler equipment.

### Atmospheric / heap leach

H₂SO₄ at ambient pressure. Slower, more acid, lower capital.

| Method | Feed | Purity | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbothermic | NiO | 97 to 99% | Simple; C and O remain |
| H₂ reduction of oxide | NiO | 99%+ | 400 to 600°C, sponge powder |
| Sherritt pressure H₂ | Ni ammine sulfate | 99.8%+ | ~175 to 200°C, 25 to 35 bar |
| Electrorefining (Hybinette) | Impure Ni or NiS anodes | 99.9%+ | Diaphragm bags, purified electrolyte |
| Electrowinning | Purified sulfate or chloride | 99.9%+ | ~3.5 to 4 kWh/kg |
| Mond carbonyl | Impure Ni or reduced oxide | 99.97%+ | Ni(CO)₄ ~50 to 60°C form, ~200 to 250°C decompose. Extremely toxic |
| Fire refining | Molten impure Ni | 99%+ | Oxidize to slag, deoxidize, cast |

### Electrowinning detail

Electrolyte: NiSO₄ (+ Na₂SO₄, boric buffer) or NiCl₂. ~55 to 65°C, pH ~3 to 4 (sulfate). Anodes: Pb alloy (sulfate) or coated Ti (chloride). Cathodes: Ni starter sheets or peelable SS/Ti. ~200 to 250 A/m², ~3 to 3.8 V. Harvest every several days.

### Mond outline

Reduce NiO → pass CO ~50 to 60°C → Ni(CO)₄ gas (Fe/Co slow; Cu/PGM no) → decompose on hot Ni pellets ~200 to 250°C → CO recycle. Avoid at small scale: delayed lethal lung injury at tiny exposures.

| Source | Ni content | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mantle xenoliths (dunite/peridotite) | Olivine ~0.3 to 0.4% NiO | Salt Lake Crater, Kaʻūpūlehu 1801, other alkalic vents | Richest local feed |
| Picrite olivine | ~0.3 to 0.4% NiO | Olivine-rich Kīlauea / Mauna Loa flows | Rock ~20 to 40% olivine |
| Green sand | ~0.25 to 0.4% NiO | Papakōlea and streaks | Protected / small |
| Tholeiitic basalt | ~100 to 200 ppm | Everywhere | Too lean |
| Old laterite soils | Low (basalt parent) | Kauaʻi, Oʻahu, Maui | Not Ni ore |
| Deep sea nodules | ~1.3% Ni | Clarion-Clipperton, far SE of Hawaii | Deep ocean tech |

Typical Hawaiian olivine: MgO 44 to 50%, SiO₂ 39 to 41%, FeO 9 to 15%, NiO 0.25 to 0.45%, MnO 0.12 to 0.20%, CoO ~0.015 to 0.02%. Yield at full recovery: ~2 to 3.5 kg Ni per tonne olivine. Mn ~half of Ni; Co ~1/20 of Ni.

### Collecting olivine

- Green glassy grains, Mohs 6.5 to 7, weakly magnetic (Nd yes, fridge no). Fresh better than iddingsite-rimmed.
- **Xenoliths:** fist to head-size nodules → crush → pick green grains (80 to 95% olivine).
- **Picrite:** crush to crystal size → LIMS out magnetite → gravity → Nd/electro for weak mags → hand pick pyroxene from olivine. Expect 20 to 40% of rock as olivine.
- **Beach (where legal):** scoop streaks, rinse salt, magnet, pan, brief dilute acid for shell.

| Product | Olivine % | Ni |
|---|---|---|
| Picrite rock | 20 to 40% | ~0.06 to 0.12% |
| Gravity + magnet concentrate | 80 to 90% | ~0.2 to 0.3% |
| Hand picked crystals | 95%+ | ~0.25 to 0.35% |

## Hydromet from Olivine

```
Olivine → grind → HCl leach → filter silica
         → oxidize Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ → pH 3.5–4 → Fe(OH)₃ out
         → H₂S at pH 2.5–3.5 → NiS+CoS (Mg, Mn stay)
            OR MgO to pH 7–7.5 → Ni/Co hydroxide
         → redissolve → remove Co/Mn → pure NiCl₂/NiO → metal
MgCl₂ liquor → pyrohydrolysis → MgO + HCl (recycle)
```

Per tonne olivine (approx): ~1 t HCl (100% basis), 30 to 60 kg Cl₂ oxidant, 5 to 10 kg H₂S (sulfide route). Products: 2 to 3.5 kg Ni, 0.1 to 0.15 kg Co, ~400 kg silica, ~450 to 500 kg MgO, ~100 to 150 kg iron oxide, ~1 to 1.5 kg Mn oxide.

### Steps

1. Grind to <75 to 150 µm.
2. Leach in stoneware/glass: 15 to 20% HCl, 10 to 20% excess, 80 to 100°C, 2 to 6 h. Avoid gel by steady concentration.
3. Settle, filter, wash silica (keep for glass/Si).
4. Oxidize Fe (Cl₂, air, or MnO₂). Raise pH 3.5 to 4 with MgO/shell/lime at 60 to 90°C. Filter Fe(OH)₃. Optional Al/Cr at pH 4.5 to 5.5.
5A. **Sulfide:** H₂S at pH 2.5 to 3.5, ~60 to 80°C → black NiS+CoS. Scrub exhaust.
5B. **Hydroxide:** MgO to pH ~7 to 7.5 → pale green mixed hydroxide.
6. Redissolve (acid ± Cl₂ for sulfides, or roast then acid).
7. Mn out: pH ~4 to 5 + mild oxidant → MnO₂ filter.
8. Co out: buffer pH ~3 to 4.5 with Ni(OH)₂ slurry + limited Cl₂/hypochlorite → Co(III) hydroxide first. Test: HCl warm blue on paper; thiocyanate acetone blue if available.
9. Products: NiCl₂ crystals, NiSO₄·6–7H₂O, Ni(OH)₂, basic carbonate, NiO (calcine 600 to 900°C).
10. **Acid recycle:** pyrohydrolyze MgCl₂ at ~500 to 800°C with steam → MgO + 2HCl. Without this, acid demand is enormous.

**Hydroxide precipitation pH (approx):** Fe³⁺ 2–3.5; Al³⁺ 4–5; Cr³⁺ 5–6; Cu²⁺ 5.5–6.5; Ni²⁺ 7–8.5; Fe²⁺ 7–8.5; Co²⁺ 7.5–8.5; Mn²⁺ 8.5–9.5; Mg²⁺ 9.5–10.5. Complete Fe oxidation before Ni precip is essential.

## Metal: Reduction, Melting, Testing

| Method | Notes |
|---|---|
| Carbon reduction | NiO + C, covered crucible 1000 to 1200°C → 97 to 99% sponge |
| H₂ reduction | 400 to 600°C in tube → 99%+ powder; passivate slowly; burn exit H₂ safely |
| Electrowinning | Cleanest: 3 to 4 V, 55 to 65°C, pH 3 to 4, ~2 to 3 A/100 cm² → 99.9%+ sheet |

**Melting:** 1455°C. Magnesia or high-alumina crucibles (clay-graphite adds C). Cover flux or Ar. Deoxidize with ~0.05 to 0.1% Mg or Mn before pour. Sulfur → hot shortness. Arc button same as Ti.

**Working:** hot forge 900 to 1200°C or cold roll/draw with anneals 700 to 900°C.

## Hazards

Ni dusts/oxides/sulfides: inhalation cancer risk. Ni salts/metal: contact dermatitis. Ni carbonyl: avoid Mond at small scale. H₂S, HCl, Cl₂: standard acid/gas controls. See also `HawaiianMinerals.md` and `TitaniumBootstrap.md` hazard tables.
