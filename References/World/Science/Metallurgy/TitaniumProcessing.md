## Fundamentals

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Symbol / atomic number | Ti / 22 |
| Atomic mass | 47.87 |
| Density | 4.51 g/cm³ (about 57% of steel, 1.7x aluminum) |
| Melting point | 1668°C |
| Boiling point | 3287°C |
| Crystal structure | Alpha (HCP) below 882°C, beta (BCC) above |
| Crustal abundance | About 0.57%, 9th most abundant element, 4th most abundant structural metal |
| Corrosion resistance | Excellent, thin self-healing TiO₂ film |
| Thermal conductivity | Low (~22 W/m·K), harder machining and welding |
| Magnetism | Nonmagnetic |

Titanium is common in the crust. It is expensive because it is hard to extract, not because it is rare.

### Ilmenite variations

- **Primary (hard rock):** often intergrown with hematite or magnetite. Lower TiO₂ (35 to 45%).
- **Weathered beach:** iron leaches out, TiO₂ rises toward 60 to 65%. Better feedstock.
- **Alteration sequence:** ilmenite → pseudorutile → leucoxene → secondary rutile.

### Harmful impurities

| Impurity | Problem |
|---|---|
| CaO, MgO | Liquid chlorides clog fluidized bed chlorinators |
| Cr₂O₃ | Colors pigment, contaminates product |
| V₂O₅ | Colors pigment, VOCl₃ boils near TiCl₄ |
| U, Th | Radioactive, regulated |
| SiO₂, Al₂O₃ | Consume chlorine, create waste |
| P₂O₅ | Pigment quality issues |

## Mining Methods

### Dredge (wet)

1. Flood a pond in the ore body.
2. Floating cutter suction or bucket wheel dredge cuts sand under water.
3. Slurry pumped to floating concentrator.
4. Pond migrates; tailings deposited behind.
- Best for: unconsolidated sand, high water table, low clay.
- Advantages: low cost per tonne, continuous, built-in water management.

### Dry mining

Strip topsoil → excavate → truck or push to mining unit plant → screen and slurry → pump to concentrator. Best for harder, clay-rich or irregular deposits, low water.

### Hydraulic

High pressure water monitors break ore faces into slurry (some dune / clayey deposits).

### Hard rock

Open pit drill/blast → jaw/gyratory then cone crush → rod/ball mill → magnetic and gravity, sometimes flotation.

### Rehabilitation

Tailings returned, topsoil respread and revegetated. Slimes (fine clay) dry slowly and are often the hardest part.

## Wet Concentration Plant (WCP)

**Goal:** raw ore 1 to 20% heavy minerals → HMC at 90 to 98% heavies.

1. Feed prep: trommel / screens remove oversize (~2 to 3 mm).
2. Surge bin.
3. Desliming: hydrocyclones remove <45 to 53 µm. Slimes to thickeners with flocculant.
4. Rougher spirals → cleaner → recleaner. Scavenger on rougher tails.
5. Upward current classifiers / teeter beds if coarse quartz contaminates.
6. Optional wet magnetic: LIMS for magnetite; WHIMS can pull ilmenite early.
7. HMC stockpile → MSP.

**Spiral:** slurry down helical trough. Heavies migrate inward, lights outward. Splitters take concentrate / middlings / tails. Feed ~25 to 40% solids. Effective ~0.045 to 1 mm.

## Mineral Separation Plant (MSP)

**Goal:** split HMC into ilmenite, rutile, leucoxene, zircon (± monazite, garnet, kyanite, staurolite).

Dry processing required: electrostatic only works on dry, hot grains. Surface coatings must come off first.

1. Attrition / wash (sometimes hot acid or caustic scrub).
2. Dry to ~100 to 150°C.
3. High tension roll (HTR): conductors (ilmenite, rutile, leucoxene, magnetite) vs nonconductors (zircon, monazite, garnet, quartz, kyanite, sillimanite, staurolite).

**Conductor circuit:** magnetic split (ilmenite vs rutile/leucoxene) → clean each with HTR / plates / gravity. Leucoxene vs rutile by slight magnetism and conductivity.

**Nonconductor circuit:** wet gravity removes quartz → magnets pull monazite, garnet, staurolite → zircon acid leach and final HTR/magnetic. Monazite (Th) under radiation licensing.

**HTR:** grains on grounded rotating steel roll; corona 20 to 40 kV. Conductors lose charge and fly off; nonconductors pin and brush off later. Humidity and temperature matter.

| Product | TiO₂ | Key limits |
|---|---|---|
| Chloride ilmenite | 58 to 62% | Low CaO, MgO, Cr₂O₃ |
| Sulfate ilmenite | 46 to 54% | Acid soluble, low Cr |
| Natural rutile | 94 to 96% | Low Zr, U+Th |
| Leucoxene | 70 to 91% | Variable |
| Premium zircon | 66%+ ZrO₂+HfO₂ | Fe₂O₃ under 0.05%, TiO₂ under 0.1% |

## Upgrading Ilmenite

Ilmenite has too much iron for most chloride plants.

### A: Slag smelting

AC/DC EAF, ~1650 to 1700°C. FeTiO₃ + C → Fe + TiO₂ + CO. Slag 75 to 80% TiO₂ (sulfate) or 85 to 87% (chloride). Pig iron tapped separately. UGS: oxidize, reduce, pressure acid leach → 94 to 95% TiO₂, low Ca/Mg. Energy ~2,000 to 2,500 kWh/t slag.

### B: Becher (synthetic rutile)

Weathered ilmenite (~60% TiO₂) + coal, rotary kiln 1100 to 1200°C → metallic Fe in grains → cool sealed → screen/magnets → aerate in water with 1 to 2% NH₄Cl (rust out Fe) → cyclones → dilute H₂SO₄ leach → synthetic rutile 90 to 93% TiO₂, porous.

### C: HCl leach (Benilite and similar)

Partially reduced ilmenite + hot HCl. Acid regenerated by pyrohydrolysis. Product 92 to 95% TiO₂.

| Route | Product TiO₂ | Iron byproduct | Feed needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slag smelting | 75 to 87% | Pig iron | Most ilmenite types |
| UGS | 94 to 95% | Pig iron | Slag |
| Becher | 90 to 93% | Iron oxide | Weathered high-Ti ilmenite |
| HCl leach | 92 to 95% | Iron oxide | Most ilmenite types |

## Pigment (90 to 95% of Ti output)

### Sulfate process

Digestion with conc. H₂SO₄ (exothermic 150 to 220°C) → dissolve cake → scrap Fe reduces Fe³⁺ → clarify → cool out copperas (FeSO₄·7H₂O) → boil with seed → hydrous TiO₂ → wash/bleach → calcine 800 to 1000°C (anatase or rutile with seeds) → mill, coat (Al₂O₃/SiO₂/ZrO₂), micronize. Waste: 3 to 4 t dilute acid and iron sulfate per t pigment unless recycled.

### Chloride process

Chlorinate (next section) → distill TiCl₄ → oxidize TiCl₄ + O₂ → TiO₂ + 2Cl₂ at 1000 to 1500°C (AlCl₃ promotes rutile) → Cl₂ recycle → finish.

| Form | Use |
|---|---|
| Rutile pigment | Higher opacity, weathering, exterior paint, plastics |
| Anatase pigment | Softer, bluer white, paper, fibers, some cosmetics |
| Nano TiO₂ | Sunscreen, photocatalysis |

## Chlorination and TiCl₄

**Feed:** TiO₂ above ~85%, CaO+MgO under ~1.5%, grains ~100 to 300 µm.

**Chlorinator:** fluidized bed, refractory lined, ~900 to 1100°C (typically ~1000°C). Feed + calcined petroleum coke + Cl₂. Main: TiO₂ + 2Cl₂ + C → TiCl₄ + CO₂ (also CO path). Fe, V, Al, Si, Zr, Mn, Cr, Nb, Sn also chlorinate.

**Off gas:** cool/cyclones (FeCl₂/₃, MnCl₂ solids) → quench with cold liquid TiCl₄ → crude yellow/red liquid → scrub tail gas.

| Impurity | Boiling / sublimation | Removal |
|---|---|---|
| SiCl₄ | 57.6°C | Distillation (light end) |
| SnCl₄ | 114°C | Distillation |
| VOCl₃ | 127°C | Mineral oil, H₂S or copper → solid VOCl₂ |
| TiCl₄ | 136.4°C | Product |
| AlCl₃ | Sublimes ~180°C | Settles / bottoms |
| FeCl₃ | ~316°C | Bottoms |
| ZrCl₄, NbCl₅ | High | Bottoms |

**TiCl₄:** colorless when pure. MP −24.1°C, BP 136.4°C, density 1.73. Fumes in moist air: TiCl₄ + 2H₂O → TiO₂ + 4HCl. Historical smoke screens / skywriting.

## Reduction to Metal

### Kroll (industrial standard)

Steel retort in electric furnace, argon/helium. Mg excess 15 to 30%. Melt Mg (~800 to 850°C), drip TiCl₄: TiCl₄ + 2Mg → Ti + 2MgCl₂ (~−430 kJ/mol Ti). Hold ~800 to 950°C (above ~1000°C Ti alloys with Fe wall). Tap denser molten MgCl₂. Stop ~70% Mg consumed. Vacuum distill residual Mg/MgCl₂ at ~1000°C days. Cool under Ar. Jack out sponge cake, shear/crush/grade (wall shell Fe/Ni contaminated). Electrolyze MgCl₂ → Mg + Cl₂ recycle.

Per tonne sponge (approx): 3.9 to 4 t TiCl₄, ~1 t Mg (recycled), ~4 t MgCl₂.

| Element | Typical premium limit |
|---|---|
| Oxygen | Under 0.07 to 0.10% |
| Iron | Under 0.03 to 0.05% |
| Nitrogen | Under 0.01% |
| Chlorine | Under 0.08% |
| Hardness | Brinell ~90 to 120 (lower = purer) |

Leach variant: dilute HCl instead of vacuum distill. Cheaper, more Cl and H in sponge.

### Hunter (historical)

TiCl₄ + 4Na → Ti + 4NaCl. Leach NaCl. Last major plants closed 1990s. Bootstrap path: `TitaniumBootstrap.md`.

### Van Arkel / de Boer (iodide crystal bar)

Impure Ti + I₂ ~200 to 400°C → TiI₄ vapor → decomposes on hot W filament ~1300 to 1700°C. Ultra pure bars, tiny output.

### Emerging / alternatives

| Process | Principle | Status |
|---|---|---|
| FFC Cambridge | TiO₂ cathode in molten CaCl₂ ~900°C | Pilot / small commercial powder |
| Armstrong | TiCl₄ vapor into flowing liquid Na | Small commercial powder |
| Calcium reduction | TiO₂ + 2Ca → Ti + 2CaO | Powder, O hard to remove |
| HAMR | Mg reduction of TiO₂ under H₂ | Research / pilot |
| Molten oxide electrolysis | Direct electrolysis of molten Ti oxide | Research |

### Aluminothermic (ferrotitanium only)

Ilmenite/rutile + Al + Fe → FeTi + Al₂O₃ slag. Cannot make pure Ti. FeTi (~30 or 70% Ti) deoxidizes/denitrides steel. Also melted from Ti scrap + Fe.

## Melting, Alloying, Mill Products

1. Blend sponge + scrap + alloys (Ti-6Al-4V: Al + Al-V master).
2. Press briquettes → weld under Ar into consumable electrode.
3. **VAR:** DC arc electrode to water-cooled Cu crucible in vacuum. Double VAR industrial; triple VAR rotating grade. Removes volatiles (Mg, Cl, H).
4. **Cold hearth:** EBCHM (electron beam, removes hard alpha and HDI, up to 100% scrap) or PAM (plasma, keeps volatile Al).

| Defect | Cause | Removal |
|---|---|---|
| Hard alpha (Type I) | N/O rich particles, sponge fires | Cold hearth dissolves |
| HDI | WC tool bits, Mo in scrap | Sink in cold hearth |

**Castings:** investment with yttria/zirconia face coats. Vacuum arc or induction skull. Alpha case (brittle O-rich surface) chemical milled off.

**Primary processing:** beta forge (~1050 to 1150°C for Ti-6Al-4V) → alpha-beta work → billet/bar/plate/sheet/strip/foil/tube/wire/extrusions → anneal / STA → grit blast + HNO₃-HF pickle.

**Powder / AM:** HDH (angular), gas atomized (spherical), plasma atomized. LPBF, EBM, DED, WAAM.

**Fabrication:** sharp carbide, low speed, flood coolant (low k concentrates heat). Galls easily. Weld with full inert coverage:

| Weld color | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bright silver | Good |
| Light straw | Acceptable |
| Dark straw / blue | Marginal to reject by spec |
| Purple, gray, white, flaky | Contaminated, reject |

## Why Ti Is Hard (Low Tech)

Carbon smelting fails: TiC / oxycarbides form; hot Ti absorbs O/N from air; MP 1668°C; molten Ti attacks almost every crucible.

| Technology level | Achievable |
|---|---|
| Ancient to early modern | Pigments, abrasives, curiosities |
| Late 1700s to 1800s | Identify element, impure powders, lab TiO₂ |
| Early 1900s | Ferrotitanium, lab Hunter Ti, TiCl₄ smoke, early pigment |
| 1940s to 1950s | Industrial sponge, VAR, aerospace alloys |
| Modern | Cold hearth, powders, AM, direct electrolysis |

**Minimum for Kroll:** Cl₂ (brine electrolysis), Mg (MgCl₂ electrolysis or Pidgeon), petroleum coke, high-grade TiO₂, corrosion-resistant chlorinator, argon (cryogenic ASU), vacuum + leak-tight steel at 1000°C, water-cooled Cu crucibles and DC for VAR.
