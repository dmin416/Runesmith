# Arrowheads

Physical heads for arrows and bolts (not runic Fire Arrow scrolls). Forge / cast routes, flight vs durability and steel-cast optimism. Metal work: `StructuralForge.md`, `Brass.md`. Era: `../../Tech/Technology.md`.

## Narrative

Bodkins punch armor. Broadheads cut flesh and sail in the wind. Shape picks the job. Steel heat-treat beats soft iron. Bronze casts easy and work-hardens at the edge.

## Detail

### Forge chain (one head)

1. Heat bar bright orange-yellow  
2. Cut 2–3 in slug  
3. Draw point, flatten blade  
4. Form tang or socket (socket: wrap wing on mandrel, forge-weld / close)  
5. File. Steel: critical (non-magnetic) → quench → temper straw/purple. Iron: often tip-steeled or case-hardened  

### Types

| Type | Shape | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Bodkin | Square / diamond, no edges | Mail and padding. Low drag, true flight, strong per gram |
| Broadhead | Flat leaf / lozenge | Unarmored, horses, hunt. Big wound. Planes in wind |
| Barbed | Swept barbs | Holds in flesh. Extra drag |
| Swallowtail | Forked | Cuts rope / rigging, bleeds. Needs good fletching |
| Blunt / round | No point | Small game pelts. Short range |
| Trilobate | Three-wing bronze | Strong section, decent pierce. Cast-friendly |

### Flight vs durability

- Compact bodkin: best flight and bend resistance per gram  
- Wide broadhead: planing past ~**40 yards** without bigger fletching  
- Balance: about **10–15% FOC** (front of center). Heavier head kills speed/range  
- Thin blades/barbs chip or bend on bone/armor. Soft iron straightens. Hard steel holds edge or chips if left too hard  

### Materials

| Stock | Note |
|---|---|
| High-carbon / spring (1075, 1084, 1095, 5160 class) | Best. Same HT logic on bloomery/crucible Caldris steel |
| Iron / mild | Cheap, soft. Hunt / target. Case-harden tips |
| Bronze | Cast + hammer edges. Corrosion OK. Thick low-stress shapes |

**Do not cast iron heads** for impact. Too brittle. Shatter.

### Shop shortcuts

- Stock removal from 1/8 in flat / spring → file → HT (fast tanged heads)  
- Square nail / bolt → quick bodkin  
- Barrier-cut blanks then grind (Caldris stand-in for laser)  
- Sheet roll + braze → socketed broadhead  

### Time / fuel (order of magnitude, charcoal + blast)

| Method | Hands-on / head | Fuel / head |
|---|---|---|
| Forge, experienced | 5–10 min | 0.3–0.6 kg |
| Forge, beginner | 30–60 min | ~1 kg |
| Stock removal | 15–30 min | ~0 (HT batch ~30 min / 10) |
| Bronze cast (batch) | 3–5 min | 0.2–0.4 kg |
| Steel cast (batch) | 10–15 min finish share | 0.3–0.5 kg |

### Optimistic steel casting

Needs ~**1550–1600 °C** crucible furnace, clay-graphite/SiC crucible, borax cover, ~**0.1%** Al or FeSi deoxidizer, ceramic shell or graphite molds preheated **800–900 °C**. Feed high-carbon scrap (~1.0–1.2% C melts lowest ~1400–1450 °C).

After pour: normalize 2–3× at ~900 °C → optional light forge → grind → harden **800–820 °C** oil quench → temper **200–300 °C** (bodkins prefer tougher temper).

~**1 kg** melt → ~**20** heads after sprue. Yield **70–90%** when dialed. Graphite mold cycle ~**30 s** pour once hot.

**Cast-then-anneal iron route:** white cast iron heads packed anneal ~**950 °C** for a day+ → malleable, straighten-able (Han tool path). Not as good as HT steel bodkins.

### Practical picks

- Most durable: forged/ground 5160 or 1075 bodkin, tempered  
- Easiest first: stock-removal broadhead  
- Best cast: bronze trilobate socketed
