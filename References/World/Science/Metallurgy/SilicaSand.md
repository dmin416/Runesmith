## Silica Fundamentals

**Silica** is silicon dioxide (SiO₂) in any form. Quartz is only one form.

| Form | Structure | Stability / origin | Density (g/cm³) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alpha quartz | Crystalline, trigonal | Stable below 573°C at surface pressure | 2.65 |
| Beta quartz | Crystalline, hexagonal | 573 to 870°C | 2.53 |
| Tridymite | Crystalline | 870 to 1470°C, found in volcanic rocks | 2.26 |
| Cristobalite | Crystalline | 1470 to 1713°C, found in volcanic rocks and calcined silica | 2.33 |
| Coesite | Crystalline | High pressure (impact sites, deep subduction) | 2.92 |
| Stishovite | Crystalline | Very high pressure (meteor impacts) | 4.29 |
| Chalcedony | Microcrystalline fibrous quartz | Low temperature, fills cavities | 2.58 to 2.64 |
| Chert / flint | Microcrystalline quartz | Sedimentary | 2.6 |
| Opal | Amorphous, hydrated (SiO₂·nH₂O) | Low temperature, springs, cavities, diatoms | 1.9 to 2.3 |
| Diatomite | Biogenic opal | Diatom skeletons | Bulk very light |
| Fused silica / silica glass | Amorphous | Man made, or natural lechatelierite (lightning, impacts) | 2.2 |

### Quartz alpha to beta inversion

At 573°C quartz changes structure with a sudden volume change. This cracks ceramics and foundry molds if heating or cooling crosses it too fast.

### Why quartz dominates continental sand

- Hardness 7 resists abrasion.
- No cleavage, so grains do not split easily.
- Chemically stable in weathering.
- **Goldich stability series** (least to most resistant): olivine → pyroxene → amphibole → biotite → feldspars → muscovite → quartz.
- Long transport and repeated recycling destroy everything except quartz, producing mature sands.

### Sand maturity

| Sand type | Composition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Quartz arenite | 90%+ quartz | Highly mature, long transport, ideal silica sand |
| Arkose | 25%+ feldspar | Near granitic source, short transport |
| Greywacke / lithic sand | Rock fragments, clay matrix | Rapid deposition near mountains or volcanoes |
| Volcaniclastic sand | Volcanic glass, crystals, rock fragments | Volcanic islands and arcs |
| Carbonate sand | Shell, coral, algae | Tropical reefs and shelves |

## Quartz Sand vs Silica Sand

| | Quartz Sand | Silica Sand |
|---|---|---|
| Type of term | Mineralogical | Commercial / industrial grade |
| Meaning | Sand made mostly of quartz grains | Sand meeting a high SiO₂ specification |
| Typical SiO₂ | Can be anywhere from about 60 to 99%+ | Usually 95% or more |
| Composition | Quartz plus whatever else is present | Mostly quartz, may include chert, chalcedony or opal |
| Defined by | What the grains are | Purity, size and performance tests |
| Usage | Geology, landscaping, general | Glass, foundry, fracking, filtration, ceramics, chemicals |

Nearly all silica sand is quartz sand. Not all quartz sand is silica sand. "Silica sand" means it passed a purity and quality bar for an industrial use.

**Related terms:** industrial sand, frac sand, glass sand, foundry sand, play / masonry sand, Ottawa sand / Northern White (St. Peter and Jordan sandstones, US Midwest).

## Specifications by Market

### Glass

| Glass type | SiO₂ | Fe₂O₃ max | Other limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amber / green container | 98%+ | 0.3% | Lenient |
| Clear (flint) container | 99%+ | 0.035 to 0.04% | Cr₂O₃ very low |
| Float (window) glass | 99%+ | 0.05 to 0.1% | Consistent chemistry |
| Low iron solar / optical glass | 99.5%+ | 0.01 to 0.015% | Ti, Cr, Co, Ni very low |
| Fiberglass | Varies | Moderate | Fine grind |

**Glass defects from bad sand:** stones (unmelted chromite, zircon, kyanite, sillimanite), color (iron green, chromium green, titanium with iron yellow-brown), seeds (bubbles from carbonate or organics).

**Grain size for glass:** typically 0.1 to 0.6 mm. Too fine causes dust and foam. Too coarse melts slowly.

## Processing Flowsheet

1. **Mining:** open pit, dredging, or ripping friable sandstone. Some need blasting.
2. **Primary crushing / disaggregation:** break weakly cemented sandstone without fracturing grains.
3. **Scalping:** remove oversize rock, roots, debris.
4. **Log / blade washing:** break clay balls, scrub heavy clay.
5. **Desliming:** hydrocyclones remove clay and silt (typically below 75 µm).
6. **Attrition scrubbing:** 70 to 80% solids, opposed pitch impellers. Grain-to-grain rubbing strips iron oxide and clay coatings. Deslime again.
7. **Hydraulic classification:** upward current classifiers or hydrosizers.
8. **Gravity:** spirals remove heavy minerals.
9. **Magnetic separation:** low intensity drums for magnetite; WHIMS or rare earth rolls for ilmenite, garnet, biotite, hematite, iron stained grains, tourmaline.
10. **Froth flotation** when needed:
    - Iron oxide / heavy mineral float: petroleum sulfonate, acidic pH.
    - Mica float: amine collector in acid.
    - Feldspar float: traditionally HF activator with amine at pH 2 to 3. HF-free systems use mixed anionic/cationic collectors in sulfuric acid.
    - Quartz stays as the sink product.
11. **Acid leaching** (glass and high purity): hot sulfuric, hydrochloric or oxalic acid for iron coatings. Oxalic with UV or reducing agents works well for iron.
12. **Dewatering:** screens, cyclones, belt filters.
13. **Drying:** rotary or fluid bed, 150 to 400°C.
14. **Dry screening:** final size grading.
15. **Optional:** cooling, grind to silica flour, calcining.
16. **Storage and load out.**

### Contaminant removal

| Contaminant | Removal |
|---|---|
| Clay, silt | Washing, desliming, attrition |
| Iron oxide coatings | Attrition, acid leaching |
| Heavy minerals | Spirals, magnetic separation |
| Magnetite | Low intensity magnets |
| Ilmenite, garnet | High intensity magnets |
| Mica | Flotation, screening (flakes) |
| Feldspar | Flotation |
| Carbonate (shell) | Acid leaching, gravity |
| Organics, lignite | Gravity, flotation, attrition |
| Chromite, zircon | Gravity, magnetic (chromite), electrostatic |

Quartz, chert and chalcedony are nearly identical in density and chemistry and are not separated in normal practice. Chert can crack in glass furnaces or fail frac crush tests. Opal (1.9 to 2.3) is lighter and can be removed by gravity. Opal and chalcedony cause alkali silica reaction in concrete aggregate.

## High Purity Quartz

- **Purity:** 99.99% (4N) to 99.999% (5N) SiO₂ or better.
- **Uses:** crucibles for silicon crystal growth, semiconductor equipment, optical fiber, quartz glass, lighting.
- **Source:** select pegmatites and hydrothermal veins (Spruce Pine, North Carolina is the best known), not ordinary sand.
- **Processing:** selective mining and optical sorting → crush and calcine, water quench → grind → multiple flotation → magnetic separation → hot HF/HCl leach → hot gas-phase chlorination → high temperature vacuum or controlled atmosphere treatment.
- **Hardest impurities:** Al, Ti, Li, Na, K substituting inside the lattice, plus fluid inclusions.

## Field and Bench Identification

| Test | Method | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Magnet (ferrite) | Drag fridge or ferrite magnet in a plastic bag through dry sand | Magnetite and titanomagnetite only |
| Magnet (neodymium N42 to N52) | Same, after removing magnetite | Ilmenite, garnet, some olivine, chromite, monazite |
| Acid fizz | Dilute HCl (or vinegar, slower) | Carbonate fizzes, silica does not |
| Streak | Unglazed porcelain | Distinguishes ilmenite, hematite, magnetite, rutile |
| Hardness | Scratch glass (~5.5) or steel | Quartz scratches glass, calcite does not |
| Panning | Gold pan | Heavy minerals stay as black sand |
| Specific gravity | Weigh in air and water | Density estimate |
| Hand lens / microscope (10x to 40x) | Grain shape, color, luster | Quartz glassy/clear, feldspar cleavage, glass conchoidal + bubbles |
| UV (shortwave) | Darkened room | Some zircon yellow-orange, scheelite blue-white |

### Bench toolkit

Sieve stack (10, 18, 35, 60, 120, 230 mesh), gold pan / sluice, ferrite and neodymium magnets in sealed bags, dilute HCl (~10%), SPT or LST heavy liquid, 0.01 g scale, hand lens / stereo microscope, drying oven or hot plate, beakers, wash bottles, filter paper.

### Simple home procedure for black sand

1. Wash and dry.
2. Pan to concentrate dark heavies.
3. Ferrite magnet: magnetite / titanomagnetite.
4. Neodymium on remainder: mostly ilmenite, garnet, chromite.
5. Remaining nonmagnetic heavies: rutile, zircon, possibly gold.
6. Acid-check the light fraction for carbonate.

## Hazards (silica)

Respirable crystalline silica (quartz, cristobalite, tridymite): silicosis, lung cancer. Wet methods, dust collection, respirators. US OSHA PEL 50 µg/m³ (8 hour), action level 25 µg/m³.
