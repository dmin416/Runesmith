## Does silver burn?

No. It is one of the least reactive metals. Heat melts it, then boils it. It does not sustain combustion in air.

## Key properties

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Melting point | 961.8°C |
| Boiling point | 2,162°C |
| Electrical conductivity | Highest of any element |
| Thermal conductivity | Highest of any metal |
| Visible reflectivity (fresh polish) | ~95%+ (best metal; mirrors, LED cups) |

## Heat

- Room temp to ~200°C: oxide only under strong oxidizers.
- ~200–300°C and up: Ag₂O decomposes back to Ag + O₂ (formation ΔG ~ −11 kJ/mol). Above that range silver stays bare and shiny.
- 961.8°C melts. 2,162°C boils. Arc or hot torch evaporates it rather than burning it.

## Molten silver and oxygen

Liquid silver dissolves up to ~20× its own volume of O₂. On freeze, solid holds far less, so oxygen bursts out through the skin (**spitting** / spratting). Melt under charcoal, a reducing flame, or flux to keep oxygen out.

## Tarnish

Tarnish is **sulfide**, not ordinary oxide:

`4Ag + 2H₂S + O₂ → 2Ag₂S + 2H₂O`

Sources: eggs, onions, rubber, wool, some paper/cardboard, polluted air. Film goes yellow → brown → black as it thickens. Same chemistry blacks silver-plated LED reflectors (`Lighting.md`).

## What attacks silver

| Agent | Result |
|---|---|
| H₂S and other sulfur | Ag₂S tarnish |
| Ozone | True oxide at room temperature |
| Chlorine | AgCl (darkens in light; old photography) |
| Fluorine | AgF, AgF₂ |
| Nitric acid | Dissolves: `3Ag + 4HNO₃ → 3AgNO₃ + NO + 2H₂O` |
| Hot concentrated sulfuric | Dissolves |
| Cyanide + oxygen | Dissolves (mining / plating) |

## Shop notes

- Best Earth mirror metal when polished; mythril inherits silver's path feel after conversion (`Metals.md`).
- Winding silver vs copper: tiny conductivity win, bad cost habit (`Generators.md`).
- Humidity + bias can grow silver dendrites between contacts (LED / electronics short).
