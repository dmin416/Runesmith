# Toxic Metal Substitutes and Disposal

Two approaches: swap the toxic ingredient out of the recipe, or remove and dispose of it safely when it is already present.

Companion to alloy recipes in `CommonAlloys.md`. Processing encyclopedia: `../../Materials/MaterialsProcessing.md`. Street stock gate: `../../Materials/Materials.md`. Brass / Zn detail: `Brass.md`.

**Status:** design loot. Earth shop and lab practice. Prefer substitutes when the beat does not need the toxic property.

## 1. Toxic-Free Substitutes

| Toxic element | Where it appears in the file | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead** | Lay pewter | Modern pewter: Sn 92, Sb 6, Cu 2 |
| **Lead** | 63/37 and 60/40 solder | SAC305 (Sn 96.5, Ag 3, Cu 0.5) or Sn-0.7Cu |
| **Lead** | Free-cutting brass C36000 | Silicon brass C69300 (Cu 75, Si 3, P 0.1, Zn bal.) or bismuth brass (Bi 0.5-1.8 in place of Pb) |
| **Lead** | 85-5-5-5 and bearing bronze | Tin bronze C90300 (Cu 88, Sn 8, Zn 4) or bismuth bronze |
| **Lead** | Statuary bronze | Silicon bronze (Cu 96, Si 3, Mn 1) |
| **Lead** | Babbitt, Bahnmetall | Tin Babbitt Grade 2 (Sn 89, Sb 7.5, Cu 3.5) |
| **Lead + cadmium** | Wood's metal, Rose's metal | Field's metal (Bi 32.5, In 51, Sn 16.5) or Bi 58 / Sn 42 (melts 138 °C) |
| **Cadmium** | Older silver solders (BAg-1 type) | Cadmium-free solders already in the file: BAg-7 and hard/medium/easy Ag-Cu-Zn |
| **Cadmium** | Zinc sacrificial anodes | Aluminum anodes (Al-Zn-In) or high-purity zinc anodes with no Cd |
| **Beryllium** | Beryllium copper | Cu-Ni-Si (C70250: Ni 3, Si 0.65, Mg 0.15), Cu-Ni-Sn spinodal (Cu 77, Ni 15, Sn 8) or aluminum bronze for non-sparking tools |
| **Mercury** | Dental amalgam | Composite resin, glass ionomer or milled ceramic (lithium disilicate, zirconia) |
| **Mercury** | Sodium amalgam (reducing agent) | Sodium borohydride, zinc dust or sodium on silica gel |
| **Arsenic** | Arsenical bronze | Standard tin bronze (Cu 90, Sn 10) |
| **Arsenic** | Admiralty and aluminum brass inhibitor | Antimony-inhibited (C44400) or phosphorus-inhibited (C44500) versions |
| **Nickel** (allergen) | Nickel white gold | Palladium white gold (Au 75, Pd 25) |
| **Barium carbonate** | Pack carburizing energizer | Sodium carbonate or calcium carbonate |
| **NaK** | Liquid metal coolant | Galinstan (Ga 68.5, In 21.5, Sn 10) for non-reactive liquid-metal uses |

## 2. Removing Toxic Metals Already in an Alloy

| Contaminant | Removal method | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Lead in gold or silver | Cupellation: melt on bone-ash cupel at ~1000 °C; lead oxidizes and soaks into the cupel | Used cupel becomes lead waste |
| Mercury in gold, silver or amalgam | Retorting: heat in a sealed steel retort at 400-500 °C, condense mercury vapor in a water-cooled tube | Never heat amalgam in open air |
| Dental amalgam waste | Amalgam separator in the suction line; scrap sent to a certified amalgam recycler | Required in US dental offices under EPA rule (2017) |
| Lead in brass, bronze or pewter | No practical small-scale removal; sell as segregated leaded scrap to a metal recycler | Industrial refiners use vacuum distillation or crystallization |
| Cadmium or arsenic | Boiling off or roasting releases toxic fume; not a home process | Send to a refiner or hazardous waste facility |

## 3. Cleanup and Disposal

**Lead, beryllium and cadmium dust**
- HEPA vacuum or wet wipe only. Dry sweeping and compressed air spread dust.
- P100 respirator for grinding, sanding or melting.
- Separate work clothes washed apart from household laundry.
- Wash hands and face before eating; no food or drink in the work area.
- Dross, slag and filings go in sealed containers labeled by metal, then to a household hazardous waste facility or scrap recycler.

**Mercury spill**
- No vacuum and no broom (both vaporize and spread it).
- Collect beads with an eyedropper or stiff paper, then cover remaining traces with sulfur powder or a commercial mercury spill kit.
- Seal in a glass jar; take to hazardous waste.

**Spent pickle and acid**
- Copper-laden pickle (sodium bisulfate or sulfuric acid): drop in clean steel wool to plate out the copper, neutralize with baking soda to pH 6-8, let the sludge settle. Liquid goes down the drain with plenty of water where local rules allow; copper sludge goes to hazardous waste.
- Nitric acid parting waste (contains silver nitrate): add copper scrap to precipitate the silver for recovery, then neutralize and dispose of the copper nitrate solution as hazardous waste.
- Aqua regia: neutralize slowly with sodium bicarbonate outdoors or in a fume hood; dispose as hazardous waste.

**Sodium and NaK**
- Small sodium scraps: add piece by piece to isopropanol in a fume hood, away from water, until fully dissolved; then dilute with water and neutralize.
- NaK: hazardous waste disposal only. The superoxide crust can detonate when disturbed.

**Zinc fume (brass melting, galvanized steel welding)**
- Local exhaust ventilation pulling fume away from the face.
- Never weld or cut galvanized steel without stripping the coating or using fume extraction.
