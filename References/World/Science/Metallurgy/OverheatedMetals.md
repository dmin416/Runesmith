# Overheated And Burned Metals

Earth metallurgy reference for craft and rune-heat beats.

## Rule of thumb

Low thermal conductivity: heat stays put. Thin edges, tips, and tapers burn first.

High thermal conductivity: heat spreads. The whole piece reaches danger together and fails with little local warning.

## Conductivity ladder (approx.)

| Metal | W/m·K | Melting | Overheat behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper (pure) | 385–400 | 1085 °C | Even heat. Sudden whole-piece slump. Sheds heat fast outside the fire. |
| Brass | 110–125 | 900–940 °C | Moderate spread. Zinc fumes near melt. |
| Bronze (tin) | 40–75 | 850–1000 °C | Edges and tips can melt locally. |
| Carbon steel | 45–55 | 1370–1510 °C | Localized. Thin sections burn before the body. |
| Stainless steel | 15–16 | 1400–1450 °C | Very localized. Thin sections overheat easily. |

## Steel

**Overheating (fixable):** Above about 1100–1200 °C grains grow coarse. Weak, brittle, glittery fracture. Normalize 2–3 times to restore fine grain.

**Burning (permanent):** Near the solidus, grain boundaries partly melt. Oxygen forms oxide between grains. Carbon burns out. High-carbon burns earlier (about 1150–1250 °C). No fix. Cut off or remelt.

**Signs:** White sparkler sparks, near-white glow, pitted or frothy surface, crumbles under the hammer.

## Copper

**Grain growth:** Orange-peel when bent. Fix with cold work then anneal. Heat alone does not refine.

**Burning:** Near melt, absorbs oxygen. Boundary oxide. Brittle.

**Gassing:** Oxide copper in hydrogen-rich fire makes steam inside and cracks boundaries. Oxygen-free copper resists.

**Signs:** Green or blue-green flame, thick black scale over red oxide, sudden sag, crack under hammer.

## Bronze

Little color warning. Can puddle with almost no visible change. Many tin bronzes are hot-short. Tin sweat / liquation weakens boundaries. Leaded bronze falls apart hot (lead melts at 327 °C). Prolonged heat oxidizes tin (white skin).

## Brass

Zinc boils at 907 °C. White zinc oxide smoke. Alloy shifts toward copper. Fume fever hazard. Porosity from vapor. High-zinc and leaded brasses crack under the hammer. Full process and fittings: `Brass.md`.

## Quick compare

| | Steel | Copper family |
|---|---|---|
| Warning | Color ladder, white sparks | Little warning, sudden slump |
| Damage start | Thin edges first | Whole piece (copper); edges (bronze) |
| Overheat grain fix | Normalizing | Cold work then anneal |
