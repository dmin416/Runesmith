## Lock conflict (read first)

This invent **prices a class / level crystal at about one year of common wages** (~**3.65 sg** at **1 ss/day × 365** on a **361-day** year calendar). The **live rewrite lock** in `Economy.md` is **2 sg**. **Do not overwrite `Economy.md`.** Treat the wage-peg crystal price and the temple **"no abuse"** rules below as **design proposals** for macro simulation and historical feel, not chapter law until promoted.

**Coin abbreviations in this invent:** **sc / ss / sg / lg** are the invent's shorthand. Map to project coins as **ss → SS**, **sg → SG**, **lg → LG**. Invent **sc** is a finer copper tick (**100 sc = 1 ss**), so **10 invent sc ≈ 1 LC** and **1,000 invent sc = 1 SG**. Project ladder: **1 SS = 10 LC**, **1 SG = 1,000 LC = 100 SS**, **1 LG = 10,000 LC**. Wage peg example: **1 ss/day × 365 ≈ 36,500 sc ≈ 3.65 sg**. Caldris year is **361** days; use **361** when converting to live calendars.

## 1. The Engine Under Everything

Terra's macro-economy runs on three stacked assets that are not interchangeable.

**Coin** is the token layer. It moves grain, rent, mercenary days, and temple candles. It is only as good as the institutions that mint, weigh, and accept it.

**Magic stones** (monster cores, refined arcanium / aetherium stock) are the **energy** layer. They fuel lamps, trains, siege engines, and rune dumps. Their street value tracks **volume and grade** more than the drama of the kill (`MonsterCores.md`, `ManaStones.md`).

**Crystals** (class-change / ascension crystals, progression capital) are the **power** layer. They unlock classes, tiers, and the status ladder everyone sees (`Progression.md`). A crystal is not consumed like a potion on every use, but the **first ascension** destroys the stone, and later reclass trials still demand fresh capital. Crystals therefore behave like **human capital stock**: scarce, hoardable, financeable, and politically dangerous.

### Crystal price ladder (design peg, not live lock)

These ratios express **how many years of unskilled labor** different power goods "feel like" when the wage peg is **1 ss/day** (~**10 sc** in the unskilled band).

| Asset (design peg) | sg | Labor-years at 1 ss/day | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trained horse (cheap mount band) | **0.3** | ~0.3 yr | Below a crystal; mobility not ascension |
| ~**2 m³** spatial bag (fair market mid) | **1.4** | ~1.4 yr | Logistics cheat; see `SpatialBagPrices.md` |
| Modest town house | **2.7** | ~2.7 yr | Brick-and-mortar capital |
| Class / level crystal (this invent) | **~3.65** | **~1 yr** | Wage peg; **live lock 2 sg** |
| Royal-grade crystal bundle / state reserve | **27** | ~27 yr | Dynasty stock, not a shop shelf item |

**Goblin-stone bridge math (design):** Street rice-grain peg **2 ss** per stone. At **~18 goblin stones per crystal** (ears, parts, and stone drops bundled at guild buy), a **~3.65 sg** crystal asks **~36 ss** of stone face value before split and fees. **~81 goblin kills** is the full-credit solo grind story (dry days, repair, party share, and non-stone loot tax). Neither ratio is law; they show why vermin farming **approaches** but rarely replaces temple or crown supply.

### What a class buys

A class is not a skill bundle alone. It is **legal identity in the status system**, access to guild rank gates, favored attribute growth on level-up, and narrative class cards (mana recovery, fire tolerance, scribe learning ease, and similar). Production and combat classes both pay the same crystal toll.

The **level curve** (`Progression.md`) makes overall level a separate grind from tier caps (T1 **25**, T2 **50**, T3 **75**, T4+ **100** each). Crystals open the **class door**; years of XP and risk open the **level rooms**. Macro-economically, that splits society into:

- **Unclassed labor** (cheap day wage, no ascension sunk cost).
- **Tier-1 holders** (one crystal sunk; capped low but still above pure labor for many jobs).
- **Multi-class careers** (several crystals over a lifetime; reclass at **25** levels minimum in the current class).
- **Tier-3+ elites** (decades of dungeon, patron stipends, or crown investment).

### Crystals are capital

Temples, crowns, and great houses treat crystals like **land deeds or war bonds**:

- They **lend** against future dungeon output.
- They **endow** orphanages and academies with a trickle of stones.
- They **gift** crystals to bind knights, runesmiths, or guild champions.
- They **seize** crystals as forfeiture when oaths break.

Because first-use ascension destroys the stone, **new entrants** always pull fresh crystals into the burn. That keeps upward demand alive even when high tiers stop reclassing.

### Who can afford (time to one crystal)

| Earner | Daily feel | Calendar time to ~3.65 sg (invent peg) | Calendar time to **2 sg** (live lock) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ditch / latrine wage (~**10 sc/day**, 15–25 days/mo) | Poverty band | **~10 years** if every coin saved | **~5.5 years** |
| Steady unskilled (**~1 ss/day** equiv.) | Common labor | **~1 year** | **~200 days** |
| Commoner household (**~4 ls/mo** income) | Shared budget | **~9–10 months** household surplus if nothing else | **~5 months** |
| Steel-rank vermin party (good week) | Variable | **~4 weeks** team gross → one crystal after split and repair | **~2–3 weeks** at lock price |
| Silver dungeon helper | Lumpy | Months to a year depending on stones | Same shape, faster |

Helci's orphanage arc (two years of work to buy a crystal) sits between latrine poverty and steady unskilled if she also keeps board and fees (`../../../People/Helci.md` path talk in world notes).

### Five temple "no abuse" rules (design proposals)

Faiths that sell crystals and healing (`World.md`, `ChurchPaidServices.md`) face a macro problem: if they dump crystals or undercut crowns, they **debauch class capital** and collapse adventurer wages. These five rules are invent **guardrails** temples claim to follow. Breaking them is a plot lever, not locked canon.

1. **Stable unit of account.** Temple crystal prices move only on feast-year councils, not on weekly spot markets. Prevents panic sales that would crash crown mint credibility.

2. **Coin credibility.** Temples accept royal coin at par; they do not run a parallel scrip unless a schism plot demands it. Debasement fights stay crown vs mint, not faith vs mint.

3. **Faith wealth into public goods.** A fixed share of crystal margin funds roads, orphanages, quelled corridors, and plague graves. Crowns tolerate church sales because the money visibly returns as **quelling** and **healing**.

4. **No predatory lending.** Crystal loans may not charge more than guild usury caps; forfeiture requires court or altar oath witness. Prevents mass peasant default → bandit class.

5. **Temple labor.** Every sold crystal pays a tithe of **cleric hours** (registration, trial witness, curse checks). Stops pure arbitrage where merchants buy temple wholesale and never staff the trials.

**Consequences when rules break:**

- **Rule 1 break:** Adventurer guilds strike; crown mint refuses temple deposits; black-market crystals appear with forged trial seals.
- **Rule 2 break:** Inflation in border towns; mercenaries demand payment in stones or bags, not sc.
- **Rule 3 break:** Monster corridors creep back; grain prices spike; faith legitimacy falls faster than tax rolls.
- **Rule 4 break:** Peasant revolts, forced conscription, crown seizure of church land.
- **Rule 5 break:** Trial fraud, hidden classes, status-hide scandals; Identify guild sells countermeasures at a premium.

### Stones are energy

Coins pay the baker. Stones pay the energy layer: rail haul, fort lamps, rune dumps, and craft furnaces. Large stones with ambient recharge feed locomotives; pouches of rice-grain stones do not (`MonsterCores.md`). Macro split:

- **Peasant economy:** coin + firewood + a little lamp stone.
- **Town economy:** coin + guild craft + small stones in shops.
- **Kingdom economy:** coin + **stone-fired rail** + dungeon loot recycling.
- **War economy:** stone dumps, mercenary coin, and **crystal grants** to champions.

## 2. How Goods Move

### Security as the binding constraint

Before ton-mile math matters, **quelled corridors** matter. A road is a line of sight through monster territory. Kingdoms invest in temples, patrols, guild posts, and dungeon contracts to keep **certain** routes **mostly** safe. Outside those corridors, von Thünen rings collapse into **survival rings** (see geography below).

War logistics fail when corridors flip. Grain still exists in the barn; it cannot reach the army. Macro planners therefore treat **security maintenance** as a fixed cost like bridge repair, not a optional police budget.

### Base economics (mode multipliers)

Relative **cost per ton-mile** for bulk goods (design bands, Earth-anchored):

| Mode | × vs best inland water | When it wins |
|---|---|---|
| Porter / back | **50×+** | Stairs, siege, last mile |
| Ox wagon on dirt | **20×** baseline | Default inland |
| Improved road + ox | **~12×** | Crown roads |
| River barge | **~4×** | Calm rivers, downstream |
| Coastal ship | **1×** (best bulk) | Salt, grain, timber |
| Rail (stone + steam) | **~0.2–0.5×** of wagon on served lanes | Main kingdom spines |
| Bag courier (classed) | Not comparable on ton-mile | High value, low volume |

**River vs sea (design):** Moving the same grain **5×** cheaper on a river than a bad wagon leg is normal; **20–50×** cheaper by coastal ship than overland wagon is the usual kingdom shape when ports exist.

### Daily ox wagon cost table (500–570 sc)

Assumes **one ox + cart**, two handlers, **15–25 km/day** on mixed dirt, **gate tolls** minor, **no battle losses**. All-in **daily** burn:

| Line item | sc/day |
|---|---|
| Ox hire / depreciation share | 180–220 |
| Handler wage (2 × ~10 sc, partial days) | 160–200 |
| Fodder + tack + shoeing reserve | 80–100 |
| Road toll + guild fees | 30–50 |
| Repair + grease + spare linchpin | 50–80 |
| **Total** | **500–570** |

At **~2,000 kg** useful load, that is **~0.25–0.3 sc per kg-day** on bad roads. Rail on the same spine might cut that **5–10×** on freight-only trains (`Roads.md`, `PlacesDesign.md`).

### Grain doubling time (18–20 days)

If wagon cost adds **~30–50%** to farm-gate grain price per **500 km** equivalent, and city demand lifts markup again, **urban grain** can **double** relative to farm-gate in **~18–20 days** of cumulative delay and middleman storage when:

- Harvest flood fills river silos but wagons cannot cover the gap.
- A corridor closes for **two weeks** (monster surge, war, plague checkpoint).

This is the macro reason crowns care about **rail to port** more than **rail to palace**.

### Modes comparison table

| Mode | Capacity | Speed | Cost driver | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Porter | 30–50 kg | 15–25 km/day | Labor sc/day | Fatigue, ambush |
| Pack mule | 80–120 kg | 20–30 km/day | Fodder + handler | Cliff, lame beast |
| Ox wagon | 1.5–2.5 t | 15–25 km/day | 500–570 sc/day table | Mud, axle, bandits |
| River barge | 20–80 t | downstream fast | Lock + crew | Flood, ice, war |
| Coastal ship | 100–500 t | good | Port fees + storm | Piracy, season |
| Rail freight | 200–1,000 t | 40–80 km/day | Stone fuel + track | Derail, stone shortage |
| Bag courier | 0.5–20 m³ | rider speed | **Capital** (bag sg) + class wage | Identify checks, seal law |

### Each mode in practice

**Backs and porters.** Armies, sieges, and mountain forts. Every kg is a person-day. Macro: porter cost sets the **price floor** for luxury goods in landlocked forts.

**Beasts.** Pack trains bridge wagon gaps. Monster zones favor **mule strings** with armed escort over single heavy wagons. Guild caravans sell **escort slots** as a side product.

**Water.** Rivers are medieval highways; sea is the global optimizer. Kingdoms without coasts pay a permanent **20–50×** tax on bulk imports unless rail arrives.

**Rail three tiers (design).**

1. **Mine / lumber spurs:** short, slow, heavy stone consumption; moves ore and timber to river or port.
2. **Freight spines:** kingdom-scale; stone+steam; Victorian-ish ton-mile on served lanes (`EconomyDesign.md` era mix).
3. **Passenger mail:** subsidized; moves clerks, trials, crystal shipments, and guild rankers faster than wagons.

Stones for locomotives need **large** recharge-friendly cores (`MonsterCores.md`). A stone shortage hits **freight before** palaces.

**Magic bags.** Spatial bags change **what moves**, not **what exists**.

- **What changes:** high-value, low-volume goods (scrolls, refined reagents, trial crystals, sealed letters, master tools) shift from guarded wagons to **one classed courier**.
- **What stays alone:** bulk grain, lime, pig iron, oxen, and most lumber still need wagons or hulls. A **6 m³** bag does not feed a city (`SpatialBagPrices.md` scale).
- **Side effects:** smuggling, tariff evasion, **Identify** guild work, and political panic over "invisible tonnage." Crowns register bag serials where guild law allows.

**Royal airships.** Fortress-scale hulls burn capital and stones at rates no merchant can match (`PlacesDesign.md`). They move **people, crystals, and urgent siege parts** over monster badlands. They do not replace rail bulk; they **skip** broken corridors for a day at a time.

### What moves how (summary table)

| Good | Usual mode | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Grain | River / coastal | Ton-mile |
| Salt | Ship | Weight cheap, source fixed |
| Iron pig | Rail → wagon | Weight + port |
| Timber | River float / wagon | Length + road |
| Live cattle | Walk / wagon | Alive |
| Class crystals | Temple escrow + guarded rail/mail | Theft = war |
| Scrolls | Bag courier or passenger rail | Value / volume |
| Dungeon stones | Adventurer pack → guild refinery | Loot chain |
| Luxury cloth | Wagon + ship | Low weight, high markup |

### Geography rings (von Thünen + monsters)

Around a city, invent rings combine **Earth farm rent theory** with **monster density**:

1. **Urban core:** crafts, temples, guild halls, zero farming.
2. **Quelled greenbelt:** intensive farms, mills, tanneries; patrol cost low.
3. **Patrol fringe:** mixed farms, risky after dark; watchtowers, palisades.
4. **Wild interface:** hunting, logging, dungeon mouths; adventurer economy.
5. **Deep wild:** cores and timber only; no rent-paying farms.

When patrol fails, rings **collapse inward**. City grain price spikes before city walls fall.

### War logistics

Armies buy **coin**, **stone**, and **corridor control** together.

- **Coin** hires mercenaries and buys local grain (if locals sell).
- **Stones** feed siege engines, field lamps, and champion gear dumps.
- **Corridors** determine whether ox tables apply or porter math returns.

A kingdom can lose a war while winning battles if **wagon burn** exceeds **dungeon inflow** of stones and coin. Crystal grants to champions are **front-loaded capital**; if the champion dies early, the sunk crystal is gone like a burned bridge.

## 3. Developmental History

Six eras explain how coin, stones, and crystals locked together. Dates are **relative**, not chapter anchors.

### Era 1. Basic Age

Coin mints appear with kings and temples. Monsters are local hazards; cores are curios and lamp fuel. No guild rank ladder, no standardized ascension trials. Power is **personal reputation** and **household stock**.

**Parallel:** late antiquity tribal / early feudal Earth; no rail, no bags.

### Era 2. First Altars

Temples formalize **ascension trials** and sell the first **regulated crystals**. Crowns copy temple law to tax registration. Adventuring is still petty hero work, not a guild industry.

**Parallel:** early universal church tithe systems (`ChurchPaidServices.md` fee shape).

### Era 3. Champion Lords

Great houses fund **named champions** with crystal grants and fiefs. Dungeons are known but not farmed systematically. Roads are Roman leftovers plus local dirt.

**Parallel:** high medieval knight culture; church wealth peaks; no printing.

### Era 4. Dungeons, Guilds, and Bags

Adventurers Guild standardizes ranks, party XP splits, and ear bounties. **Spatial bags** enter as noble loot; prices later fall into merchant reach (`SpatialBagPrices.md`). Dungeon floors become **renewable stone farms** where crowns invest.

**Parallel:** late medieval merchant leagues plus early guild monopolies.

### Era 5. Rune Age

Runecrafting and scroll economies scale (`ScrollEconomy.md`, `GuildPatents.md`). Partner guilds treat **process secrets** like patents. **Rail** spreads on stone fuel; kingdoms resemble **industrializing states with magic fuel**.

**Parallel:** Earth **Russia / India railway rush** (see below).

### Era 6. Present

Steam + stone rail on main spines; airships for royals and wars; bags common among mid-rank adventurers; runesmith prestige high but **person-bound**. Map stays **multi-kingdom** because crystals and corridors do not scale like coal.

**Parallel:** Victorian transport + medieval political fragmentation, glued by faith and guild.

### Real parallels: Russia and India railways

**Russia:** A vast land empire builds rail late but fast to move grain, troops, and raw materials. Lines serve **state power** first, merchant profit second; distance collapses for **some** cities while Siberian interior stays wagon-bound.

**India:** Rail ties port cities to interior cotton and grain; colonial and local states mix ownership; bulk cheapens while **labor and class** stay hierarchical; famine politics follow **corridor** choices not lack of food.

Terra copies the **geometry** (spine lines, port bias, troop mobility) with **stone engines** instead of coal alone, and **monster corridors** instead of empty steppe.

## 4. Power Structure

### Six pillars

Macro power pools into six pillars that overlap (detail catalog: `PowerTypes.md`).

1. **Faith** (Solaria, Lunaria, cult threats): legitimacy, trials, healing, orphanages, crystal sales.
2. **Crown** (kingdoms, empires): tax, mint, law, army, land grant.
3. **Great houses** (dukes, barons): fiefs, private champions, dungeon deeds.
4. **Adventurers Guild**: rank ladder, corridor contracts, loot laundering, party law.
5. **Runesmith / mage towers**: scrolls, bags, fort engines, knowledge monopolies.
6. **Merchant + craft guilds**: trade routes, patents (`GuildPatents.md`), city government.

Free cities, mercenary companies, and **free legends** (tier-4+ individuals) sit **between** pillars, selling loyalty to the highest bidder.

### Actors (quick roles)

| Actor | Holds | Wants |
|---|---|---|
| Faiths | Trial legitimacy, crystal margin, tithe | Stable doctrine, no schism |
| Crowns | Tax, corridors, mint | Armies paid, no elite revolt |
| Great houses | Land, heirs, champions | Dungeon deeds, marriages |
| Adventurers Guild | Rank proof, contracts | Stone inflow, low death rate |
| Runesmiths | Seating secrets, prosthetics fame | Patrons, materials, time |
| Merchants | Fleets, warehouses, credit | Tariff stability |
| Craft guilds | Training monopolies | Patent royalties |
| Mage towers | Grimoires, golem cores | Students, royal grants |
| Free cities | Charter, mixed guild rule | Neutrality rent |
| Free legends | Personal tier cap | Autonomy, interesting work |

### Pyramid table (who sits where)

| Tier | Typical members | Capital form |
|---|---|---|
| Apex | Legends, archmages, royal heirs with T4 backup | Crystals + bags + fiefs |
| Upper | Gold-rank houses, bishop-princes, fleet magnates | Stones + ships + deeds |
| Middle | Silver parties, master crafters, knights | Gear + house + guild stake |
| Lower | Steel / Bronze, clerks, porters | Coin + small stones |
| Base | Unclassed labor, orphans, refugees | Day wage |

### How the wealthy build power

- **Sponsorship:** pay ascension for a talented orphan → lifelong oath.
- **Stipends:** monthly sg to a champion → dungeon priority rights.
- **Fiefs:** land plus peasants → tax coin; conscript corridor labor.
- **Headroom:** keep a tier-3 bench class inactive (`Progression.md`) → flexible response.
- **Portfolio:** mix coin (liquid), stones (energy), crystals (power), bags (logistics).
- **Altar oaths:** public trial witness → legal grip on reclass.
- **Crystal finance:** lend at **2 sg** lock price while buying at invent peg **3.65 sg** only in simulation; in story use live **2 sg** unless promoted.

### Why the map stays fragmented

Crystals **do not scale** like steam factories. One legend cannot hold a continent any more than one Victorian tycoon could hold India without a navy. Corridors, language, and faith differ; **dungeon deeds** are local; rail spines connect **kingdoms**, not erase them.

### What holds the balance

- Faith sells crystals but crowns grant **land and law**.
- Guilds need crowns for **corridor quelling**; crowns need guilds for **stone inflow**.
- Runesmiths need **patrons**; patrons need **runes** for wars and prestige.
- Merchants need **stable coin**; mints need **merchant trade** to justify metal.

No single pillar wins without triggering coalition fear (see stress points).

## 5. Lopsided Technology

Nine rules bend Terra's tech tree away from Earth industrial smoothness.

1. **People beat machines in combat.** A tier-3 champion with stones and skills beats a wagon-load of clockwork unless siege scale applies. Armies invest in **class capital**, not only musket lines.

2. **Magic is weak in bulk, strong in bursts.** Continuous industrial heat from runes is **mana- and J-expensive** (`ManaStones.md`, `Energy.md`). Factories stay **steam + labor**; magic wins in **lamp, dump, and seat** niches.

3. **Energy comes from killing.** Dungeon and wild drops feed stones; rail and war **recycle loot** into fuel. Peasant economies cannot "mine mana" without adventurers or crown hunts.

4. **Knowledge lives in people.** Runesmith seating, trial secrets, and Identify tricks die with masters unless guild registry captures them (`GuildPatents.md`). Printing does not flatten spell cost (`EconomyDesign.md` books stay dear).

5. **Science short-circuits.** A single rune prosthetic or bag can skip a decade of mundane R&D; conversely, **no rune** for bulk power keeps chemistry and steam relevant.

6. **Elite pull.** Top tiers attract stones, crystals, and patrons like magnets; the median village sees **coin** change faster than **tech**.

7. **Mana gradient.** High ambient regions heal and recharge stones; low regions tax adventurers. Geography sets **energy rent** like rainfall.

8. **Cheap metal (where steam applies).** Iron and steel goods sit at Victorian-ish bands while **mythril-tier** stays medieval treasure (`EconomyDesign.md` era mix table).

9. **Logistics cheats are capital-heavy.** Bags and airships are **purchase + class** gates, not public infrastructure.

### Where Terra is ahead / lags

| Ahead of Earth baseline | Lags Earth baseline |
|---|---|
| Personal burst magic, lamps, prosthetics | Mass electrification |
| Stone-fired rail on spines | Paved road net everywhere |
| Dungeon renewable rare mats | Cheap bulk mage heat |
| Status system literacy (Identify culture) | Secular public schooling |

### Signature contradictions

- **Victorian freight, medieval politics.**
- **Cheap iron sword, dear handwritten spellbook.**
- **Rail beside ox wagon on the same kingdom map.**
- **Orphan can ascend in theory; crystal still years of saving in practice** (live **2 sg** eases vs invent peg).

### Gaps outside knowledge would hit hardest

If all rune masters vanished overnight: **bag repair**, **rail stone seating**, **fort wards**, and **trial seals** fail before peasant plows do. If all **adventurers** vanished: **stone supply** collapses, rail throttles, and **class mobility** freezes at temple stock. If **faith** collapses: **trial legitimacy** and orphan pipelines fracture; crowns scramble to nationalize crystal sales.

## 6. Stress Points

Bullets below are **design stressors** from **runesmith death** through **legend defection**. Use as plot levers or simulation shocks.

### Runesmith and guild secret chain

- Hidden runesmith dies; partner guild loses **staged patent pipeline**; copycats close within a season unless a decoy master holds the line (`GuildPatents.md`).
- Registry oath broken; magical patent proof fails; **black market scrolls** flood; crown seizes guild warehouses.
- Compartmentalized workshop sabotaged; **one process leak** collapses 50/50 contract; guild turns on invisible inventor.
- Prosthetic rune line exposed; nobles fear **body-snatch** espionage; Identify guild hired to scan retainers.
- Rune seating error on a royal bag; **nested freeze** accident; merchant league demands bag registration law.
- Scroll mat shortage; Fire Arrow economy stalls; Bronze parties starve for lack of **1 ls** stones.
- Golem core auction hits **12 lg**; one house corners cores; institute convocation rigged.

### Crystal finance and temple peg

- Temple breaks **no-abuse rule 1**; crystal spot price crashes; adventurer wages riot; crown closes mint to temple deposits.
- Wage peg (**3.65 sg**) argued in synod while shops charge **2 sg** lock; smugglers arbitrage border temples.
- Crystal loan mass default; peasants sell ears instead of grain; corridor patrol budget cut.
- Fake ascension crystals; trial fraud; status-hide armbands spike; Identify wait times weeks.
- Crown nationalizes crystal sales; faith schism; Lunaria border hosts black market trials.
- Orphanage crystal ladder stalls; street gangs recruit unclassed youth.
- Duke gifts crystal with **marriage chain**; recipient dies in dungeon; house demands refund; temple refuses.

### Corridor, stone, and grain

- Quelled corridor lost for **20 days**; city grain **doubles**; army ox table burns **500–570 sc/day** extra per column.
- Rail stone shortage; freight throttled; port piles grain while inland starves.
- Dungeon floor monopoly war; two houses block each other's lift access; stone price **+40%**.
- Monster resurgence in greenbelt; farms burn; refugees hit city wage band.
- River flood; barge cheapness gone; wagon monopoly price gouges.
- Coastal piracy season; salt spikes; preserved food riots.

### Bag, rail, and smuggling

- Bag courier caught with **trial crystals**; tariff law rewritten; passenger rail searches slow mail.
- **6 m³** bag found hauling weapons to rebels; crown bans private bags above **2 m³** without seal.
- Rail workers strike unless paid in **ss** not debased sc; kingdom switches wage coin.
- Airship sortie skips siege resupply; fort falls while rail line intact.
- Merchant under-invoices wagon loads; guild wants **spatial audit** magic that does not exist yet.

### Crown, house, and guild politics

- Free city charter revoked; league merchants embargo crystal imports.
- Mercenary company seizes dungeon mouth; tolls exceed guild contract.
- Banking house calls crown debt; default → mercenary mutiny.
- Great house withholds corridor patrol; guild posts pull out; synchronized strike.
- Mage tower sells golem to both sides; convocation neutrality shattered.
- Craft guild blacklists partner guild cities; pencil / match monopolies fracture (`MatchMonopoly.md`, `PencilMonopoly.md`).

### Legend defection and apex individuals

- Tier-4 legend breaks patron oath; takes **map of dungeon deep floor** to rival kingdom.
- Legend demands crystal **stipend > crown surplus**; regency accepts; taxes rise; peasant march.
- Legend fakes death; reappears as bandit king; corridors unsafe despite guild posters.
- Legend captures bag courier chain; learns **temple shipment schedule**; heist pulls **2 sg×100** crystals.
- Legend refuses convocation; institute loses prestige; foreign mages poach students.
- Legend joins cult; abyss threat becomes **state-sponsored** raid on greenbelt.
- Two legends duel in city; collateral burns warehouse district; merchants flee coin to stones.
- Legend sells **one season** to highest bidder; war outcome decided without army battle.
- Defecting legend opens **training camp** in wild; mass ascension attempts; monster wave follows.
- Crown assassinates legend; patron states retaliate; rail spine sabotaged.

### Systemic cascades

- Coin debased **and** stones hoarded; bimetallic panic; adventurers demand payment in rice stones.
- Faith **rule 3** broken; public goods cut; plague grave pits overflow; labor supply shock.
- Multi-kingdom war; **three corridors** close; famine in neutral city triggers free-city militia.
- Progression law rumor (false reclass cap change); crystal hoarding; temple sales freeze.
- Mana gradient drop in capital region; stones recharge slower; rail schedules slip.
- Cheap metal import flood; local smiths riot; armor prices diverge from `EconomyDesign.md` bands.
