**Under those terms, the guilds become his engine, army and front**

With the contract forcing active protection and a 50/50 split, betrayal is off the table. Every guild master's fortune depends on the traveler staying alive, anonymous and productive. The guilds take the public credit and handle production, sales and enforcement. He supplies a steady stream of proven ideas from behind a wall of guild secrecy.

**Patents as the backbone**

Guilds petition the crown for letters patent, which in a medieval setting are royal monopoly grants. The crown happily signs them because each one comes with a fee and a tax stream. A magical registry, sworn before guild mages, makes theft provable. Copycats face guild lawsuits, confiscation and blacklisting from every allied market.

**The pen and pencil alone change the realm**

- **Pencil:** the guild buys or claims the best graphite deposit, which gives it control of the raw material outright. Encasing the graphite in wood makes it cheap, portable and erasable. Merchants, soldiers, surveyors and students adopt it within a decade.
- **Steel-nib pen:** the smiths stamp the nibs while the stationers sell them. This kills the quill trade and makes writing faster and cleaner.
- **The ripple effect:** cheap writing tools drive up literacy, contracts, bookkeeping and schooling. The guilds that sell the tools also end up selling paper, ink, ledgers and training.

**Other patents and which guild runs each**

- **Stationers/Scriveners:** pen, pencil, paper improvements, printing press
- **Smiths:** crucible steel, standardized screws and tools
- **Glaziers:** clear glass, spectacles, telescopes
- **Alchemists:** soap, distillation, dyes, matches
- **Weavers:** spinning wheel, then flying shuttle
- **Merchants:** double-entry bookkeeping, letters of credit, insurance

**How it plays out**

1. **Years 1–5:** the pen and pencil launch, and the guilds' names become household words. The traveler gets rich in total obscurity.
2. **Years 5–15:** staged releases keep the guilds a step ahead of copycats. Rival guilds and foreign realms try to reverse-engineer the products but always trail the next patent.
3. **Years 15–30:** the partner guilds are wealthier than most noble houses. They fund roads, schools and private guards, and they lend to the crown. Kings need them too much to move against them.
4. **Long term:** the partner guilds operate like modern corporations with royal charters. The traveler is the unseen founder of half the realm's industry, guarded by people whose wealth depends on his secret.

**The main remaining threat is outsiders**

Foreign spies, non-partner guilds and curious nobles will hunt for the source of all these inventions. The guilds counter with decoy "inventor" masters, compartmentalized workshops where no one sees the full process, and magical oaths on every apprentice.
