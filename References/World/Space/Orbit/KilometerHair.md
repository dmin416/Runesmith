## 1. Volume of a 1 km Hair

A single 1 km human hair has a volume of about **3.85 cm³** (roughly 3.85 mL).

**Calculation** (cylinder, average diameter 70 µm):

- Radius: 35 µm = 3.5 × 10⁻⁵ m
- Cross-section: π × (3.5 × 10⁻⁵)² ≈ 3.85 × 10⁻⁹ m²
- Volume: 3.85 × 10⁻⁹ m² × 1,000 m ≈ 3.85 × 10⁻⁶ m³ ≈ **3.85 cm³**

| Hair type   | Diameter | Volume (1 km) |
|-------------|----------|---------------|
| Very fine   | 17 µm    | 0.23 cm³      |
| Average     | 70 µm    | 3.85 cm³      |
| Very coarse | 181 µm   | 25.7 cm³      |

At a keratin density of about 1.3 g/cm³ the average strand weighs about **5 g**.

## 2. Length of a 2 m³ Hair

- Length: 2 m³ ÷ 3.85 × 10⁻⁹ m² ≈ 5.2 × 10⁸ m ≈ **519,500 km**
- About 1.35 times the Earth to Moon distance (384,400 km)
- About 13 wraps around Earth's equator

| Hair type   | Diameter | Length (2 m³)  |
|-------------|----------|----------------|
| Very fine   | 17 µm    | 8.8 million km |
| Average     | 70 µm    | 519,500 km     |
| Very coarse | 181 µm   | 77,700 km      |

Mass: about **2.6 metric tons**.

## 3. Stability When Driven Into the Earth at Hawaii

Assumptions: 70 µm diameter, 519,500 km long, indestructible, perfectly rigid, pointing radially outward from Hawaii (about 20°N).

The hair stands as long as it is anchored deep enough. The serious problem is the Moon.

### Forces on the Hair

- Tip speed: about 35.6 km/s from Earth's rotation.
- Everything beyond geostationary distance (42,164 km from Earth's center) is flung outward harder than gravity pulls it in. The hair as a whole pulls **outward** on its anchor with about 2,900 N (roughly 300 kgf). It never falls over or sinks.
- At 20°N the centrifugal pull has a sideways component toward the equator. This puts roughly 4 × 10¹¹ N·m of torque on the base.

### Anchoring

- A 70 µm rod under that torque cuts through rock like a wire through clay unless buried around 10 km or deeper.
- Driven through the crust into the upper mantle (about 100 km) it holds on human timescales.
- Over geologic time the mantle creeps and the hair slowly tilts toward the equator.

### The Moon

- The hair reaches past the Moon's orbit (363,000 to 406,000 km).
- It sweeps a cone aimed at 20°N declination once per day.
- The Moon crosses 20°N declination twice a month for most of its 18.6-year cycle.
- Result: the hair strikes the Moon roughly 3 to 4 times per year.

At lunar distance the hair moves about 25 km/s relative to the Moon. Each strike:

- Slices a 70 µm cut up to 3,474 km long through the Moon in about 2 minutes
- Dumps on the order of 10²¹ J (about 400,000 megatons TNT) along that line, blasting debris into space
- Shifts the Moon's orbit by about one part in a billion
- Lengthens Earth's day by about 0.3 ms

The Moon's gravity closes each cut. Repeated strikes leave long linear scars on the Moon and add about 1 ms per year to the day, roughly 40 times faster than tidal slowing.

### Satellites

- The hair passes through low and medium Earth orbit at 20°N.
- Satellites in inclined orbits are eventually sliced.
- The geostationary belt sits over the equator and stays clear.
