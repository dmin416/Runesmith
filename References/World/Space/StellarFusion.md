# Stellar Fusion Ladder

Earth astrophysics and nuclear process notes for element origins. Ambient / orbit location: `Space.md`. Magic metal conversion (story): `../Materials/Metals.md`. Human fusion energy feel: `../Science/Energy/FlywheelStorage.md`.

**Stellar fusion ladder** (Kelvin; at these temperatures K and °C are effectively identical, and °F is about 1.8 times the number)

| Stage | Core temperature | Fuel → Products | Duration (25 solar-mass star) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrogen burning | 10-30 million K (Sun's core: 15.7 million) | H → He | ~7 million years |
| Helium burning | 100-200 million K | He → C, O | ~500,000 years |
| Carbon burning | 600-900 million K | C → Ne, Na, Mg | ~600 years |
| Neon burning | 1.2-1.7 billion K | Ne → O, Mg | ~1 year |
| Oxygen burning | 1.5-2.5 billion K | O → Si, S, P | ~6 months |
| Silicon burning | 2.7-3.5 billion K | Si → Ni-56 → Fe-56 | ~1 day |

Carbon burning requires a star of roughly 8+ solar masses. Silicon burning (the iron step) requires roughly 10+ solar masses. Densities at the silicon stage are around 10-100 million g/cm³.

Iron is not made directly. Silicon burning produces nickel-56, which decays to cobalt-56 (half-life 6.1 days) then to iron-56 (half-life 77 days).

## Why it stops at iron

Iron-56 and nickel-62 have the most tightly bound nuclei of any element. Fusing anything past iron absorbs energy instead of releasing it. The iron core loses support and collapses. At 5-10 billion K, gamma rays smash iron back into helium and neutrons (photodisintegration), which helps trigger the supernova.

## Elements heavier than iron

| Process | Temperature | Where | Makes |
|---|---|---|---|
| s-process (slow neutron capture) | 100-300 million K | Red giant (AGB) stars over thousands of years | Strontium, barium, lead, about half of heavy elements up to bismuth |
| r-process (rapid neutron capture) | 1-10 billion K | Neutron star mergers and some supernovae, in seconds | Gold, platinum, uranium, thorium |

## Fission

Fission does not depend on temperature. A U-235 nucleus splits when it absorbs a neutron, even one moving at room-temperature speed. The fragments cluster around mass 95 (strontium, krypton, zirconium, molybdenum) and mass 140 (xenon, cesium, barium, rare earths). Iron is not a fission product.

## Human-made fusion

| Fuel | Required plasma temperature |
|---|---|
| Deuterium-tritium | 100-150 million K |
| Deuterium-deuterium | 300-500 million K |
| Deuterium-helium-3 | 700 million-1 billion K |
| Proton-boron-11 | 1-3 billion K |

Reactors need temperatures far above the Sun's core because their plasma is hundreds of thousands of times less dense than air and stays confined for only seconds. The Sun makes up for its lower temperature with enormous density and billions of years.

Making iron by fusion on Earth would require about 3 billion K combined with stellar-core densities, which no existing technology can produce. Particle accelerators can transmute individual atoms into other elements (mercury into gold has been done), at a cost of millions of dollars per microscopic amount.
