# Slimes

> Metal slime / living crucible design loot. Conversion law: `../Materials/Metals.md`. Ambient soak `A = √C`: `../Science/Energy/ManaConcentration.md`. Hub: `Creatures.md`. Source fire-slime card: `CreaturesDesign.md`.

## Metal Slime: A Living Crucible

The slime works as a living smelter. It takes in ore or scrap, pulls the metal out, drops impurities and stores pure metal in its body. Held in enough ambient mana, the metal it carries converts, so a slime kept in the right place long enough turns into a magic-metal slime.

### Real Anchors

| Real Thing | Slime Trait It Supports |
|---|---|
| **Bioleaching bacteria** (Acidithiobacillus) dissolve copper and iron out of ore. Real mines use them. | Cold slimes digest ore with acids |
| **Gold-precipitating bacteria** (Cupriavidus, Delftia) turn dissolved gold back into solid gold grains | Slimes deposit pure metal nuggets inside their bodies |
| **Magnetotactic bacteria** grow chains of magnetite crystals | Iron slimes become magnetic |
| **Scaly-foot snail** grows armor plates of iron sulfide | Metal slimes can harden their outer skin into metal scales |
| **Smelter slag** is molten rock that floats on molten metal and absorbs impurities | A lava slime is living slag |
| **Bloomery furnace** makes solid iron from ore and charcoal at ~1,200 °C without melting the iron | A lava slime fed ore and charcoal grows iron blooms |
| **Liquid-metal research robots** made of gallium alloys melt, flow and re-solidify on command | Mana-held cold metal slimes are plausible |
| **Molten metal + water** causes violent steam explosions. It is a top foundry hazard. | Water is a weapon against hot metal slimes |

### Conversion Rules Inside a Slime

**Soak rule.** Conversion runs on ambient soak (A = √C past each metal's threshold), not on the slime simply having mana. A slime's core does not raise soak to dungeon thickness. A farm slime converts its metal only as far as local soak allows, the same as metal lying on the ground in that spot. Fine and higher grades, and any adamantium pearl, need the slime kept in a dungeon, at altitude or at a spiritual site.

**Cook order.** Silver starts first (lowest threshold). Once started, the rate is gold > silver > copper > iron > steel > titanium.

**Preferred steel path.** Convert iron first, then carburize with charcoal into steel. Carbon slows conversion, so an iron slime fed charcoal after conversion is the efficient farm route.

### Two Digestion Routes

| Route | Slime | How It Eats Metal | Speed | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold** | Gel slime | Acid and chelating fluids dissolve metal into ions. The slime re-deposits pure metal in pockets. | Slow (days to weeks per kg) | Very pure nuggets. Real electro-refining reaches 99.99%. |
| **Hot** | Lava slime | Body is molten rock at ~1,100-1,200 °C. Metals melt and sink to the bottom as a liquid pool. Impurities stay in the rock body. | Fast (minutes to hours per kg) | Molten metal ready to pour |

### Digesting Converted Metal

Converted metals keep their grade through melting and through acid digestion. Digestion slows as grade rises.

| Converted Stock | Cold (Acid) Route | Hot (Melt) Route |
|---|---|---|
| Mythril, aurium, darkiron/star iron, darksteel/star steel | Digests slower than the mundane parent, in proportion to grade. Re-deposited nuggets keep their grade. | Melts hotter than its parent as grade rises (planning feel). Keeps its grade. |
| Orihalcum | Resists in proportion to conversion %. High grades barely dissolve, so farm acid routes don't recover high-grade orihalcum economically. | Melts hotter than gold. High grades need hotter lava. Keeps its grade. |
| Adamantium | No | No. Never melts after set. |

### Growth Path

| Stage | Body | Temperature | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Gel slime | Water gel with acid pockets | Ambient | Eats scrap slowly. Common pest and street cleaner. |
| 2. Lava slime | Molten rock held hot by mana-fire | ~1,100-1,200 °C | Canon mana converts to elemental fire with little loss, so it keeps itself molten. |
| 3. Metal-core lava slime | Rock skin over a growing liquid metal core | ~1,100-1,200 °C | The metal pool settles to its base like a furnace hearth. |
| 4. Metal slime | Mostly liquid metal under a thin skin | Depends on metal | Rock skin shrinks to a crust. The metal becomes the body. |
| 5. Magic-metal slime | Converted metal | Depends on metal | Conversion follows the soak rule and cook order above. |

### Orihalcum Slime Stall

Orihalcum blocks mana, including the slime's own. The two slime types fail differently:

- **Hot orihalcum slime:** its body stays liquid from heat, not mana. As conversion rises, its mana-fire fails. Without another heat source, it cools below gold's melting point and solidifies.
- **Cold mana-held orihalcum slime:** once its own orihalcum cuts its mana, it freezes immediately.

A dead orihalcum slime leaves an orihalcum nugget. It only reaches high grade if the slime lived in extreme soak, deep in a dungeon or at high altitude. That fits canon: nature stalls orihalcum at low grades unless mana concentration is overwhelming.

### Physical Traits

**Weight.** Stage 4-5 metal slimes are nearly all metal and far heavier than they look:

| Slime | 30 cm Diameter | 50 cm Diameter |
|---|---|---|
| Gel slime | ~14 kg | ~65 kg |
| Lava slime | ~38 kg | ~175 kg |
| Copper / aurium (stage 4-5) | ~125 kg | ~580 kg |
| Silver / mythril (stage 4-5) | ~140 kg | ~640 kg |
| Gold / orihalcum (stage 4-5) | ~270 kg | ~1,250 kg |
| Iron / star iron (stage 4-5, blooms in slag) | ~70 kg | ~330 kg |
| Steel / star steel (stage 4-5) | ~110 kg | ~510 kg |

Stage 3 metal-core lava slimes carry only a small metal pool in a rock body, so they sit much closer to lava slime weight. A 50 cm stage 5 gold slime weighs over a tonne. Its main attack is rolling onto a target.

**Shape.** Liquid metals have very high surface tension: molten copper is about 18× water. Small droplets bead tight and round. Anything bigger than about a centimeter slumps into a puddle under its own weight. A metal slime needs a skin to hold its shape: a rock crust for hot slimes, a mana membrane for cold ones. Breaking the skin makes it spill.

**Heat.** Hot metal slimes glow orange at 1,000+ °C and scorch anything close. Their heat can be felt from meters away.

### Combat: Core First

- **The core is the kill.** The body shrugs off blades. Breaking the core, or a mana-imbued hit that drops the core, kills the slime. Everything else on this list either opens a path to the core or harvests the body.
- **Core location.** The core usually sits in the densest, coolest pocket. In hot slimes that is the base of the metal pool. Stripping the skin with water or freezing the body and cutting down to the base reaches it.
- **Blades pass through.** Cutting liquid metal does nothing lasting. Edged weapons that touch it get eaten or heat-softened.
- **Water causes steam explosions.** Dumping water on a hot metal slime blasts molten spatter in every direction. It strips the skin and exposes the core, and it hurts anyone standing close.
- **Freezing disables and harvests.** Chilling a slime below its metal's melting point turns its body to a solid ingot. A fully frozen slime is helpless but not dead until its core is broken or cut out.
- **Armor is food.** Steel, iron, copper and silver armor gets dissolved or melted. Adamantium is indigestible and high-grade orihalcum resists it. Full adamantium or high-orihalcum kits are slime-proof.
- **Durasteel leaves grit.** Durasteel is the durium cermet line: hard durium particles in a darksteel matrix. The slime digests the darksteel matrix and leaves the durium particles behind as sand.

### Cold Variant: Mana-Held Metal Slime

Instead of heat, mana keeps the metal fluid at room temperature, like real gallium alloys. It is touchable and doesn't glow. It depends on mana: orihalcum blocks it, aurium weapons damp its mana flow and slow it, and cutting its mana freezes it immediately. Dense-mana zones like dungeons and high altitude make it stronger.

### Farming and Uses

Canon already has tamed monsters doing farm work. A tamed metal slime is a living foundry.

- **Refinery.** Ore in, pure metal out. The slime keeps a share as upkeep (suggested 10-20%).
- **Conversion vessel.** Kept where soak is high enough, the metal it carries converts. Farmers move slimes to dungeon edges, high pastures or spiritual sites to reach higher grades.
- **Alloying.** Feed copper and tin for bronze. Feed converted iron and measured charcoal for darksteel or star steel of an exact carbon grade.
- **Living ladle.** A trained hot slime pours its metal into molds on command.
- **Breeding.** Slimes split. Each daughter takes part of the metal. A slime's metal mass caps its size, so splitting is also how farmers harvest.
- **Live harvest.** Freezing a portion or splitting works only if the core stays whole. Any freeze that shatters the core, or any cut through it, kills the slime.

### Slime Pearls

Soak converts solid titanium. Melting is not required. Two routes produce adamantium:

| Route | How | Result | Rarity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold nodule** | A slime swallows solid titanium and holds it in high soak. The lump converts as a solid while inside. | An adamantium nodule shaped like the original lump. The slime survives, but it can't digest, remelt or reshape the set nodule. It can only carry it or spit it out. | Uncommon. Needs high soak and a long hold, since titanium converts slowest. |
| **Hot pearl** | A lava slime pushed past ~1,700 °C by extra mana-fire melts titanium. The molten titanium converts and sets. | Any contiguous titanium mass that sets becomes dead metal all at once. If the titanium is the slime's whole body, the slime dies and the pearl takes the shape of its last moment. If it is only a pool inside a larger lava body, the pool sets as a partial pearl and the lava slime survives carrying it. | Rare. Needs an exceptionally hot slime and high soak. |

Set adamantium is cast-final. Harvest is spitting or cutting the slime away from the piece, never reprocessing it. Both routes give natural sources of adamantium outside casting, with random shapes. Pieces that happen to be useful shapes are prized.

## Slime Physics

### Water: When "Incompressible" Stops Being True

Water's bulk modulus is about 2.2 GPa, so it barely compresses. "Incompressible" is still only an approximation, and it breaks in two places.

**1. The first instant of impact.** When something hits water, the contact point can't push water out of the way instantly. For a few microseconds the water acts compressible and the pressure spikes to the water-hammer value:

**P ≈ ρ × c × v** (density × sound speed × impact speed)

For water that is about **1.5 MPa per m/s** of impact speed. A 20 m/s kick spikes about 30 MPa at the contact point for microseconds. Then the pressure falls to the ordinary flow level, about ½ρv², roughly 0.2 MPa at 20 m/s. That spike is why a belly flop stings and why high-speed water impacts damage ship hulls.

**2. Very fast bulk flow.** Compressibility starts mattering at roughly 0.1-0.3 of the speed of sound, about 150-450 m/s in water. No kick gets close.

### When Water Cavitates

Cavitation happens when local pressure drops near the vapor pressure of water, effectively zero absolute pressure. Pushing on water raises pressure, so the kicked side never cavitates. Cavitation comes from where pressure drops.

| Cause | Threshold | Does a Hard Kick Do It? |
|---|---|---|
| **Wake behind the foot** | Flow faster than about 10-14 m/s at sea level (cavitation number below ~1) | Yes. A strong kick moves the foot at 15-25 m/s. |
| **Foot pulling back** | Contact face retreats faster than water can follow | Yes, on rebound |
| **Reflected tension wave** | The compression spike bounces off the far surface as tension. Needs a hit faster than the wave's round trip (~0.4 ms across a 30 cm ball). | Only with a rigid, hard impactor. A soft shoe spreads the hit out too slowly. |
| **Container yanked away** (bottle-slap trick) | Acceleration greater than atmospheric pressure ÷ (ρ × depth). About 500 m/s² (~50 g) for 20 cm of water. | Not on a free ball. Yes if the water sits in a container that gets struck. |

**A free ball of water** has a bigger problem than cavitation: it has no shear strength. Fragmentation is set by the Weber number (inertia vs surface tension). A 20 m/s kick on a 30 cm water ball scores over a million against a breakup threshold around a thousand. It explodes into spray. Water only cavitates instead of splashing if something holds its shape, like a slime's skin.

**Rigid boots raise cavitation.** An adamantium boot doesn't deform, so the impact rises fast enough to send a full tension wave through the body.

### Gels

A gel is a polymer network holding water. It is still mostly water, so it doesn't compress either. It does have elastic shear strength and holds a shape.

- **Low-speed hit:** wobbles and springs back.
- **High-speed hit:** forms a temporary cavity. Ballistic gelatin shows this at bullet speed: the cavity balloons outward, then snaps shut. Damage happens only where the stretch exceeds the gel's fracture strain.
- **Toughness range:** plain gelatin tears at about 1-10 J/m² of fracture energy. Real tough hydrogels reach 1,000-10,000 J/m². A combat slime should be the tough kind.

### Non-Newtonian Fluids

| Type | Real Example | Under a Slow Push | Under a Hard Kick |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shear-thickening** | Cornstarch in water | Flows. Fingers sink in. | Jams solid. Acts like a wall, can crack, then reflows. |
| **Shear-thinning** | Ketchup, blood | Stiffish | Gets runnier and splashes more |
| **Viscoelastic** | Silly putty | Flows slowly | Bounces. At very high speed, shatters. |
| **Yield-stress (Bingham)** | Toothpaste | Holds shape until pushed past a threshold | Flows once the threshold is exceeded |

**Best slime body:** viscoelastic and shear-thickening with a tough skin. Weapons and kicks bounce or jam, while slow engulfing still works. Reflexes can't react fast enough to stiffen against a millisecond hit, so a passive non-Newtonian body is what a slime would actually evolve.

### Lava Slime Rheology

Lava is about 2.7× denser than water and enormously more viscous. Its behavior depends on silica content.

| Lava Type | Viscosity (Pa·s) | Temperature | Kick Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Basaltic** (low silica) | 10-1,000 | 1,100-1,200 °C | Flows like very thick honey. The foot sinks and gets dragged. No splash. |
| **Andesitic** | 10³-10⁶ | 900-1,100 °C | Sluggish, starts tearing at fast strikes |
| **Rhyolitic** (high silica, obsidian family) | 10⁶-10¹¹ | 700-900 °C | **Shatters like glass** on a hard kick, flows when pushed slowly |

**Why rhyolite shatters:** molten rock has a relaxation time of viscosity ÷ shear stiffness. Strike faster than it can relax and it fractures as a solid. Volcanologists measure exactly this as the cause of explosive magma fragmentation. A kick at ~70 strains per second shatters any lava above about 10⁶ Pa·s.

**The trade-off:** a glassy rhyolitic slime resists blows but runs too cool (700-900 °C) to melt gold, copper or silver. Only the runny basaltic slime works as a metal crucible.

**Lava "cavitation" is foaming.** Lava carries dissolved water and CO₂. Any tension wave or sudden pressure drop lets the gas come out of solution as bubbles, the same way pumice forms. A hard enough strike makes a lava slime puff into hot froth at the impact zone. At large scale, that same decompression drives explosive volcanic eruptions.

## Lava Slime + Adamantium

The effusivity and heat-flow figures in this section are planning feel, in the same class as the inspirational tables in Materials, not locks.

### Contact Quench

When two bodies touch, the interface temperature is set by each one's thermal effusivity (√(k·ρ·c)), which measures how fast it can absorb heat.

| Material Touching 1,150 °C Lava | Effusivity | Contact Temperature |
|---|---|---|
| Steel at 20 °C | ~13,900 | ~174 °C |
| Copper at 20 °C | ~37,100 | ~83 °C |
| **Adamantium at 20 °C** | **~84,700** | **~50 °C** |
| Lava itself | ~2,200 | |

Wherever adamantium touches lava, the lava surface is instantly quenched to about 50 °C, far below its ~700 °C glass point. A glass skin forms on contact and thickens over time: about 0.2 mm in 0.1 s, 0.7 mm in 1 s, 2 mm in 10 s. An adamantium hammer, boot or blade strikes a lava slime and hits glass. The glass shatters and opens a wound toward the core where an ordinary weapon would just sink into hot fluid.

### Heat-Sink Weapon

An adamantium blade left stuck through a lava slime drains its heat out the exposed end. A 2 cm thick, 30 cm exposed section pulls on the order of 1-3 kW out of the slime. That freezes roughly 1 kg of lava every five minutes around the blade unless the slime's mana-fire keeps up. Leaving the blade in place grows a freeze zone outward from the adamantium, which pins the slime and slows it while someone goes for the core. The grip reaches slime temperature within a minute or two, so the wielder lets go fast or uses an insulated grip.

### Adamantium Inside the Slime

- **Indigestible.** It never melts, dissolves or reacts.
- **Thermal equilibrium.** Fully submerged, it reaches slime temperature in seconds and does nothing further for heat. If any part sticks out, it acts as a constant heat leak. It stays indigestible and stays a sound target the whole time.
- **Teeth and skeleton.** A lava slime that swallowed adamantium mail or blades can carry them as rigid internal pieces and drive them through its body like a blender, or extrude a mail coil as a whip.
- **No mana path.** Adamantium carries little to no mana, so a slime can't use it to hold or protect its core without cutting that core off from the rest of its body.
- **Resonance predator.** Canon locks adamantium's only weakness as resonant sound or mana vibration after set. A slime that hums at an adamantium piece's resonant frequency is a natural predator of adamantium gear. Each casting has its own frequency (per `../Materials/Metals.md`), so it has to find each piece's tone separately. Mail links all differ.
