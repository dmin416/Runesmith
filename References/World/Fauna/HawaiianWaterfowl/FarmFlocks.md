## 11. Domestic Breeds for a Friendly Egg-Laying Flock

| Breed | Eggs per year | Temperament | Foraging | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Welsh Harlequin | 240 to 330 | Calm, friendly, easy to handle | Good | Strong all-around pick |
| Ancona | 210 to 280 | Sociable, hardy | Active | Strong all-around pick |
| Pekin | 150 to 200 | Most docile and people-friendly | Lazy | Heavy; adds the friendliest temperament to a mixed flock |
| Khaki Campbell | 250 to 300 | Nervous unless raised around people | Strong | Top egg layer |
| Indian Runner | 150 to 200 | Skittish but flock-oriented | Best bug hunter | Easy to herd |
| Buff Orpington | 150 to 220 | Calm, friendly | Good | Dual-purpose |
| Muscovy | 60 to 120 | Quiet | Strong fly and bug eater | Only breed allowed into Hawaiʻi; not mallard-derived; flies well |

**Keeping the group easy**
- **Flock size:** at least 3 to 4 ducks. Ducks get stressed alone.
- **Unfertilized eggs:** all-female flock. No drake means no fertilized eggs and a calmer group.
- **Friendliness:** raise from ducklings; hand-feed peas, greens and mealworms.
- **Water:** a tub or kiddie pool deep enough to dunk their heads.
- **Shelter:** predator-proof house at night. Most ducks lay early in the morning, so eggs end up in the house.

## 12. Hawaiian Species as a Farm Flock

The arrangement: the birds give up unfertilized eggs, eat at the farm, get fed and stay sociable with the people there.

| Rank | Species | Group living | Bug control | Egg giving | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Laysan duck | Small groups; naturally tame | Excellent: flies, larvae, insects | ~4 per clutch, more with steady feeding | Weak flier, stays close. Forages at dusk and night. Best overall fit. |
| 2 | Nēnē | Strongest; lifelong pairs, family flocks; most sociable with people | Poor: grazes grass and weeds | 2 to 5, one clutch a year | Excellent sentinels and grass control. |
| 3 | Koloa maoli | Weak; pairs, wary | Best of all: snails, larvae, worms | ~6 per clutch, year-round breeding | Good egg output. Slow to settle into a group. |
| 4 | Moa-nalo | Calm; flightless, stays put | Poor: browses leaves and shrubs | Few large eggs | Clears brush; must be kept out of crops. |
| 5 | Kauaʻi mole duck | Unknown; nocturnal | Good: digs grubs and bugs from soil by touch | Estimated 3 to 6 | Kauaʻi only. Too specialized for a main flock. |

**Best mixed group**
- Laysan ducks and koloa for bugs and eggs.
- A few nēnē as lookouts and grass grazers.

**Egg supply**
- Taking eggs regularly pushes all of these birds to lay again.
- A well-fed flock gives up more unfertilized eggs over a season than wild birds would.
