# Living Furnace Slime

Process catalog for a slime that smelts, casts, forges and works glass. Species locks stay in `Slimes.md`: soak and cook order, orihalcum stall, adamantium no ordinary melt, and the hot lava body at about 1,100–1,200 °C.

That lava band covers tin through copper and soft glass. Iron at bloomery heat (~1,200 °C) stays solid spongy metal in slag. Steel above about 1,450 °C is the hotter variant in `Slimes.md`. A titanium hot melt kills the slime. The magic heat organ below is how a furnace slime pushes past the lava band. It does not replace those rules.

Lost-wax pairs with wax from `../../Science/Biomaterials/PrinterWorm.md`. Alloy recipes: `../../Science/Metallurgy/CommonAlloys.md`. Beryllium handling and the preferred spark-proof swap: `../../Science/Metallurgy/ToxicMetalSubstitutes.md`.

## Body structure

- **Crust skin:** a self-renewing ceramic and glass outer layer that insulates the interior and protects surroundings. Thin enough to flex, thick enough to hold heat in.
- **Slag mantle:** molten silicate layer that floats on the metal. Absorbs impurities, acts as flux and serves as the lava body most people see.
- **Metal core:** dense molten metal that sinks to the bottom by gravity. Can hold several separate metal pools in sealed internal chambers.
- **Heat zones:** independently controlled regions from warm to white-hot. One pseudopod can be at 300 °C for tin while a magical core sits at 1,600 °C for steel. The ordinary lava body stays in the 1,100–1,200 °C band in `Slimes.md`.
- **Atmosphere pockets:** internal gas bubbles kept oxidizing (oxygen-rich) or reducing (carbon monoxide-rich) depending on the job.
- **Mana or electric organ (magic):** drives electrolysis, induction heating and temperatures beyond any natural lava.

## Feeding

- **Ore:** oxides, sulfides, carbonates, native metals, sand.
- **Scrap:** any metal object, which it sorts and recycles.
- **Fuel and reductant:** wood, charcoal, coal, coke. Carbon both heats the body and strips oxygen from ore.
- **Flux:** limestone, shells, bone, borax. Binds impurities into slag.
- **Waste output:** slag, ash and gases such as carbon monoxide and sulfur dioxide. All of these are usable products.

## Metal processes

- **Smelting:** reduces ore with carbon so metal drops out and impurities float off in the slag.
- **Roasting:** burns sulfide ores in an oxygen pocket to turn them into oxides before smelting. The sulfur dioxide released becomes sulfuric acid.
- **Density sorting:** layers molten metals by weight so lead, copper, iron and gold separate on their own.
- **Cupellation:** absorbs gold and silver into molten lead then oxidizes the lead away into a bone-ash crust. Leaves pure precious metal.
- **Zone refining:** sweeps a narrow melted band through a metal bar repeatedly. Impurities ride along with the molten band and the rest becomes ultra-pure.
- **Degassing:** squeezes dissolved gases out of the melt for flawless castings.
- **Electrolysis (magic):** runs current through molten salts to extract aluminum, magnesium, sodium, lithium, calcium and titanium. These metals cannot be smelted with carbon.
- **Carburizing and decarburizing:** adds or removes carbon to make iron, steel or cast iron on demand. Preferred farm path in `Slimes.md`: convert iron first, then carburize. Carbon slows conversion.

## Shaping processes

- **Casting:** forms molds from its own crust or from sand, then fills them. Bells, cannons, statues, engine-style blocks, coins.
- **Lost-wax casting:** pairs with wax from the printer worm for jewelry and fine detail.
- **Wire drawing:** extrudes metal through narrowing ceramic pores in its skin.
- **Rolling:** presses metal between two hardened pseudopods into sheet, plate and foil.
- **Forging:** hammers with dense pseudopods while controlling temperature along the whole piece.
- **Pattern welding:** folds and fuses layers of high- and low-carbon steel into Damascus-style blades.
- **Powder sintering:** fuses metal or ceramic powder below melting point for tungsten parts, cermets and porous filters.
- **Single crystal growth:** slowly pulls a seed out of the melt to grow one continuous crystal. Turbine-style blades, silicon ingots, giant gem crystals.
- **Metallic glass:** flash-cools a thin film on the crust so atoms freeze before forming crystals. Amorphous metal is extremely springy and hard.

## Heat treatment

- **Quenching:** rapid cooling with water, oil or a cold pseudopod to harden steel.
- **Tempering:** controlled reheating that trades some hardness for toughness.
- **Annealing:** slow cooling that softens metal for working.
- **Differential hardening:** hard edge with a flexible spine on one blade, like Japanese swords.
- **Case hardening:** carbon-rich surface over a tough core.

## Alloys

- **Copper family:** bronze, brass, bell metal, speculum metal (mirror alloy), nickel silver, beryllium copper (spark-proof tools). Beryllium dust is the hazard in `ToxicMetalSubstitutes.md`. The preferred non-sparking swap there is Cu-Ni-Si, Cu-Ni-Sn or aluminum bronze.
- **Iron family:** wrought iron, cast iron, carbon steel, tool steel, wootz, stainless steel, spring steel, invar (barely expands with heat).
- **Precious family:** sterling silver, electrum, rose gold, white gold, platinum-iridium.
- **Low-melting family:** pewter, solder, type metal, babbitt bearing metal.
- **Specialty:** alnico magnets, titanium alloys, nickel superalloys, tungsten carbide cermet.

## Glass and volcanic materials

- **Soda-lime glass:** windows, bottles, beads.
- **Lead crystal:** heavy, brilliant tableware and optics.
- **Borosilicate:** heat-resistant labware and cookware.
- **Float glass:** pours glass across an internal bath of molten tin to produce perfectly flat sheets.
- **Glassblowing:** inflates glass with internal gas pockets.
- **Obsidian:** volcanic glass that fractures into edges sharper than steel scalpels.
- **Pumice and foam glass:** gas-filled glass. Light, floats, insulates.
- **Basalt fiber:** spun lava fiber stronger than glass fiber and fire-resistant. Rope, fabric, composite reinforcement.
- **Rock wool:** spun slag fiber for fireproof insulation.
- **Pele's hair and Pele's tears:** natural lava glass threads and droplets. Decorative or used as abrasive.
- **Enamel:** glass fused onto metal for cookware, signs and jewelry.

## Ceramics and building materials

- **Fired clay:** brick, tile, terracotta, stoneware, porcelain.
- **Refractory brick:** heat-proof linings for other furnaces.
- **Crucibles:** clay, graphite or alumina vessels.
- **Quicklime and slaked lime:** from roasted limestone. Mortar, plaster, whitewash.
- **Cement clinker:** limestone and clay roasted together for Portland-style cement.
- **Slag cement and aggregate:** waste slag ground for concrete or crushed for roads.
- **Glazes:** glass coatings in any color from metal oxides such as cobalt blue, copper green and iron red.

## Gems and crystals

- **Ruby and sapphire:** flame fusion of alumina with chromium or iron and titanium.
- **Spinel:** same flame fusion method with magnesium oxide.
- **Cubic zirconia:** melted inside a shell of its own unmelted powder (skull melting).
- **Quartz:** grown in pressurized hot water chambers.
- **Synthetic diamond (magic pressure):** carbon dissolved in molten iron-nickel under crushing pressure, then crystallized.
- **Moissanite and silicon carbide:** sand and carbon fused at extreme heat. Gems and abrasives.
- **Emerald:** grown from molten flux.
- **Silicon crystal:** single crystal ingots for electronics.

## Chemicals and byproducts

- **Charcoal and coke:** baked from wood and coal for fuel and steelmaking.
- **Graphite:** high-temperature carbon for crucibles, lubricant and pencils.
- **Calcium carbide:** lime and carbon fused. Makes acetylene gas when wet for lamps and welding.
- **Tungsten carbide and silicon carbide:** abrasives and cutting tips.
- **Sulfuric acid:** from captured sulfur dioxide.
- **Phosphorus:** from bone or phosphate rock in electric heat.
- **Litharge and red lead:** lead oxides for glassmaking and pigment.
- **Metal oxide pigments:** ochres, umber, cobalt blue, chrome yellow, lead white, vermilion.
- **Borax and soda glass fluxes.**

## Combined abilities

- **Recycler:** digests mixed scrap and separates every metal into pure ingots.
- **Assayer:** senses exact composition of anything it absorbs.
- **Mobile foundry:** casts, forges and heat-treats a complete item from ore in one body.
- **Welder:** fuses metal joints with molten filler.
- **Repairer:** fills cracks, recasts broken parts and rebuilds edges.
- **Weapon:** engulfs, sprays molten slag, launches obsidian shards, burns through doors.
- **Heating plant:** warms buildings, boils water, drives steam engines.
