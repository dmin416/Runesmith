## Perfectly Rigid Material

A truly perfectly hard material whose molecules don't vibrate.

### Core Rule

A perfectly rigid object has no internal vibration. The only way it can take in energy is by moving or rotating as one whole piece.

### Does Heat Get Through?

No. The particles that carry heat are far too small to move a perfectly rigid object enough to pass anything to the other side. A perfectly rigid solid is a near-perfect thermal insulator, not a conductor.

| What Hits a 2 kg Brick | Mass | Speed | Brick Moves At |
|---|---|---|---|
| One air molecule (room temp) | ~0.00000000000000000000000005 kg | ~500 m/s | ~0.00000000000000000000001 m/s |
| A falling snowflake | ~3 mg | ~1 m/s | ~1.5 micrometers per second |
| A sword strike | ~1.5 kg | ~10+ m/s | Several m/s |

- **One molecule:** Transfers roughly one ten-trillion-trillionth of its energy to the brick. Effectively nothing.
- **Billions of molecules:** They hit from every side at random and cancel each other out.
- **The whole brick's thermal motion:** At room temperature, all the heat the entire rigid brick can hold makes it drift at about 0.00000000005 m/s. That's about one atom's width every two seconds.

### Snow on a Brick

- **The snowflake's weight and impact:** A real push. It moves the whole brick a tiny but real amount, and the bottom moves the same amount instantly. That's mechanical force, not heat.
- **The snow's coldness:** Lives in the jiggling of individual water molecules. Those are far too light to move the brick, so the cold never reaches the other side.

### Properties

- **Heat and cold:** Blocked almost completely. Fire on one side, comfortable on the other.
- **Radiant heat:** Blocked as well if the surface reflects light, since it can't absorb it.
- **Touch:** Feels faintly warm to the hand because it never draws body heat away.
- **Impacts:** Swords, arrows, hammers and snowballs are massive enough to move it as a whole. Collisions rebound fully, knockback still applies and force spreads evenly across every contact point.
- **Light:** Can't absorb light without heating, so it reflects or transmits all of it. A perfect mirror or perfectly clear.
- **Sound:** Silent when struck and immune to resonance. The only sound comes from whatever hits it.
- **Electricity:** Insulator.
- **Fabrication:** Can't be cut, bent, melted or engraved. Its final shape and any inscriptions have to be set at creation.

### Uses

- **Armor:** Protects anyone from fire, frost and elemental heat. Rebounds weapon strikes back into the attacker's blade.
- **Insulation:** Perfect walls for forges, furnaces, cold storage and Lili's lava chambers.
- **Weapon breaker:** Shields and bracers that send full impact energy back into enemy weapons.
- **Light reflector:** Turns flash attacks and light spells back toward the caster.

### Weaknesses

- **Knockback:** Momentum still moves the wearer.
- **Unrepairable and unalterable:** Any mistake in its shape is permanent.
- **Mana behavior:** Undecided.

### Name Ideas

- Stillstone
- Null Lattice
- Stasis Steel
- Mirror Adamant
- Quiet Metal

## Mana-Saturated Nitinol

Nickel-titanium shape memory alloy saturated with mana, built on adamantium (mana-saturated titanium) being truly indestructible.

### Real Nitinol Baseline

- **Composition:** Roughly half nickel and half titanium by atom count.
- **Two phases:** A rigid high-temperature phase and a soft, easily bent low-temperature phase.
- **Shape memory:** Bend it in the soft phase, heat it and it snaps back to its trained shape.
- **Superelasticity:** Flexes several percent and springs back fully.
- **Trigger temperature:** Set by the exact nickel ratio. Real high-temperature shape memory alloys trigger at several hundred degrees.

### Trigger: High Heat

- **Room temperature:** Soft and malleable. Bends, folds and can be shaped by hand or hammer.
- **Heating up:** Snaps back to its trained shape as it passes the trigger temperature.
- **Fully hot:** Locked and indestructible.
- **Cooling down:** Softens again once it drops below the trigger.

**Why heat:**

- True to real nitinol.
- D is immune to heat and cold, so he can handle locked gear that burns anyone else.
- Heat is in his pure-mana spell set.
- Lili is a living heat source.
- Most people can't use high-heat gear near their bodies, which keeps it rare.
- Fire mages could force a lock and ice mages could force a soften, giving story counterplay.

### Two Lock Types

**Hold-Heat Lock**

- Indestructible only while kept hot. Softens as soon as it cools.
- Needs a constant heat supply from the Heat spell, Lili or a rune heater.
- Best for armor and weapons that only need to be invincible in combat.

**One-Shot Lock (Wide Hysteresis)**

- Locks at one temperature and unlocks at a much lower one. Real pipe couplings work this way.
- Once heated and locked, it stays locked at room temperature.
- Has to be chilled far below room temperature to soften again.
- Best for vehicle frames, glider spars, pitons and permanent structures.

### Properties

- **Dual state:** Indestructible when locked. Bendable and workable when soft.
- **Perfect memory:** Always returns to its trained shape. No fatigue.
- **Retraining:** Changing its trained shape likely takes overwhelming mana.
- **Perfect spring:** Fatigue-free superelasticity stores mechanical energy indefinitely.
- **Artificial muscle:** Wire contracts when heated, enabling golems, prosthetics, locks and powered joints.
- **Mana conductivity:** Better than pure adamantium thanks to the nickel half.
- **Inherited weakness:** Takes on adamantium's weakness (magic or sound, undecided). Locked adamantium weakness today is sound resonance (`Metals.md`).

### Flavor Details

- **Glow:** Locked gear above about 525°C glows dull red to orange.
- **Burn hazard:** Anyone grabbing D's locked gear gets burned.
- **Heat loss:** Hold-heat gear radiates heat fast and drains mana.
- **Rain and water:** Cool hold-heat gear and pull on its heat supply.
- **Counterplay:** Ice magic, water or quenching can soften hold-heat gear mid-fight. One-shot gear needs extreme cold to undo.
- **Storage:** Folds small at room temperature, snaps to full shape when heated.

### Fabrication

- **Shape first, lock after:** Forge, sharpen and inscribe in the soft state. Once locked, nothing can cut, file or engrave it.
- **Conversion difficulty:** Titanium converts slowest. Nickel may give it a head start.
- **Grades:** Low conversion gives fatigue-free superelastic metal that can still break. High conversion gives a true indestructible locked state.
- **Canon fit:** No titanium alloys exist on Terra, so nitinol is a new invention from D's Earth knowledge. Nickel is SPECIALTY (`Materials.md`). Mundane titanium is not street stock.

### Uses

- Reactive armor that locks at the instant of impact
- Self-straightening blades with edges that never dull once locked
- Folding glider spars
- Collapsible staffs, spears and shields
- Restraints that wrap and lock
- Climbing grapnels and pitons
- Flexing armor joints
- A skeleton frame inside Lili's living armor

### Name Ideas

- Memory Adamant
- Living Adamant
- Adamantine Memory
- Nitamant
- Shiftsteel

## Hard Insulating Heat Conductor

A material that is extremely hard, conducts no electricity and conducts heat nearly perfectly.

### Real Basis

Diamond already does it:

- **Hardness:** The hardest natural material.
- **Electrical conductivity:** Essentially none.
- **Thermal conductivity:** About 2,000 to 2,200 W/m·K, roughly five times copper. Isotopically pure diamond reaches around 3,300.

### Why the Three Go Together

- Metals carry heat with free electrons, so good metal heat conductors also conduct electricity.
- Insulators carry heat with lattice vibrations instead.
- Stiff lattices of light, tightly bonded atoms carry those vibrations fast and efficiently.
- Those same stiff lattices make materials hard.

### Real Examples

| Material | Hardness | Electrical | Thermal Conductivity (W/m·K) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diamond | Hardest | Insulator | 2,000 to 3,300 |
| Cubic boron arsenide | Hard | Semiconductor | ~1,300 |
| Cubic boron nitride | Second hardest | Insulator | ~740 |
| Beryllium oxide | Hard ceramic | Insulator | ~330 |
| Copper (comparison) | Soft | Excellent conductor | ~400 |

### Limits

- Near-perfect hardness and conductivity are possible. Truly perfect versions of either aren't, and magic covers that last step.
- It needs some internal vibration to carry heat, so it can never be perfectly rigid. A perfectly rigid material becomes an insulator instead.

### Fantasy Fit

- Carbon-based, not metal. Titanium and adamantium conduct electricity and titanium is a poor heat conductor. Locked adamantium is already a heat spreader (`Metals.md`). This note is the carbon / diamond path, not a retcon of that.

### Uses

- **Heat delivery for nitinol gear:** Carries heat from the Heat spell or Lili into locked gear with almost no loss.
- **Forge tools:** Tongs, anvil faces and crucibles that spread heat evenly and never wear.
- **Rune substrates:** Electrically inert surfaces that draw heat away from runes.
- **Armor heat spreading:** Spreads fire attacks across the whole surface instead of one spot.
- **Heat sinks:** Cooling for vehicles, runes and mana systems.
