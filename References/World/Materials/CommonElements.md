## Alkali Metals

### Lithium (Li)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 6.94 | Solid | 0.534 | 180.5 | 1342 |

- **Harvest:** Hard-rock spodumene mines in Australia. Evaporated salt-flat brines in Chile, Argentina and Bolivia (the "Lithium Triangle"). China mines and refines a large share.
- **Uses:** Lithium-ion batteries (largest use), heat-resistant glass and ceramics, lubricating grease, aluminum-lithium aerospace alloys, psychiatric medication (lithium carbonate).
- **Notable:** Lightest metal. Soft enough to cut with a knife. Floats on water.

### Sodium (Na)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | 22.990 | Solid | 0.968 | 97.8 | 883 |

- **Harvest:** Rock salt (halite) mines, solar evaporation of seawater, brine wells. Metallic sodium is made by electrolysis of molten salt (Downs process).
- **Uses:** Table salt, sodium hydroxide, baking soda, soda ash for glassmaking, sodium vapor street lamps, coolant in some nuclear reactors.
- **Notable:** Reacts violently with water. Stored under oil.

### Potassium (K)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 19 | 39.098 | Solid | 0.862 | 63.5 | 759 |

- **Harvest:** Potash minerals (sylvite, carnallite) mined underground or by solution mining. Canada (Saskatchewan) is the top producer, followed by Russia, Belarus and China.
- **Uses:** Fertilizer (over 90%), potassium hydroxide for soap, salt substitutes, potassium nitrate for gunpowder and fertilizer.
- **Notable:** More reactive than sodium. Burns with a lilac flame. Essential electrolyte for nerve and muscle function.

## Alkaline Earth Metals

### Magnesium (Mg)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | 24.305 | Solid | 1.738 | 650 | 1091 |

- **Harvest:** Magnesite, dolomite, seawater and natural brines. China produces most of the world's magnesium metal using the Pidgeon process.
- **Uses:** Lightweight alloys for car parts and electronics casings, aluminum alloying, steel desulfurization, flares and fireworks, antacids and supplements, heat-resistant furnace linings (MgO).
- **Notable:** Lightest structural metal. Burns with a brilliant white flame. Third most abundant element dissolved in seawater.

### Calcium (Ca)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 40.078 | Solid | 1.55 | 842 | 1484 |

- **Harvest:** Quarried worldwide as limestone, chalk, marble and gypsum. Metallic calcium is made by aluminothermic reduction of lime or electrolysis of calcium chloride.
- **Uses:** Cement and concrete, lime for steelmaking and water treatment, drywall and plaster (gypsum), filler in paper and plastics, dietary supplements, dental materials (calcium hydroxide, hydroxyapatite).
- **Notable:** Main mineral in bones and teeth (hydroxyapatite).

## Transition Metals

### Titanium (Ti)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 22 | 47.867 | Solid | 4.506 | 1668 | 3287 |

- **Harvest:** Ilmenite and rutile from mineral sands in China, Mozambique, South Africa, Australia and Canada. Refined to metal by the Kroll process.
- **Uses:** About 95% becomes titanium dioxide white pigment (paint, paper, sunscreen, toothpaste). Metal goes into aerospace alloys, dental and orthopedic implants, chemical plant equipment, sporting goods.
- **Notable:** Strength comparable to steel at about 45% less weight. Bonds directly with bone (osseointegration). Highly corrosion resistant.

### Vanadium (V)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 23 | 50.942 | Solid | 6.11 | 1910 | 3407 |

- **Harvest:** Vanadium-bearing titanomagnetite ore and recycled steel slag. China, Russia, South Africa and Brazil lead production.
- **Uses:** Steel alloying (about 90%) for tool steel, rebar and springs. Ti-6Al-4V titanium alloy for aircraft and implants. Vanadium redox flow batteries. Catalyst for sulfuric acid production.
- **Notable:** Compounds show a wide range of colors due to multiple oxidation states. Named after the Norse goddess Vanadís.

### Chromium (Cr)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | 51.996 | Solid | 7.19 | 1907 | 2671 |

- **Harvest:** Chromite ore smelted into ferrochrome. South Africa produces the most, followed by Kazakhstan, Turkey and India.
- **Uses:** Stainless steel (at least 10.5% chromium), chrome plating, superalloys, pigments, leather tanning, furnace bricks.
- **Notable:** Forms the invisible oxide layer that keeps stainless steel from rusting. Gives rubies their red color and emeralds their green.

### Manganese (Mn)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 54.938 | Solid | 7.21 | 1246 | 2061 |

- **Harvest:** Pyrolusite and other manganese oxides. South Africa, Gabon, Australia and China lead production.
- **Uses:** Steelmaking (about 90%) as an alloy and to remove oxygen and sulfur, aluminum beverage can alloy, alkaline and lithium-ion battery cathodes, fertilizer, water treatment (potassium permanganate).
- **Notable:** Essential trace nutrient. Large deposits sit on the deep ocean floor as manganese nodules.

### Iron (Fe)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 26 | 55.845 | Solid | 7.874 | 1538 | 2862 |

- **Harvest:** Hematite and magnetite from open-pit mines in Australia, Brazil, China and India. Smelted in blast furnaces with coke and limestone. Scrap is remelted in electric arc furnaces.
- **Uses:** Steel, cast iron, construction, vehicles, machinery, tools, magnets, iron supplements.
- **Notable:** Most used metal by tonnage worldwide, followed by aluminum and copper. Makes up most of Earth's core. Fusion in stars stops at iron because fusing it consumes energy rather than releasing it. Carries oxygen in blood as part of hemoglobin.

### Cobalt (Co)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 27 | 58.933 | Solid | 8.90 | 1495 | 2927 |

- **Harvest:** Mostly a byproduct of copper and nickel mining. DR Congo supplies about 70%, followed by Indonesia, Russia and Australia.
- **Uses:** Lithium-ion battery cathodes, jet engine superalloys, cobalt-chrome dental and orthopedic alloys, permanent magnets, blue pigment, catalysts.
- **Notable:** Magnetic. Sits at the center of vitamin B12.

### Nickel (Ni)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 28 | 58.693 | Solid | 8.908 | 1455 | 2913 |

- **Harvest:** Laterite ores in Indonesia, the Philippines and New Caledonia. Sulfide ores (pentlandite) in Russia (Norilsk), Canada (Sudbury) and Australia. Indonesia now produces over half the world's supply.
- **Uses:** Stainless steel (about two thirds), battery cathodes, superalloys, plating, coins.
- **Notable:** Magnetic. The Sudbury Basin deposit was formed by an ancient meteorite impact. Common cause of skin allergies.

### Copper (Cu)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 29 | 63.546 | Solid | 8.96 | 1085 | 2562 |

- **Harvest:** Porphyry deposits (chalcopyrite) mined in huge open pits. Chile is the top producer, followed by DR Congo, Peru, China and the US. Refined by smelting or acid leaching followed by electrowinning.
- **Uses:** Electrical wiring and motors (about 60%), plumbing, roofing, brass and bronze, electronics, electric vehicles, wind and solar power systems, antimicrobial surfaces.
- **Notable:** Second best electrical conductor after silver. Among the first metals worked by humans, around 9000 BC. Weathers to a green patina.

### Zinc (Zn)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 65.38 | Solid | 7.14 | 419.5 | 907 |

- **Harvest:** Sphalerite ore, often found with lead and silver. China, Peru, Australia and India lead production. Refined by roasting and electrolysis.
- **Uses:** Galvanizing steel (about 50%), brass, die casting, zinc oxide for rubber and sunscreen, batteries, supplements, dental cements (zinc phosphate, zinc oxide eugenol).
- **Notable:** Used as a sacrificial anode to protect ship hulls and pipelines. Essential for immune function.

### Molybdenum (Mo)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 42 | 95.95 | Solid | 10.28 | 2623 | 4639 |

- **Harvest:** Molybdenite from dedicated mines or as a byproduct of copper mining. China, Peru, Chile and the US lead production.
- **Uses:** High-strength and stainless steel alloys (about 80%), oil refining catalysts, molybdenum disulfide lubricant, furnace heating elements, electronics.
- **Notable:** One of the highest melting points of any element. Essential trace nutrient used by enzymes, including those that fix nitrogen in soil bacteria.

### Tungsten (W)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 74 | 183.84 | Solid | 19.25 | 3422 | 5555 |

- **Harvest:** Wolframite and scheelite ores. China produces about 80%, followed by Vietnam, Russia and Bolivia.
- **Uses:** Tungsten carbide cutting tools, drill bits and mining tools (about 60%), alloy steels, light filaments, welding electrodes, counterweights, armor-piercing ammunition, radiation shielding.
- **Notable:** Highest melting point of any metal. Nearly as dense as gold.

## Precious Metals

### Palladium (Pd)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 46 | 106.42 | Solid | 12.02 | 1555 | 2963 |

- **Harvest:** Byproduct of nickel and platinum mining. Russia (Norilsk) supplies about 40%, followed by South Africa, Canada and the US (Stillwater, Montana).
- **Uses:** Catalytic converters (about 80%), electronics, dental alloys, hydrogen purification, white gold jewelry.
- **Notable:** Absorbs up to 900 times its own volume of hydrogen.

### Silver (Ag)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 47 | 107.87 | Solid | 10.49 | 961.8 | 2162 |

- **Harvest:** Mostly a byproduct of lead, zinc, copper and gold mining. Mexico is the top producer, followed by China, Peru, Chile and Poland.
- **Uses:** Electronics and electrical contacts, solar panels, jewelry and silverware, mirrors, antimicrobial coatings, brazing alloys, dental amalgam alloy, historically photography.
- **Notable:** Highest electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity and reflectivity of any element.

### Platinum (Pt)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 78 | 195.08 | Solid | 21.45 | 1768 | 3825 |

- **Harvest:** South Africa's Bushveld Complex supplies about 70%, followed by Russia, Zimbabwe, Canada and the US.
- **Uses:** Catalytic converters, jewelry, chemical and oil refining catalysts, hydrogen fuel cells, lab equipment, pacemaker electrodes, chemotherapy drugs (cisplatin), hard drive coatings.
- **Notable:** Extremely corrosion resistant. Rarer than gold in Earth's crust.

### Gold (Au)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 79 | 196.97 | Solid | 19.32 | 1064 | 2970 |

- **Harvest:** Hard-rock lode deposits and riverbed placer deposits. China, Russia, Australia, Canada, the US and Ghana lead production. Extracted mainly by cyanide leaching and gravity separation.
- **Uses:** Jewelry (about 50%), investment and central bank reserves, electronics contacts, dental crowns and alloys, satellite and astronaut visor coatings, medicine.
- **Notable:** Most malleable metal. One gram can be beaten into a sheet about one square meter in size. Never tarnishes.

## Post-Transition Metals

### Aluminum (Al)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | 26.982 | Solid | 2.70 | 660.3 | 2470 |

- **Harvest:** Bauxite ore from Australia, Guinea, China and Brazil. Refined to alumina (Bayer process) then smelted by electrolysis (Hall-Héroult process). China produces over half of the world's aluminum.
- **Uses:** Aircraft, car bodies, cans, foil, construction, power lines, electronics casings.
- **Notable:** Most abundant metal in the crust. Recycling uses about 5% of the energy needed to make new aluminum. A thin oxide layer prevents corrosion.

### Tin (Sn)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 118.71 | Solid | 7.29 | 231.9 | 2602 |

- **Harvest:** Cassiterite from riverbed and hard-rock deposits. China, Indonesia, Myanmar, Peru, DR Congo and Brazil lead production.
- **Uses:** Solder (about 50%), tinplate for food cans, chemicals, bronze, pewter, molten tin baths for making flat window glass.
- **Notable:** Makes a crackling "tin cry" when bent. Below 13.2 °C it slowly turns into brittle gray powder (tin pest).

### Lead (Pb)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 82 | 207.2 | Solid | 11.34 | 327.5 | 1749 |

- **Harvest:** Galena ore, usually mined with zinc and silver. China, Australia, Peru, the US and Mexico lead production. Recycling supplies more than half of all lead used.
- **Uses:** Lead-acid car batteries (about 80%), radiation shielding including X-ray aprons, ammunition, cable sheathing, weights.
- **Notable:** Toxic heavy metal. Stable end product of several natural radioactive decay chains.

## Metalloids

### Boron (B)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 10.81 | Solid | 2.34 | 2076 | 3927 |

- **Harvest:** Borate minerals (borax, colemanite, ulexite). Turkey holds about 70% of world reserves. The US mines it at Boron, California.
- **Uses:** Borosilicate glass, fiberglass insulation, detergents, boron carbide body armor, nuclear reactor control rods, plant micronutrient.
- **Notable:** Boron carbide is one of the hardest known materials.

### Silicon (Si)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | 28.085 | Solid | 2.329 | 1414 | 3265 |

- **Harvest:** Quartz and quartzite reduced with carbon in electric arc furnaces. China dominates production.
- **Uses:** Aluminum and steel alloys, computer chips, solar cells, silicones (sealants, lubricants, medical implants), glass, concrete.
- **Notable:** Second most abundant element in the crust. Chip-grade silicon is refined to about 99.999999999% purity.

## Nonmetals

### Hydrogen (H)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.008 | Gas | 0.0899 | -259.2 | -252.9 |

- **Harvest:** Steam reforming of natural gas (most production), coal gasification, electrolysis of water.
- **Uses:** Ammonia fertilizer production, oil refining, methanol, fuel cells, rocket fuel.
- **Notable:** Lightest and most abundant element in the universe, about 74% of normal matter by mass.

### Carbon (C)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 12.011 | Solid | 2.27 (graphite), 3.51 (diamond) | Sublimes ~3640 | N/A |

- **Harvest:** Coal, crude oil and natural gas. Graphite from China, Mozambique, Madagascar and Brazil. Diamonds from Russia, Botswana, Canada and DR Congo.
- **Uses:** Fuels, coke for steelmaking, plastics, graphite electrodes and battery anodes, diamond jewelry and cutting tools, activated carbon filters, carbon fiber.
- **Notable:** Basis of all known life. Forms millions of compounds. Turns directly from solid to gas at normal pressure.

### Nitrogen (N)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 14.007 | Gas | 1.251 | -210.0 | -195.8 |

- **Harvest:** Fractional distillation of liquid air or membrane separation.
- **Uses:** Ammonia fertilizer (Haber-Bosch process), inert food packaging gas, liquid nitrogen cryogenics, tire inflation, explosives, electronics manufacturing.
- **Notable:** Makes up about 78% of the atmosphere. Its strong triple bond makes N₂ gas very unreactive.

### Oxygen (O)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 15.999 | Gas | 1.429 | -218.8 | -183.0 |

- **Harvest:** Fractional distillation of liquid air or pressure swing adsorption.
- **Uses:** Steelmaking (largest use), medical oxygen, welding and cutting torches, rocket oxidizer, water treatment, chemical production.
- **Notable:** Most abundant element in the crust. Produced by photosynthesis. Liquid oxygen is pale blue and magnetic.

### Phosphorus (P)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | 30.974 | Solid | 1.82 (white) | 44.2 (white) | 280.5 |

- **Harvest:** Phosphate rock (apatite) from open-pit mines. China, Morocco, the US and Russia lead production. Morocco holds about 70% of world reserves.
- **Uses:** Fertilizer (about 90%), animal feed, detergents, matches, food additives such as phosphoric acid in soda, flame retardants.
- **Notable:** White phosphorus glows in the dark and ignites on its own in air. Essential to DNA and ATP.

### Sulfur (S)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 32.06 | Solid | 2.07 | 115.2 | 444.6 |

- **Harvest:** Mostly recovered from oil refining and natural gas processing (Claus process). Also found in volcanic deposits. China, the US, Russia, Saudi Arabia and the UAE lead production.
- **Uses:** Sulfuric acid (the most produced industrial chemical, mostly for fertilizer), rubber vulcanization, gunpowder, fungicides, matches.
- **Notable:** Bright yellow crystals. Burns with a blue flame.

## Halogens

### Fluorine (F)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | 18.998 | Gas | 1.696 | -219.7 | -188.1 |

- **Harvest:** Fluorite (fluorspar) mined in China, Mexico, Mongolia and South Africa. Pure fluorine is made by electrolysis of potassium fluoride in hydrogen fluoride.
- **Uses:** Hydrofluoric acid, refrigerants, Teflon (PTFE), fluoride toothpaste and water fluoridation, lithium battery electrolytes, aluminum smelting.

### Chlorine (Cl)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Harvest:** Electrolysis of saltwater brine (chlor-alkali process), which also produces sodium hydroxide.
- **Uses:** PVC plastic, water disinfection, bleach, pharmaceuticals, titanium refining, hydrochloric acid.
- **Notable:** Toxic yellow-green gas. First used as a battlefield weapon at Ypres in 1915.

### Iodine (I)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 53 | 126.90 | Solid | 4.93 | 113.7 | 184.3 |

- **Harvest:** Chile supplies about 60% from caliche ore in the Atacama Desert. Japan and the US extract it from natural gas brines.
- **Uses:** X-ray contrast agents, disinfectants (povidone-iodine), iodized salt, LCD screen polarizing film, catalysts, animal feed.
- **Notable:** Solid crystals turn directly into violet vapor. Deficiency causes goiter.

## Noble Gases

### Helium (He)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 4.0026 | Gas | 0.1786 | Does not solidify at normal pressure | -268.9 |

- **Harvest:** Separated from natural gas fields where radioactive decay has trapped it underground. The US, Qatar, Algeria and Russia lead production.
- **Uses:** MRI magnet cooling, balloons and airships, welding shielding gas, leak detection, deep-sea diving gas mixes, semiconductor manufacturing.
- **Notable:** Lowest boiling point of any element. Nonrenewable on Earth because it escapes into space once released.

### Neon (Ne)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 20.180 | Gas | 0.900 | -248.6 | -246.1 |

- **Harvest:** Fractional distillation of liquid air, where it makes up about 18 parts per million.
- **Uses:** Neon signs, helium-neon lasers, excimer lasers for computer chip manufacturing, cryogenic refrigerant.
- **Notable:** Glows red-orange in electric discharge. Forms no stable compounds.

### Argon (Ar)

| Atomic # | Atomic Mass | Phase | Density | Melting Pt | Boiling Pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | 39.948 | Gas | 1.784 | -189.3 | -185.8 |

- **Harvest:** Byproduct of fractional distillation of liquid air.
- **Uses:** Welding shielding gas, stainless steel refining, light bulbs, insulated window filling, semiconductor manufacturing.
- **Notable:** Most abundant noble gas in the atmosphere. Most of it comes from radioactive decay of potassium-40 in rocks.
