## Baseline read

Runesmith World features hunters, swordsmen, knights, monsters, goblins and kingdoms. That points to a medieval European baseline with a fantasy overlay. The closest historical window is the High Middle Ages (about 1000 to 1300 AD).

### Baseline technology

- Iron and steel weapons and armor: swords, spears, axes, crossbows and longbows
- Plate armor for heavily armored knights, mail and gambeson otherwise
- Castles, stone walls and fortified towns
- Horse-drawn transport, sailing ships, water mills and wind mills
- No firearms: magic fills the role of ranged and heavy weaponry
- No printing press, no mass manufacturing
- Candles, torches and hand-copied books

### Historical parallels

- Kingdoms, knights and feudal lords match the feudal systems of France, England and the Holy Roman Empire
- Hunters fit forest-dwelling, game-keeping or bounty-style work
- Swordsmen as a distinct class are mostly a fantasy trope: real medieval fighting leaned on spearmen and archers
- Guilds, taverns and adventuring parties are fantasy inventions layered onto medieval society

### Era rule of thumb

- Full plate with crossbows leans toward the 1300s to 1400s
- Most fantasy blends these eras into "generic medieval"

### Revised historical approximation

Social structure stays medieval (kingdoms, nobles, knights, Royals) while technology reaches toward the early Industrial Revolution (roughly 1760 to 1850). The result is a hybrid closest to fantasy steampunk / magitech. Magic stones fill the role that coal, oil and electricity fill in real history. Airships have no real historical parallel and serve as a prestige item similar to a royal yacht or private railcar.

### Economy and agriculture effects

- A three-tier coin system matches the real medieval and early modern pattern of copper for commoners, silver for merchants and gold for nobles and large trade (project ladder is finer: SC/LC/SS/LS/SG/LG/RG in `Economy.md`)
- Widespread coinage supports merchants, banks and taxation beyond barter and feudal obligation
- Tamed farming monsters replace or supplement draft animals such as oxen and horses, which raises farm output and supports larger towns
- Monster taming implies professions (tamer / breeder / beastmaster) and links to skills: Roland's **Basic Taming** (Ch 12) / Ned path in `../People/Ned.md`, `Mounts.md`
- Trains and steam power mean long-distance travel and bulk hauling exist beyond horse and cart
- Magic stones replace or supplement fuel and lighting
- Magic takes the place of firearms and explosives in warfare and industry
- Royal control of airships gives the crown a strategic and status advantage
