# Mainland Mountain Range (Edelgard) Flora

> Design loot. Temperate Alpine and Central European model for Edelgard's cold, autumn-like, rocky, heavily mined range split by deep gorges. Not chapter lock. Place: `Places.md`. Nav: `Index.md`.

---

## Foothill forest (300–800 m)

**Trees**
- **Sessile oak** (*Quercus petraea*) and **pedunculate oak** (*Q. robur*): main timber and charcoal trees. Bark for tanning.
- **European beech** (*Fagus sylvatica*): copper-orange autumn color. Excellent charcoal and tool wood.
- **Hornbeam** (*Carpinus betulus*): very hard pale wood for mill cogs, mallets and chopping blocks.
- **Sweet chestnut** (*Castanea sativa*): staple nut. Rot-resistant poles and posts.
- **Hazel** (*Corylus avellana*): coppiced for wattle, hurdles and stakes. Nuts.
- **Field maple** and **Norway maple:** yellow autumn color.
- **Ash** (*Fraxinus excelsior*): shock-proof wood for tool handles, spear shafts and wheels.
- **Small-leaved lime / linden** (*Tilia cordata*): soft carving wood. Inner bark (bast) makes rope.
- **Wild cherry** (*Prunus avium*): fine cabinet wood. Fruit.
- **Walnut** (*Juglans regia*): nuts, oil and gunstock wood. Husks give brown dye.
- **Wild service tree** (*Sorbus torminalis*): dense wood for screws and instrument parts.
- **Crab apple** and **wild pear:** sour fruit for cider and perry.
- **Yew** (*Taxus baccata*): bow wood. Every part toxic.
- **Holly:** evergreen. White dense wood for inlay.
- **Scots pine** (*Pinus sylvestris*): on poor dry ridges. Resin for pitch and tar.
- **Box** (*Buxus sempervirens*): very fine dense wood for engraving blocks, rulers and precision tool parts.

**Shrubs and ground plants**
- **Blackthorn**, **hawthorn**, **dog rose** and **elder** on forest edges.
- **Juniper** on dry slopes. Berries flavor spirits.
- **Bracken**, **bilberry** and **heather** on acid soil.
- **Wild garlic / ramsons**, **wood anemone**, **bluebell** and **lily of the valley** in spring woods.
- **Foxglove:** heart medicine and poison.
- **St John's wort:** wound oil.

## Montane mixed forest (800–1,600 m)

- **Silver fir** (*Abies alba*), **Norway spruce** and **beech** together.
- **Sycamore maple** (*Acer pseudoplatanus*): white wood for kitchen tools and instrument backs.
- **Rowan / mountain ash** (*Sorbus aucuparia*): red berry clusters. Folk protection tree.
- **Wych elm** (*Ulmus glabra*): water-resistant wood for pumps and pipes.
- **Goat willow:** early pollen and catkins.
- **Wood sorrel**, **oak fern**, **lady fern** and **herb Paris** on the forest floor.
- **Monkshood / wolfsbane** (*Aconitum*): blue hooded flowers. Deadly poison.
- **White false hellebore** (*Veratrum album*): broad pleated leaves. Toxic.
- **Martagon lily:** pink turban flowers.
- **Baneberry:** glossy toxic berries.

## Subalpine forest and treeline (1,600–2,300 m)

- **European larch** (*Larix decidua*): needles turn gold then drop in autumn. Gives the range its autumn look. Rot-proof wood for mine timbers, shingles and bridges.
- **Norway spruce** (*Picea abies*): straight pit props and construction beams. Resin.
- **Swiss stone pine / arolla** (*Pinus cembra*): edible pine nuts. Fragrant soft wood for carving, chests and paneling.
- **Mountain pine** (*Pinus mugo*): twisted krummholz thickets at the treeline. Oil for chest salves.
- **Green alder** (*Alnus alnobetula*): dense scrub on avalanche tracks.
- **Alpenrose** (*Rhododendron ferrugineum*): pink-red flowering shrub carpets.
- **Bilberry**, **lingonberry** and **crowberry:** berry ground cover.
- **Dwarf juniper:** low mats on rock.

---

## Alpine meadows (2,300–3,000 m)

- **Edelweiss** (*Leontopodium nivale*): woolly white star flower on high limestone.
- **Trumpet gentian** (*Gentiana acaulis*) and **spring gentian** (*G. verna*): deep blue flowers.
- **Yellow gentian** (*Gentiana lutea*): tall plant. Root makes bitter tonic and spirits.
- **Arnica** (*Arnica montana*): orange-yellow daisy. Bruise salve.
- **Alpine aster**, **alpine forget-me-not** and **alpine pasque flower.**
- **Globeflower** (*Trollius europaeus*): yellow globe flowers in wet meadows.
- **Mountain avens** (*Dryas octopetala*): white flowers and feathery seed heads.
- **Black vanilla orchid** (*Nigritella*): dark red, vanilla-scented.
- **Dwarf willows** (*Salix herbacea*, *S. retusa*): trees a few centimeters tall.
- **Moss campion** (*Silene acaulis*): pink cushion plant.
- **Purple saxifrage:** first bloom after snowmelt.
- **Iceland moss** (*Cetraria islandica*): lichen used as famine food and cough medicine.
- **Reindeer lichen:** gray-green carpets.

## Rock, scree and cliffs (all heights)

- **Houseleek** (*Sempervivum*): succulent rosettes on rock and roofs.
- **Stonecrops** (*Sedum*): yellow and white flowers on ledges.
- **Saxifrages** (*Saxifraga paniculata*): silver-encrusted rosettes.
- **Rock jasmine** (*Androsace*): tight cushions.
- **Roseroot** (*Rhodiola rosea*): rose-scented root. Fatigue remedy.
- **Mountain sorrel** (*Oxyria digyna*): sour edible leaves on scree.
- **Alpine toadflax:** purple and orange flowers on moving scree.
- **Glacier crowfoot** (*Ranunculus glacialis*): highest flowering plant. White to pink flowers near glaciers.
- **Parsley fern**, **maidenhair spleenwort**, **wall rue** and **rustyback fern** in cracks.
- **Map lichen** (*Rhizocarpon geographicum*): yellow-green patches with black borders. Grows slowly enough to date rockfalls.

---

## Gorge, rivers and waterfalls

- **Gray alder** and **black alder:** stream banks. Underwater-durable wood for sluices, mill wheels and foundations.
- **Alder buckthorn** (*Frangula alnus*): finest charcoal for gunpowder.
- **White willow** and **purple osier willow:** basket rods, hurdles and willow-bark pain remedy.
- **Butterbur** (*Petasites*): huge leaves along streams.
- **Marsh marigold**, **meadowsweet**, **valerian** and **angelica** on wet banks.
- **Touch-me-not balsam** (*Impatiens noli-tangere*): yellow flowers in shaded ravines.
- **Hart's-tongue fern**, **ostrich fern** and **filmy ferns** in the waterfall spray zone.
- **Horsetail** (*Equisetum*): silica-rich stems used to scour and polish metal.
- **Liverworts** and **water moss** (*Fontinalis*) on wet rock.

## High moors and bogs

- **Sphagnum moss:** peat former. Wound dressing.
- **Peat:** cut and dried as fuel.
- **Cotton grass:** white tufts.
- **Round-leaved sundew:** carnivorous.
- **Cloudberry:** orange berries.
- **Bog bilberry**, **cranberry** and **bog rosemary.**
- **Bogbean:** bitter tonic.

## Ore indicators and mine ground

**Metal-ground plants**
- **Alpine catchfly** (*Viscaria alpina*): copper indicator.
- **Bladder campion** (*Silene vulgaris*): metal-tolerant forms on spoil.
- **Spring sandwort / leadwort** (*Minuartia verna*): lead and zinc ground.
- **Zinc violet** (*Viola calaminaria*): yellow violets on zinc ore.
- **Alpine pennycress** (*Noccaea caerulescens*): zinc and cadmium accumulator.
- **Halleri thrift** (*Armeria maritima halleri*): pink flowers on metal-rich soil.
- **Halleri rockcress** (*Arabidopsis halleri*): zinc and cadmium hyperaccumulator.
- **Metal-tolerant grasses** (*Agrostis capillaris*, *Festuca ovina*): carpets old mine dumps.

**Mosses and lichens on ore**
- **Copper mosses** (*Mielichhoferia*, *Scopelophila*): grow almost only on copper-rich rock.
- **Rusty lichens** (*Acarospora sinopica*): red-brown crusts on iron sulfide rock.

**Serpentine barrens**
- **Alyssum** (*Alyssum*): nickel accumulators with yellow flowers.
- **Serpentine spleenworts** (*Asplenium cuneifolium*, *A. adulterinum*): ferns found only on serpentine.
- Stunted pines and bare patches mark the barrens.

**Acid mine drainage**
- Orange iron-bacteria slime and green *Euglena* films in runoff streams.
- Dead zones where acid water spreads.

**Spoil heaps and slag**
- **Silver birch** and **downy birch:** first trees on spoil. Bark tar for glue and leather dressing.
- **Fireweed / rosebay willowherb:** pink spires on burned and disturbed ground.
- **Coltsfoot:** yellow flowers on bare clay. Cough remedy.
- **Nettle:** thick around smithies, privies and middens. Fiber and greens.
- **Dock**, **mugwort** and **elder** around settlements.

## Managed woods for a blacksmith city

- **Coppice rotations** of oak, hornbeam, hazel and beech every 15–25 years for charcoal.
- **Charcoal platforms:** flat burned hearths across the slopes with fireweed and birch moving in afterward.
- **Collier camps** of turf-and-pole huts beside the burns.
- Bare hillsides near the oldest mines from centuries of cutting.
