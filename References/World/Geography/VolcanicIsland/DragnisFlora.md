## Reef coast and strand (0–20 m)

**Beach and dune**
- **Beach naupaka** (*Scaevola taccada*): dense salt-proof shrub with half-flowers. First green line above the sand.
- **Beach morning glory** (*Ipomoea pes-caprae*): purple-flowered vine that binds dunes.
- **Sea purslane** (*Sesuvium portulacastrum*): succulent red-green mat on salty flats. Edible pickled.
- **Seashore dropseed** (*Sporobolus virginicus*): wiry salt grass on dunes.
- **Beach heliotrope** (*Heliotropium anomalum*): silver-leaved ground cover.
- **Beach vitex** (*Vitex rotundifolia*): aromatic gray shrub on sand.
- **ʻIlima** (*Sida fallax*): orange-flowered shrub from dunes to dry hills. Lei flower.

**Shore trees**
- **Coconut palm**: food, oil, coir rope, thatch and timber.
- **Hala / screwpine** (*Pandanus tectorius*): stilt-rooted tree. Leaves woven into mats, baskets and sails.
- **Milo** (*Thespesia populnea*): shade tree. Fine reddish wood for bowls. Shore and windbreak.
- **Kou** (*Cordia subcordata*): orange-flowered tree. Prized carving wood for bowls and cups. Shore and windbreak.
- **Hau** (*Hibiscus tiliaceus*): tangled yellow-flowered tree. Bark gives cordage and light wood makes floats and outriggers.
- **Kamani** (*Calophyllum inophyllum*): broad shade tree. Seed oil for lamps and skin salve. Hard boat wood. Shore and windbreak.

**Estuaries and warm lagoons**
- **Hau** thickets, **bulrush** (*Schoenoplectus*), **makaloa** (*Cyperus laevigatus*) and **ʻākulikuli** (*Sesuvium*) around fishpond and stream-mouth margins. Tannin work uses other barks if leather is needed; mangroves are **[Post-contact]** only.
- **Makaloa sedge**: brackish pools. Woven into the finest sleeping mats.
- **Bulrush**: marsh edges. Thatch and matting.

**Reef and tide pools**
- **Seagrass** (*Halophila*) beds in calm lagoons.
- **Limu** (edible seaweeds such as *Asparagopsis*, *Gracilaria* and *Ulva*): harvested as food and seasoning.
- **Coralline red algae**: pink crusts that cement reef limestone.

**[Post-contact] out of default:** red mangrove (*Rhizophora*), black mangrove (*Avicennia*), beach ironwood (*Casuarina equisetifolia*).

## Lowland wet, windward (20–900 m, reaching ~1,000 m)

- **Kukui / candlenut** (*Aleurites moluccanus*): pale silver-green tree that marks gulches. Nut oil for lamps, nuts strung as candles and soot for ink and tattoo pigment.
- **Mountain apple** (*Syzygium malaccense*): red pear-shaped fruit in wet valleys.
- **Loulu palm** (*Pritchardia*): fan palm. Fronds for thatch and fans.
- **ʻIeʻie** (*Freycinetia arborea*): climbing screwpine. Aerial roots woven into baskets and fish traps.
- **Hāpuʻu tree fern** (*Cibotium glaucum*): trunks up to 6 m. Silky pulu fiber stuffs pillows and mattresses. Also common higher in montane wet.
- **Olonā** (*Touchardia latifolia*): shrub with one of the strongest plant fibers known. Fishing line, nets and rope.
- **Māmaki** (*Pipturus albidus*): shrub. Leaf tea and bark fiber.
- **Kōpiko** (*Psychotria*) and **kōlea** (*Myrsine*): understory trees. Kōlea bark gives red dye.
- **Ti / kī** (*Cordyline fruticosa*): leaves for wrapping food, thatch, rain capes and sandals. Root is sweet and fermentable.
- **Noni** (*Morinda citrifolia*): pungent fruit used as medicine. Roots give yellow dye.
- **ʻAwa / kava** (*Piper methysticum*): root drink that relaxes and numbs.
- **Bamboo / ʻohe** (*Schizostachyum*): pipes, containers, stamping tubes and knives.

## Lowland dry, leeward (20–900 m, reaching ~1,000 m)

- **Wiliwili** (*Erythrina sandwicensis*): drought-deciduous tree. Orange flowers on bare branches and very light wood for floats and surfboards.
- **Lama / native ebony** (*Diospyros sandwicensis*): dense dark-hearted tree with orange fruit. Dense dry-forest fuel with naio and kauila.
- **Sandalwood / ʻiliahi** (*Santalum*): fragrant heartwood. Incense, perfume oil and a natural luxury trade good.
- **Naio / false sandalwood** (*Myoporum sandwicense*): scented wood that burns well as torches. Also reaches montane dry and subalpine.
- **Kauila** (*Colubrina oppositifolia*): one of the hardest woods on the island. Spears, clubs and tool handles. Dense dry-forest fuel.
- **Uhiuhi** (*Mezoneuron kavaiense*): extremely dense dark wood. Weapons and sledge runners.
- **ʻOhe makai** (*Polyscias sandwicensis*): pale-barked tree that drops leaves in drought.
- **Kokiʻo** (*Kokia*): rare red-flowered hibiscus tree. Bark gives red-brown dye.
- **Hawaiian cotton** (*Gossypium tomentosum*): yellow-flowered shrub with short rust-colored lint.
- **ʻAʻaliʻi** (*Dodonaea viscosa*): tough shrub with red papery seed capsules. Used in dye.
- **ʻŪlei** (*Osteomeles anthyllidifolia*): sprawling shrub. Tough wood for tools and fish spears.
- **Pili grass** (*Heteropogon contortus*): best thatch grass. Burns readily in the dry season.
- **Prickly poppy** (*Argemone glauca*): spiny white-flowered herb on dry plains.

**[Post-contact] out of default:** kiawe / mesquite (*Prosopis pallida*).

## Montane wet and cloud forest (~900–2,000 m; core 1,000–2,000 m)

**Canopy**
- **ʻŌhiʻa lehua** (*Metrosideros polymorpha*): dominant tree. Red, yellow or orange bottlebrush flowers. Dense hard wood for posts, pegs and charcoal.
- **Koa** (*Acacia koa*): tallest tree on the island. Curly golden-red wood for canoes, furniture, paddles and weapon hafts. Also open montane dry / mesic woodland.
- **ʻŌlapa** (*Cheirodendron*): fluttering-leaf tree. Fragrant leaves and wood for kindling.
- **Kāwaʻu** (*Ilex anomala*): native holly.
- **Pilo** (*Coprosma*): orange-berried shrubs and small trees.

**Understory and ferns**
- **Tree ferns** in several species form a second canopy.
- **Hōʻiʻo** (*Diplazium*): edible fiddlehead fern.
- **Pala ʻā / lace fern** (*Odontosoria chinensis*): red-brown dye.
- **Uluhe** (*Dicranopteris linearis*): forked fern that forms thickets taller than a man.
- **Filmy ferns** (*Hymenophyllum*) and mosses hang thick on every branch in cloud zones.
- **Lobelioids** (*Clermontia*, *Cyanea*): strange tufted trees and shrubs with curved tube flowers.
- **Kanawao** (*Broussaisia arguta*): hydrangea relative with blue or pink flower heads.
- **ʻĀkala** (*Rubus hawaiensis*): Hawaiian raspberry with large red to purple berries.
- **Mountain naupaka** (*Scaevola gaudichaudiana*): half-flowered shrub that pairs with the beach form in legend.
- **ʻŌhelo kau lāʻau** (*Vaccinium calycinum*): tree blueberry.
- **ʻAlaʻala wai nui** (*Peperomia*): succulent herbs on mossy logs.

**Montane bogs**
- **Sphagnum moss**, sedges (*Oreobolus*) and dwarf ʻōhiʻa a hand high.
- **Sundew** (*Drosera*): sticky carnivorous rosettes.
- **Bog lobelias** and dwarf blueberries.

## Montane dry (leeward, ~900–2,000 m)

- **Koa** and **ʻōhiʻa** in open mesic woodland.
- **Māmane** and **naio** reaching down from the subalpine belt.
- **ʻAʻaliʻi**, **pūkiawe** and **ʻōhelo** in the shrub layer.
- **Native bunchgrasses** in openings.
- Higher-elevation sandalwood species where the belt stays dry.

---

## Subalpine (2,000–3,000 m)

- **Māmane** (*Sophora chrysophylla*): yellow-flowered tree. Rot-proof wood for fence posts and sledges. Core belt here; also dips into montane dry.
- **Naio** forms open woodland with māmane.
- **Pūkiawe** (*Leptecophylla tameiameiae*): small needle-leaf shrub with white, pink or red berries.
- **ʻŌhelo** (*Vaccinium reticulatum*): red berries on lava fields and highlands.
- **Naʻenaʻe** (*Dubautia*): resinous silversword relatives.
- **Native bunchgrasses** (*Deschampsia*, *Eragrostis*): open highland grassland.
- **Kūkaenēnē** (*Coprosma ernodeoides*): creeping shrub with shiny black berries on cinder.

---

## Alpine summit zone (3,000–4,000 m)

- **Silversword / ʻāhinahina** (*Argyroxiphium sandwicense*): silver rosette that grows for decades then sends up one tall flower spike and dies.
- **Alpine grasses** (*Trisetum*, *Deschampsia*) in sheltered cinder hollows.
- **Racomitrium moss** and crustose lichens on bare rock.
- **Cliff ferns** (*Asplenium*, *Pellaea*) in cracks.
- Near the summit the land is nearly bare cinder desert with frost at night.

## Fresh lava and ash below the Albrook side vent

**Succession sequence**
1. **0–1 year:** cyanobacteria films and algae in damp cracks. Bare black rock otherwise.
2. **1–5 years:** **Stereocaulon lichen** (*Stereocaulon vulcani*) silver crusts. Moss tufts. Fern sporelings.
3. **5–20 years:** **ʻAmaʻu fern** (*Sadleria cyatheoides*) with red new fronds. **Sword fern** (*Nephrolepis*). **ʻŌhiʻa** seedlings, ʻōhelo, pūkiawe and kūkaenēnē.
4. **20–100 years:** scrubby ʻōhiʻa woodland.
5. **100–400+ years:** full ʻōhiʻa forest on wet slopes.

**Features**
- **Kīpuka:** islands of old forest left standing where lava flowed around them. Pockets of tall trees in a black field.
- **Tree molds:** hollow stone casts where trees were swallowed by lava.
- **Lava tube skylights:** ferns and mosses ring the openings and ʻōhiʻa roots dangle into the tubes.
- **Ash-killed fringe:** gray standing dead trees. Ground ferns resprout first.

## Vents, fumaroles and sulfur ground

- **ʻŌhelo** clusters beside steam vents.
- **Steam-vent mosses** (*Campylopus*) on warm wet ground.
- **Hot-spring red algae** (*Cyanidium*): blue-green to olive films in acid hot pools.
- **Sulfur bacteria mats:** white, yellow and orange slime in vent runoff.
- **Stunted ʻōhiʻa** with yellow and burned leaves on the downwind side.
- **Bare kill zones** where sulfur fumes pool in hollows.

## Laterite and peridotite ground (ore-bearing)

### Hawaii-class native laterite vegetation

- **Uluhe** fern thickets over red clay.
- **Stunted ʻōhiʻa** a few meters tall.
- **ʻAmaʻu** ferns, **pūkiawe** and **ʻōhelo.**
- **Native sedges** (*Machaerina*) on hard iron crust.
- Bare red erosion scars on ridges.
- Sharp line where tall forest stops and waist-high scrub starts can mark thin soil over hard crust even without invent ore plants.

### [Invent ore-scrub] (New Caledonia import pack on peridotite ground)

Use only when the story wants visible hyperaccumulator / ultramafic markers on peridotite. Not Hawaii-class native flora.

- **Nickel tree** (*Pycnandra acuminata*): blue-green sap loaded with nickel.
- **Column pine** (*Araucaria columnaris*): tall narrow conifer that stands out on ore ridges.
- **Kauri** (*Agathis*): massive straight-trunked conifer. Resin (kauri gum) for varnish and lamp fuel.
- **Mountain she-oak** (*Gymnostoma*): needle-twigged trees on metal soils.
- **Pitcher plant** (*Nepenthes vieillardii*): carnivorous pitchers on poor ultramafic soil.
- **Nickel shrubs** (*Phyllanthus*, *Hybanthus*, *Psychotria gabriellae*): hyperaccumulators.
- **Grass trees** (*Dracophyllum*): tufted stiff-leaved shrubs.
- **Hibbertia** and **Grevillea:** yellow and red-flowered hard-leaved shrubs.

## Gulches, streams and wetlands

- **Taro** in terraced wet fields fed by stream diversions.
- **Hau** thickets, **kukui** and **mountain apple** along streams.
- **Ginger** (*ʻawapuhi*, *Zingiber zerumbet*): shampoo ginger in shaded gullies.
- **Turmeric / ʻōlena:** yellow dye and medicine.
- Native sedges and ferns at spring heads.

**[Post-contact] out of default:** watercress.

---

## Farm, town and Albrook valley plants

**Field and garden crops (pre-contact)**
- **Taro** (wet), **sweet potato** (dry), **breadfruit**, **banana**, **yam**, **Polynesian arrowroot**, **sugarcane**, **coconut** and **bottle gourd**.
- **Paper mulberry / wauke:** bark beaten into kapa cloth.
- **Upcountry / cooler ash soils:** sweet potato and dryland taro. No coffee, potato, cabbage or onion on the pre-contact default.

**Albrook yards and roadsides**
- **Kukui**, **breadfruit** and **banana** in house yards.
- **Ti** hedges along property lines.
- **ʻUhaloa** (*Waltheria indica*), **ʻilima** and **purslane** as weeds on disturbed ground.
- **Gray ash-dusted leaves** on everything after eruption bursts.

**[Post-contact] out of default:** amaranth and plantain as roadside weeds; coffee / temperate truck crops.

**Roland's half acre**
- Ash-fed soil supports breadfruit, banana and taro in damp corners. Sweet potato on dry patches.
- Ferns and ʻōhiʻa seedlings move in wherever the plot sits unworked.
