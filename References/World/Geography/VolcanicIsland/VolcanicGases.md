## Damage Sorting Sheet

The four families are **Thermal**, **Kinetic**, **Bodily** and **Sustenance**. A hit is filed by the mechanism that harms the body, then combined if more than one mechanism is in the same event. This sorting sheet is design loot. Live skill tracks win where they disagree. Acid and Electricity stay outside the four until they are written in.

| Family | Types | What Goes There |
|---|---|---|
| **Thermal** | Heat, Cold | Tissue gets hotter or colder. Fire, steam, hot metal, friction, infrared, microwaves and laser burns are Heat. Frost, cryogenics and cold metal are Cold. |
| **Kinetic** | Blunt, Sharp | Force or a cut. Punches, falls, crush, blast, pressure, whiplash and loud noise are Blunt. Points, edges, arrows, shrapnel, claws and saws are Sharp. |
| **Bodily** | Pain, Poison, Alcohol | Felt pain, toxin or drink. Pepper spray and tear gas are Pain. Venom, infection, drugs, heavy metals and nerve agents are Poison. Drinking alcohol is Alcohol. Methanol is Poison. |
| **Sustenance** | Sleep, Thirst, Hunger, Suffocation | A missing need. No air, no blood, carbon monoxide, altitude and drowning are Suffocation. No water is Thirst. No food is Hunger. Sleep loss or forced sleep is Sleep. |

### Five Ways to Sort a Multi-Mechanism Event

- **Split.** One pool is divided. Radiation is about one third Sharp and two thirds Poison. Lightning is about half Heat, one fifth Sharp and 30% Suffocation. House-fire smoke is half Suffocation, two fifths Poison and one tenth Heat. Concentrated acid is 70% Poison and 30% Heat.
- **Parallel.** Separate hazards, each checked on its own. An explosion is blast (Blunt) plus fragments (Sharp) plus heat.
- **Gated.** The payload lands only if the gate fails. A snakebite or poisoned blade is Sharp first. If the skin holds, the venom stays outside.
- **Converted.** A stopped blade, bullet or fragment becomes Blunt. A round that does go in is Sharp, plus Blunt for the shock cavity.
- **Amplified.** Burned skin is one Sharp level weaker there. Frozen tissue is one Sharp level and one Blunt level weaker there.

### Extra Rules

- A bleeding Sharp wound also takes a Suffocation check.
- Blindness, deafness and stun are results, not types. Only the skill for the damage underneath them helps. Dazzle and electric muscle lockup have no skill.

### Volcanic Filing Notes

These calls apply the sheet to cases it does not name directly.

| Case | Filing | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Irritant gas at low dose (SO2, HCl, H2S, NH3, vog) | **Pain** | Same mechanism as tear gas: nerve irritation and reflex coughing or airway spasm without tissue destruction |
| Acid gas at high dose (SO2, HCl, HBr, sulfuric aerosol) | **Acid Split:** 70% Poison, 30% Heat | Concentrated acid rule |
| Hydrogen fluoride | **Split:** about 90% Poison, 10% Heat | Weak acid burn; fluoride binding calcium does most of the harm |
| Hydrogen sulfide at high dose | **Suffocation** | Cells cannot use oxygen; filed with carbon monoxide |
| Carbon dioxide | **Suffocation** | Displaces air and acidifies blood; both are "no air" |
| Ammonia at high dose | **Acid Split:** 70% Poison, 30% Heat | Alkali burn filed with acid until a caustic rule is written |
| Airway swelling, pulmonary edema, eye damage, loss of smell | **Results** | Only the skill for the underlying type helps |

## Gas Profiles

### 1. Water Vapor (Steam)

- **Abundance:** The most common volcanic gas, typically 60-90% of emissions.
- **Mechanism:** Steam carries far more heat than dry air because it releases latent heat when it condenses on cooler surfaces such as skin, eyes and the airway lining. Dry hot air mostly burns the upper airway; steam can carry heat deep into the lungs before condensing.
- **Effects:** Scalding burns, airway swelling, laryngeal obstruction, pulmonary edema.
- **Body processing:** None needed; harm is entirely thermal.
- **Treatment:** Cooling, airway management (early intubation if swelling), burn care.
- **Neutralization:** Distance; steam cools and condenses quickly in open air.
- **Filing:** **Thermal: Heat.** **Amplified:** scalded skin is one Sharp level weaker there. Airway swelling is a result; only Heat skill helps.

### 2. Carbon Dioxide (CO2)

- **Abundance:** Second most common volcanic gas; can leak from the ground long after eruptions stop.
- **Behavior:** About 1.5 times denser than air. Pools in valleys, craters, basements, caves and tents. Can sit invisibly in a hollow on a still day.
- **Thresholds:** 3% causes headache and rapid breathing; 5% causes dizziness and confusion; 10% causes unconsciousness within minutes; 20-30% causes collapse within a few breaths and death.
- **Mechanism:** Two parts:
  1. Displaces oxygen.
  2. High CO2 in blood forms carbonic acid, dropping blood pH (respiratory acidosis). This depresses the brain and heart directly, so CO2 is more than an inert suffocant.
- **Notable events:** Lake Nyos, Cameroon (1986): a CO2 cloud killed about 1,700 people overnight. Dieng Plateau, Indonesia (1979): about 140 killed fleeing an eruption into a CO2 pocket.
- **Body processing:** Carbonic anhydrase converts CO2 to bicarbonate; it is carried to the lungs and exhaled. Recovery is fast if removed in time.
- **Treatment:** Immediate removal to fresh air, oxygen, ventilation support. Rescuers entering a pool without breathing air often become victims.
- **Neutralization:** Ventilation; degassing pipes (installed at Lake Nyos); alkaline absorbents (soda lime) in confined spaces. Filter respirators provide **zero** protection; only supplied air (SCBA) works.
- **Filing:** **Sustenance: Suffocation.** Both displacement and blood acidification are "no air."

### 3. Sulfur Dioxide (SO2)

- **Mechanism:** Highly water soluble. Dissolves on moist eyes, nose, throat and upper airway to form sulfurous acid. Triggers reflex bronchoconstriction. Asthmatics can react at 0.25-0.5 ppm.
- **Effects:** Burning eyes, coughing, airway spasm; at high levels, chemical burns of the airway and pulmonary edema.
- **Body processing:** Absorbed mostly in the upper airway; converted to sulfite, then oxidized to sulfate by the enzyme sulfite oxidase in the liver; excreted in urine.
- **Treatment:** Fresh air, eye irrigation, bronchodilators, oxygen; monitor for delayed pulmonary edema.
- **Neutralization:** Alkaline scrubbing (lime or limestone slurry converts it to gypsum). Acid gas respirator cartridges work at moderate levels.
- **Filing:** **Low dose: Bodily: Pain** (tear gas class). **High dose: Acid Split, 70% Poison and 30% Heat.** Pulmonary edema is a result.

### 4. Hydrogen Sulfide (H2S)

- **Thresholds:** Rotten egg smell at well under 1 ppm; eye and throat irritation at 10-50 ppm; **smell disappears at 100-150 ppm** due to olfactory nerve paralysis; pulmonary edema around 300 ppm; collapse at 500-700 ppm; near-instant "knockdown" at 1,000 ppm or more.
- **Behavior:** Heavier than air; pools with CO2 in low areas.
- **Mechanism:** Blocks cytochrome c oxidase (Complex IV) in mitochondria, the same target as cyanide. Cells cannot use oxygen even when blood is fully oxygenated. Brain and heart fail first. At lower levels it also irritates mucous membranes.
- **Body processing:** Oxidized in mitochondria by sulfide quinone oxidoreductase to thiosulfate, then to sulfate; excreted in urine. The body clears it quickly, so survivors who reach fresh air often recover; prolonged exposure can leave lasting brain injury.
- **Treatment:** Fresh air, 100% oxygen, supportive care. Sodium nitrite is sometimes used to create methemoglobin, which binds sulfide.
- **Neutralization:** Oxidation (bleach, hydrogen peroxide); iron oxide scrubbers; industrial Claus process converts it to elemental sulfur.
- **Filing:** **Low dose: Bodily: Pain.** **High dose: Sustenance: Suffocation** (filed with carbon monoxide). Loss of smell is a result.

### 5. Hydrogen Chloride (HCl)

- **Mechanism:** Extremely water soluble; forms hydrochloric acid on contact with moisture. Burns the eyes, skin and upper airway. Can cause laryngeal spasm and swelling that blocks breathing.
- **Body processing:** Dissociates into hydrogen and chloride ions. Chloride is a normal body ion; the acid load is buffered. Damage is local, not systemic.
- **Treatment:** Fresh air, copious water irrigation of eyes and skin, humidified oxygen, airway support. Nebulized sodium bicarbonate is sometimes used.
- **Neutralization:** Water or alkaline scrubbing (sodium hydroxide, lime). Acid gas cartridges work.
- **Filing:** **Low dose: Bodily: Pain.** **High dose: Acid Split, 70% Poison and 30% Heat.** Laryngeal swelling is a result.

### 6. Hydrogen Fluoride (HF)

- **Mechanism:** The most insidious volcanic gas. HF is a weak acid, so much of it stays undissociated and **slips through skin and mucous membranes into deep tissue** before releasing fluoride ions. Fluoride then binds calcium and magnesium, killing cells from the inside (liquefactive necrosis) and pulling calcium out of the blood.
- **Systemic effects:** Low blood calcium, low magnesium, high potassium leading to fatal cardiac arrhythmia. Pain can be delayed and deep, out of proportion to visible damage.
- **Chronic effects:** Fluoride adsorbs onto volcanic ash. Grazing animals eating coated grass develop fluorosis (bone and tooth damage). Laki, Iceland (1783-84): fluorine poisoning killed much of the island's livestock; the resulting famine killed about a fifth of the population.
- **Body processing:** Fluoride is deposited in bone and teeth; the rest is excreted by the kidneys.
- **Treatment:** Calcium gluconate (gel on skin, nebulized for inhalation, IV or injected for severe cases); cardiac monitoring; magnesium replacement.
- **Neutralization:** Lime or calcium compounds bind it as insoluble calcium fluoride. Acid gas cartridges work.
- **Filing:** **Split: about 90% Poison and 10% Heat.** Deep penetration is not Sharp; no point or edge is involved. Fluorosis from ash is Poison.

### 7. Carbon Monoxide (CO)

- **Sources:** Minor in most volcanic gas; significant where lava burns vegetation or in enclosed spaces.
- **Mechanism:** Binds hemoglobin about 200-250 times more strongly than oxygen, forming carboxyhemoglobin. Blood looks oxygenated (cherry red) but cannot deliver oxygen. Also binds myoglobin in heart muscle and cytochrome oxidase in cells.
- **Effects:** Headache, nausea, confusion, collapse, death; survivors may have delayed neurological damage.
- **Body processing:** Not metabolized; exhaled unchanged. Half-life about 4-6 hours on room air, about 60-90 minutes on 100% oxygen, about 20-30 minutes in a hyperbaric chamber.
- **Treatment:** 100% oxygen; hyperbaric oxygen for severe cases.
- **Neutralization:** Ventilation; catalytic oxidation (hopcalite) converts it to CO2. Standard cartridges do not stop it.
- **Filing:** **Sustenance: Suffocation.**

### 8. Sulfuric Acid Aerosol and Vog

- **Formation:** SO2 reacts with oxygen, moisture and sunlight to form fine sulfuric acid and sulfate droplets. The resulting haze is called vog (volcanic smog), well known downwind of Kīlauea, Hawaii.
- **Mechanism:** Droplets are small enough to bypass the upper airway and settle deep in the lungs, where they irritate and inflame the small airways.
- **Effects:** Aggravated asthma and bronchitis, eye irritation, headaches; acid rain damages crops and corrodes metal.
- **Body processing:** Neutralized by airway mucus and ammonia in exhaled breath; sulfate excreted renally.
- **Treatment:** Bronchodilators, staying indoors with filtered air.
- **Neutralization:** Atmospheric dilution; indoor air filtration; alkaline scrubbing.
- **Filing:** **Vog: Bodily: Pain.** **Concentrated aerosol near vents: Acid Split, 70% Poison and 30% Heat.**

### 9. Laze (Lava Haze)

- **Formation:** Lava hitting seawater boils it, producing a plume of steam, hydrochloric acid (from sea salt chlorine) and tiny shards of volcanic glass.
- **Mechanism:** Triple threat: acid burns, scalding steam near the entry point and fine glass particles that abrade the eyes, skin and lungs.
- **Notable event:** Two deaths near a Kīlauea ocean entry in 2000 were linked to laze.
- **Treatment:** Same as HCl plus eye irrigation to remove glass.
- **Neutralization:** Avoidance; stay upwind of ocean entries.
- **Filing:** **Parallel:** Acid Split (70% Poison, 30% Heat) plus Heat (steam, near the entry only) plus Sharp (glass shards). **Amplified:** steam-scalded skin is one Sharp level weaker against the glass. Bleeding glass cuts take a Suffocation check. Eye damage is a result. At a distance the plume drops to Pain.

### 10. Radon (Rn-222)

- **Sources:** Released from magma and fractured volcanic rock; elevated in some volcanic and geothermal regions.
- **Mechanism:** Radon itself is mostly exhaled. Its short-lived decay products (polonium-218, lead-214, bismuth-214, polonium-214) stick to dust and lung tissue. The polonium isotopes emit **alpha particles** that tear straight tracks through cell nuclei, breaking both strands of DNA.
- **Effects:** No immediate symptoms. Raises lung cancer risk over years of exposure; it is the second leading cause of lung cancer after smoking.
- **Body processing:** Decay products are cleared slowly by airway mucus; most decay in place.
- **Treatment:** None acute; reduce cumulative exposure.
- **Neutralization:** Ventilation; sub-slab depressurization in buildings; sealing foundations.
- **Filing:** **Radiation Split: about one third Sharp and two thirds Poison.**

### 11. Hydrogen (H2) and Methane (CH4)

- **Sources:** Hydrogen from high-temperature gas reactions; methane mostly from lava burying and cooking vegetation.
- **Mechanism:** Neither is toxic. Danger is ignition: hydrogen burns between 4% and 75% in air; methane between 5% and 15%. Methane trapped under lava flows can cause sudden explosions blocks away from the front. In high concentrations both displace oxygen.
- **Treatment:** Burn and trauma care; fresh air for asphyxia.
- **Neutralization:** Ventilation; elimination of ignition sources; controlled flaring.
- **Filing:**
  - **Explosion: Parallel.** Blast (Blunt) plus rock fragments (Sharp) plus fireball (Heat). Fragments stopped by armor are **Converted** to Blunt. Burned skin is **Amplified**, one Sharp level weaker against the fragments. Bleeding fragment wounds take a Suffocation check. Deafness and stun from the blast are results; only Blunt skill helps.
  - **Burning without blast:** Heat.
  - **Unlit high concentration:** Suffocation.

### 12. Carbonyl Sulfide (COS) and Carbon Disulfide (CS2)

- **Mechanism:** COS is converted inside the body by carbonic anhydrase into H2S, giving it H2S-type toxicity. CS2 is a nerve poison; chronic exposure damages nerves, the brain and blood vessels.
- **Body processing:** COS to H2S to thiosulfate and sulfate; CS2 is partly exhaled, partly metabolized in the liver (partly to COS).
- **Treatment:** Same as H2S for acute exposure; removal from exposure for chronic.
- **Neutralization:** Oxidation; activated carbon.
- **Filing:** **Bodily: Poison.**

### 13. Mercury Vapor (Hg)

- **Mechanism:** About 80% of inhaled elemental mercury vapor is absorbed. It crosses the blood-brain barrier, gets oxidized to ionic mercury (Hg2+) and becomes trapped in the brain and kidneys. It binds sulfur groups on proteins and enzymes, disabling them.
- **Effects:** Tremor, irritability, memory loss, kidney damage; high acute doses cause chemical pneumonitis.
- **Body processing:** Slow excretion in urine and feces; half-life around two months.
- **Treatment:** Chelation (DMSA, DMPS, dimercaprol).
- **Neutralization:** Sulfur-impregnated activated carbon; sulfur powder binds spilled mercury.
- **Filing:** **Bodily: Poison** (heavy metal).

### 14. Metal Aerosols (Arsenic, Lead, Cadmium, Thallium)

- **Mechanism:** Volatilized metals condense into fine particles in the plume. Each binds enzymes or substitutes for essential minerals (lead for calcium, thallium for potassium, arsenic disrupts energy metabolism).
- **Effects:** Mostly chronic: nerve, kidney, blood and cancer effects; contamination of water and crops downwind.
- **Treatment:** Chelation specific to each metal; Prussian blue for thallium.
- **Neutralization:** Particulate filtration (P100/HEPA); water treatment.
- **Filing:** **Bodily: Poison** (heavy metals).

### 15. Ammonia and Bromine Compounds (Minor)

- **Ammonia:** Alkaline corrosive; forms ammonium hydroxide on moist tissue and causes liquefactive burns that penetrate deeper than acid burns. Found mainly at some geothermal and hydrothermal sites.
- **Bromine compounds (HBr, BrO):** Acid corrosive like HCl; BrO also destroys stratospheric and plume ozone.
- **Treatment:** Irrigation, airway support.
- **Neutralization:** Ammonia by acid scrubbing or water; HBr by alkaline scrubbing.
- **Filing:** **Low dose: Bodily: Pain.** **High dose: Acid Split, 70% Poison and 30% Heat** (ammonia filed with acid until a caustic rule is written).

## Compound Threat: Pyroclastic Density Currents

Not a single gas but the deadliest gas-driven event. A ground-hugging cloud of hot gas (commonly 200-700 C) and ash moving at over 100 km/h.

**Sort: Parallel.** Each hazard is checked on its own.

| Component | Family | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Superheated gas and ash | Thermal | Heat |
| Dynamic pressure knocking down walls and bodies | Kinetic | Blunt |
| Rock and glass fragments | Kinetic | Sharp |
| Ash filling the airway | Sustenance | Suffocation |
| Acid gases in the cloud | Bodily / Thermal | Acid Split: 70% Poison, 30% Heat |

- **Amplified:** Heat lands first. Burned skin is one Sharp level weaker against the fragments that follow.
- **Converted:** Fragments stopped by armor or cover become Blunt.
- **Bleeding:** Bleeding fragment wounds take a Suffocation check on top of the ash.
- **Notable events:** Mount Pelée, Martinique (1902): about 28,000 killed in minutes. Vesuvius (79 AD): Herculaneum residents killed by extreme heat. Mount St. Helens (1980): lateral blast flattened forest across about 600 square kilometers.

## Synergy and Field Hazards

- **CO2 + H2S pooling:** Both sink into low ground and both file as Suffocation at lethal levels, so they stack in the same hollow. H2S smell vanishes at dangerous levels while CO2 gives no warning at all.
- **SO2 + particles:** Particles carry absorbed acid deeper into the lungs than the gas alone, pushing a Pain exposure toward Acid Split.
- **HF on ash:** Long-term Poison for grazing animals and water supplies far from the eruption.
- **Steam + acid gases:** Moist air speeds acid formation directly on tissue. Steam-scalded skin is also one Sharp level weaker against any glass or fragments in the same event.

## Protection Summary

| Threat Type | Effective Protection |
|---|---|
| CO2, CO, oxygen deficiency | SCBA (supplied air) only |
| SO2, HCl, HF, HBr | Acid gas cartridges at moderate levels; SCBA at high levels |
| H2S | SCBA; cartridges only for escape |
| Vog, laze, metal aerosols, ash | P100/HEPA particulate filters, sealed goggles |
| Radon | Building ventilation, cumulative exposure limits |
| H2, CH4 | Distance, no ignition sources, gas detectors |
| Pyroclastic flows | Evacuation; no wearable protection is adequate |

## Hawaiian Volcanic Gases: Sources, Accumulation, Detection and Avoidance

### Most Common Gases

| Gas | Formula | Where It Dominates | Filing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water vapor | H2O | Every vent and eruption; the bulk of all emissions | Heat |
| Carbon dioxide | CO2 | Summit vents, ground cracks, low areas near vents | Suffocation |
| Sulfur dioxide | SO2 | Active eruptions; the source of vog downwind | Pain (low); Acid Split (high) |
| Hydrogen sulfide | H2S | Steaming vents and fumaroles | Pain (low); Suffocation (high) |
| Hydrogen chloride | HCl | Laze at ocean entries | Pain (low); Acid Split (high) |

Water vapor, carbon dioxide and sulfur dioxide are released continuously during an eruption (USGS).

### Where They Are Found

| Location | Gases | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Kīlauea summit (Halemaʻumaʻu) | SO2, CO2, H2O | Main active source; episodic lava fountaining began December 2024 and continued through 2026 |
| Sulphur Banks (Haʻakulamanu), Hawaiʻi Volcanoes NP | H2S, SO2, CO2 | Gas seeps from the ground along the trail |
| Steam Vents near Kīlauea Visitor Center | Mostly H2O | Minor gas |
| Kaʻū district (Pāhala, Nāʻālehu, Ocean View) | SO2, vog | Closest populated area downwind; higher SO2 |
| Kona coast | Vog | Vog wraps around the island and is trapped by afternoon sea breezes |
| Ocean entries | HCl, steam, glass shards (laze) | Only when lava reaches the sea, as in 2018 |
| Hilo, Maui, Oʻahu | Vog | During southerly "Kona wind" days |

### Where They Accumulate

- **CO2 and H2S:** Both are heavier than air. They collect in low spots, ground cracks, pit craters, lava tubes, caves and hollows near vents.
- **Vog:** Builds up beneath the trade wind inversion layer and in leeward areas. Worst on calm days with little wind to disperse it.
- **Plume direction:** Normal trade winds blow from the northeast and push the plume toward the southwest of Halemaʻumaʻu.
- **Tephra:** Most ash, pumice and Pele's hair from fountaining falls within about 1-2 km of the vents.

### How to Notice Them

| Gas | Senses | Physical Signs |
|---|---|---|
| SO2 | Pungent burned-match smell | Burning eyes and throat, coughing, chest tightness |
| Vog | Blue-gray haze hiding Mauna Loa or the horizon | Headache, sore throat, watery eyes, fatigue |
| H2S | Rotten egg smell | Eye irritation; if the smell suddenly vanishes near a vent, levels are high enough to paralyze smell. Leave immediately. |
| CO2 | No smell, no color | Dead insects, birds or plants in a hollow; sudden headache; breathing faster than normal; dizziness |
| Laze | White plume rising from the shoreline | Stinging eyes and skin, throat burning |

#### Monitoring Tools

- Hawaii Interagency Vog Dashboard: vog.ivhhn.org
- Hawaii Department of Health SO2 monitoring network
- USGS Hawaiian Volcano Observatory daily updates
- Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park closure signs and alerts
- Personal CO2 meter or multigas meter for fieldwork near vents

### How to Avoid Them

#### Near Vents and in the Park

- Check the wind direction and stay upwind or crosswind of vents.
- Never sit, lie or camp in depressions, cracks or lava tubes near active areas.
- Obey park closures.
- People with heart or lung conditions, pregnant people, infants and young children are advised by the park to avoid high-gas stops like Sulphur Banks.
- Stay well back from ocean entries and out of any laze plume.
- Leave immediately if eyes burn, coughing starts or a headache develops near vents.

#### On Vog Days

- Stay indoors with windows closed and air conditioning running.
- Limit strenuous outdoor exercise.
- Keep asthma and respiratory medication on hand.
- Drink plenty of water.

#### Respiratory Protection

| Protection | Stops | Does Not Stop |
|---|---|---|
| N95 mask | Ash, Pele's hair, fine particles | SO2, H2S, CO2 |
| Acid gas cartridge respirator | SO2, HCl, low H2S | CO2, oxygen deficiency |
| SCBA (supplied air) | Everything | Nothing; required for CO2 or high H2S |
