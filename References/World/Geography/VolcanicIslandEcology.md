# Volcanic Island Ecology

Hotspot / shield-island ecology for places like Dragnis-class volcanic islands. Geology first, then what can live there. Places: `Places.md`. Resources for an old island with renewed volcanism: `VolcanicIslandResources.md`.

## Narrative

A hotspot builds islands in an age line as the plate drifts. Young shields are raw lava and thin soil. Older islands get valleys, reefs and leached soils. Isolation filters what colonizes. Windward slopes drown in rain. Leeward slopes sit in rain shadow.

## Detail

### Geology

- Plate drift over a hotspot ≈ **7–10 cm/year** → island chain ages in one direction
- Life cycle: shield → eroded ridges/valleys → subside → atoll → seamount
- Shield volcanoes: fluid basalt, broad domes (summit km-scale; roots much deeper from the seafloor)
- Lava: **pāhoehoe** (smooth, tubes) vs **ʻaʻā** (clinker, hard travel). Also cinder cones, calderas, pit craters, black/green sand, vog, laze where lava hits sea

### Isolation

Far from continents. Colonists arrive by bird gut/feathers, wind (spores, tiny seeds, bugs) or salt-tolerant float seeds. Successful plant arrivals are rare (tens of thousands of years between). Native land mammals often absent except bats (and a seal if the setting wants one). No native reptiles/amphibians/ants/mosquitoes/conifers in the Hawaii-type filter unless the story imports them.

**Radiation pattern:** one ancestor → many bill / form niches (finch line, silversword-like plants, fly swarms). Without grazers, plants and birds often lose thorns, stings, flight.

### Lava succession

- Wet forest on new flow: ~**100–400 years**. Dry sites much slower
- Pioneers: lichens, mosses, ferns, hardy tree seedlings
- ʻAʻā colonizes faster than pāhoehoe (cracks hold water and seed)
- **Kīpuka:** older forest patches ringed by new lava = seed banks and refuges
- Lava tubes: cave fauna on hanging roots

### Soils and climate

- Young basalt: nitrogen poor. Old leached soils: phosphorus poor. Ash → fertile andisols that hold water. Old wet islands → red laterites
- Trade winds: windward wet, leeward dry. Extreme pairs: ~**10 m/year** rain vs **<0.5 m** a few km away
- Inversion ~**1800–2400 m** caps cloud → dry alpine summits, frost / rare snow

### Zone ladder (elevation feel)

| Zone | Elev (approx.) | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Coastal strand | 0 | Salt, sand, rock creepers |
| Lowland dry | 0–1000 m leeward | Seasonal, fire-prone woodland |
| Lowland wet | 0–1000 m windward | Dense, fern-rich |
| Montane wet / cloud | 1000–2000 m | Fog drip, epiphytes, bogs |
| Montane dry / mesic | 1000–2000 m leeward | Open woodland |
| Subalpine shrub | 2000–3000 m | Dry frost shrubs |
| Alpine desert | >3000 m | Cinder, UV, specialists |

### Coast and people patterns

- Young islands: little reef. Older: big reefs. Anchialine (brackish lava pools linked underground to sea) possible
- Ridge-to-sea land divisions give one community forest, farms, stream and fishing
- Irrigated taro-style terraces and stone fishponds fit volcanic island agriculture
- Imports (stock, mosquitoes, fire grasses) wreck native birds and dry forest fast. Road/dungeon boom towns amplify that
