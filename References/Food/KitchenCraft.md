## Kitchen knife metal picks

Metal names follow `../World/Materials/Metals.md`. This table is cookware / kitchen kit only.

| Use | Metal |
|---|---|
| Peasant or starter knife | Iron |
| Everyday chef's knife | Steel (tier-1 default) |
| Runesmith's working knife | Darksteel (tier-2 default) |
| Enchanted knife cast often | Star steel or mythril |
| Luxury reusable runic knife | Mythril (thin plate over a core) |
| Noble or prestige set | Orihalcum (no enchant path) |
| Legendary heirloom | Adamantium |
| Handle fittings and ferrules | Copper or bronze |
| Decorative inlay | Silver or gold |

### Substitutions for cookware sections below

- **Stainless:** use steel for plain cookware. For hex lattice / tri-ply faces use mythril.
- **Magic steel:** darksteel for durable runes, or star steel for a high-conductivity host.
- **Rune disc (pan):** copper is cheap mid conductivity. Mythril or star steel for luxury.
- **Iron skillet:** worst common host. Rune-core pans want steel or better.

## Production methods by item

### Core and Full Minimal Kit

#### 1. 12 inch skillet
- **Material:** Cast iron or carbon steel. Stainless is not available without chromium alloy knowledge or magic. Use steel for plain cookware; for corrosion-resistant faces see knife-section substitutions (mythril).
- **Cast iron method:** Carve a wooden pattern (two halves for a lip). Pack green sand around the pattern in a two-part flask. Remove the pattern and cut a gate and vent. Melt iron in a furnace with a bellows or a heat rune. Pour. Let cool. Break out the sand. Clean the casting with files and a grindstone. Machine the cooking surface smooth on a lathe if available. Season with oil and heat.
- **Forged carbon steel method:** Heat a sheet or billet. Hammer over a stake or into a swage die to raise the sides. Planish (smooth hammering) the interior. Rivet or forge-weld a handle on. Season.
- **Fast version:** A press with a die stamps a flat disc into a pan shape in one stroke.
- **Tools needed:** Forge or furnace, hammers, stakes, lathe (optional), files.
- **Magic assist:** Even-heat enchantment on the base. Non-stick enchantment on the surface.

#### 2. 3 qt saucepan with lid
- **Material:** Copper, iron, or tinned iron.
- **Method:** Cut a disc for the base and a strip for the wall. Raise or roll the wall into a cylinder. Braze or rivet the seam. Attach the base by seaming (folding and hammering the edges together) or by riveting. Roll a rim around a wire for stiffness. Rivet a handle on. For the lid, hammer a disc into a shallow dome over a stake and add a knob.
- **Tools needed:** Snips, stakes, planishing hammer, rivets, brazing flux, solder.
- **Alternate:** Cast iron saucepan using a sand mold.
- **Tinning:** Dip iron in molten tin to prevent rust and food contamination. Tin is a separate supply chain.

#### 3. Stockpot or Dutch oven
- **Dutch oven method (cast):** Make a pattern for the pot and a separate one for a flat lid with a raised rim. Sand cast both. Cast three short legs and a bail handle attachment points. Finish by grinding the lid seating surface so it fits tightly. Season.
- **Stockpot method (raised):** Same as the saucepan but larger. Use thicker sheet. Rivet two loop handles.
- **Scaling note:** A large cast pour needs enough furnace capacity. Otherwise pour in sections or use a water-wheel blower.
- **Magic assist:** Heat distribution rune. Weight-reduction enchantment for transport.

#### 4. Sheet pan
- **Material:** Iron sheet, steel sheet, or aluminum (unlikely without magic).
- **Method (no mill):** Forge a billet flat with a sledge and hand hammers. Repeat heating and hammering until thin. This is slow and uneven.
- **Method (rolling mill):** Pass heated bar through rollers repeatedly. Cut to size with shears. Fold or wire the edges for stiffness. Hammer the corners.
- **Method (press):** Stamp a flat sheet with a rimmed die.
- **Tools needed:** Rolling mill or heavy hammer, shears, brake (folding jig).

#### 5. Chef's knife
- **Material:** High-carbon steel. Wrought iron is too soft. Steel comes from a bloomery with carburizing, crucible steel, or a magic-assisted alloy. For darksteel / star steel / mythril knife roles see **Kitchen knife metal picks** above.
- **Method:**
  1. Forge a billet into a flat bar.
  2. Draw out the blade shape and tang with hammer blows.
  3. Forge in the bevel lines.
  4. Normalize by heating and air cooling three times to relieve stress.
  5. Rough grind on a grindstone.
  6. Harden by heating to critical temperature (non-magnetic) and quenching in oil or water.
  7. Temper in an oven or on a hot plate to relieve brittleness.
  8. Final grind and polish.
  9. Fit a handle (scales riveted to a full tang or a hollow handle epoxied with pitch).
  10. Sharpen on a whetstone.
- **Tools needed:** Forge, anvil, hammers, grindstone, files, whetstone, drill (bow drill or lathe).
- **Magic assist:** Edge-keeping enchantment. Self-sharpening rune. Hardening rune for a perfect quench.

#### 6. Cutting board
- **Material:** Hardwood (maple, beech, oak, walnut).
- **Method:** Mill planks. Joint the edges. Glue strips (hide glue or pine pitch glue) into a panel. Clamp. For end grain boards, cut the panel into strips, rotate 90 degrees, and reglue. Plane flat. Sand or scrape. Rub with oil.
- **Tools needed:** Saw, plane, clamps (forged or wooden screw), glue pot.
- **Alternate:** Single slab cut from a tree round.

#### 7. Large mixing bowl
- **Material:** Wood, copper, or ceramic.
- **Wood method:** Rough out a blank with an axe. Turn on a lathe with hook tools. Hollow the inside and shape the outside. Sand and oil.
- **Copper method:** Raise a disc over a bowl-shaped stake with repeated annealing and hammering. Planish smooth.
- **Ceramic method:** Throw on a wheel, dry, glaze, fire in a kiln. This is a separate trade and may need a potter.
- **Tools needed:** Lathe (pole or powered), hook tools, or raising hammers and stakes.

#### 8. Wooden spoon
- **Method:** Split a billet from green wood. Rough out with an axe. Carve the bowl with a spoon knife or gouge. Refine the handle with a drawknife and knife. Dry slowly. Sand and oil.
- **Tools needed:** Axe, spoon knife, knife.
- **Alternate:** Turn the handle on a lathe and carve the bowl.

#### 9. Tongs
- **Method (riveted):** Forge two matching arms from flat bar. Draw the jaws and flatten the reins. Punch a hole at the pivot. Rivet the arms together. Add a stop if needed.
- **Method (spring tongs):** Forge a single bent bar with a flat spring at the bend. Shape the jaws. Heat treat the spring section.
- **Tools needed:** Forge, hammers, punch, rivet.
- **Note:** Smith tongs and kitchen tongs differ in jaw shape and spring tension.

#### 10. Spatula (metal turner)
- **Method:** Forge a thin flat blade with a tang. Bevel the leading edge. Bend the neck slightly upward. Fit a wooden handle by burning in the tang or riveting scales on.
- **Tools needed:** Forge, anvil, files, grindstone.

#### 11. Measuring cups and spoons
- **Material:** Brass, copper, or tinned iron.
- **Method:**
  1. Decide the volume standard. Calibrate with water and a scale, since one unit of water has a known mass.
  2. Raise or roll a cylinder of the right internal volume. Check by filling with water.
  3. Solder or braze the seam and base.
  4. Add a handle (soldered or riveted).
  5. For spoons, stamp or hammer a small bowl on a swage and add a stem.
- **Tools needed:** Balance beam scale and reference weights, stakes, solder.
- **Key point:** The scale comes first because it defines the units. Without it every cup is a different size.

#### 12. Colander or fine mesh strainer
- **Colander method:** Raise a bowl shape from sheet. Punch holes in a pattern with a hardened punch and a lead or wooden backing block. Deburr. Add a rim and feet.
- **Mesh method:** Draw fine wire through the drawplate. Weave it on a small frame loom. Solder or crimp the weave into a rim hoop. Add a handle.
- **Tools needed:** Punch, drawplate, loom, snips, solder.

### Upgrades

#### 13. Paring knife
- Same process as the chef's knife at smaller scale. A smaller blade quenches more evenly and heat treats more easily, so this is a good first knife for apprentices.

#### 14. Instant-read thermometer
- **No electricity options:**
  - **Bimetallic coil:** Bond two metals with different expansion rates (brass and steel) into a strip. Coil it. The coil twists as temperature changes and turns a needle over a graduated dial. Calibrate against boiling water and ice.
  - **Liquid-in-glass:** Fill a thin glass tube with mercury or colored alcohol. Seal. Mark a scale. Requires a glassblower.
  - **Magic version:** An enchanted gem or rune that glows or displays a number tied to local heat. Easiest to build.
- **Tools needed:** Drawplate, soldering tools, precision lathe, calibration references.

#### 15. Whisk
- **Method:** Draw wire to a consistent thin gauge. Cut equal lengths. Bend each into a loop. Gather the loops and bind the ends into a handle with a wire wrap or a ferrule. Spread the loops into a balloon shape.
- **Tools needed:** Drawplate, snips, pliers, small mandrel.
- **Alternate:** Bundle of birch twigs (traditional, no metalwork).

#### 16. Peeler
- **Method (swivel blade):** Forge a small steel blade with a slot. Make a U-shaped frame from flat bar. Pivot the blade between the arms with a rivet. Add a handle.
- **Method (fixed blade):** Forge a loop of steel with a sharpened inner edge. Add a wooden handle.
- **Tools needed:** Forge, files, whetstone, drill.

#### 17. Box grater
- **Method:**
  1. Roll or hammer thin sheet.
  2. Punch raised teeth by driving a pointed punch from the back with a hammer. Each hit makes one tooth.
  3. Bend the sheet into a four-sided box and rivet or solder the seam.
  4. Add a handle on top.
- **Faster:** A press with a tooth die stamps many teeth in one stroke.
- **Tools needed:** Rolling mill or sheet, pointed punches, backing block, solder.

#### 18. Kitchen scale
- **Balance beam method:** Forge a beam with a central knife-edge pivot hardened and ground. Hang two pans. Make standard weights by casting brass and filing to match a reference mass. Calibrate against the reference.
- **Spring scale method:** Coil a spring from tempered wire and attach it to a dial. Calibrate with weights.
- **Magic version:** Gravity or mass rune with a readout.
- **Tools needed:** Precision files, drawplate, lathe for weights, reference mass (the hard part).
- **Reference mass:** Defining the standard may be a political or guild question in the setting.

#### 19. Blender or immersion blender
- **Hand crank version:**
  1. Cast or turn a cylindrical jar from metal or glass.
  2. Forge and file a blade assembly with two or three angled blades on a shaft.
  3. Mount the shaft through a sealed bearing (leather gasket and bushing) in the base.
  4. Add a gear train (bevel gears) to a hand crank. Gear ratio multiplies blade speed.
- **Treadle or water power:** Connect the shaft to a belt drive.
- **Magic version:** A spinning rune on the shaft. Mana cost scales with load.
- **Tools needed:** Lathe for the shaft and bearings, gear cutting setup (hobbing or hand filing), file work, leather seals.
- **Alternate:** A mortar and pestle or a sieve and a spoon does most of the same work.
