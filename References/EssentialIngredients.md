# Essential Ingredients

**Earth research anchors** for adventure cooking. Star ingredients and field methods. Flavor partners refer to `EssentialFlavorings.md`. Map flavor to Caldris; do not paste Earth crop or dish names into prose as if they are local unless the setting already has them.

## Animals

**Flavor range:** Boar and pork run rich, sweet and nutty with a mild wild edge, and bacon adds salty smoke. Beef is deep, savory and iron-rich with a mineral depth in the organs. Deer is lean, earthy and herbal with forage notes. Fish is clean, briny and sweet, with oily richness in fatty species and delicate mildness in lean ones. Chicken is mild and savory with roasty, sweet skin. Eggs are rich, creamy and faintly sulfurous. Fat carries all of it, so it decides how much seasoning each one can take.

### Boar
**Best cuts:** Neck and shoulder for skewers and braises. Belly for fat. Leg for roasts. Loin only when wrapped in fat.
**Method:** Young boar takes skewers, spit roasting and quick grilling. Mature boar needs low and slow braising or a pit oven.
**No-pot method:** Earth oven for shoulder, cubes on green sticks for skewers.
**Flavor partners:** cumin, rosemary, ginger, wine, vinegar, chili, black pepper
**Doneness:** 160°F (71°C)
**Tip:** Trim the fat on mature animals since it carries the gamey flavor. Add pork or bacon fat to lean cuts.

#### Pork (subset of boar)
**Best cuts:** Shoulder and belly for slow cooking and skewers. Ribs for pit BBQ. Loin and chops for fast grilling. Leg for roasts.
**Method:** Cooks like young boar with more fat and a milder flavor. Any boar method works on pork.
**No-pot method:** Pit BBQ for shoulder and ribs, spit roast for a whole leg, skewers with belly and shoulder cubes.
**Flavor partners:** garlic, rosemary, black pepper, vinegar, apples, cumin
**Doneness:** 145°F (63°C) for whole cuts after a 3-minute rest. 160°F (71°C) for ground pork.
**Tip:** Pork fat is the fix for lean boar. Render trimmings into lard.

#### Bacon (subset of boar)
**What it is:** Cured and smoked belly from boar or pork.
**Uses:** Threads onto skewers, wraps lean meat like loin, breast and venison, and seasons beans, greens and potatoes as it renders.
**No-pot method:** Lay strips across a hot flat stone, or wind them around meat on a stick over medium coals.
**Flavor partners:** black pepper, honey, vinegar, onion, cabbage, eggs
**Doneness:** Cook until the fat renders and the edges crisp.
**Tip:** Keep the rendered fat. It sears and seasons the next thing in the pan.

### Cow
**Best cuts:** Ribeye, strip and sirloin for fast heat. Chuck, brisket, short rib and shank for slow cooking. Ground beef for patties. Liver for a quick sear.
**Method:** Steaks go directly over hot coals or on a hot stone for 2 to 4 minutes per side. Tough cuts braise or smoke for 4 to 8 hours.
**No-pot method:** Directly on coals, flat rock griddle, pit BBQ for brisket and shoulder cuts.
**Flavor partners:** garlic, black pepper, rosemary, thyme, wine, butter
**Doneness:** 130 to 135°F (54 to 57°C) for medium-rare steak. 160°F (71°C) for ground beef.
**Tip:** Salt early and rest the meat for 5 to 10 minutes after cooking.

### Deer
**Best cuts:** Backstrap (loin) and tenderloin for fast searing. Haunch (leg) for roasts and skewers. Shoulder, neck and shank for braising. Liver and heart for a quick sear.
**Method:** Very lean, so cook loin cuts hot and fast and stop at medium-rare. Braise tough cuts low and slow with fat and wine.
**No-pot method:** Backstrap on a hot stone or over coals, cubes on skewers with bacon, shoulder in a pit oven.
**Flavor partners:** juniper, rosemary, wine, black pepper, thyme, butter
**Doneness:** 130 to 135°F (54 to 57°C) for backstrap and tenderloin. 160°F (71°C) for ground meat and braised cuts.
**Tip:** Remove the silver skin and wrap or thread with bacon or pork fat. Overcooked venison turns dry and livery.

### Fish
**Types:** Fatty fish (salmon, trout, mackerel, sardine) handle high heat. Lean fish (cod, halibut, pike) need gentle heat and protection.
**Method:** Grill whole over medium coals, roast on a plank, split-stick clamp or wrap in leaves. Fatty fish also smoke well.
**No-pot method:** Salt crust in coals, clay wrap, leaf packet, split stick over the fire.
**Flavor partners:** lemon, parsley, butter, ginger, garlic, vinegar
**Doneness:** 145°F (63°C), or when the flesh flakes and turns opaque.
**Tip:** Leave the skin on. It protects the flesh and crisps into the best part.

### Chicken
**Best cuts:** Thighs and drumsticks are forgiving and juicy. Breast dries out fast. Whole birds spit roast well. Skin, hearts and liver suit skewers.
**Method:** Spit roast or tripod hang whole birds for 1 to 2 hours. Grill thighs skin side down over medium coals and keep the fat off the hot zone.
**No-pot method:** Spit, clay crust, leaf wrap in coals, skewers.
**Flavor partners:** garlic, thyme, lemon, rosemary, smoked paprika, black pepper
**Doneness:** 165°F (74°C)
**Tip:** Dry the skin and salt it ahead of time for a crisp finish.

### Eggs
**Method:** Bury in warm ash for 10 to 15 minutes, fry on a hot flat stone in fat, cook in a hollowed orange half on coals, or crack into a packet of foil or leaves beside the fire.
**No-pot method:** Ash, hot stone, hollowed citrus, foil packet.
**Flavor partners:** butter, black pepper, chili, smoked paprika, parsley, bacon
**Doneness:** Whites fully set. Yolks to preference.
**Tip:** Eggs are the most compact protein to carry. Unwashed fresh eggs keep for weeks in a cool place.

## Vegetables

**Flavor range:** Sweet and caramel (carrot, sweet potato, winter squash, corn, beets) improves with roasting. Starchy and earthy (potato, beets) is neutral and filling. Sulfurous and nutty (cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower) sweetens with browning and turns harsh when overcooked. Bitter and peppery greens (kale, spinach) mellow with fat, salt and acid. Fresh and crisp (lettuce, celery, green beans) are best raw or barely cooked. Bell pepper is grassy raw and smoky-sweet once charred.

### Potato
**Method:** Bury whole in coals and ash for 30 to 60 minutes, roast in chunks with fat, or slice thick and sear on a hot stone.
**Flavor partners:** butter, rosemary, garlic, parsley, black pepper
**Tip:** Start boiled potatoes in cold salted water. Mash fat and salt into the hot flesh of an ash-baked one.

### Sweet Potato
**Method:** Bury whole in coals for 30 to 60 minutes until soft and leaking syrup. Slice and sear on a hot stone for a faster cook.
**Flavor partners:** butter, chili, cumin, honey, black pepper
**Tip:** Sweet and filling with no prep. Chili and salt balance the sweetness.

### Carrot
**Method:** Roast whole in coals until soft and caramelized, grill in halves, or eat raw. Peels and tops go into stock.
**Flavor partners:** butter, honey, cumin, thyme, ginger
**Tip:** Roasting concentrates the sweetness. Cumin and honey make a fast glaze.

### Celery
**Method:** Cut small and cook with onion and carrot as the aromatic base for soups, stews and braises. Eat raw for crunch.
**Flavor partners:** onion, garlic, thyme, parsley, black pepper, lemon
**Tip:** The leaves work as an herb and the tough outer stalks belong in the stock.

### Pepper (bell)
**Method:** Char whole over coals until the skin blackens, rest covered and peel. Thread chunks on skewers between meat.
**Flavor partners:** garlic, vinegar, smoked paprika, onion, parsley
**Tip:** Charring turns raw sharpness into smoky sweetness. Peeled peppers keep well in vinegar.

### Winter Squash
**Method:** Halve and scoop the seeds, then roast cut side down on coals or beside them for 30 to 45 minutes. Butternut, acorn and pumpkin all work.
**Flavor partners:** butter, honey, cumin, black pepper, chili
**Tip:** Roast the seeds in the coals with salt for a snack.

### Corn
**Method:** Roast in the husk directly on coals for 10 to 15 minutes, turning once. Husk and silk peel off together.
**Flavor partners:** butter, chili, lemon, black pepper, parsley
**Tip:** Soak the ears briefly first if the coals are very hot. The husk steams the kernels and protects them.

### Beets
**Method:** Bury whole in ash for 45 to 90 minutes depending on size and peel the skin off after. Slice thin and eat raw. Cook the greens like kale.
**Flavor partners:** vinegar, butter, parsley, honey, black pepper
**Tip:** Wear gloves or expect stained hands. Vinegar and salt sharpen the earthy sweetness.

### Lettuce
**Method:** Serve raw and torn, dressed just before eating. Halve romaine or gem and sear cut side down for 1 to 2 minutes. Use large leaves as wraps for grilled meat.
**Flavor partners:** lemon, vinegar, black pepper, parsley, garlic, butter
**Tip:** Dry the leaves fully so dressing clings. Dress at the last moment.

### Cabbage
**Method:** Bury whole in coals for 30 to 40 minutes, char wedges over the fire, shred raw for slaw, braise in fat, or ferment into sauerkraut for storage.
**Flavor partners:** vinegar, butter, black pepper, cumin, onion
**Tip:** Cabbage is the best keeper of the group. It lasts weeks in a cool place and cuts richness in fatty meat.

### Broccoli
**Method:** Roast or char over high heat until the edges blacken, or blanch briefly and finish in butter. Peel and use the stems.
**Flavor partners:** garlic, lemon, chili, butter, soy sauce
**Tip:** High heat gives sweet, nutty browning. Overcooking turns it sulfurous and mushy.

### Cauliflower
**Method:** Roast whole in coals, cut into thick steaks and sear, or break into florets for skewers. It also mashes into a rich puree.
**Flavor partners:** cumin, butter, lemon, garlic, chili, black pepper
**Tip:** Deep browning matters. Pale cauliflower tastes flat.

### Kale
**Method:** Char on a stick over medium coals for 1 to 3 minutes, wilt in hot fat and garlic, or braise low and long. Massage raw leaves with oil and salt.
**Flavor partners:** garlic, lemon, chili, vinegar, butter, onion
**Tip:** Strip the ribs. Salt and acid soften raw leaves and take off the bitterness.

### Spinach
**Method:** Wilt in hot fat and garlic for under a minute, or fold into a leaf packet with butter and set at the edge of the coals.
**Flavor partners:** garlic, lemon, butter, cream, black pepper
**Tip:** Spinach shrinks to a fraction of its volume, so cook a much larger pile than expected. The fastest green to cook.

### Green Beans
**Method:** Blister over high heat on a hot stone or grate until charred in spots, or thread onto skewers alongside meat.
**Flavor partners:** garlic, lemon, butter, chili, soy sauce
**Tip:** Cook hot and fast so they stay crisp and sweet.

## Staples

**Flavor range:** Rice and flour are neutral and starchy, and they take on the flavor of whatever sits beside them. Beans and lentils are earthy, nutty and creamy. Cooking fat is pure richness, with lard and tallow tasting of the animal and oil tasting clean. Bone stock is deep, savory and full of umami. Staples give bulk and body, and the rest of the meal gives them flavor.

### Beans and Lentils
**Method:** Lentils cook in 20 to 30 minutes and need no soaking. Beans need an overnight soak and long cooking in a vessel such as a clay pot, a sealed bamboo tube or a foil packet with water buried in coals.
**Flavor partners:** cumin, smoked paprika, onion, garlic, vinegar
**Tip:** Add acid like vinegar and tomato at the end since it stops beans from softening.

### Rice
**Method:** Steam in a bamboo tube with water leaned over coals, in a leaf wrap, or in a foil packet buried in coals. Use about 1 part rice to 1.5 parts water.
**Flavor partners:** butter, soy sauce, ginger, garlic, chili
**Tip:** Rinse first for separate grains. Rice also soaks up the juices of grilled meat.

### Flour
**Method:** Flour, water and salt make flatbread on a hot stone. Wind dough around a peeled stick and rotate over low coals for 8 to 12 minutes. Also thickens stews and coats meat before searing.
**Flavor partners:** butter, garlic, rosemary, black pepper, cheese
**Tip:** Rest the dough for 20 minutes so it rolls thin without tearing.

### Cooking Fat (lard, tallow, oil)
**Method:** Sears, roasts and fries. Lard and tallow handle high heat better than butter. Oil works for dressing and finishing.
**Flavor partners:** garlic, rosemary, black pepper, onion
**Tip:** Render boar and pork trimmings into lard and strain it into a tin. Ghee is the shelf-stable option for searing.

### Bone Stock
**Method:** Roast bones until browned, then simmer with onion, carrot and celery. Chicken takes 3 to 4 hours and beef 8 to 12.
**Flavor partners:** onion, thyme, parsley, black pepper, garlic
**Tip:** Skim the surface early. A base of stock turns scraps into soups, braises and sauces.

## Dairy

**Flavor range:** Milk is mild, sweet and lightly creamy. Cream is rich and smooth. Butter is rich and nutty, and it browns toasty. Cheese ranges from mild and melting to sharp, salty and savory with age. Yogurt is tangy and sour with a cooling creaminess. Dairy softens heat and sharpness, and the tangy forms add acid while the rich ones add fat.

### Cream
**Uses:** Finishing sauces, enriching soups and stews, whipping, and softening sharp flavors.
**Flavor partners:** black pepper, garlic, wine, thyme, parsley, lemon
**Tip:** Heavy cream with 35 percent fat or more resists curdling when reduced. Add acid or wine after the cream has warmed to avoid splitting.

### Milk
**Uses:** Braising pork and boar, soaking liver and fish to mellow strong flavor, poaching fish, and making white sauce.
**Flavor partners:** butter, black pepper, garlic, thyme, ginger
**Tip:** Simmer gently since milk scorches and boils over fast. Powdered milk covers the trail.

### Butter
**Uses:** Searing, basting, finishing, and carrying herbs and garlic. Brown it for a nutty sauce.
**Flavor partners:** garlic, thyme, rosemary, parsley, lemon
**Tip:** Butter burns at high heat. Use clarified butter or ghee for searing and butter for basting and finishing.

### Cheese
**Uses:** Hard cheese (parmesan, aged cheddar) grates over anything and keeps for weeks. Firm cheese (halloumi, paneer) grills without melting away. Melting cheese goes on flatbread and potatoes.
**Flavor partners:** black pepper, garlic, butter, rosemary, chili
**Tip:** Wrap in cloth and keep cool. Hard cheese travels best.

### Yogurt
**Uses:** Tenderizes meat in marinades (4 to 12 hours), cools chili heat, and makes a fast sauce with garlic and lemon.
**Flavor partners:** cumin, garlic, lemon, parsley, chili
**Tip:** Wipe the marinade off before grilling so it browns instead of burning. Do not boil it since it splits.

## Fruit

**Flavor range:** Sweet and jammy (berries, plums, figs) suits glazes and sauces. Sweet and tart (apples, pears, cherries) balances fatty meat. Tangy citrus (lemon, orange, lime) lifts and cuts richness. Aromatic and floral (quince, peach, apricot) adds perfume to slow braises. Tropical and juicy (pineapple, mango) tenderizes and sweetens grilled meat. Fruit brings acid and sugar, which are the two things fat needs for balance.

### Apples
**Uses:** Roast beside pork and boar for sweet-sour balance, bake whole in coals with butter, or slice raw with cheese.
**Flavor partners:** butter, honey, vinegar, black pepper, pork
**Tip:** Apples keep for weeks in a cool place and pair with fatty meat better than any sugar.

## Classic Combinations

- **Aromatic base:** onion, carrot and celery cooked slowly in fat
- **Roast dinner:** chicken or boar, potato, carrot, butter
- **Fish and greens:** fish with lemon, butter and charred kale or broccoli
- **Rich and sharp:** boar or cow with cabbage and vinegar
- **Cream sauce:** cream reduced with garlic, black pepper and a splash of wine over chicken or fish
- **Pork and apples:** roasted pork or boar with apples, butter and black pepper
- **Camp breakfast:** bacon, ash-baked eggs and flatbread
- **Game night:** venison wrapped in bacon with juniper, rosemary and a wine sauce
- **Yogurt marinade:** boar or chicken skewers with cumin, garlic and lemon
