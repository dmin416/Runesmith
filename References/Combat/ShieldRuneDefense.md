# Best Use of Rune Magic on a Shield

Redirecting the strike is the most effective option. Stopping it, hardening the shield and bashing back each solve a different part of the problem and cost far more energy against a strong attacker.

Pairs with the light kit in `LightArmorLoadout.md` (buckler bracer as active deflection). Strike energy bands: `AttackScale.md`. Cast / path Useful: `../Science/ManaCast.md`, `../Runes/Energy.md`.

**Example strike:** **20 kg** effective mass at **10 m/s**, so **200 kg·m/s** of momentum and **1 kJ** of energy (`½mv²`).

## Options compared

| Approach | What it does | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Deflect (lateral velocity + angle)** | Changes the strike's direction so most of its momentum stays with the attacker | Needs timing and a good angle |
| **Mass spike (sudden stop)** | Turns the shield into a momentum sink so the shield barely moves and the weapon takes the damage | The momentum still reaches the wearer and only lands slower |
| **Hardness** | Resists penetration at the contact point | Does nothing about momentum |
| **Bash back** | Reverses the weapon and staggers the attacker | Raises relative speed at contact and so raises the load on the shield |

## Why deflection wins

Stopping a strike removes all **200 kg·m/s**. Turning it **20°** needs only about **70 kg·m/s** of lateral impulse (about a third: `|Δp| = 2mv sin(θ/2)`). The energy the rune spends on the sideways push is roughly **120 J** (**12** mana raw at **10 J**/mana), which is small next to the **1 kJ** being avoided. The attacker also ends up off-balance and overextended.

A rune that adds velocity on the axis perpendicular to the strike gives the most defense per mana. The reactive velocity can also run in the direction of the blow to lengthen contact time. Peak force equals impulse divided by time, so doubling the contact time halves the peak load on the arm.

## Role of each effect

- **Mass spike:** best against fast light hits such as arrows, bolts and blade edges. Against a heavy attacker the shield still gets thrown at high speed unless its mass is huge. The spike should be inertial only with no added weight on the strap or wrist, or the bracer fails first.
- **Hardness:** best applied only at the impact patch. Piercing enemies focus force into a tiny area (a spear tip or pick head) so a local hardness spike at the contact point handles penetration while the rest of the shield stays light.
- **Bash:** works best when the attacker is weak or already committed. Moving the shield toward the weapon early in its swing meets it near the hilt or shoulder where speed is low. A forward bash into the peak of a heavy swing adds the shield's speed to the collision and makes the hit worse.

## Best sequence

1. **Intercept early** by pushing the shield forward at the start of the swing where the weapon is slowest.
2. **Angle and kick sideways** at contact so the strike slides off the face.
3. **Spike mass and hardness** for the few milliseconds of contact to protect the shield and the arm.
4. **Bash after contact** once the weapon has been turned and the attacker's momentum is spent.

## Defense against plate-piercing enemies

For attackers strong enough to punch through plate, absorption fails because the momentum has to go somewhere. Redirection sends it into empty air. A shield that never takes the full hit needs neither thick metal nor heavy armor behind it, which fits the light loadout of mail, enchanted leather and buckler bracer (`LightArmorLoadout.md`).
