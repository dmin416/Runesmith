# Light Armor vs Plate-Piercing Foes

Loadout design when the main threat **reliably pierces plate**. Heavy plate then loses its job. Weight, heat and stamina drain become pure cost. Prefer a light mobile kit that covers stray hits and supports **active** defense.

**Caldris note:** enchanted leather / runic wards carry the magic layer. Plain mail stays the mundane backup if the enchant drains. Pair with `Weapons.md`, `MageDefense.md`, hardness gates in `../World/Science/Energy/ManaCast.md` and rune-shield deflection math in `ShieldRuneDefense.md`.

## Springs on segmented plate

Springs help very little and often hurt.

**Minor benefits:**

- Return overlapping lames to their default position after bending
- Reduce rattling and snagging
- Keep a raised visor or articulated piece from flopping

**Drawbacks:**

- A spring is a weak point that blade tips, arrowheads and picks can exploit
- Spring tension makes the wearer fight the armor on every movement
- Thin spring steel bends, snaps, corrodes and jams with mud, sweat and blood
- Added complexity means more maintenance and more failure points

**Better alternatives:** sliding rivets, leather or textile strap suspension, arming points tied to a gambeson and precise fit.

For fiction, springs fit best as part of a powered or assisted exoskeleton element (runic assist), not as passive field plate.

## Armor vs no armor

**Armor still has some value when pierced:**

- Piercing costs energy, so hits penetrate less deeply
- Curved surfaces deflect off-angle strikes
- Weaker foes, arrows, falls and stray blows still get stopped
- A fraction of a second of resistance can enable a counterattack

**Drop heavy armor when:**

- Every hit from the strong enemy is lethal or disabling regardless
- Survival depends on dodging, parrying or staying out of reach
- Shedding **20 to 25 kg** gives a real stamina and speed edge

## Recommended loadout

| Piece | Role |
|---|---|
| Chain mail (short-sleeved or elbow length) | Stops cuts and slashes, spreads minor impacts, base layer for stray hits |
| Enchanted leather over mail | Carries the real defense through enchantment / runes, adds flexibility and quiet movement |
| Heart shield | Small plate over the highest-lethality target |
| Buckler bracer | Forearm-mounted buckler for active deflection and parrying |
| Padded gorget or mail coif | Covers the neck |
| Layered leather flaps | Cover armpits and groin without much weight |

Total weight: roughly **8 to 12 kg**.

## Design tips

- Mount the buckler bracer high on the forearm so it rotates with the wrist and does not restrict weapon grip.
- Give the heart shield a slight dome and a beveled edge so strikes skate off instead of meeting it square.
- Put the enchantment on the leather (impact dispersal, reduced piercing depth or a reactive ward) rather than on the mail, so the mail stays reliable if the enchantment fails or gets drained.
- Keep the mail sleeves short to preserve arm mobility.

## Weak points

The neck, armpits and groin remain the exposed areas. The gorget or coif and layered leather flaps cover them at low weight cost.

## Key takeaway

Against enemies too strong to tank, **active deflection beats passive resistance**. The buckler bracer is the standout piece of the setup.

In Caldris this explains why evasive light-armored fighters dominate high-threat work while heavy plate stays a status symbol, parade kit or specialist tool against foes that still bounce.
