## Panzer Kunst (Battle Angel Alita)

Panzer Kunst ("armor art") is a fictional cyborg martial art from Yukito Kishiro's *Battle Angel Alita* and *Last Order*. Its founder Tiger Sauer built it as the first martial art designed for cyborg combat. He trained students to fight in zero gravity and against armed opponents. The art centers on unifying the senses and is especially effective against larger enemies.

### Core Ideas
- **Penetrating strikes.** The Hertz Haeon is an oscillating palm strike that sends a powerful pulse through the opponent's body.
- **Rhythm reading and counters.** A signature counter syncs to the opponent's timing and drives an elbow or knee into the temple as they attack or lower their guard.
- **Weapons as limbs.** Alita's elbow blades belong to the Mauser School branch.
- **Defeating armor and size.** Strikes target joints, seams and weak points instead of trading force with heavier bodies.

## Fishman Karate (One Piece)

Fishman Karate is used by Jinbe, Arlong's crew and Koala. Its premise is that water exists everywhere: in the air, in the ground and inside living bodies. The practitioner strikes the water rather than the surface. The shockwave travels through internal fluids and damages organs while bypassing toughness or armor.

### Core Ideas
- **Internal damage.** Blows ripple through the body instead of stopping at skin and muscle.
- **Environmental water.** Uchimizu flicks droplets hard enough to act like bullets.
- **Range through water.** Fishman Jujutsu, the advanced form, controls water at a distance and is strongest in the sea.
- **Learnable by humans.** Humans can train it at lower power than fishmen.

## Real-World Methods That Approximate Them

### Penetrating or "Internal" Strikes (Hertz Haeon and Fishman Karate Shockwaves)
- **Chinese internal arts.** Bajiquan, Xingyiquan and Taijiquan train fajin, an explosive whole-body release meant to drive force through a target.
- **Wing Chun short power.** The one-inch punch and chain punches generate force over tiny distances through hip and body alignment.
- **Body shots in boxing and Muay Thai.** A liver shot drops fighters through internal effect with little visible damage. The body is roughly 60% water and fluid-filled organs transmit blunt force, which is the real physics behind the Fishman Karate idea.
- **Okinawan karate kime.** Full-body tension at impact concentrates force into a short, deep strike.
- **Palm heel strikes.** Used across karate, kung fu, Kenpo and Krav Maga. Lab data shows the palm strike produces the highest peak force of any hand strike.

### Armored and Oversized Opponents (Panzer Kunst)
- **German armored fencing (Harnischfechten).** Half-swording and thrusts into gaps at the armpit, groin, visor and joints. This is the closest historical match to Panzer Kunst's German naming and logic.
- **Ringen.** Medieval German wrestling designed for fighting in armor.
- **Kumi-uchi.** Samurai armored grappling and the root of jujutsu.
- **Judo and Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu.** Leverage, joint locks and chokes neutralize larger opponents.

### Elbows, Knees and Timing Counters
- **Muay Thai and Muay Boran.** Elbows and knees to the head are core weapons, matching the Panzer Kunst temple counter.
- **Jeet Kune Do stop-hits.** Intercepting the attack mid-motion.
- **Boxing counterpunching.** Reading rhythm, slipping and landing during the opponent's commitment.

### Blades as Part of the Body
- **Filipino Kali/Eskrima and Pencak Silat.** Blade work integrated with empty-hand motion. The karambit and Silat elbow work resemble the elbow blade style.

### Unstable Footing and Zero-Gravity Analogs
- **BJJ and wrestling.** Ground fighting removes the stable stance.
- **Systema.** Fluid movement, breath control and striking from awkward positions.

### Fighting in Water (Fishman Jujutsu)
- **Nihon eiho (samurai swimming).** Schools such as Kobori-ryu trained swimming and fighting in armor in water.
- **Military combat swimming.** Modern special forces train underwater grappling and weapon retention.

## Force by Martial Art

### Wing Chun (Ving Tsun)

| Technique | Peak Force | Approx. lbf | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vertical fist / chain punch | ~1.5 to 3 kN | ~340 to 675 | Estimate. Kung fu punches measure significantly below boxing straight punches and karate reverse punches (mean ceiling 3.4 kN) |
| Palm strike | Higher than the Wing Chun punch | ~1,000+ at elite level | Measured on kung fu practitioners. Real-world palm ceiling ~4.75 to 5+ kN |
| Elbow (pai jarn / gwai jarn) | ~5 kN class | ~1,125 | Estimate from elbow strike data in other systems |
| Front/stomping kick | ~2.4 kN class | ~540 | Estimate from push-kick data |

Key points:
- A study on Ving Tsun practitioners found the palm strike transferred significantly more force than the punch with similar arm acceleration. The likely reason is fewer rigid links and joints in the palm.
- Strikes to chest height produced more force and momentum transfer than strikes to head height.
- The same study notes each Wing Chun strike carries less power than a boxer's punch. The system compensates with rapid repeated strikes and precision at close range.
- A meta-analysis confirmed straight punches and reverse punches produce larger mean impact forces than the kung fu punch.
- Best Fishman Karate analog in Wing Chun: short-range palm strikes to the chest and torso.

### Muay Thai

| Technique | Peak Force | Approx. lbf | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roundhouse kick (body) | ~6.7 kN mean, upper range ~10 kN | ~1,500 to 2,250 | Measured (beginner sample with wide spread); trained pros sit at the top of this range |
| Roundhouse kick (head) | Lower than the body kick | ~1,100 to 1,400 | Measured trend: middle kicks beat high kicks for force and impulse |
| Elbow strike | ~5 kN class | ~1,125 | Measured in combat athletes; elbow gives the highest impulse of any arm strike |
| Knee (khao yao, flying knee) | ~5 to 7 kN | ~1,125 to 1,575 | Estimate. Drives full body mass forward and pulls the target into the strike in the clinch |
| Teep (push kick) | ~2.5 to 4 kN | ~560 to 900 | Estimate. Push-style kick built for displacement rather than peak impact |
| Punches | Same as boxing (~2.4 to 4.8 kN) | ~545 to 1,080 | Measured boxing data |

Key points:
- Muay Thai roundhouse kicks easily generate enough force to cause neurological impairment and fractures of the skull, face and ribs.
- Peak force and impulse track with ankle velocity at impact, not with raw leg strength.
- Swinging the arm backward during the kick produced higher force at upper and middle levels than swinging it forward.
- Best Panzer Kunst analog: Muay Thai elbows and knees combined with counter timing. A knee or elbow landing as the opponent steps in adds their forward momentum to the collision.

### Boxing

| Technique | Peak Force | Approx. lbf |
|---|---|---|
| Jab | ~1.6 kN | ~370 |
| Cross / straight | ~2.4 to 4.8 kN (max recorded ~5 kN) | ~545 to 1,126 |
| Hooks | ~2.5 to 2.6 kN mean | ~567 to 590 |

Key points:
- Across studies, punch force ranged from 989 to 5,008.6 N. Rear straights and rear hooks produced the most force.
- Heavier fighters hit harder mainly through effective mass.

### Taekwondo

| Technique | Peak Force | Approx. lbf |
|---|---|---|
| Sine-wave punch | 6.9 kN mean | ~1,548 |
| Reverse-step punch | 5.1 kN mean | ~1,136 |
| Roundhouse (bandal chagi) | 6.4 kN mean | ~1,439 |
| Side kick | 2.4 kN (master) to 9 kN (highest recorded) | ~541 to 2,027 |

The sine-wave punch uses a rise and drop of the body to add gravitational energy and beat the reverse-step punch.

### Hapkido and Shaolin Wushu

- Both right crosses measured below the Taekwondo sine-wave punch in raw force.
- The Wushu cross produced the highest force-to-body-weight ratio of the group at 82:1.

### Chinese Internal Arts (Bajiquan, Xingyiquan, Taijiquan, Yau-Man Kung Fu)

- Yau-Man palm strikes reached power values up to 1,200 W. Trained practitioners outperformed untrained subjects.
- No lab data shows fajin exceeding the palm and elbow ranges above. Practical ceiling: ~4.75 to 5+ kN with palm strikes.

### Judo

- Throws are measured by landing impact rather than strike force. Black belts thrown with ippon seoi nage recorded wrist impacts up to 351.95 g of vertical acceleration. Hip impacts lasted longest at 0.58 s.
- The force comes from the full body mass of the thrown person hitting the ground. On hard ground without a mat this exceeds any single strike.

### Krav Maga, Systema and Self-Defense Systems

- These systems prioritize palm strikes, elbows, knees and groin kicks. Their force ceilings match the palm (~4.75 to 5+ kN) and elbow (~5 kN) data above.
- Male athletes landed palm and elbow strikes above the 5.1 kN skull fracture threshold about 36% of the time.

## Realistic Ceiling for a Practitioner Fighting at Their Best

| Weapon | Top-Tier Peak Force | Approx. lbf | Best Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fist (straight punch) | ~5 to 6.9 kN | ~1,125 to 1,550 | Taekwondo sine-wave, boxing cross |
| Palm heel | ~5 kN and above | ~1,125+ | Kung fu, Wing Chun, Krav Maga |
| Elbow | ~5 kN and above | ~1,125+ | Muay Thai |
| Knee | ~5 to 7 kN (estimate) | ~1,125 to 1,575 | Muay Thai |
| Roundhouse kick | ~6.4 to 10 kN | ~1,440 to 2,250 | Muay Thai, Taekwondo |
| Side kick | ~9 kN | ~2,025 | Taekwondo, karate |

Notes on these numbers:
- These are single maximal strikes. Force during simulated fights runs lower than isolated test strikes because of fatigue, movement and setup.
- Target type changes readings heavily. Rigid plates record higher peaks than bags, padding or dummies.
- Experienced strikers average about 3.5 times body weight in a direct punch and reach about 4 times body weight. Inexperienced subjects average about 3 times body weight.

## Mapping to the Fictional Styles

- **Fishman Karate (human-equivalent).** Wing Chun and kung fu palm strikes or a Muay Thai body kick to the liver, solar plexus or ribs. Peak output: ~4.75 to 5+ kN with the palm and ~6.7 kN with the body kick. The "internal" effect comes from fluid-filled organs transmitting the impulse. Chest-level targeting maximizes force transfer.
- **Panzer Kunst (human-equivalent).** Muay Thai elbows and knees, rhythm-based counters and armored-combat targeting. Peak output: ~5 kN elbows, ~5 to 7 kN knees and ~6.5 to 10 kN kicks. Counter timing adds the opponent's forward momentum to the impact.
