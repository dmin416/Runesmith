# Cloak Air Cooling (Roland experiment)

Earth-physics note for a personal cooling cycle Roland works out then **forgoes** in favor of a **cold rune** (simpler on-page kit). Related: `Compression.md` (Frost Breath / Fire Piston). Cast law if mana pays the work: `ManaCast.md` (`Mana = J / (10 × η × μ)`).

**Story stance:** he understands the sphere-compress-expand cloak path well enough to price it. He drops the build for a cold rune that keeps him cool without babysitting a heat plume and a beach-ball reservoir.

## Sphere sizing

A **5 m³** sphere is **2.1 m** across (radius **1.06 m**). Filled with **35 °C** air at **1 atm** it holds about **198 mol** or **5.7 kg**. That is the full working charge. Compressed **10:1** it shrinks to **0.5 m³** (**0.98 m** across), about the size of a large beach ball hovering above the character.

## Plain release does almost nothing

A real spray can gets cold because its liquid propellant boils. Compressed air alone released through a nozzle cools only about **2 K** for a **10 atm** drop. The cold has to come from the gas **doing work** as it expands. The magic must hold the expanding gas back and absorb the push so the energy leaves the gas as motion instead of heat. If the push goes uncaught the gas arrives at ambient temperature and the compression work is wasted.

## The cycle at 10:1 (ideal control)

1. Compress slowly to **0.5 m³** while dumping **1.17 MJ** of heat into the outside air at **35 °C**. Fast compression would push the gas to about **320 °C**.
2. Hold at **10 atm** above the character. The gas sits at ambient temperature and stores the work like a battery.
3. Expand against the magic push down to **1 atm**. Gas temperature drops to about **−113 °C** and **0.61 MJ** of work is recovered.
4. Meter the cold gas into the cloak. It warms to **20 °C** while absorbing **0.77 MJ** from the character and the cloak air. The spent air vents out the hem.

Net cost is **0.55 MJ** ideal (about **130 kcal**). Applying a **2× to 3×** factor for imperfect control gives **1.1 to 1.7 MJ** (about **260 to 400 kcal**).

At ημ = 1, **1.1 to 1.7 MJ** is **110k to 170k** mana of mechanical work if the pool paid every joule with no path tricks. That scale is why he forgoes the machine for a cold rune.

## Compression ratio inside the 5 m³ cap

| Ratio | Sphere after compression | Gas temp after expansion | Cooling per charge | Net work (ideal) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5:1 | 1.0 m³ | −79 °C | 0.57 MJ | 0.35 MJ |
| 10:1 | 0.5 m³ | −113 °C | 0.77 MJ | 0.56 MJ |
| 20:1 | 0.25 m³ | −142 °C | 0.94 MJ | 0.79 MJ |

Going from 10:1 to 20:1 costs **41%** more work for **22%** more cooling. Returns diminish quickly.

## Running it

- One **10:1** charge lasts about **2 hours** at resting body heat (**100 W**) and about **26 minutes** at **500 W** of exertion. Heat leaking through the cloak from a **35 °C** environment shortens both.
- **100 W** of cooling needs a flow of about **0.75 g/s** of air from the sphere.
- Gas at **−113 °C** burns skin. It has to mix with cloak air before touching anything. Venting the air warm extracts the most cooling per gram because each gram keeps absorbing heat up to its exit temperature.
- A **10 minute** compression dumps about **2 kW** of heat upward. That is a visible heat plume and a thermal signature above the character. Compressing ahead of time in shade or during rest avoids it during action.

## Why the cold rune wins

- No hovering charge sphere in a fight.
- No compression heat plume as a beacon.
- No skin-burn metering of −113 °C air.
- Same comfort goal (stay cool) for far less babysitting once the rune is on the kit.
