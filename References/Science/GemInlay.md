# Inlaying an Unbreakable Gem

Craft method for locking a hard, unbreakable gem (or stone that will not crack) into steel. Hub: `Science.md`. Companion forge metallurgy: `CraftMetal.md`. Product difficulty: `BlacksmithProducts.md`. Mana-stone size and rates: `ManaStones.md` (this file is about the metal seat, not stone nature).

## Premise

The gem is unbreakable. It survives any impact, any heat and any thermal shock. The setting is the only part that can fail, so the design goal is to lock the gem in place and keep the surrounding metal from deforming or cracking.

## Method Comparison

| Method | Grip | Serviceable | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cast-in-place (molten steel poured around the gem)** | Excellent | No | Steel shrinks ~1.7% while cooling and clamps the gem in compression |
| **Shrink-fit collar (collar heated to dull red then dropped over the gem)** | Excellent | Only by reheating | Simple and needs no mold |
| **Threaded collar or screw-down ring** | Very good | Yes | Best when the gem must come out again |
| **Pinned or brazed screws** | Good | Yes | Backup retention and never the primary hold |
| **Bronze or silver solder flowed around the edge** | Good | With effort | Lower heat and a cleaner finish |

## Cast-in-Place (Best Permanent Method)

1. **Cut the gem seat as a dovetail.** The pocket in the cast piece is wider at the bottom than the opening so the steel forms an undercut lip over the girdle. Steel does not bond to a gem so the grip is mechanical and the lip is what holds it.
2. **Lip size:** overlap the girdle by 1 to 2 mm. Keep the lip narrow so the gem face stays exposed.
3. **Set the gem in the mold** on a flat seat with a core plug or ceramic coating over the face so molten steel cannot cover it or stick to it.
4. **Pour at about 1,500°C.** No preheat is needed for the gem.
5. **Cool slowly** so the steel shrinks evenly and grips the whole girdle.
6. **Heat-treat the casting with the gem in place** (normalize then quench and temper). Full-back bearing under the gem keeps the load spread across the back face and never on the edges.
7. **Attach the finished piece to the weapon or shield.** Pour into a separate pommel cap or boss and never pour onto a finished blade or shield rim because the heat ruins the temper of hardened parts.

## Shrink-Fit Collar (Best Simple Method)

- Machine a steel collar with an inner bore about 0.3% to 0.5% smaller than the gem girdle.
- Heat the collar to dull red (about 700 to 800°C) and drop it over the gem.
- The collar cools and clamps the gem with a permanent compression fit.
- Combine with a dovetail seat on the base for retention in both directions.

## Screws and Fasteners

- Use screws as secondary retention on a threaded collar or seat plate.
- Impact vibration loosens screws so lock each one by peening the end, brazing the head or using a cross pin.
- Threadlocker alone does not last on a shield or pommel.

## Placement

**Pommel**
- Cast the gem into a cap that threads over the tang so the same nut that locks the blade also holds the gem.
- Keep the cap profile domed and flush so it does not catch on cloth or armor.

**Shield**
- Cast the gem into the boss or a raised skirt plate.
- Rivet the plate through the shield body with backing washers on the inside so the whole shield carries the load.
- An unbreakable gem can sit flush or slightly proud since it never takes damage. The surrounding metal lip is what needs protection so a small recess of 1 mm keeps strikes off the lip edge.

## If the Gem Is a Mana Stone with a Rune

Craft constraint only (rates and capacity stay in `ManaStones.md`):

- Mana output and input scale with exposed surface area so a wide bezel throttles the stone.
- Keep the rune channel in contact with the back and sides for the output path.
- Keep the face open for ambient input.
- Use the narrow undercut lip as the retention and skip any wide covering rim.

## Best Combination

Cast-in-place with a dovetail undercut lip and full-back bearing as the primary hold. Add pinned screws or a threaded cap only when the piece needs to come apart for servicing.
