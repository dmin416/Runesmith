# Chapter 19

"*What do you mean he is gone?*"

A loud booming voice was heard followed by a sound of shattering furniture. A chair was thrown at a wall and it fractured into many pieces on impact.

This all was happening inside a large room. It looked to be some kind of noble house. There were various paintings and bookshelves in this room along with a big crest that had Arden written underneath.

"*Please my lord calm down.*"

Adam the butler used a handkerchief to wipe some sweat from his brow. He had just arrived at the Baron's office with bad news. His son was missing. The man responsible for keeping watch over him was nowhere to be found and just like him, the boy was gone. The man did not send in the weekly report as usual and by the time they sent someone else to check there was no Roland to be found.

"*T-The report said that the adventurer party that he was traveling with wasn't in the city of Carwen either. They might have just left the city together with the young master.*"

Wentworth Arden, Roland's father stood there behind his desk. In a fit of rage he had tossed his chair at the wall, missing the butler by a hair.

"*Get out and send more people! And bring me a new chair!*"

The butler just bowed while getting out of the room with haste. The Baron moved over to the couch that was to the side and grabbed himself a bottle of schnapps to soothe his nerves. He looked to be quite angry while he drank. He had seen the whole report. The man that was supposed to keep watch was gone.

There were a couple of possibilities, some better than the others. The possibility of the boy just going to a different city was there but the disappearance of one of his men was strange. The Baron smelled some foul play and he was angry that the possibility of his son's death was out there.

"*Did he escape?*"

The man spoke out before taking a big gulp straight from the bottle while contemplating. He knew well that finding his son would be difficult if he decided to run away. He did not have enough pull to get the Adventurer's Guild to give out his location. Only large Ducal houses or the King could achieve such a thing. The adventurer guilds around the kingdom were very adamant with their rules. They also had some powerful adventurers that the kingdom would not be willing to go against if push came to shove.

"*Or did he perish?*"

This was the other option on the table, one that he did not want to believe in. He shook his head while taking a swig from the bottle again. His chair was already on the way and he needed to get back to work.

A week alone, aside from eating and sleeping, gave Roland time to test what his skills could actually do. He sat in a train car for the entire stretch, watching the scenery pass while he worked through drills he hadn't tried before. Each morning he wiped face, neck and underarms with a damp cloth from his canteen water. It was not a bath but it kept the worst of the road smell down between stops.

He scratched Ned's head and thought about the storage bag his party had given him before he left. It held ten small gold coins, far more than he expected. The most he had expected was an even cut of five gold. He almost turned back to return the rest, given that he had already gotten the most valuable sword. With no way back, he had to hold onto it.

Rune scribing was not possible without materials. Doing it openly in a crowded train car would have drawn attention anyway. He had no books to study either. His Sleep Resistance skill kept him too alert to nap through the empty hours. That left mana manipulation, so he decided to explore it from the most foundational aspects.

He began with sound. The cabin was small and the train noise gave him a steady baseline to work against. He clicked his tongue once and listened to how it sounded in the space. Then he shaped mana into sharper clicks of his own, using the same base technique behind Heat but pressure instead of heat. He kept the pulses soft and brief at first. His long-term goal was pitch control, pushing each pulse high or low enough that ordinary ears could not catch it, so only the returning echo would matter to him. The source of each click did not need to touch his body, so he placed one in front of his face, another under the seat, a third near the luggage rack and a fourth beside the far window. He closed his eyes and worked out how each placement changed the return.

He sent Ned off his shoulders to test the system properly. The worm crawled under the seat, climbed the wall panel and settled into the luggage rack while Roland kept his eyes shut and moved his clicks around the cabin. The echo changed depending on where Ned's body sat and where each click originated. Hard wood produced a clean ring, while soft flesh muddied the returning sound. He pointed toward where he believed Ned was sitting, then opened his eyes to check his accuracy. His first guesses were wrong. Then they grew less wrong. Then he started guessing correctly more often than not.

**You have gained the Sound Production skill.**

**You have gained the Echolocation skill.**

That unlock changed how he trained. From that point forward, every click counted as progress toward the skill. Tongue clicks cost him nothing but a bit of spit. Mana clicks cost almost nothing at cabin volume. He could fire them from any point his shaping could reach. He did not spam them. He waited a few seconds between each pulse so the return had time to settle into a picture and so he could keep working the other drills in the same stretch. Most skills required a full cast or a full swing to register progress. This skill advanced with every single pulse instead. As he practiced producing sound for its own sake, his echolocation started using the returns and the ambient sound around him to build a mental picture of his surroundings.

He kept close watch over his mana pool while he trained. Soft cabin-scale clicks barely moved the needle. He kept his pool near full on purpose, since his regeneration pulled fastest around ninety-nine percent. He refused to waste time grinding from a depleted state. He spent exactly what his Regulation skill returned to him and never more than that. Across the week the spaced pulses added up into the tens of thousands. The picture forming in his mind sharpened along with it, moving from a vague shape under the seat to a clear outline he could hold between clicks.

By the middle of the trip, he still needed his eyes for fine detail work. The sound map no longer demanded his full attention to maintain. His early tongue clicks stayed within the normal human hearing range, buried under the noise of the train. The mana pulses he actually cared about kept climbing and dropping in pitch until nobody around him could detect them at all. That was exactly the outcome he wanted. Ned had free run of the cabin during these sessions. The blood he spent still came from Roland himself, drawn through his usual Mana Hands drip. His Recovery skill covered the cost of stray pokes and missed needle catches without falling behind on regeneration. Losing that volume and knitting it back was its own grind. Each bled measure trained how his body handled oxygen starve from blood loss, separate from how fast the wounds closed.

He also let Ned work on him directly. When the worm crawled back up to his shoulder or lap, Roland held still and allowed a fang to nick the skin of his forearm or the back of his hand. Sometimes Ned simply chewed on a soft fold of skin for the taste of it. He kept these bites shallow on purpose. Each one carried a trace of numbing toxin that came with the wound. Roland counted every one of these bites for Pain and Poison, and the blood he lost for Suffocation Resistance. Whenever a bite went too deep, he tapped Ned's chin plates. The worm pulled back immediately.

Once that groundwork was solid, he made the drill more difficult. He sat with his eyes shut and his pool near full, then had Ned climb up to the luggage rack or move across to the far seat. Quiet mana clicks kept the cabin mapped out for him alone the entire time. On his signal, Ned launched a needle at him, a short controlled flick aimed at his hand or at the open air in front of his chest.

Roland had to hear the launch, track it and catch the needle before he was allowed to open his eyes and check his result. A missed catch stung and drew a small bead of blood. A successful catch landed cleanly in his palm or between his fingers. He fed every needle he caught straight back into Ned's mouth, where the worm chewed the spent tip down and folded the material back into new growth. Nothing from these sessions was ever left behind to bother others.

He missed far more than he caught during the earliest attempts. By the middle of the week, the returning signal sketched a clear line in his mind well before his hand had finished moving into position. He kept the soft clicks coming every few seconds between launches so that neither Sound Production nor Echolocation went idle, and so each return still had room to be read.

He continued the sound training every single day. He eventually stacked heat training directly on top of it, since soft clicks alone were leaving too much of his regeneration unused. He sat completely still with Ned resting across his shoulders again and turned his attention fully inward. His Mana Sight skill showed him the fog building up inside his limbs. His Mana Regulation skill held his draw steady right at the top of his pool. He shaped thin patches of heat directly onto the skin of one forearm, packing them tight in a grid. He made the heat strong enough to redden and blister the skin. Whenever his pool crept back up past his intended mark, he adjusted the grid by making the patches hotter or more densely packed, or he stretched the breath holds between sets, until his draw matched his regeneration rate again.

Each individual patch hurt badly on its own. The packed grid hurt considerably worse. Roland wanted genuine Heat Resistance, the kind that only comes from real tissue damage, so he kept the intensity high enough that his flesh had no choice but to respond.

Red welts rose steadily under every hot square in the grid. His Pain Resistance skill kept him from flinching off the seat whenever the grid bit into his skin, whenever Ned's fang found a clean spot between the squares, or whenever a launched needle struck bare skin instead of his palm. His Recovery skill knitted the shallow damage back together each time he eased the mana off his arm. Whenever a patch went too deep, or his healing started to lag behind the damage, he tipped a small amount of low-grade potion onto the hidden skin beneath his sleeve, waited for the brew to close the worst of the wound and then started the grid again rather than let unused regeneration go to waste. Careful, fine-grained shaping kept the burns fairly small in scale. To every other passenger on the train, he looked like a quiet boy staring out the window. Underneath his sleeve, his forearm cycled steadily between raw damage and something close to normal.

He ran both drills at that same intensity for the remainder of the week. On some days he moved the heat grid down to his palm or across to the back of his hand instead of his forearm. Ned flinched once when a hot patch touched too close to the scarf line around his neck. Roland immediately shifted the patch away before letting the worm take another careful bite from cooler skin nearby. Between each set on the grid, he returned to his eyes-shut needle catches and kept feeding the spent tips back to Ned. When the sting peaked he held his breath until the patch eased, then breathed out slowly and did it again.

**You have gained the Suffocation Resistance skill.**

Every hold counted. Fifteen seconds of kept air under the grill sting was one clean tick. Longer holds stacked. The drip and the nicks counted too, by how much blood left his veins, each measure of lost volume its own tick once he survived it and Recovery put the tank back. Held air and open bleed were the same skill, two different ways of starving him of oxygen. Running the clicks, the locating, the catching and the grid all within the same stretch of hours gave his Multitasking skill genuine work to do.

By the time the mountains finally rose up outside the window, the sting from his training still came. It no longer stole his focus. He was also using noticeably less potion to sustain the same intensity of training. His sound map was solid for tracking anything cabin-sized or for following a needle in mid-flight, as long as he kept giving it a click every few seconds. His mana bar spent the entire week hovering just under full.

This long journey had taken over a whole week but now it was close to being over. He had seen the scenery change quite a bit through his travels. The Caldris Kingdom's climate was not too warm or too cold. You could see many grass fields and forest areas scattered throughout the land.

The more you went in, the more the climate changed. Where he was going it looked to be colder, more of autumn-like weather. He was also traveling into lands with a higher altitude as many rocky structures appeared. While on the last stretch of his travels he saw the scenery turn rocky and they were going into a mountain range. There was a reason why this city was known for having blacksmiths.

"*Now arriving at Edelgard, please take all of your belongings while going out.*"

The train drove up at the last stretch. The city of Edelgard was built into a large mountain. You could even see some mine shafts sprinkled in the background and smoke coming out of them. He could see this while the train moved through a large bridge that connected two city parts. Under it was quite the large gorge. A fall from here would spell certain death.

This city made him think about dwarves from some of the fantasy books he used to read back on Earth. In them it was always stated that this race liked to build their cities on or in mountains. Some even had ones placed deep underground. He was finally here. He did not need to carry his luggage thanks to his spatial bag this time around.

*I have arrived!*

He walked out half nervous and half excited. This was a new place filled with opportunities that could come to fruition. This was also a place filled with uncertainty which made his belly feel like he had a swarm of butterflies in it. There was no one to greet him here or help him out. He was truly alone. He was not lonely though as he was used to working solo. The short time he spent with those three girls was the exception to the rule.

**Ding...ding...ding**

He heard the sound of a large bell. He turned to it and saw two people in robes. The robes were white but due to time they now looked gray. There were some yellow patterns here and there but the most glaring thing was the large yellow sun symbol on their backs. These strange robe-wearing people were close to the exit so he needed to move there. Roland already had an idea of who these two people were.

"*Praise the sun traveler, may the Goddess Solaria bless you on your travels. Would you like to join us for prayer?*"

The person in the robe called out while waving the bell around. The voice was an older man. Next to him stood a second person that was smaller and of the female gender. She was holding a large plate in both her hands and holding it out as if asking for something.

Roland knew about the identity of these people. They belonged to the Church of the Sun. They worshipped the Goddess Solaria. From his more modern point of view he found people like this a bit strange. Nevertheless, people from this church were granted the Acolyte class that could then evolve into the Cleric. You could even become a Paladin.

Roland smiled as he passed the two, dropping a silver into the plate.

"*Praise the Son.*"

The coin made a crisp sound as it fell into the collection plate. Though in his old world Roland knew of the many cults and false religions that pulled people away from God, he had no idea how it worked in this world, but given that his faith had unlocked the Acolyte class, he knew that it held real power. In this world they were in a strong position. The class changing stones that were a necessity were actually produced by these people.

No one knew exactly how it happened. The Churches only revealed that it was all thanks to the power of their god. The stones were rumored to be a gift from the gods that allowed people to change classes and grow in power. This church was not the only one that was able to produce this commodity. Thanks to that the prices did not go that overboard even though the stones were not that cheap.

The two priests saw their next target behind him. He finally left the train station and went outside. He could see a lot of smoke coming out of the chimneys from this city. Most of the houses were made from rock or bricks. It was hard to spot any wooden structures here as he passed.

His first destination was the Adventurer's Guild. Even though there was no dungeon located next to this city, it still operated in it as in the others. The adventurers here were mostly used as hired muscle and they worked as bouncers, guards and even cleared out some monsters that showed up from time to time. Even without dungeons monsters existed in the wild so there was always some work to be done.

He asked for directions and headed out. He followed a dark rocky road that would lead him to the guild. While walking he took in the sights. The first thing he noticed was a large number of dwarves. There was also another interesting race that he did not see before: Gnomes. From what he knew, this race was very creative and intelligent which made them good crafters by default.

In contrast to Dwarves that liked to work with heavy weapons and armor, the Gnomes prided themselves in making more intricate machinery. The magical trains that he was using were apparently invented by the gnome tinkerers. They also worked as normal craftsmen specializing in smaller items like trinkets and magical accessories.

Roland needed to find a new base of operations. From what he could tell he needed to get himself a job at one of the scribing shops. Making magical scrolls was also a business and many adventurers used these one-time magical items for various occasions. While going through the robust-looking city he finally arrived at the Adventurer's Guild.

The guild was slightly different than the previous one but it had a similar layout with the same job listing notice board. The people inside did not care much about the small robed figure that entered. His short height would not really raise any eyebrows as there were many gnomes and dwarves sprinkled all over the place.

He got himself a city map which he had to pay but thanks to his Steel adventurer ranking he got a small 5% discount. It wasn't much but it was a nice gesture.

He looked at this map first. He needed lodging and there were some inns and taverns that he could stay nearby. He preferred the former as they did not smell like booze all the time and were not as loud all into the night.

Roland stretched out the stiffness from the train ride and saw that it was getting late. Looking around he looked for a nicer inn. He considered splurging on a really nice one after a week on the road but he just needed some place clean and quiet to rest. With a nice cloak, it wasn't too much to sleep outside but he didn't work hard his entire time here just to suffer or raise his skills. In the morning he could take a proper look at the city and see what opportunities there were to work.

Skipping past the city peasant hostel, Roland headed towards the closest Steel ranked inn. He was lucky back in Carwen so he thought that he might be lucky this time around as well.

*The Singing Crow Inn*

Roland looked at the wiggly sign that was getting pushed by the wind. The inn was quite large and had four levels to it. He pushed the creaky doors open and was greeted by a nice smell. Inside he saw a couple of patrons sitting on some wooden benches. They were eating food and drinking some beverages but were calm enough.

While distracted he moved forward, looking for some kind of counter with the bartender or someone that looked like an owner. While going through the crowd of people he felt someone bumping into him.

"*Hey, watch out where you are going!*"

He was still wearing his robe and his face was slightly covered so not everyone would notice that he was nearly 11. Roland raised a brow as the person that shouted out had quite the high pitched voice. He looked down and saw someone smaller than him with pointy ears.

"*Uh, sorry about that. Didn't see you there...*"

The person that he was looking at had a more prolonged face, making her look more like an elf than a human. Besides the long ears, her eyebrows were quite long and she had noticeable high cheekbones. This was apparently a gnome girl of unknown age.

"*You got eyes, use them!*"

"*Hey Helci! Stop flirting with the customers and go back to work!*"

Someone called out from the back which made the small gnome girl grit her teeth before moving away. She was carrying a plate with unwashed dishes that looked oversized in her small hands. Roland shrugged and moved over to the bar counter where he requested a room to stay in. The pricing was a bit higher here than at his first inn but he attributed it to this being a larger city in which people probably earned more.

The door creaked open and he was greeted to a small room with one bed and a table with one chair next to it. These items were made from wood in contrast to all the stone and brick buildings. He tested out the mattress that was again just some straw stuck into some cloth.

He smirked to himself while thinking that he was already used to bad sleeping conditions like these. Before he sat to work he found where the Singing Crow let guests wash and rinsed the train week off his neck and arms. Then he sat down on the chair and started unpacking his belongings. He placed his quill along with some other materials for drawings on the table. He could finally draw the schematics for that Lesser Fire Orb Rune.

**You have created the schematic for the Lesser Fire Orb Rune [High].**

**You have gained 600 experience points.**

It took him over an hour to draw this schematic. Smacking his lips, Roland reflected that it had been a week since he had drawn his last rune and that he had never drawn this rune in real life.

*Well, it's not bad, I guess.*

Looking at the rune it was neater than his first drawings but still uneven. Even if he could vividly remember what Michelangelo's David looked like didn't mean he could sculpt it out or even draw it instantly.

Looking at the drawing more closely, he could see the correct lines appearing and where he had made mistakes more clearly. Looking at it closely it wasn't even all that different. But that is still what separated a high level rune from a highest level rune.

Putting a thin sheet of paper over the first, Roland was able to quickly trace the correct version in a fraction of the time.

**You have created the schematic for the Lesser Fire Orb Rune [Highest].**

**You have gained 400 experience points.**

Looking at the three schematics that he had, Roland considered the possibilities. While the experience was barely worth it compared to adventuring, the knowledge contained within the runes had endless potential.

Roland looked at each schematic. In order to master runes he would need to understand exactly what each part of each rune did and how they could be manipulated. Eventually with even more samples there would be little he couldn't achieve after creating his own runes.

He nodded to himself while rubbing his chin. From the Fire Orb trial he could name the four large regions and what each stage did in the chain: mana collector, fire core, shape and constraint. Even though the other two runes he had did not have explanations or books like the Fire Orb, their linear structure and overall progression indicated that they must operate on similar rules.

From what he was able to understand, every rune had a number of segments that did different things. These magical pathways carried the mana or energy from the user through the rune to create an effect. From his perspective all runes and by extension magic would follow some sort of law or causality that it was designed for.

Each segment was clear. The Fire Orb trial book had explained each part of that particular rune and his skill started filling in more details but they were quite sparse at his low level.

He tickled his nose with his quill while pondering the universe. There were so many ideas in his mind but just thinking about them wouldn't realize them. He would have to experiment and learn and improve.

Roland nearly couldn't contain his excitement as he wondered what the future might look like. Before randomly making up runes, he first tried to understand what he could do to make the process more efficient. He wondered if he could make a modular system out of scraps of paper.

He tried drawing some of the large segments onto a smaller piece of paper and placing them over another stage on the Fire Orb rune. His Diagnosis skill did not activate. It seemed that the whole linear chain had to be drawn on one piece of paper as a whole.

Roland clicked his tongue.

"*Tch, too bad about that, but given my understanding of how runes work, it shouldn't be too difficult.*"

Leaning back onto Ned, Roland looked at his quill. It wasn't a bad tool and even better when he used Mana Hands to refill it, which let him practice multitasking and several of his skills. When he got really focused it wasn't as reliable as a pencil. While Carwen wasn't the best place to get supplies, Roland was sure that he would be able to get proper tools here in Edelgard. Everything happened fast so he only brought over other necessities, like food, water and clothes.

His work continued into the night. Redrawing a single large segment was a lot easier without drawing all the pathways that joined the chain. He knew what the Fire Orb stages did in order. What he still lacked was practice at changing that order on purpose.

If he could swap a shape stage for a constraint stage or feed a different converter from the same collector he might build new effects from the same linear rules. Detonation and Impact were built from the same kind of large-stage chain. More samples would show him which stages were shared and which ones were unique.

There were many possibilities but they would have to wait till tomorrow as he was now dead tired. He also needed to check the stores in the city to either find a place to work or try doing it himself. He had a reason to believe that selling his scrolls would be difficult by himself so he wanted to find a regular job first.

He blew out one of the candles that he had brought with himself. Tomorrow he would finally begin his new life in this place.

# Chapter 20

Roland woke up the next day earlier than expected. The sound of carriages moving and people talking filled his ears. The residents in this city started the day a bit faster than back in Carwen. They also made a lot more noise. He could hear the sounds of hammers hitting metal even from here.

Scratching Ned's chin, Roland waited until he had grabbed on and then kipped up onto his feet. There was little fatigue that couldn't be solved with a nice warm bed. Packing up his items into his bag he went downstairs.

Fortunately, breakfast was slightly less bland than before. While the gruel was still very much mealy and plain, it had a pinch of salt and the aroma of meat even though he didn't see any actual pieces. The common people did not really have much seasoning besides salt and pepper. The small gnome girl Helci was up and about today. He decided to try not to bump into her again as to not be disrespectful. He needed to inspect the new city. Besides this inn and the Adventurer's Guild, there were other interesting places to see.

First he needed to find a general store for seasonings. A city this large had to have something. He also had to find a specialty store with items for Scribes. He needed to get himself some paper or blank scrolls made from monster skin. He was not sure which ones were better but it would probably be easier to draw on paper than rough scrolls.

He kept looking around quite curious about the people living here. He had been in this world for six years now. The time that he had spent adventuring was already about nine months: three months of goblins alone, then half a year with the girls. The adventurers were a peculiar bunch but he found them a well-knit group.

There were still dangers in this world though. Bandits and monsters prowled in the night and you could get yourself a death sentence if you bumped into a person in a high enough position. Luck was on his side as besides that one encounter he had managed to fly under the radar. This was probably thanks to the adventurer party he had joined. The three girls were quite cautious in their hunts.

He did not show it back then but he noticed that they were different from the usual adventurer group. They did not wander into the deeper parts of the dungeon even though they should be strong enough. He had reason to believe that they were not willing to go there with a child-like him.

He shook his head around, trying to forget about the recent but now old days. He needed to get some shopping done and he had reached one of the larger stores. He entered inside and could hear a bell ringing the moment he pushed the door open.

He could see some people inside looking at the items on display. The first thing he noticed were the long display cases. They had items placed in them with small text which showed the name and the pricing akin to modern-day price tags.

There were some bookcases placed at the walls and some decorations in the form of shields and swords at the walls. This was a general item store so you could find things like potions, scrolls and even provisions here.

"*Good day customer, feel free to look around the store.*"

He heard someone calling out to him. In front behind the display cases stood an older gentleman. He had large glasses on and was partially balding with some hair on the sides. He was wearing a heavy apron over his regular clothes with some tools sticking out from the pockets.

Roland nodded and moved closer to the display cases. He could see some potions of various grades. They had the same grading system as the runes, ranging from lesser all the way up to legendary. Here he found mostly lesser and common ones though.

*Oh, they do have some scrolls here!*

In one of the display cases he saw some rolled-up scrolls. This one he was looking at had a Mana Arrow spell inscribed onto it. He looked at the price and saw that this one went for three small silver coins. He was not sure if that was a large sum for an item like this so he compared it to monster kills, the value of monster stones and the price on a monster's head.

Typical scrolls were useful but only in specific cases. One was when the spell itself was effective and the mage simply could not cast it, whether from the wrong affinity or from lacking the spell. The other was when the scroll could be made cheaply enough compared to the enemy being fought that the coin spent still made sense.

A goblin fight rarely cleared that second bar. If a stone from a goblin could make several scrolls then the shop price could look fair on paper but buying one to kill that same goblin was still a poor trade unless you liked gambling. Against something worth more than the scroll the math flipped.

Most mages still found the work time consuming and boring. They would rather get a higher tier mage class than take a boring crafting class.

He looked further and spotted more scrolls that were being sold. The next one in line was a Fire Arrow spell scroll. This was a direct upgrade from a Mana Arrow spell and was tier 2 magic. A person making this scroll had to be a scribe and at least a tier 2 elemental fire mage.

The cost of this spell was about twice what the inferior one cost. Roland was a bit surprised by it only being this much. He was inclined to think that this spell should cost a lot more. It was always best to ask when you had some questions instead of overthinking so he looked at the store owner and gave his question.

"*Excuse me, why does this Fire Arrow spell only cost six small silvers when it's a tier 2 spell while this Mana Arrow spell costs three small silvers?*"

The store owner moved a bit closer to look at what his customer was looking at and then gave the answer.

"*Even though it's a tier 2 spell it isn't that much more potent. It also depends on the person making these scrolls and materials. If it was a Fireball spell it would cost about three large silvers per scroll.*"

"*Also dear customer, these scrolls get identified by a professional that can distinguish their true attacking value. An Arch-mage could very well create a simple Mana Arrow spell that would be several times stronger than a Fire Arrow spell. It all depends on the person making it.*"

The shop owner was quite happy to explain while taking out one of the scrolls and showing them off. These scrolls also had gradings just as the runes. They were a bit different as the determining factor was not how cleanly they were made but how strong the spell was designed to be. The people made it simple and rated them from Lowest to Highest and they were checked by a special device before a price was assigned.

"*I see, thank you.*"

Roland nodded and wondered how high his grading would be if he wrote down his own Mana Arrow and Mana Bolt spell. He had a lot more Intelligence and mana than a regular mage so he should be able to make more powerful spell scrolls.

Before asking more questions Roland went around the store and found the things he was looking for. He found some magic ink and the more crude looking monster skin scrolls. He also looked for some paper and pencils but could only find quills and paper. He asked for a pen and the owner pointed to a set of fountain pens with ornate finery and a very high price tag behind the counter. Ignoring the pen, Roland asked the manager about the scarcity of magic paper.

Paper was normally affordable but couldn't beat monster hide, not only for its availability but because the magical trees the paper was made from were hard to find in this region.

The empty scrolls came in bundles of ten and they were close to the size of A4 paper. This meant that they were not all that big but considering the spells he was going to write on them were not that high level this was enough. Larger spells required larger scrolls but these were enough for tier 1 and tier 2 spells.

The ten blank scrolls cost him 5 small silver, which put them at 5 large copper coins apiece. Ink for ten Mana Arrows would run about another 10 small silver. Materials came to 15 small silver. At the shop's Intermediate price of three small silver each the ten would sell for 30 small silver. Subtracting the store's cut or auction percentage that was still a tidy profit, depending on the demand. Though going alone he would have to eat those losses and pay full price for the material. There was also the fact that most of the scrolls he found were quite dusty and old.

Roland wondered how much more he could make if he joined one of the existing groups. If he worked for someone he would not need to worry about manufacturing costs and he would be still able to earn experience points while getting paid.

He also did not mention that he was from a noble house. The most important clause though was the one stating that the gnome could not undersell his work. There was a minimal price negotiated while taking into account what the scrolls were selling in the auction. Any other items he created would be negotiated further but he did not feel like the gnome was trying to rip him off, at least not too much. He still wanted to make a profit.

He would not be surprised if they knew that. How far the gnome's identification skill was able to peek was unknown. He himself could only use it on certain items and he would mostly just get the general name. He could not analyze people well either. If he looked at Zilyana the only info he got was that she was a Sun Elf.

This was the default elven race around here. Besides Sun Elves there were also Moon Elves that were similar looking to dark elves from most stories. For one reason or another these two races did not get along with each other.

The woman rolled up the contract after it was finalized. He had finally accepted the offer and his life as a contract worker had begun. Luckily he did not really have to make that many scrolls and should be able to do his research as well. He was even going to receive a personal assistant if he did well.

He was led down into the basement. He did not occupy any of the rooms closer to the manager's room. The place he received was a bit dim and had a very dungeon-like vibe to it. He had gone through some dungeon training against goblins so he was fine with places like this.

"*We weren't really using this one. That's why it's a bit dirty.*"

Zilyana said while opening the door and showing Roland in. There was not much inside this room. Some empty boxes in the corner along with a wooden table and some chairs. The cleaning lady would come soon and some furniture would be carried over as well.

"*Here is the key. Don't lose it.*"

He was handed the key. He would be able to come to this place whenever he wanted and even work through the nights. The gnome manager knew that some craftsmen liked to work hard. A bed would be supplied for him. Roland was thinking about moving from the inn here. He did not need to pay for rent but the air was kind of stale.

"*You can start working tomorrow. Welcome to Grimboodle's Magic Emporium's staff.*"

"*You can call me big sis from now on!*"

The elf struck a pose by placing her hands to her hips while looking at Roland. He just looked back with narrowed eyes while replying.

"*Alright.*"

He almost forgot that he was an 11-year-old kid. Which had its pros and cons.

Roland also asked if the manager was the owner of this store and if the store was named after him. It was revealed that he was only someone akin to a regional manager of a larger company. It had a lot of chain stores strung throughout the city and the main one was in the capital city. When he asked the elf about the gnome's full name she just smiled at him without revealing it.

Roland was getting the facilities and the basic materials. He would not be getting the research materials though. That was on him. He returned to the first floor and bought out every single runic spell that he could. With the contract in place he felt like he could invest in his future more.

He did not inform his new boss about his unique skill. Even if the gnome saw his status screen, the name Diagnosis probably did not ring a bell. He got some new spells that did various things. The orb of light spell felt similar to his fire orb spell as it just produced a light of a different color.

He made sure to find spells that worked similarly. So if he already figured out how to produce the fire arrow spell, he wanted to get his hands on a water arrow spell or an earth arrow spell. He could then compare the schematics and see if the parts or runes were similar.

This store had a limited variety of these spells. This forced him to search them elsewhere. Luckily the manager had supplied him with an armband that would hide his status. He would seem like an ordinary Mana Scribe to everyone else.

After a day of searching he had managed to find a couple of elemental variations of the mana bolt spell and the mana arrow spells. Surprisingly there were none of the basic lesser runes of those two spells. Probably the runesmiths creating these deemed those two spells below them.

He returned to the inn after a full day of running around town. Helci was running around the inn again. He was glad that she was still alive. He was slightly worried that she might have wandered into the woods again. He hoped that she had learned her lesson but he did not really have the time to be her nanny. She had to make her own decisions.

The next day Roland returned to the store. His office was now filled with some boxes that had blank scrolls in them. There were a couple of higher quality inkwells that were supplied and some sketching paper as well. He brought some of his own crafting utensils from the inn as he was already used to the quill he was using. He closed the door behind him and walked around the place before taking a seat at his new desk.

"*Well, time to get to work.*"

This was still his job so he decided to perform his main task of scribing by recreating his staple runic fire arrow spell. He was able to scribe it down within an hour and had enough mana left to make another one. The contract stated that he was to make a minimum of ten common grade spell scrolls a week. He was of course allowed to make more of them and would get a bonus accordingly.

Roland was not only interested in the money though. He wanted to progress. Increase his levels and also to find out how these runes operated. The problem was that to progress he needed money and lots of it. He managed to get a temporary sponsor but it was on him if he proved himself as a sound investment.

He brought out a scroll that he had bought. It had the runic frost arrow spell on it. He also had a runic gale arrow scroll. He took out a pencil and some paper and activated his Diagnosis skill.

**You have created the schematic for the Common Frost Arrow Rune [Highest]**

**You have created the schematic for the Common Gale Arrow Rune [Highest]**

He received 2000 XP for each schematic. He had also tried making schematics of lower grades to see if he could stack more experience. He made an intermediate one of a common rune for 1000 XP. When he went for the perfected one he was rewarded with another thousand. The trick did not work. There was some kind of cap on gaining experience from making schematics.

He had spent his first working days scribing the minimal number of runic scrolls while using the rest of his time on creating schematics of the various runic spells that he got from the other stores. They were mostly of the common grade which caused him to level up faster.

He wanted to stick all of the made schematics to a wooden notice board so that he could look at them while doing his research. He abandoned this notion as he did not want to leave a board with multiple Highest graded rune schematics out in the open. This would give away his secrets right from the get-go.

He was sure to include a clause in the contract that there would not be any spying devices or people watching over his room. Still he did not know if those contracts were 100% safe so he would keep those schematics in his storage bag for now. When he needed to do some research he would just take them out.

He was doing this now. He placed all of the elemental arrow spells that he had gathered. The schematic that he was the most familiar with was in the middle while the two others were to the sides. He could instantly see that some of the larger runic symbols were the same.

*That's probably the component responsible for the whole arrow part of the spell.*

He glanced at the other two spells. Besides the middle rune that was identical in all the other spells, there were four other symbols. Each of these runes was composed of smaller parts and they all connected with each other to form a complete spell schematic. He just needed to figure it out. This was easier said than done.

*I wonder what will happen if I rearrange some of these components...*

He had all the correct pathways on the schematics. What would happen if he replaced one of the fire arrow runic symbols with one from the gale arrows? Could he produce a new spell? Would it blow up? He had multiple attacking spells to work with. He could exchange the components with each other as if he was playing with legos.

*Well, there is only one way to find out...*

He began his first test. Combining the gale arrow with the fire arrow. The spells were not that different. They both produced elemental arrows. The gale arrow was a lot faster than the fire arrow but it lacked the added fire damage that the other spell had.

Firstly he drew a schematic while replacing the last runic symbol from the fire arrow spell with the one from the gale arrow. He only needed to place the pathways in the correct order and to make it a closed mana circuit to finish the deal.

He instantly moved his head closer to examine his creation. The most glaring thing he saw was the mass of red after he connected the largest runic component at the end. His Diagnosis skill was telling him that it would not work. He did not get a prompt that he had created a new spell either.

He was not deterred. He started dissecting all of these spells into parts and his research continued. Thanks to his Diagnosis skill he was saving a lot of time. He could only imagine how many blank scrolls and magic ink he would have to burn through if he did all of this manually.

**You have created the schematic for the Lesser Smoke Arrow Rune [Lowest]**

In the end a combination pattern that worked was made. He created a lesser runic spell by combining higher grade runes with each other. He theorized that combining two elements with each other was not such an easy thing. This might have been tier 3 territory as he was hoping for something like a frostfire arrow but got a glorified smokescreen instead.

There were a lot of problems with this particular rune. After combining two perfect schematics he was left with a barely working one. His Diagnosis skill showed him red lines everywhere and even some of the components needed replacing.

He had somehow managed to graft these two spells together. He shoved quite a bit of parts together while hoping for the best. His experiment showed results but the spell he created was less than stellar.

*This isn't going too well...*

He could spend months and years doing it like this. The Diagnosis skill was aiding him but he was only randomly combining the runes with each other. This could allow him to get some new spells but he would not really understand how. Also the whole process was too random and time-consuming.

His main goal was to figure out the way these runes worked. He already knew that they were similar to circuits and programs. They followed some kind of binary language and maybe there were even parts that had complex algorithms working in them that were responsible for the spell's shape or power output. His Technology skill lined up with that view.

He managed to produce this spell within a week after randomly combining the larger runes with each other. The detail in drawing these schematics also played a big role in everything. If he missed one important section the Diagnosis skill did not activate. He then had to go through the entire thing to find the part he missed.

Roland finally decided to perform some live tests. He took the lesser runes as the base for his next experiment. It was the fire orb rune and the light orb spell that were quite similar. Both produced a sphere of light that varied in brightness and the element used.

He had isolated some elements that he thought were responsible. He got to work but he was not aiming to create proper schematics. No, he wanted to use the supplied scrolls for his research.

The way the runes started was all the same. They always had the same element there that was responsible for absorbing mana from a user. It varied in size, being larger in the common grade runic spells.

A craftsman needed to close the whole circuit onto this element. You also had to fashion additional magical pathways around all the runic symbols that were not visible to the naked eye. These pathways were a bit thicker than the rest and their purpose was to siphon mana into this starting component. It was more or less akin to a power input or power plug. While the person inserting mana was seen as a big battery.

The mana would be gathered through all of the pathways and focused through this power input. The other traces would then carry the mana into the other rune parts. Sometimes it was quite linear while other times the mana was distributed to multiple of them at once. Just like in series and parallel circuits, the way the mana flowed changed depending on which circuit type was being used.

He also made a distinction between the mana flow and the mana pressure in the whole circuit. This pressure was akin to voltage in a battery. It would push the mana energy into the traces and be responsible for how large the flow was.

In a series circuit type this mana voltage would be split between its components equally and it also would cease to work if one of the parts was hindered in any way. In a parallel circuit it would not lose this pressure but it would use up mana faster. This was some of the basics. Now he continued as he needed to figure out the rest of it.

*I think this one might be something similar to a resistor.*

He glanced at both the spell diagrams of the orb spells. He focused on something that he believed looked like a resistor. The fire orb one was smaller in size than from the regular orb of light. His first test would be to replace the two with each other to see if the orb of light spell dimmed.

He went to scribing and completed the altered version of the orb of light spell. To his satisfaction this little test worked. The spell activated and the light was indeed dimmer which proved his theory to be correct.

The ones he was working here were probably fixed resistor types as he did not think he could alter the resistance mid spell activation. There were also variable ones. As their name stated they were in place to resist the injected energy that was mana. If he installed a variable resistor on a spell then depending on the quality of the material he might be able to switch it on the fly to get a stronger spell effect. It could also blow up in his face if he lowered it too much though.

*That's one down. Now to find all the other basic ones.*

Inductors, capacitors, amplifiers, fuses and more. If he managed to distinguish between all of the basic circuit components that were in these magic runes he would be able to alter the spells. What kind of changes he could bring he was not sure. Removing all of the resistance from the circuit would probably make the spell unstable.

If he wanted to connect the various runic spells that he had he needed to know this. Maybe if he knew where to remove or add the basic rune circuit components he would be able to correct all the badly made schematics he tried to combine. The fix could be really easy. Maybe he only needed to add another resistor to make a working Firegale arrow spell.

Roland needed to get the basics down first before he moved on to the real meat and potatoes of these runes. Then he might be able to figure out the inner workings of the motors. The components that were storing the spell programs inside!

He gave out a sigh. Understanding this would be hard. Luckily he had a lot of time on his hands. He had signed a three-year contract and was getting charged less for these materials. His experience was increasing at a rapid rate but the higher his level became the harder it became to level it up.

His hand moved to his stiff neck. He had spent the whole day working. He did not feel tired. This was more interesting than he anticipated. He was slowly discovering how this magic runic language ticked and it was making him all giddy. He just needed to stick with it and maybe in due time he would finally be a proper runic scholar.
