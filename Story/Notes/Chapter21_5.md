## Homemade ink + Fire Orb first

- Brews own ink from **his and Ned's blood + powdered goblin cores** (his stone-bank stock). Not Trox / butcher blood; Trox stays shop-cheap beast blood only (`BloodNotes.md`). Rough hide hosts.
- **Powdering a magic stone / goblin core (two ways he finds):** intact stones are normally extremely robust and durable.
  1. **Drain:** suck out all the mana held in the stone → it becomes **crumbly** and can be powdered.
  2. **Overload:** dump a whole lot of mana into it → also breaks, but **extremely violent, noisy, and hard to collect** the pieces. Drain is the practical path for ink.
- Same pour-cost band as shop/trial work; **clearly higher seating quality** than Ch 16 trial success / Ch 21 red practice (~**75** MP practice was waste; designed Orb cast **25**).
- **Perfects Fire Orb quickly** to the same working level shown in the class test (functional seating, clean enough to match that trial outcome) even on rough hide + homemade ink. Fast, not a month wall.
- **Immediately after** that Fire Orb lock he isolates segments **basically instantly**: **water** from the **Aqua Ball** sample, **light** from the **Orb of Light** sample. No long research beat.

### Resistance grind (hide Fire Orb patch)

## Shape family + Mana Bolt

### Reads (Arrow + Ball + Orb in hand)

- **Ball ≈ Orb** — nearly the same machine; difference is a **couple of extra lines**; Ball is **bigger**.
- **Arrow** keeps a **similar overall form** on the shaping portion but packs a **very different set of sub-symbols inside** that shaping segment.
- Shared skeletons with swap-in cores / extras; not three unrelated drawings.

### Orb / Bolt / compression

- **Orb and Bolt share the same shaping** (same initial shape).
- Difference is **compression / restraint**, not a separate Bolt outline:
  - **Orb:** soft restraint (no explode-on-form).
  - **Bolt:** **small-mana** explosive compression on that shared shape.
  - **Ball:** **moderate-mana** compression (more load than Bolt).
- Working **runic Mana Bolt** built by adding the small-mana compression block to the shared Orb-shape spine. First pure-mana runic attack scroll in his kit (0% affinity still fine).
- Designed cast **25** (same bill as Orb).

### No detect-contact on Bolt

- Even system-identified **Mana Bolt** has **no detect-contact rune** portion.
- The pop is the **compression constraint coming undone** when the packet hits anything denser than air — like a **ball of straw** coming undone on a hard hit.
- Contact triggers stay Detonation-family, not Bolt.

## Experimentation locks

### Ratios / scale

- Component **size ratios** are extremely important for how mana is processed.
- Forcing ratio changes to make parts bigger/smaller or nudge effects works **only barely** — slight altered results, heavy inefficiency.
- Scaling the **whole** diagram smaller lowers mana **input** and power **output** together (clean enough path for quiet/cheap variants).
- Enlarging the **simple compression** segment alone to make Bolt more explosive is more likely to **implode** than to give a fiercer explosion.

### Component swaps (much more successful)

- Swapping converters / fills onto known shapes beats ratio surgery by a wide margin.
- Once he can make **Aqua Ball**, **Fireball** is almost **laughably easy**.
- Same path for **water arrow** and **light ball**.
- Lazy grafts with **unmodified ratios** still let **Diagnosis** mark what is wrong, as long as the paper does not **spontaneously burn up** (occasional).

### Freeze-flip (fails)

- Flipping the fire runic sequence upside down to freeze water does **not** work. Already in chapter prose.

### Skill feel (not a brain dump)

- As Rune Scribing / Mastery / Diagnosis rise he gets a **vague sense** of how other runes might need to change — the same feeling from messing with a **clock mechanism**.
- He does **not** get schematics dumped straight into his brain.
- Looking at runes gives **familiarity**: things will make sense the moment he sees them.
